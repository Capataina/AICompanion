This folder answers one question for the layers above: what does this weapon actually do. It decides nothing. The arsenal in `../Interactions/Firing/` reads it every time it values an attack — the arithmetic is the prior and these tables correct it — and the firing interaction's hooks feed it every observation. That split landed in phase 0 of the combat plan, which moved the two learners out of the dissolved `Companion/Weapons/` with no logic change; phase B grew `Recording/` and the volley learner beside them on 16 September 2026, and phase C grew `Simulation/` and replaced the four-number arc with fitted flight laws. Knowledge survives a character save under the item's full name; a planted extra-projectile or pierce modifier changes the next simulated use without relearning.

## A hit pushes, and what it pushes into is charged to the shot

Every hit the companion lands pushes its enemy, and the push is part of what the hit is worth. The owner's playtest of 15 September 2026 showed the companion knocking enemies into the player and into itself again and again, because nothing modelled knockback at all: an arrow from the far side of a zombie was worth exactly what the same arrow from the player's side was worth. Three pieces answer it, and they divide by what each can know.

**What a hit does is learned per weapon item and per enemy type, with the game's own arithmetic as the prior.** `LearnWeaponEffectsOnEnemies.cs` holds, for each pair, a signed push factor on the prior's horizontal velocity change and a damage ratio on the arsenal's own damage prediction, each a median over a small ring of recent samples. The prior is `NPC.GetIncomingStrikeModifiers` and `NPC.StrikeNPC` as decompiled: the knockback is multiplied by the enemy's resistance, a resistance of zero disables it, it is compressed above a series of bands and capped, and then one of two branches runs by whether ten times the damage (fifteen in expert) exceeds the enemy's maximum life. A heavy hit moves the horizontal speed toward the knockback in the hit's direction and clamps there; a light hit sets it to the knockback times the direction times the resistance **again**, so the resistance lands squared on that branch. The native-match row holds the prior to the game's own strike on both branches. It is learned rather than read because knockback resistance differs per enemy, bosses resist it entirely, and a mod can change a hit's knockback or damage inside a hook no reading reaches. The key is the item rather than the projectile, because a push is the item's knockback plus its ammo's, and a swing has no projectile. The factor is signed so a weapon whose pushes go the other way from the direction the prior assumed learns the direction by the same arithmetic as the magnitude. Only the horizontal is learned, because only the horizontal side decides whether a push carries an enemy toward the player or the orb.

The direction the prior assumes is the game's. A projectile's hit is pushed along its own `direction`, which `Projectile.Update` sets from the sign of its horizontal speed, and a swing along the side of its aim — except for the types `Projectile.Damage` overrides to push away from the **owner**, which for every companion shot is the player: types 697, 699, 707, 708 and 759, flails (aiStyle 15) and the 1.4.4 swing projectiles (aiStyles 188 to 191). The flails never reach a slot; the swing projectiles do, because the slot's predicate refuses flails, spears, yoyos and whips by style and these are none of them. That is recorded rather than fixed here: whether the arsenal should fire a projectile that follows its owner is a gear decision, and until it is made the push rule is right about them.

Samples arrive from two places, because the two kinds of use reach the game by two paths. A projectile's strike passes through the NPC hooks: the modify hook runs before `StrikeNPC` writes the push, so `TrackLandedHits` keeps each struck NPC's velocity there, and the on-hit hook reads the velocity the strike left and teaches the table. A swing's strike runs no NPC hook, so `ItemWeapon` observes around its own call. A sample is skipped where it cannot be read: a crit, whose extra push the prior does not model; a push the prior predicts as nothing, which is an enemy already at the speed the heavy branch clamps to; and a killing swing, whose body is gone and whose push therefore says nothing. The table is session state, never saved, cleared by process restart and by the engine replay's per-case reset.

**The arsenal charges a hit for the danger its push adds, and never for danger the enemy already carried.** For each body a forecast strikes, `Arsenal.InducedDanger` displaces the enemy horizontally by the push the table expects — the push's added speed held for a settle window, since a knocked walker keeps that speed until its own AI turns it, and its airtime would read every push as a pixel — and re-weighs its urgency to the player, and to the orb standing at the muzzle the forecast is asked about, at the displaced centre with `ThreatUrgency`, the rule the threat sense uses. The orb is weighed at the muzzle rather than at the live body because the same forecast prices candidate stands, and a push toward the stand the orb would fire from is the push that comes back at it. Only an increase is charged, per body, times what one of that enemy's hits takes off that body; speed, sight and reach are held at what the sense decided this tick on both sides of the difference, which is what keeps the baseline out. The charge rides on the hit into the evaluator, which subtracts it from a wound and never from a kill, because a dead enemy is pushed nowhere. It is weighted by the same learned factor as the hit's damage, capped at one, so a weapon the learner has seen miss is charged only for the pushes it lands; the factor above one is damage the forecast did not see, not extra pushes, which the table already learns per hit. Sight is not re-tested at the displaced centre, so a push that brings an enemy into a sight line it lacked is under-charged; the alternative is a line-of-sight test per hit per forecast. The assumed attacks that price a reposition carry no charge, because their stand is not chosen yet and the positioner prices the side at the stand.

**A wound to a dangerous enemy is worth something before it kills.** The evaluator used to credit an enemy's expected harm only on the killing hit, so a wound to a zombie beside the player was worth what the same wound to a harmless slime was. A hit that does not kill now earns a share of that credit in proportion to the fraction of the enemy's remaining life it removes, weighted by the enemy's danger; the kill still earns the full credit on top of whatever its earlier wounds earned.

## What a weapon's attacks achieve is learned, and weapon, target, stand and aim are one decision over it

The owner ruled on 15 September 2026 that a decision evaluates every option and learns from what its own actions did, and asked that learning how to use a weapon go together with choosing which weapon and which target. Before this, the arsenal's arithmetic was the whole answer: a homing staff was valued as though its bolt flew the straight trace, a splitting shot as though it never split, and a debuff as nothing, so any weapon whose value lived outside the trace was used for the wrong reasons or not at all. No shipped game learns weapon effectiveness at run time; the research behind this weighed contextual bandits, Gaussian-process optimisation, a prior with a residual, and case-based memory, and a contextual bandit won because it learns unmodelled behaviour from outcomes alone, takes aim as an input so each weapon finds out whether aim matters to it, explores by construction, and costs little per decision.

**The arithmetic is the prior and the learner corrects each forecast hit's damage by a factor.** The forecast still solves the arc, walks the bodies the flight crosses, reads each hit's damage after armour through the weapon-effects table and prices the push charge; that is the prior mean. `AttackLearning` (`LearnAttackOutcomes.cs`) then multiplies each hit's damage by what this weapon's attacks have achieved against their forecasts in this context. With nothing observed for a weapon the factor is exactly one and no random number is drawn, which is what keeps every forecast, and every combat fixture that never fires, identical to the arithmetic.

**What is learned is the outcome ratio minus one, per weapon item, by Bayesian linear regression sampled with Thompson sampling.** When a use's window closes, its realised yield — damage landed plus a value per body struck, per second of the weapon's use — is divided by the yield the forecast predicted and clamped, and the learner regresses that ratio less one on the context the use was fired in: a bias, distance as a share of the weapon's reach, the aim's offset from the intercept as a share of the widest candidate, the target's speed relative to the orb, the other hostiles along the lane, the orb's own speed, and whether the target carries a debuff the other weapon applied. A plain difference lost because it is in each weapon's damage units, and a log-ratio lost because a miss, the commonest outcome of an aim-sensitive weapon, has no logarithm. Each outcome is a rank-one update of the posterior mean and covariance, the covariance's Cholesky factor is rebuilt only when the posterior changes, and a decision draws one coefficient vector per weapon per tick that every target, stand and aim candidate in that tick shares. **Every draw a decision reads is kept for the decision**: an enemy type the weapon has never struck used to add a fresh number on each call, so the aim candidates inside one forecast were compared against different noise, and a fixture's seed reproduced nothing. **Whether a weapon lands on an enemy type is that type's fact; how the context changes what lands is the weapon's.** Each enemy type carries the intercept — the outcome at the contexts that type has been shot in, shrunk toward the arithmetic — and the context coefficients are shared by every type but learned only from how outcomes moved as the context moved within one type, each observation's context and outcome measured from that type's running means with the scaling that makes the online update equal a regression on fully centred data. That is the fixed-effects form of a panel regression, and it is the only shape of the three weighed in which one type's misses cannot reach another type: a type missed at one context cannot tell "this type is missed" from "shots here are missed", so a coefficient that learns from raw contexts carries the misses to every type, and letting the type absorb the error first only halves it. The first shape put the intercept in the weapon model and handed each type the residual, and three demon-eye misses left a never-shot zombie at a mean factor of 0.36 with no target found on 37 of 200 decisions (12 on the same evidence as hits). A type never shot reads the arithmetic at the weapon's mean context and borrows nothing from other types, which costs exploration: context coefficients with no within-type evidence keep their prior variance, so on 15 September 2026 the same scene found no target on 19 of 200 decisions whether the eye shots had missed or landed.

**A use's outcome window holds its projectile and every projectile descended from it.** The windows live in `../Interactions/Firing/ObserveShotOutcomes.cs`, because opening one is part of firing; this folder only reads what they teach. A window opens when the arsenal fires, against the forecast's own prior — the damage it predicted before any correction, the bodies it expected to strike and the charge it carried — and the context with the aim as it actually left, noise included. The global spawn hook in `TrackLandedHits.cs` forgets a slot's window first and then, when the spawn's source is `EntitySource_Parent` naming a projectile whose slot belongs to an open window, joins the child to that window; the companion's own spawn names the companion NPC, never a projectile, so it never attributes itself. The window closes when its last projectile dies or its bound elapses. **A use whose aimed body was gone before the forecast had it landing, and which never struck that body, teaches the learner nothing**: the player, a trap or another shot killed it, or its slot went to a new enemy, so a zero there is an outcome of the fight rather than of the weapon, and a companion fighting beside the player would otherwise learn that it misses whatever he finishes first. A body that leaves after the landing tick is a miss as usual, and the struck bodies' debuffs are taught either way. The NPC modify hook keeps the struck body's buffs and the on-hit hook compares them, because the game applies a projectile's status between the two (decompiled `Projectile.Damage`), so the diff is exactly what that hit added; each added buff is remembered against the body and the item, which is what "debuffed by the other weapon" reads. A swing's window opens and closes around its own strikes, which `ItemWeapon` reports with the buffs each body carried before.

**A debuff is a rate and a context, and the evaluator carries it through the continuation.** For each weapon and enemy type the learner counts how many struck bodies came away carrying a buff they had not, and for how long. Every forecast hit carries two damages — in the body's current state and against a body the other weapon has debuffed — and that weapon's chance and length of debuff. Once an applied attack lands in the continuation, its body counts as debuffed by that weapon until the debuff runs out, and a later hit from the other weapon is valued between its two damages by that chance. That is how a debuff-then-burst pair opens with the debuff without an eligibility trace or a written combination rule.

**Aim is a decision variable whose prior is the intercept's.** A forecast asks the learner about the intercept and a few offsets off it, all sharing the intercept's geometry, and keeps the aim whose learned factor on the aimed target is largest; a straight arrow learns that aiming off costs its whole value and keeps the intercept, a homing bolt learns that it does not matter. Because the aim input is the offset's size and the model is linear, the answer is either the intercept or the widest candidate, never something in between. An offset shot is fired only if it flies clear of terrain for the intercept's flight (`TrajectoryAimer.TryClear`), since reaching the target's box is what it is finding out; one that meets a wall falls back to the intercept. A swing has no launch to aim.

**Exploration stops when a listed threat's urgency to either body is above the declared ceiling**, and then every forecast uses the posterior mean instead of a draw, so the companion does not try a worse attack while something is about to hurt someone. What crosses that ceiling is narrower than "danger" reads: urgency is the threat sense's rule (`../Observation/WeighThreatUrgency.cs`), the hit's share of a quarter of the body's remaining life, floored low and capped at one, times closeness and a sight factor. An ordinary enemy's hit against a healthy, armoured player is a small share, so it stays under the ceiling even at contact; the gate closes for a boss, whose weight is one, for an enemy whose hit is a large share of what a hurt player's or a hurt companion's left, and for a shooter with a sight line close enough to matter. Against a crowd of ordinary enemies at full health the companion keeps exploring, which is the gate as built rather than a defect in it. **A firing stand is priced by every weapon in hand**: the positioner reads the best learned attack any weapon could make from a stand, at arrival, as a share of the best those weapons could do with no geometry in the way (`../Position/CLAUDE.md`), which closed AIC-395.

What this deliberately does not do. **Damage over time is not counted**: life a debuff burns after the hit arrives through no hit hook, and crediting a struck body's later life loss would also credit the player's own attacks, so a weapon whose value is its burn is under-credited by exactly its burn. **The push is not in the learned ratio**: the weapon-effects table already learns each weapon's push per enemy type from the same hits and the forecast charges it, so putting the realised push into the ratio as well would count it twice; the closed use's reward subtracts the forecast's charge for the record only.

## The engine's hook order, as the learners are written against it

Phase 0 read the decompiled `Projectile.Update`, `Main`'s projectile block and `ProjectileLoader`'s dispatch rather than trusting the plan's sketch, and the learners below are written against what that probe established. Line numbers are the decompile's, kept so the next reader can re-check rather than re-derive.

Per tick, `Main` runs `PreUpdateProjectiles`, each projectile's `Update`, then `PostUpdateProjectiles`. Inside one `Update`, the `extraUpdates` loop runs first (`numUpdates = extraUpdates; while >= 0`): each sub-step runs `AI()` — `PreAI` across every global hook AND-combined, then `ModProjectile.PreAI`; if all pass, `VanillaAI` and then `AI` on the mod projectile and every global; then `PostAI` on the mod projectile and every global, which runs even when `PreAI` vetoed — then `Damage()`, the NPC collision, then `timeLeft--` with the kill at zero. So a `GlobalProjectile.PostAI` observer sees one velocity per sub-step, after the AI and before anything moves, and NPC hits land per sub-step too.

Tile collision and movement run once per `Update`, after the loop: `lastVelocity` is captured from the velocity the last sub-step's AI left, `Collision.TileCollision` overwrites the velocity, and `OnTileCollide` receives that pre-collision velocity with the globals AND-combined before the mod projectile's own — then the vanilla bounce-or-kill handling, then `UpdatePosition`. A `ModSystem.PostUpdateProjectiles` observer therefore sees every projectile once per tick, settled after all of its sub-steps and its move.

A full `Projectile.Update` does not run on the EngineReplay host. A temporary probe (thrown away after it answered) news an arrow and calls `Update`: it dies on the first call inside `HandleMovement` → `Collision.SwitchTiles` → `TileLoader.SwitchTiles`, on a null `HookSwitchTiles` table, which only a mod load builds. So headless ground truth stays `VanillaAI` plus the engine's `Collision.TileCollision`, and the plan's fallback for its bounce row stands.

## Every flight watched, every use grouped, every volley learned

Phase B watches every projectile use, the player's included, because he fires most weapons many times before handing one over. `RecordProjectileFlights` opens a trace for each: his from the item-use source, the companion's explicitly from the hand (its source names no item and no aim), and every child of a watched flight from its parent source. Each step arrives twice — post-AI velocities per sub-step before movement, settled positions once per tick after it — with walls, body hits and the death cause filed beside them. Use samples feed the arc learner until phase C's fitter replaces it; children trace only, because a retargeting child would bend its type's gravity. A companion shot's death files one `shot-event` record: the bounded-per-shot form of its walls, hits, children and cause.

`GroupSpawnsIntoUses` turns the spawn stream into uses. A player use opens when his animation resets and collects every spawn until it ends, which captures same-tick spreads, sky rains and bursts across the animation with one rule; a companion use is opened and closed by the firing call itself. A use that spawned nothing is a swing or a tool stroke, not a volley: kept for inspection, taught and recorded nowhere.

`LearnVolleyShapes` keeps, per item, the count distribution and the slots ordered by delay then angle, each with its angle, speed ratio, damage share, origin and delay. The origin is whichever anchor — muzzle, aim point, a learned height above it, or around the shooter — leaves the smallest offset variance, so the sky-rain case is found without being named. Shapes learn only from the player's uses: the companion's own volleys are reproductions of the medians, and feeding them back would narrow every slot toward what it already fires. Uses under count-changing buffs the companion lacks are counted aside and teach nothing; no vanilla buff is verified to change a count, so the set is empty and the mechanism waits on it. The hand expands the shape at its medians when it fires.
