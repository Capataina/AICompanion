# Activity coordination — one grant for movement and the hand

The brain's branches propose controls and the common finaliser applies them through the companion motor. The granted record binds the current activity, its phase, the request and the motor's actual AI-phase output. A request for downed holding can become recovery clearance inside terrain, so requested owner and applied owner remain distinct.

```
Grants/
├─ CLAUDE.md
├─ GrantActivityControls.cs          movement application and the compatible hand permission snapshot
├─ DescribeActivitySnapshot.cs       completed family/activity and shared-controller presentation state
└─ ConsiderIncidentalInteractions.cs in-passing pot and torch uses from the current pose, proposed to the course and credited to no activity
```

Distant recovery flight lives in `../../SharedBehaviours/Recovery/`. It is a shared behaviour, not a grant helper.

**An incidental interaction is a proposal the course accepts, never a choice made here, since 23 September 2026.** After the hands run, on an ordinary execution tick only (recovery and downed grants belong to responses that own the body for another purpose), `ConsiderIncidentalInteractions` may act when the final grant left the hand Available and the arm did not fire that tick. On a BehaviourWeights cadence it takes the census's own tile sites from the course's current frozen observation — collection's pots and lighting's torch sites — whose work tile is within the tool's reach of the body, nearest first, skipping every site the published course holds — the executing step's because that activity will do it and one benefit must not be counted twice, and a later step's because breaking it early would invalidate that step and replace the running course, which acceptance may not do. Each is put to `DecideCourseEachTick.AcceptIncidental`, which builds a zero-travel one-step binding through the domain's real binder (`AssistanceOpportunityBinder.BindInPlace`), validates it with the same `ValidateNextUse` a retained step is held to, and refuses it when the danger the course prices optional work against leaves its need worth nothing — the same `CourseComparisonEpisode.RelevanceFor` over the same encounter intensity and protection urgency, so a boss, a surface blood moon or a threatened player that stops the course breaking a pot stops the hand breaking one in passing (`incidental-optional-work-suppressed-by-danger`). Only an accepted binding reaches the native call, after the method's own preconditions are asked of the live world once more; the interaction event's detail carries the binding's id, so a reader joins the effect to the acceptance that licensed it, and the course keeps its last answer in `LastIncidental` and writes it to the course trace when it changes. Acceptance replaces no course and switches no activity: the step is zero travel from the current pose, so nothing about where the body goes is decided here.

**What it replaced is the plan's post-grant second chooser.** Until then the scan ran each method's candidate check over every tile in reach and made the native call on the first that passed, pricing nothing, so it broke pots in the middle of a boss fight the course had just refused to break a pot in (`Tools/EngineReplay/Assistance/VerifyAssistanceTrips.cs`'s `A3` row, which removing the relevance check reds). It never requests a position, writes movement or records productive work, so no activity's value or attempt conclusion includes the effect. It is optional work at the end of a tick planning may already have filled, so a tick whose shared planning allowance is spent skips the scan without using up its cadence, and the scan stops between proposals if the allowance runs out part way. A detour that needs a new destination is not incidental; it is a course step. Contact pickup of loose items already behaves this way in `CharacterBody` and is not part of this component.

**The plan asks for this source to be consumed before grants, and it still runs after them**, because the free hand it needs is the grant's own answer and the brief that built it kept the grant check; what moved is that the scan no longer decides anything the course has not accepted. A torch site the census withheld by rank is refused as not observed rather than guessed at.

Each grant also names the activity attempt open when it was issued, or zero when none is executing. A recovery or downed grant issued while the ordinary attempt is suspended therefore carries zero, which is what stops a reader charging that movement to the interrupted work.

Available hands permit the independent arsenal and later held light; they do not prove a shot or torch use. WorkTool reserves the hand throughout the activity's coherent tool phase, including strike cooldowns. Unavailable is the downed lifecycle grant. Native interaction effects and the subsequently integrated body remain separate observations. Diagnostics consume the retained grant rather than making another decision or applying controls themselves.

The brain publishes ActivitySnapshot after its final control, hand and progress handling, including recovery and downed paths. Family, activity, activity identity, phase and shared-controller flags therefore refer to one completed coordination step. The HUD consumes that record without invoking a chooser or inspecting an independent set of mutable controllers. The snapshot's tick denotes publication, not a fresh utility comparison or a successful interaction.

Distant recovery requires an explicit WithPlayer reunion request with no occupied tool, beyond the recovery distance in the current companion preferences. Admission depends on that purpose rather than the action's class. Guarding, hunting, collecting and work destinations do not grant recovery even when their coordinates equal the player's. The coordinator interrupts the route before asking the sole motor for continuous flight through terrain. Flight ends near the owner only with a clear body, cancels on downing or owner death, and never becomes a traversal or archive entry. This is reunion recovery, separate from shared tactical safety and future mastery movement abilities.
