# World observation — one shared account of the world

This folder rebuilds facts from Terraria once per brain tick. It observes; it never decides, chooses a position, plans a route, or writes movement. `CoordinateBrainTick` passes its aggregate to reflex assessment and behaviour selection, so a new fact belongs here when more than one downstream system could otherwise derive it differently.

```
Observation/
├─ CLAUDE.md                 this guide
├─ Senses.cs                 aggregate and update order
├─ ObserveCompanion.cs       fire, life and recent damage; derives self danger
├─ ObservePlayer.cs          position, travel intent, fighting and sight
├─ InferPlayerActivity.cs    bounded displacement/work evidence and travel confidence
├─ ObservePlayerIntentRegion.cs where the player is going, as a box that always holds him and sits mostly above him: where the companion lives, and what every separation measures to
├─ ObservePlayerWork.cs      player tool hits and terrain-change notification
├─ ObserveThreats.cs         hostile records, chain heads, reachability, danger and safety horizon
├─ ObserveEncounterContext.cs whether the world is about one boss or event, from native facts or inferred from sustained pressure
├─ EstimateEffectiveDamage.cs what one hit takes off the player or the companion after defence, by the game's arithmetic
├─ WeighThreatUrgency.cs     the one urgency rule — a hit against life left, arrival time and sight — read by the threat sense and by the arsenal's push charge
├─ ObserveHostileAttackSources.cs native hostile projectile ownership and generation reset
├─ ObserveProjectiles.cs     hostile projectile predictions for reflexes
├─ PredictObservedMotion.cs  shared terrain-constrained enemy forecast used by aiming and reflexes
├─ CalculateRegroupUrgency.cs pure return-pressure calculation from distance and travel cost
├─ ObserveLoot.cs            collectible drops that fit a player stack or bag
├─ CaptureAssistanceOpportunities.cs sliced drop census and partial pot/light adapters before opportunity discovery
├─ CaptureLightingOpportunities.cs native torch sites and shared darkness deficits projected from one frozen light map
├─ CaptureCourseTravel.cs     retained travel queries using the native route, steering and contact model
├─ ScheduleCourseModels.cs    travel and enemy motion sharing one-operation turns under the common allowance
├─ RetainCourseModelQueries.cs one observation's native query queue and append-only model catalogue
├─ CaptureEnemyCourseMotion.cs budgeted native enemy samples certified against spatial terrain edits
├─ CaptureCourseContactCensus.cs frozen potential contact sources with explicit native-slot coverage
├─ CaptureContactVictim.cs     recorded life, defence and separate native immunity channels for each victim
├─ ResolveCapturedMeleeShape.cs vanilla victim-dependent melee geometry over captured enemy fields
├─ ProjectMeleeContactGeometry.cs captured motion and victim paths priced with frozen native defence
├─ CaptureCompanionshipInputs.cs frozen region geometry, player travel and distance preference for course costing
├─ ObserveLight.cs           brightness per open-air tile on a lattice, read from the world's own light, and one tile's placement reading
├─ ReadWorldLight.cs         the world's own light, taken from the engine between its scan and its blur, where nothing anybody carries is in it yet
├─ ObserveReach.cs           where the orb can fly to, as one flood over free corners and a tri-state every consumer reads
├─ ThreatRecord.cs           a hostile, its predicted hitbox, ChainHead and IsChainRepresentative
├─ ForecastEnemies.cs        the combat simulator's snapshot of one hostile this tick
└─ LineOfSight.cs            the named game sight query: the single-tile walk, never the three-row beam a twenty-pixel body's own floor breaks
```

`Senses.Update` runs player, the intent region, threats, the encounter, projectiles, loot, light and self observation in that order before selection reads any value; the intent region follows the player directly, because it reads his freshly observed intent and every consumer this tick must see one region rather than two, and the encounter follows threats because it reads them. The reach sense is the one member `Update` does not drive, for the reason given below. Player travel inference retains bounded two-axis displacement and local-work evidence. Net displacement relative to distance travelled distinguishes a journey from repeated local movement; sample support and recent work temper confidence. Both climbing and descent remain visible. Separate player and self danger are intentional: leaving a threatened player and walking into danger are different risks. Threats also derive a safety horizon, which selection uses to discount a behaviour that would keep the companion away too long.

`CaptureAssistanceOpportunities` is the observation-side bridge for retained-course assistance. Drop capture copies the finite item observation before slicing landing/contact work, accumulates a complete census, and publishes a shared type-and-prefix denominator only when that census finishes. The source reads its explicit coverage fact and retains the consumed unchanged input prefix across snapshot IDs. A world-item reference cannot be held as a frozen stack, and a single slice cannot retire every slot it did not examine. Item identity advances when observation sees a changed object/type; same-object replacement between observations remains unknown and native use must validate the live slot. Missing landing/contact coordinates serialize as null. The pot and lighting drafts remain incomplete: their scan/admission and overlapping persistent-light coverage do not yet meet the canonical plan and must not feed the live chooser.

`CaptureCourseTravel` retains a query from pose and momentum to a concrete destination. It runs the existing free-space search, steering and circle contact under the caller's decision allowance, records its terrain reads spatially, and returns nominal arrival time and velocity. Timed body states at segment changes and arrival retain the actual model position, including arrival slack, with storage bounded by the monotonically advancing route index. They support coarse interval costing without assuming uniform speed between route corners. A changed read footprint or capability makes the query unresolved; a distant edit leaves its result usable. Pure binders consume the immutable fact through `ReadCourseTravel`, never by searching the live world from inside hypothetical evaluation.

`ScheduleCourseModels` owns pending travel and enemy-motion queries in one queue. Requests deduplicate by their model fact key; a full pending store reports capacity pressure without evicting unfinished work. Queries rotate after one operation borrowed from the same decision budget, retaining their native search and simulation state. Enemy queries must share the owner's capture tick, terrain source and original revision: unchanged terrain does not imply an unchanged enemy pose. Validation precedes pending-key deduplication and catalogue reuse. A query captured with its owner can be queued and completed on later frames; a newly captured enemy cannot enter that older observation. Completed facts transfer to the observation owner's model catalogue and release their pending slots. That catalogue owns reuse, so callers request only absent facts. World replacement or abandonment clears the pending queue; frame advance does not. The live observation owner and its configured capacity remain part of the course-coordinator integration.

`RetainCourseModelQueries` holds the frozen observation and that pending scheduler. It indexes the captured contact census by slot and generation after validating its observation tick. A pure course projection can request enemy motion by identity, horizon and model revision; the owner constructs it from the indexed captured track and its original terrain revision. A source absent from the snapshot yields an unresolved answer through the same budgeted queue, never a live NPC lookup or a claim of known absence. Completion creates an immutable model-only extension; existing answers are reused from the catalogue, and abandonment refuses further requests. The scheduler captures its terrain revision and movement pace when the observation owner is created, not when a deferred request eventually starts. Before a query certifies either success or refusal, it checks the entire accumulated terrain footprint against that original revision, catching edits to tiles first read in later slices. The brain's live coordinator still needs to own this component and select its capacity.

`ContinueSearch` advances the frozen course search through its pending native travel requests. Queued models run before the search resumes, using the same allowance, so a tiny slice cannot be consumed forever by rebinding without advancing the model. Completed answers extend the search's frozen catalogue; newly discovered requests queue for the next call. Capacity pressure leaves the request on the suspended search for retry, rather than treating unavailable queue space as an unreachable destination.

The legacy enemy forecast is not an immutable cross-frame input. `EnemyForecast.Track` retains an NPC, `PredictedBoxAtTick` calls `PredictObservedMotion.Predict`, and that call begins by observing the live body again. `RolledCopy` retains the same NPC reference. Scalar enemy fields therefore stay captured while a later prediction can use newer motion and terrain. Retained-course consequence evaluation must consume captured motion samples with explicit coverage and dependencies; calling this live forecast from a suspended pure projection would mix observations. Eagerly extending every enemy to the maximum horizon is not a free substitute: capture must share the decision allowance and expose its unfinished coverage.

`PredictObservedMotion.Capture` provides a separate enemy-state snapshot for that native query. Its continuation borrows one operation per simulated tick and runs the same movement calculation as the live predictor, retaining the captured dimensions, physics and motion history without retaining the NPC. `CoveredTicks` distinguishes computed samples from an unanswered horizon. It still reads native terrain during collision, so the enclosing query must validate terrain evidence before treating the samples as a model of the frozen observation; this component alone does not certify the world stayed unchanged.

Captured motion exposes `ReadContains` over the accumulated native collision footprint. The footprint includes the foot rows read by `WalkDownSlope`, the skirt scanned by `TileCollision` and `SlopeCollision`, and the extra horizontal neighbours read by tile collision. Each native call records its input pose before running, including slope resolution after movement. A body with tile collision disabled records no terrain reads. These bounds derive from the decompiled collision loops; changing the native mechanism requires checking those loops again rather than selecting a behavioural safety margin.

`CaptureEnemyCourseMotion` owns one generation-and-horizon query. Partial samples stay private; completing the horizon produces a modelled fact only if the full accumulated footprint remains unchanged since query capture. Relevant edits or unavailable edit history conclude with unresolved evidence, while distant edits preserve the retained computation. The payload names entity generation, captured dimensions, sample coverage and the terminal reason. Modelled motion is still a prediction of observed continuation, not certainty about future enemy AI. The live observation coordinator and harm evaluator still need to consume these facts.

`CaptureCourseContactCensus` copies active nonfriendly NPCs with positive contact damage in native slot order, preserving generation, damage, melee geometry and the existing exported motion track. That track holds the captured position, velocity, acceleration and native physics parameters; `CaptureEnemyCourseMotion` can build a fresh private simulation from it on a later frame without observing a live NPC again. The query retains the track's original tick and accepts an explicit original terrain revision for deferred construction. Geometry and motion must agree on slot, type and initial position. The exported track's vectors carry their own JSON converter so coordinates survive both default serialization and the combat audit's field-inclusive options.

The census charges each examined slot to the shared allowance and publishes the examined/total counts with completeness. A cut returns a partial census for that observation tick; it does not resume over moving live objects on another tick. No living NPC reference survives capture. This is a census of potential contact sources, not a verdict that a victim can be hit: native victim immunity, special eligibility, loader hooks, projectiles and future AI need their own evidence. Live coordinator ownership and the allowance needed to complete observation remain integration obligations.

The positioner's legacy `PredictedHarmAt` is a stand-ranking quantity, not a timed damage-event catalogue. Its `ThreatProximity` includes a distance-based walker contact pocket even without a forecast hit, and `EvaluateAttackOutcomes.ValueParts` grants partial harm-prevention credit for a wound. Neither quantity can be copied into the retained course's harm sum as native predicted damage. That sum needs actor identity, effective damage, event time, supported forecast coverage and dependence on earlier target outcomes; a predicted kill removes later harm events rather than earning a second prevention reward. The existing native collision and observed-motion mechanisms are reusable, while their old ranking arithmetic is not the consequence contract.

Native melee geometry is victim-dependent. `NPC.GetMeleeCollisionData` receives the victim rectangle and can substitute an attack rectangle only when that victim intersects it, change the damage multiplier, or select a special hit channel. It also invokes `NPCLoader.ModifyCollisionData`. Capturing its answer once beside the live player and translating it into a universal enemy hitbox would therefore misprice another victim or pose. The course contact input has separate per-victim geometry and channel readiness; a native capture must preserve those conditions and account for loader-hook semantics before being used for hypothetical calls.

`ResolveCapturedMeleeShape` isolates the vanilla portion of that native method over `CapturedMeleeEnemy`, which contains only the geometry, facing, frame and AI fields the method reads. Resolving a hypothetical shape touches no live NPC slot and invokes no loader hook. Native type IDs, attack dimensions and multipliers are mechanics copied from the decompiled method, not behaviour tunables; the engine fixture compares the captured result against native calls across each branch's type, facing, frame, AI state and victim placement. Modded collision-hook changes remain outside this vanilla model's evidence.

`ProjectMeleeContactGeometry` combines those shapes with captured enemy centres and a victim's per-tick trajectory under the shared allowance. `EstimateEffectiveDamage.Capture` freezes the actor's native defence inputs as a serialisable value, so a later equipment change cannot alter an unfinished forecast and replay can reconstruct the same arithmetic without either living actor. Its actor kind, defence, player effectiveness, final multiplier and NPC armour/debuff flags reconstruct Terraria's modifier structs; no delegate or native object is required in the recorded value. Each contact sample carries its own effective damage and channel readiness: player contact applies the native shape multiplier before rounding, while `NPC.BeHurtByOtherNPC` uses the attacker's unmultiplied damage. The player's ordinary immunity check precedes melee geometry: a geometry-selected special channel retains that earlier gate, whereas an enemy whose base channel was already special bypasses it. The output feeds `ForecastContactHarm`; missing trajectory coverage and unsupported channels remain visible. This models the captured AI/frame state continuing, not a future attack-state transition, and does not include loader-hook changes.

`CaptureContactVictim` records each actor's life, dimensions, defence and native immunity inputs with its observation tick. Player ordinary immunity and special-channel cooldowns remain separate; the companion uses the native NPC immunity slot. Player no-aggro flags are copied with their coverage intact, and capture charges the array reads before returning a value, so an insufficient allowance yields no partial victim fact. Body-level contact enablement describes the captured state rather than proving future eligibility: per-enemy exceptions, parries, dodges and loader hooks still require their own evidence before the complete harm forecast can certify them.

**A worm is one enemy for danger and still many records for pierce, and that is not the spawn-slot rule.** `ObserveThreats` still writes every harmful segment onto `Threats` — no distance filter, every piece of a Giant Worm or Eater of Worlds — so heat, forecasts and pierce can see the spine. After the list is built, `MarkChainRepresentatives` groups by `ChainHeadOf`: vanilla worms store the head in `npc.realLife`; a live head at that slot is the chain, otherwise the NPC itself. Exactly one record per chain is `IsChainRepresentative`: the head when it is on the list, else the loudest remaining segment. Player danger, companion danger, `MostUrgent`, `SetInterventionEstimate` and combat eligibility (Planning, outside this folder) walk representatives only, so eight body pieces no longer multiply as independent hazards. Pierce still reads every member. Eater of Worlds after a split is several chains, one per live head. Capping how many pieces are visible was refused: the parts are what pierce aims along.

That grouping is not the encounter's spawn-slot count. Pressure still mirrors `NPC.CheckActive`: a segment that `DoesntDespawnToInactivity` (worm body and tail) contributes nothing, so the game already counted a worm as its head's `npcSlots`. `realLife` is never consulted there. The two agree on an intact Giant Worm and diverge when they should: a split is several representatives and several heads with slots; a head missing from the threat list still leaves one representative (the loudest piece) while spawn weight stays on whichever NPCs the inactivity check still counts. Treating them as one property would hide the case where danger used eight records while pressure already used one head.

A threat's consequence is what its hit costs the body it would land on, not the attacker's raw number. `EstimateEffectiveDamage` reproduces the game's damage after defence — the player's defence at the effectiveness the game assigns by difficulty plus endurance, the companion's defence at the fixed NPC half — by calling the native modifier arithmetic directly, without running loader hit hooks, because a mod may spend a shield charge or play a sound inside a hook that exists for real hits. Each threat then weighs that effective hit against a quarter of the life the victim has left: armour lowers urgency, a wounded body raises it, and the player and the companion differ exactly where the game makes the same hit cost them differently (the companion copies the player's defence but keeps the NPC effectiveness). At full health with no defence the weight equals the older raw-damage share, so the danger thresholds keep the meaning they were tuned with for an unhurt, unarmoured body. Downstream of that weight sit player and companion danger, protection urgency, the safety horizon, guard pressure, hunt candidate ordering and the arsenal's candidate order and prevented-harm danger; the arsenal's own prevented-harm amount still reads raw expected damage, so its algorithm is unchanged while its inputs are not. Modded resistances applied through hit hooks are not reflected, and the estimate is the median hit because contact damage variation is left out.

**The urgency rule is one pure function, and a second reader is why.** `WeighThreatUrgency.cs` holds the weight, closeness and sight arithmetic as `ThreatUrgency`, taking only values a record already carries, and `ObserveThreats` calls it about where each hostile is. The arsenal calls the same function about where a hit's push would leave the hostile, and charges the shot the difference (`../WeaponKnowledge/CLAUDE.md`); a copy of the arithmetic in the arsenal would drift from this one the next time either was tuned, and the drift would be charged to shots as danger nobody caused. The two bodies keep one difference, preserved from before the extraction: the companion's urgency returns zero past closeness range before it reads sight, because the sense only pays for the companion's sight test where closeness says it could matter. That sight test's answer is now kept on the record as `HasSightOnCompanion`, so a consumer re-weighing the threat at another distance uses the sight this tick decided on; it is false wherever the test was not paid for, which reads the threat as half as urgent.

Whether the world is currently about one thing — a boss fight or a world event — is an observation, `Senses.Encounter`, updated right after threats because it reads them. It answers from the state of the world and never from a monster's identity. Recognised sources are native facts: an observed hostile carrying the game's boss flag at any depth, the Old One's Army at any depth, and a blood moon, solar eclipse, pumpkin or frost moon or active invasion only while the player is at or above the surface line, which is where the game runs them. A player inside a lunar pillar's zone is recognised too, because the spawner raises its invasion flag there and the pillars themselves carry no boss flag, deal no damage and weigh no spawn slots, so nothing else would name them. Slime rain is deliberately not an encounter. An invasion counts only where the game would send its enemies to this player, which is the spawner's own gate rather than the invasion flag: arrived (no delay left), enemies still to send, the player above one screen below the surface line, and within the invasion's spawning band. An event nothing names — a modded invasion that sets no vanilla flag — cannot be recognised, so the fallback is observed pressure measured against the game's own spawn accounting. The game's spawner adds enemies near a player only while the spawn-slot weight of NPCs around that player is below a cap it recomputes every tick from depth, biome, time, events, buffs and every mod's spawn-rate hook, this mod's doubling included. The spawner admits another enemy only while that weight is under the cap in force at that moment, so ordinary spawning can never lift the weight above the highest cap applied since the oldest enemy still counted arrived, the admission ceiling, and pressure is the reachable hostiles weighing more than that ceiling: enemies arriving from somewhere ordinary spawning would not have allowed. The ceiling is measured over time rather than read from the tick's cap because the cap follows the player's own state while a crowd stays put: a calming potion, a peace candle, town NPCs moving in, a change of depth, or this mod's own doubling lapsing while the companion is downed all lower it under an unchanged crowd. Arrivals are kept per hostile by slot, type and spawn generation and survive a step out of range, and the caps are kept as a run in which a later cap at least as high replaces the earlier ones, so the ceiling costs a handful of entries however long a crowd stays. The ceiling can only overstate what spawning admitted, so its error is a missed inference rather than a false one: enemies arriving under a lowered cap but still under the old ceiling are not inferred. The weight mirrors the game's count exactly — each NPC's slots, only inside the game's active range of the player, and nothing for an NPC that does not despawn to inactivity, which is how the game counts a worm as its head alone — restricted to hostiles able to reach either actor, so enemies walled off from both do not count. The cap is the game's private spawn-cap field, bound by reflection and read as the spawner left it; the spawner runs before the NPC update the companion's AI runs in, so it is this tick's value except on ticks the spawner skipped (straight after any despawn or death, with the player dead or beside the Moon Lord), which keep the last computed one. Pressure ramps in over a window, ends the first tick the weight is back at or under the ceiling, and is always marked unrecognised so a reader can tell an inferred encounter from a known one. Its blind spot follows from the design: a modded event that raises the cap and spawns through the vanilla spawner never exceeds the cap it raised, so only enemies added outside ordinary spawning (a mod calling NPC creation directly) are inferred. The observation only reports; the shared evaluator is the one place it changes a value, and the wider leash and survival-first conduct the README describes for encounters are not built on it yet. The ramp length lives in BehaviourWeights.

The first fallback was a count of observed hostile records able to reach an actor, at a fixed threshold, and it failed as a property rather than a tuning: a count of NPC records measures threat-list size, not whether the world has changed. Threat observation has no distance filter and still lists every segment of a multi-segment body, so pierce and heat can see the whole worm; danger, protection urgency and combat eligibility count one representative per chain (the head, or the loudest piece). Encounter pressure already counted a worm as its head in the game's spawn slots. A count of records as the encounter rule made one Giant Worm underground eight threats, an ordinary cave under the doubled spawn cap passed any small threshold, and both stopped every optional activity; raising the threshold would only have moved the guess until the next spawn-rate change broke it. The second rule compared the weight with the cap in force on the tick and failed as a property too: the cap is a function of the player's current state, so every choice a player makes to thin spawns lowered it under a crowd that had not moved and read as enemies arriving. Both failures compared a crowd against a number that was not the baseline the rule assumed; the admission ceiling is the one the spawner actually enforces.

The intent history advances once per observed engine tick. Missing intervals, death and large position corrections clear its evidence rather than inventing a traversed path. Recent pick/axe contacts and active tile/wall placement are local-work evidence, not a command to remain still or proof that placement succeeded. A short reversal weakens an existing journey before sustained backtracking replaces it; a pause ages motion out. The confidence-weighted vector feeds reunion's meeting places, position preference and return pressure, while raw velocity remains available for immediate physical questions. Prediction is bounded to the observation horizon and is not a known destination or a route the companion can perform. Tunables live in BehaviourWeights.

`PlayerSense.Interference` is the one courtesy fact: the tiles the player is asking the companion to vacate, derived from what the player is already doing rather than from the cursor alone. A held solid, non-platform block or a wall aimed within the player's placement range at a tile the companion's body covers yields that tile; a grounded player walking at travel speed under a roof directly over a standing body, with the companion nearby on that floor, yields the passage ahead of the player while the roof continues, bounded. The passage is measured from the player, never from the companion, because anchoring it on the companion let every step the companion took move the footprint's edge, and the spot just past that edge was still in the player's way. A torch is deliberately not placement evidence here even though torch placement is local-work evidence for travel intent above: a torch does not close the space a body stands in. The footprint is held for a bounded window after it was last seen and carries a revision that moves when a different footprint appears. It is evidence, not a command, and whether a player would physically be blocked by the companion's body is not established: the fixtures move the player by position.

A recent-velocity smoother can report confident travel after the player repeatedly crosses the same small area. The local-motion regression exercises that ambiguity through PlayerSense itself; pure sequence tests additionally cover work, reversal, pause, vertical travel, duplicate ticks and observation gaps. These do not establish recognition of every player intention. Turning the interpretation into a place to meet belongs to position selection's meeting places, whose activity pairs end the player on one tile after different recent activity.

Player tool hits are evidence, not animation guessed as intent. `ObservePlayerWork` records real axe and pick hits for work behaviours and tells shared movement whenever a relevant tile changes, invalidating cached movement facts. Doors report their own changes because the game’s door helper bypasses those tile hooks.

## Light is a field, and darkness is a question you ask of a place

`ObserveLight.cs` stores a brightness per open-air tile on a coarse lattice through a window around the companion, and every consumer asks it about somewhere rather than reading one number. A mean over the whole window stood here before and could not answer the question anyone asks: averaged across a lit chamber and the dark wings beside it, the mean is wrong about both, so the companion put a torch up in a lit room and left a cave beside it dark. Three queries replace it. `DarkAirNear` is the share of measured air under the dark level within a radius, which the torch decision reads twice — around the body and at the player's predicted feet, so a torch is up before the companion reaches the hole rather than after. `NearestDarkRegion` and `DarkRegionsNearest` cluster adjacent dark samples into areas and rank them by the nearest dark air in each, which is what lighting walks to. `ReadForPlacement` answers the placing question about one tile — dark, lit, sky or unread — from the world's own light at that tile, because a lattice cannot resolve anything narrower than its own step and a neighbourhood around a site counts air the site itself does not see.

Packed tiles are not darkness, and `IsOpenAir` is the one place that ruling lives, so every query inherits it. Averaging solid tiles in made a block of stone read as a dark room and hid the real air pockets.

**A zero is not darkness, because the engine answers zero for a tile it has not computed.** The field reads brightness only inside the bounds of the scan it reads, snapshotted once per refresh, so a tile outside is absent rather than dark and an engine that has never scanned an area measures nothing at all. `MeasuredSamples` and `ReadTick` say so out loud, and a query over unread air answers `Unmeasured`, which holds a decision rather than making one. In colour mode those bounds are the processed area of the scan the world light took, which is the engine's own `_workingProcessedArea` for it. Legacy modes instead index `_states` relative to the requested rectangle and OffScreenTiles, with camera-sized bounds, assigned before the processed values are copied. So neither the current camera rectangle nor an allocated buffer proves a completed observation. Reproduce with `sh Tools/decompile.sh Terraria.Lighting`, `… Terraria.Graphics.Light.LightingEngine` and `… Terraria.Graphics.Light.LegacyLighting`. Only the colour-mode bounds are exercised headlessly (`Tools/EngineReplay/Assistance/VerifyUsefulAssistance.cs` writes the engine's own light map and area); the legacy bounds rest on the decompiled source, because the headless legacy engine has no camera. The window is screen-sized and centred on the companion but never clipped to where the screen is, because the coverage test is that question asked exactly and clipping only discards readings the engine has; it is also never smaller than a neighbourhood around the body, since with no screen at all a screen-derived window collapses to one column and reads as a world where nothing has been measured.

**The light every consumer reads is the world's own, taken from the engine at the one moment its map holds exactly that.** `ReadWorldLight.cs` owns the read. `LightingEngine.ProcessArea` is a four-state machine — minimap, metrics, scan, blur — advancing one state per call. The scan state fills the working map from `TileLightScanner.ExportTo` alone: each tile's own light and its mask, which is placed torches, lamps, lava, glowing tiles and the sky, and nothing else. Everything a body carries — a held torch, a light pet, a glowstick in flight, a mining helmet, a modded lantern, the companion's own torch — reaches the engine through `Lighting.AddLight` into a per-frame list that only the next call's `ProcessBlur` merges, by maximum, before it blurs and `Present` swaps the map in. So while the state reads Blur the working map is the world's light unblurred; the sense copies it then and blurs the copy with the engine's own `LightMap.Blur`, which gives the light the world would show with nobody carrying anything, by the engine's own arithmetic rather than by a model of it. Without this the companion follows a player carrying a torch, reads everything near him as lit, places nothing, and the passage is dark the moment he walks on — the opposite of lighting a mine as you go.

**The scan is taken from the draw as well as from the brain tick, because a brain tick alone can sit at one phase for ever.** With frame skip off the game draws on every frame and updates only once a sixtieth of a second has passed, so a 120 Hz display draws twice per tick and an observer at the tick sees the same two states every time; at two of the four phases it never sees Blur at all and every tile would read unread for the whole session. `CaptureWorldLight` observes after every draw's lighting, which sees every state, and the tick's observation adds the lightning frames, whose two extra lighting calls run inside the update. A scan is taken once however often it is seen: the sense arms on any other state and takes the next Blur it meets. The cost is a copy of the map and one blur of the copy per scan something asks about — measured 2026-09-16 on a 296x191 scan, the lighting area of a 3840x2160 screen, at 0.02 ms to take and 2.6 to 3.0 ms to blur on this machine, against a whole tick's planning allowance; at 1920x1080 the map is a third of the size.

**Reading the quantity ended three guesses at it, and the three are worth knowing because each looked like caution.** Under a carried light the world's own light lies anywhere between nothing and that light, and every attempt to answer that from the presented map was a defect. Read as room light, a companion or a player holding a torch made every dark site beside it read lit, so the torch came out because the cavern was dark and nothing was ever placed. Read as darkness, every lit room a carried light crossed took torches, and a room a standing torch already lit took a second one at the game's spacing, measured at a world light of 0.471 against a dark level of 0.22. Remembered — dark only where the sense had read the tile before the light arrived, the rule until 16 September 2026 — a pocket the player walked into already holding a torch was never known to be dark at all, and one he mined open was forgotten on the edit that opened it, so neither was ever lit. Three fixes at one boundary is what ended the patching: the world's own light is a quantity the engine computes, and reading it removed the case each guess was a patch for rather than moving the failure next door.

**A model of a carried light could never have been inverted, which is why reading the map was the answer.** `LightMap.BlurLine` keeps a running value per channel and, for each tile along the line, takes `if (zero.X < _colors[i].X) zero.X = _colors[i].X;` and otherwise writes the running value back into the tile, multiplying it by `LightDecayThroughAir` (0.91) or `LightDecayThroughSolid` (0.56) per step and abandoning the channel below 0.0185. There is no addition anywhere in that loop, and `ApplyPerFrameLights` merges each carried light in with `Vector3.Max` before it runs. So a carried light never adds to a tile: it loses to what was there or replaces it, and a reading it could account for says only that the world's light is at most that — subtracting a modelled torch from a maximum is meaningless arithmetic, and it produced a false dark blob at the hand and a companion that walked to its own feet to light them.

**A tile daylight reaches is never a placement site, whatever it reads.** `DaylightReaches` is the colour engine's own surface rule, `TileLightScanner.ApplySurfaceLight`: no deeper than `Main.worldSurface`, a wall that passes the sun (`Main.wallLight`, true for no wall, plus the few walls the engine names by number) and nothing in the tile that blocks it. `ReadForPlacement` answers `Sky` for such a tile before it reads any light. The owner ruled this on 15 September 2026 once it was measured, through the game's own sky colour and tile scanner, that surface air reads 0.089 to 0.165 across the night against a dark level of 0.22, so a sense answering the light alone would line the ground outside with torches every night for the morning to make pointless. It is a property of the tile rather than a depth cutoff: a surface house behind background walls the sun does not pass is lit like any dark room, and a wall-less cave mouth above the surface line is sky, as the engine lights them.

**The rule now, by the owner's ruling, is that a tile is dark to placing when the world's own light there is below the dark level.** `ReadForPlacement` answers `Dark`, `Lit`, `Sky` or `Unread` from the scan, and there is no fourth answer for a tile under a carried light, because the scan never held that light: the ground beside a player holding a torch reads as dark as the cave it is in, and a room a standing torch lights reads lit whoever walks through it. `Unread` is the tile no scan covers — outside the engine's processed area, or before the first scan of a world — and it is refused with lit rather than treated as dark. What the scan is up to four frames behind is the world as the engine last scanned it, which is what every reader of the presented map is behind too. Not covered: nothing. A torch the companion places, a lamp switched by wire, a wall mined open and lava flowing in all reach the next scan without anything announcing them, which is the whole difference from the memory this replaced.

The held torch, its light and map reveal still follow the interaction layer's `Shown` answer; observation does not decide whether the hand is free. Light read from the field is never permission to spend a supplied torch: placement asks its own question about the site.

## The player's intent is a region, and its gradient is the reason it is one

`ObservePlayerIntentRegion.cs` is the third sense, reached as `Senses.Intent`, and it replaces a shape rather than adding one: following used to compare the body against a symmetric box on the player's current feet, and the work radius, the reunion pull, the meeting place's fallback anchor, the lighting search's bound and the collect radius each read the player's position with a radius of their own. They now all measure to one object, which is what makes a "how far from the player" question have one answer.

**A radius measures to the region's heading, and a separation measures to its rectangle; the box's centre is only where the companion lives.** The region carries two points because it answers two questions. `Heading` is the player's own centre carried by the lead — where he is going, as a point — and the work radius, the collect and hunt allowance, the tree and ore search centres, the lighting search's centre and the regroup distance all read it, because each of them already subtracts a comfortable distance of its own and wants a point near the player to subtract it from. `Centre` is the box's centre, a third of the box above the player, which is where a drone idles and nothing a work radius should favour: centred there, every radius would lean five tiles upward for no reason anyone gave. How far outside the region a place is reads `GapBeyond`, zero anywhere inside the rectangle and rising beyond it, which is what the owner ruled every pull and every separation is measured from.

A request refines the sense's own objective towards an anchor with `At`, and the sense's objective anchors at the region's centre unless a consumer names somewhere else. Which places count as being with the player is the region's answer alone; the anchor only records what a request aimed at. It used to widen acceptance by a growth factor of its own, which let a far-off meeting place enlarge the arrival test it was supposed to be aiming inside.

**The defect the old box had was that a moving player was never itself a reason to move, and the answer turned out to be motion rather than a pull.** Inside it the reunion pull was exactly zero, so the body coasted to whichever edge it entered by and any rival offer won; no comfort size fixes that, because the missing quantity is velocity and the box carried none. A continuous pull inside the region was the first answer, and it was deleted on 15 September 2026 by the owner's ruling that there is no pull inside the region: the region is where the companion lives, and a body inside it moves about the region carried by the region's own motion (`../Movement/Steering/CLAUDE.md`). `Pull` survives as the normalised Chebyshev distance from the centre — zero there, one at the edge, rising beyond, the two deliberately different axes not collapsed into one radius — for readers that want how far through the box a point is, and it is not a demand to move.

**The player is inside his region on every tick, by construction, and he is not at its centre.** His centre sits at the centre of the box's bottom third, so with no lead the box's centre is a third of its height above him: a drone idles mostly above the person it goes with, underground as much as on the surface. The box is the follow comfort with no extra scale; it grows by up to fifteen percent with its lead. The lead moves the box within two different clamps: horizontally he sits on the third-lines that split the box into three equal strips (`|lead.x| ≤ h.x/3`), so two thirds of the box stays ahead of a walking player and the box cannot run out to the far edge; vertically he stays inside by the navigator's settle radius, `p − (h.y − s) ≤ lead.y ≤ p + (h.y − s)`. At the bottom third, `p = 2h.y/3`, which leaves an upward allowance of `h.y/3 − s` and a downward one of `5h.y/3 − s` — a small lift and a long drop, the shape of a companion that hovers over a player and is dragged below him when he falls. A held direction that is not moving him still feeds the lead, so holding right into a wall or down into the floor slides the box the same way walking would. Vertical travel is a second input on top of that hold: the box takes a fifth of his own displacement, so walking off a single block is a nudge and dropping into a cave still fills the clamp. Treating any downward velocity as "go to the floor" was the snap the 16 September play found. Screen y grows downward, so up is negative. Where a third of the half-height is under the slack, which is the Close distance mode's box, the player sits as low as containment allows instead, and the box cannot lead upward at all. The clamp that stood here until 15 September 2026 was half the screen, which deliberately let the box leave him: on the 14:16 capture of that day he was outside it on 14.9% of rows and on 37% of the rows he was falling. Replaying that capture's player track through the real lead filter now leaves him outside on none, and `Tools/EngineReplay/Observation/VerifyIntentRegionHoldsThePlayer.cs` is that replay, run against the track committed in `Tools/Scenarios` so a clone without the capture runs it too. The screen no longer shapes the region at all, which retires the headless clamp floor and the reason fixtures had to declare a play-sized screen for the region's sake; the screen still sizes the light field's window and hunting's on-screen rectangle.

Four properties of the lead are each there for a reason. It **chases a pose**, rest when nothing is held and the third-line or the vertical edge when a direction is, so a reverse at the clamp starts on that frame rather than unwinding a number piled past it. It **takes a fraction of his own vertical travel** on top of that pose, so how far he actually fell is what moves the box when he is not holding a key. It is **low-pass filtered**, which matters at the stop rather than the start: a player who halts has his intent fall to nothing in one observation, and an unfiltered region would snap back onto him and take the companion's destination with it. It is **clamped so the player stays inside**, above, including after the travel add, so a long fall cannot pile past the clamp. And it **resets on the discontinuities that clear the intent history** — death, a teleport, an unobserved interval — because a filtered lead outliving its evidence points at where the player was going before he stopped being there. The region also publishes its own `Velocity`, the player's smoothed travel plus the change in the applied lead, which is the frame a body moving about inside the region moves in.

The region **grows with its lead, on both axes and to a cap, and reaches the cap exactly when the lead reaches the clamp.** A player crossing broken ground is somewhere in a band rather than at a point. The growth is the lead's share of how far the fully grown box allows it to lead on that axis, the larger share of the two axes, and the lead is then clamped against the box it actually is, so "fully grown" and "led as far as it may" are the same moment and, short of it, the clamp does not bind: the grown box's limits exceed the lead's share of the full box's by `(1 − share)(base − slack)` on each axis, which the geometry row sweeps rather than trusts. Growth that scaled with a fixed lead distance, as it did before, reached its cap at a lead unrelated to where the clamp stopped, so the two disagreed about when the box had led as far as it could.

**Being with the player is being inside his region, and the edge has a width.** The sense holds one latched fact, `Inside`: a body enters when the region contains its centre and leaves only once it is more than the settle radius beyond the edge, so a body carried along the boundary by its own motion does not leave and re-enter on alternate ticks. It replaced a settled streak — at rest inside for a rescore, latched while inside — and a published speed of the region's centre, and both could go for a reason that is a property rather than a tuning. They existed because inside was where a body arrived and stopped. At tick 8127 of the 13:27 capture of 2026-09-14 both bodies were mid-jump and momentarily inside the box, keeping company took that for arrival and released the body, and the release cut the walker's jump one tick after take-off; at three times the player's speed on 15 September 2026 the same class flipped keeping company's method thirty-nine times in six hundred ticks, and a body at rest in a region still sliding back onto a stopped player was outside it a second later. Every one of those harms was a change of request kind at the edge. Keeping company now asks for the same kind inside and outside, so nothing at the edge cancels anything, and the only thing left to guard is the edge's flicker, which the latch's width answers. The follow reason column lost its two unsettled values with the streak, and says only whether the body is with the player and, when it is not, which axis it is outside on.

**A body inside the box on the far side of a sealed wall is outside it, because being with the player needs a way to him inside his region.** The owner ruled this on 15 September 2026, after a door scene found a companion keeping the player company from inside his region across a wall it had no way through. The sense answers it with a second flood: rooted at the corner nearest the player, unpriced, bounded by the region grown by the latch's width and a tile and snapped outward to whole tiles, and grown a slice per tick under its own share of the tick's allowance until it exhausts those bounds. A finished flood answers while a replacement grows under it for the player's newer corner or the region's newer box, and a replacement is never abandoned for a newer target, because the box slides a tile at a time under a walking player and restarting on every tile would never let one finish; an edit inside what either read drops it. `WayToPlayer` is the answer in the three values every sense uses — reachable or unreachable only off a finished flood that joined the corner the player stands at now and holds the body's corner inside its bounds, not yet for everything else — and `Inside` is the latch less a proven cut-off. It is not the reach disc, and that is the point of it: the disc is rooted at the body and a way round outside the region joins both sides of a wall inside it, so the disc holds the player from either side and cannot tell them apart. A way round that runs inside the region does count — a trench under the wall within the box connects the two sides, which is why the door scenes still stand the player far east. **Not yet known takes nothing away, and it is never read as a proof either.** The middle answer covers an unfinished flood as well as a question with no root — the body pressed into a gap it cannot centre in, the player in a shaft, no world plugged in — and its consumers treat it as the reach sense's consumers treat theirs: `Inside` loses only a proven cut-off, because reading an unanswered question as outside would send rejoining to find a place its own route search cannot root either, and the positioner passes over only a corner `ProvenCutOff` names as the way back in, because the nearest inside corner to a body on the wrong side of a wall is otherwise the one it already hovers at, and the body would be admitted, cut off and re-sent in a loop. **The first build ran this flood to exhaustion in one call on every change, and that was measured wrong twice over.** A sentinel timed it at four to ten milliseconds of the tick's twelve on the headless map, rebuilt on more than half the ticks of a walking player and ahead of the positioner and the navigator on the same deadline; and under the live deadline the call stopped short, was never resumed, and read connected for as long as the player stood still, because an unfinished flood and a proof shared one boolean. `Tools/EngineReplay/Observation/VerifyWayToPlayerIsAnsweredInThreeValues.cs` holds the resume, the three values and the cost beside a reconstruction of the first build. The numeric update a replay drives has no world and answers not yet, which leaves the body with the player.

**The region deliberately keeps leading while the player's interference footprint is live, and the opposite was tried and measured.** The hypothesis was that a lead carries the companion into the very tiles the player is asking it to vacate, so a live footprint should pull the region back onto his feet; it is wrong twice over. The courtesy contract stayed red with the suppression in and went green without it, because what actually held the body in a one-body-tall passage was keeping company waiting a rescore before it could change method, and exempting an overlapped footprint from that wait is what released it. With the red gone, the suppression's only measured effect was on the open-floor walk, where it made courtesy *worse* — measured 2026-09-14 on the `--courtesy` fixture, one variable at a time, 44 stationary ticks in the player's way with the suppression in against 28 with it out, both runs three green — because a region pulled back onto a walking player's feet is a region asking the companion to stand exactly where he is going. The property to keep: **courtesy is answered where the body moves — the accompanying motion refuses the player's footprint as a place to move to, and the positioner's way back into the region passes over it — never by blinding the region that says where the player is heading.**

## Reach is the sense whose middle answer is the whole point

`ObserveReach.cs` owns one flood over the corner graph, rooted at the usable corner nearest the orb's centre, that position selection used to keep to itself. It is one flood and not two because an orb's free space has no one-way edge: a walker could drop somewhere it could not climb back from, so its sense kept a returnable flood beside a raw one and opened the raw one only to follow a player down a drop; wherever the orb can fly it can fly back, so reachable and returnable are one set, and the names the walker's consumers read (`Returnable`, `ReachableOneWay`, `InScoredRegion`, `ScoredComplete`, `PlayerOnlyOneWay`, which is always false) are kept as one-line answers from the same flood so that nobody had to be renamed for no behaviour. Two recorder columns still written from them are therefore vestiges rather than measurements: `reach_two_way` always equals `reach_any` and `player_one_way` is always zero. `spot_home` beside them is still live — it says whether the chosen spot lies inside the flood, which is false for an exact request accepted while the flood was unfinished and for the positioner's fallback to the nearest hoverable cell outside reach. A tile is reachable when any of its four corners is in the flood, and the rules the flood runs under — which liquids are walls — are the body's immunities, set once per tick by the brain before anything plans.

`Reachable` answers Reachable, NotYet or Unreachable, and the middle value is why it is a sense rather than a boolean. A tile missing from a flood that has not finished is unknown, not absent, so an activity refusing NotYet is declining to start on an unanswered search rather than asserting the place does not exist — the ruling that optional work does not start on an unanswered search, expressible in the type instead of reconstructed by each caller. Unlike the walker's, this flood finishes, and the reason it finishes is a bound the first orb tree got wrong. A flood over an open world grows until something stops it, and the search's node limit does stop it — finished, but not exhausted, so `Complete` stayed false for the life of every session and nothing could ever be proven unreachable; the recorded-route run measured it frozen at the node limit from about tick four hundred with nineteen of twenty checkpoints reading "not yet". The flood is a disc now: bounded at twice the known radius in `Selection/BehaviourWeights.cs` of straight-line distance from its root, so it exhausts inside the disc and `Complete` means complete for the disc, and a tile inside the known radius that it never claimed has no route shorter than about three times its straight line. A travel-cost ball would give the tighter guarantee of two, and was tried: it is sound only in unpriced units, and stripping the clearance price off the flood changed the travel estimates the reunion charge reads — every reunion weight was tuned against the priced number, and with distances instead keeping company strolled beside a player fifteen tiles off — so the flood keeps its pricing and the bound is geometric. The verdict is three-valued inside the known radius and only two-valued beyond it — a tile beyond the radius answers not yet, never unreachable, because the flood was not asked to go there — and consumers that turn an unreached corner into a proven refusal (the positioner's candidates, hunt's stand sweep) ask the sense's own proven-absent question rather than reading `Complete` and the region themselves. Once the body has travelled half the known radius from the root, a replacement flood grows there while the old one keeps answering, so ordinary travel never empties the region; only a body outside the region, or a terrain edit inside what the flood read, empties it. Underground the ball is mostly rock and closes in a few rescores; in open sky it is the whole disc and takes a few hundred ticks, which is where not yet stays the common answer for long.

**Every activity and every interaction now reads this and none of them searches, and that is enforced rather than agreed.** `Tools/check-navigation-boundary.sh` refuses any route search named under `Activities` or `Interactions`; those folders may hold `Reachability.Reach`, which is the verdict they pass around, and may not call `FreeSpaceSearch`, `CornerGraph`, `ClearanceField`, `Navigator.MoveTo` or their kin. The rule is worth the enforcement because breaking it is invisible at the call site: a bounded search per candidate answers Unknown on its expansion budget, a caller cannot remember an Unknown without writing a place off on evidence that does not exist, and a caller that rations itself therefore re-asks the same nearest candidates for ever. The 09:28 capture of 2026-09-14 spent 79% of its lighting offers — 11,880 of 15,105 rows — saying the question could not be finished, with a settled flood beside 7,003 of those rows holding the answer.

**Growth is the brain tick's and rooting is the resolve's, and every kind of resolve roots, a hold included.** `Grow` advances whichever flood is unfinished by one slice every tick, because growth only adds corners and breaks no admission, and growing only on the rescore had the first flood of the recorded route take three hundred ticks to complete and its replacement a hundred and fifty to catch a travelling body. Rooting — creating a flood where there is none, replacing one the body has left — happens in `Refresh`, which every branch of the positioner's resolve reaches. `ChooseUsefulPosition.IsReturnable` also refreshes before it answers, so a consumer asking that query roots the flood too. `TheReachFloodSettles` in SessionReport is the instrument for it, and on the 09:28 capture of 2026-09-14 it reports one unbroken stretch of 6,478 rows, 43% of that session, from tick 1, where the flood never finished. That is the thing to check first when optional work is not starting and every offer reads honestly.

**A sense that only grows when a consumer asks must be asked by the plain path, never by a test that happens to fail.** With no region at all — spawn, and every terrain revision — work reads NotYet and offers zero, so keeping company wins and asks for a hold. Until 15 September 2026 the hold branch returned before `Refresh`, so nothing the brain *decided* left that state; what opened it was `KeepCompany.SafeStrollGoal` asking `IsReturnable` of each candidate stroll tile, rejecting every candidate on a null region while growing the region, so the first flood of a companion's life arrived as a side effect of a test that failed. The hold branch refreshes now, so the opener is the decision itself rather than a query somebody might remove.

`Age` never clears the region, only stales it, so this bites at a null region and not at an old one. `VerifyOreWork.AColdFloodDoesNotLeaveTheBrainResting` is the standing check, and it detects the closed case rather than being assumed to: neutering `Refresh` on a null region freezes the companion beside its spawn with `keep-company` holding every tick, and that is the signature it showed on the day the stroll went. `Tools/EngineReplay/Movement/VerifyResponsiveFollowing.cs`'s company row asserts the same class from the other side, that a companion whose only request is a hold still completes its region.

**A verdict of absent must rest on the exhaustion of the set it was looked up in.** There is one set now, so `Complete` and `ScoredComplete` are one flag; the walker had two floods and one flag serving both once let a tile its returnable flood had not yet reached be graded Unreachable because the raw flood had finished — the same not-yet-is-not-no error the middle answer exists to prevent, arriving through the completeness rather than through the membership. It bites harder than it reads, because a consumer that remembers a proven No keeps it for the whole no-return wait rather than re-asking on the next rescore, so the mistake does not correct itself. Anyone adding a second flood here adds a second completeness with it.

**A refusal proved from a finished flood is remembered against that flood, not against the world.** `FloodGeneration` advances whenever a different flood object starts answering — a reflood, or a replacement taking over from a reroot — and a consumer that remembers a proven absence, or a place beyond the known radius, keys the memory on it. A finished flood does not grow, and an edit it read would have replaced it, so the answer cannot change while the generation holds. The world's terrain revision, which nearby work keyed on before, moves on every edit anywhere in the loaded world, the companion's own placed torch included, and discarded every proof with each placement.

**The flood's unit of time is the rescore, not the game tick.** `Age` is called once per resolve and `Refresh` refloods when that count passes, because the flood is bounded per advance and grows across successive resolves: a caller resolving repeatedly inside one tick is asking the region to keep expanding, and a cadence keyed to the tick advances it once and never finishes. Readers never age it, so a consumer asking a question during a hold cannot expire the region out from under the scoring it is about to be compared against.

**A terrain edit refloods only where the flood actually read.** The trigger is the retained search's own validity — the box it explored, asked of the world's edit record — rather than a world-global counter, and it sits ahead of the cadence test so an edit the flood read lands on the next resolve rather than up to a cadence later. A body whose nearest usable corner the flood has not reached also refloods — at once when the flood had finished, after a short patience while it is still growing — because a flood rooted where the body was answers about a region the body may no longer be in; a body inside something has no usable corner and keeps the last flood until it is out. The counter compare that stood here meant any announced tile change anywhere in the loaded world threw the flood away, so a player mining a screen off restarted a search that had never looked there. `Refloods` counts the discard rather than the advance, so a cadence that keeps growing one flood does not register and the number means what a reader assumes it means. What has not changed is the clock: only the invalidation is spatial, and the reach-complete share the 13:27 capture of 2026-09-14 measured — unfinished on 9,812 of its 22,473 rows, the 56.34% complete the ledger carries — has two named causes, of which this is one.

The sense is refreshed by position selection's resolve rather than by `Senses.Update`, and that is deliberate: the flood is bounded per advance and its unit of time is the rescore, so a flood driven from the top of every tick would be advanced once per tick and aged once per tick and never finish under a hold. Consumers reading between resolves get the region the last resolve left, a rescore old at worst.

## Traps

- A harmful hostile and an attackable target are different observations. Threat collection keeps harmful NPCs even when Terraria declines `CanBeChasedBy`; the arsenal applies that targetability filter when it considers a shot. Player death clears player protection pressure, but does not erase danger to the companion.
- Motion continuation carries measured one-tick forecast error and a decaying confidence. Low confidence shortens protection time conservatively; it does not invent type-specific future AI.

Reachability is destination-specific: `CanReachPlayer` gates player urgency and the safety horizon, while `CanReachCompanion` gates personal urgency. The two results use the same bounded searches and staggered refresh, with a shared answer only when their destination tiles coincide. **A walker's route to the companion is not enough to reach a body that hovers.** Its target is the floor under the orb, and a route there says nothing about whether it can touch a circle flying above that floor, so a walking enemy also has to be inside its jump: its own height plus the highest jump of the game's fighter AI — the jump it makes at the tallest step it will climb, not its ordinary hop, read off the decompiled `NPC.AI_003_Fighters` rather than tuned — above the floor under the orb or above where the walker already stands, whichever is higher. The first orb play read companion danger at nine tenths and more with zombies underneath while the orb took no hit for well over a minute, and that permanent reading was what kept the deleted combat-spacing response holding the body. A modded walker that jumps higher than the fighter AI is under-read by the difference. A cached negative becomes unknown/potential danger when the enemy, destination, movement class or terrain changes; that invalidation does not add unbudgeted searches. Neither endpoint's reachability determines whether an enemy occupies a proposed future position or route.

Enemy predictions share one observed-motion history across aiming and reflexes. Consecutive observations supply acceleration; collision impulses and abrupt jumps are not repeated as acceleration. The forecast snapshots each NPC's current `gravity`, `maxFallSpeed`, liquid flags and movement-speed fields, then uses native tile and slope collision with the same wet slowdown before restoring the game's shared collision scratch state. Forecasts are cached per observed entity state and tick, and cleared on spawn, death and world closure. They predict current motion over a bounded horizon; they cannot know an arbitrary modded enemy's next scripted decision or reproduce a future NPC-specific step/slope policy. Hostile projectile velocity is converted from sub-update units to game-tick units before reflex projection.

The predictor primes itself from an observation, then measures its next observed continuation against the forecast. Confidence is accompanied by its sample count; zero samples is unmeasured, never proof of stable enemy motion. Native projectile ownership is attributed at spawn with generation-aware identity, so a reused projectile slot cannot inherit an earlier hostile's attack source.

- Never calculate a world fact inside a behaviour: selection runs every behaviour every tick and duplicated readings drift.
- `PlayerDanger` and `SelfDanger` answer different questions. A formula of `1 - danger` must name whose danger it consumes.
- The player's recorded trail can cross a gap a mount carried him over; it is evidence for a scenario, not proof the planner is defective.
