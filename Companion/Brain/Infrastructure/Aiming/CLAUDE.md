# Projectile aiming — the arc solver, and the arcs it learns

```
Aiming/
├─ CLAUDE.md
├─ SolveProjectileTrajectory.cs   the flight model, one flight step, solved impact facts and swept terrain/target traces
└─ LearnProjectileArcs.cs         the motion per projectile type: priors for two vanilla styles, then a fit from the companion's own shots
```

Shared by every weapon in `../../Weapons/` and by the positioner, which asks it whether a candidate spot has a line of fire. It is in the brain because it is how the companion aims, whatever it holds; the weapons are what it holds. This folder is compiled twice — into the mod and into `Tools/EngineReplay` as source — so nothing in it may name a weapon, the arsenal or the NPC, and a fixture that watches samples into one copy and solves from the other proves nothing about what the arsenal fires with.

## A flight model is what the aimer flies, and a motion is the part nobody can read off an item

`FlightModel` is one use of a weapon as the aimer sees it: launch speed, the motion, how long to trace, the box the game will collide, and how far a use is worth attempting at all. An item says what it fires and how fast, and its projectile's sample says the box and the lifetime; the motion — some ticks straight, then a constant gravity on the vertical speed and a drag factor on the horizontal, capped at the fall speed the game caps every gravity style at — is not a stat anywhere. `LearnedMotion` is that shape, and `ProjectileArcs.MotionFor(type)` is where it comes from: a prior for an arrow-flagged style-1 projectile and for the thrown-object style 2, both read from the decompiled AI and cited in the class, a straight line at launch speed for every other style vanilla or modded, and then whatever the recorded first ticks of the type's own flights fit. A style the companion has not seen fly is not assumed to fall, because a wrong gravity misses in a way the learner then has to unlearn while a straight guess misses in the one way a first observation corrects outright.

The learning is the arsenal's registration of every shot it spawns, an observer in `../../Weapons/TrackLandedHits.cs` that feeds each post-AI velocity until the watch is full or the projectile dies, and a fit by medians over consecutive velocity pairs: the onset is the first sample that differs from the one before, drag the median horizontal ratio, gravity the median vertical difference with the vertical left undamped, the cap an observed plateau or the game's universal one. Pairs at the cap are kept out of the gravity estimate; a flight watched to its full length with no change says straight, and one that struck something early says nothing about a gravity that had not started. Learned motions are session state and never saved, so the first shots of a new projectile in a session are calibration shots that may miss — the story in README's fight scene — and a fit replaces the prior only once it has enough pairs behind it. The learner names its own floor for that. Samples taken in liquid are skipped, because the game multiplies gravity and drag there and `ProjectileFlight.Advance` already applies that multiplier itself. Two bounds keep the learner honest over a session. A fit outside what the aimer can fly — a negative gravity from a projectile that rises, a drag past one — never becomes the type's motion: the prior stands, the type is named unfittable so a slot can say why, and the next flight is still watched, because a fit the solver cannot fly would otherwise solve no shot, fire none, watch nothing, and leave the type dead for the session with nothing that could correct it, which a sentinel measured on two vanilla items. And the pairs a type keeps are a ring of eight flights' worth, the newest kept, because the list once grew for the life of the session and the median fit was measured at twelve times its early cost after a few thousand shots, inside the projectile hooks on the main thread.

A swung weapon is a one-tick flight whose speed is its reach and whose motion is straight, so the same sweep of angles that finds an arc finds a swing with terrain in the way; what a swing hits beyond the aimed body is the weapon's own sector count, not this folder's.

## Two questions, and the step they share

Two questions are asked of a shot and they are different. `Solve` asks whether the target can be hit at all and returns the launch velocity that does it, flattest arc first so a clear shot is a straight shot and a lob is a last resort. `PathHits` takes that launch and flies it again to ask what *else* is in the way — the bodies the arc actually crosses, in the order it reaches them — which is what a piercing weapon is worth. Counting hostiles near the target instead would be wrong in both directions: two enemies abreast of the muzzle are on the arc and two abreast of the target may be behind a wall the arc stops at.

`ProjectileFlight.Advance` is the one motion step used by solve, pierce scoring and final firing validation. It applies the model's motion in the game's own order, gravity then drag then the cap, and a candidate translation is sampled across its whole distance with `Collision.SolidCollision`, so a fast projectile cannot tunnel past a thin tile merely because both endpoints are clear.

**A shot can be asked for now or for a number of ticks from now, and the positioner asks for later.** `TrySolve` carries a tick-offset overload, because a firing stand is chosen now and stood on after a walk, and an arc proved against the target's present centre is a proof about a moment that will have passed by the time the body arrives. The offset is a *time*, not a displacement, and that distinction is the whole reason the overload exists rather than the caller shifting the target box itself: the motion forecast is terrain-constrained, so a straight-line shift equals it only while the motion happens to be straight. Every interception test inside the trace is taken at the offset plus the flight tick, so the flight lead the aimer already applies survives on top of the wait.

Where the forecast carries too little measured confidence — no error samples, or a trip long enough that the measured continuation has decayed past its floor — the current position is asked instead and the answer records which was used, because refusing a stand on an unmeasured guess is worse than the staleness it replaces. The limitation is worth knowing rather than rediscovering: **on a long walk the forecast is not confident, so a far stand is still judged on where the target is now**, and the rule therefore bites on short and middling trips only.

`TrySolve` returns a `TrajectorySolution`, including the launch, actual predicted impact point and impact tick. `TryTrace` reuses the same walk after accuracy noise rotates a launch; the arsenal fires only if this concrete rotated launch still reaches the target through clear terrain. This preserves imperfect aim without turning an earlier proof into permission for a different, unsafe trajectory. Target boxes come from `WorldObservation.PredictObservedMotion`, the same terrain-constrained observed-motion forecast used by threat records; projectile aiming does not carry a second enemy-physics model.

`PathHits` runs over a hostile list the caller supplies rather than scanning every NPC slot, because it is asked once per weapon per target while the brain is deciding, and a scan of the world inside a per-tick flight loop is a few tens of thousands of rectangle tests a frame.

## What the fixture proves, and what it does not

`Tools/EngineReplay/Combat/VerifyArcLearning.cs` holds both priors against `VanillaAI` tick for tick, shows a bullet's prior straight, keeps the thin-wall and accuracy-rotation refusals, and takes the measure the learner is judged by: hits per shot at a zombie standing still, with a vanilla arrow's prior from the first shot, and for a type the learner is told flies straight — which is what any modded projectile starts as — before learning, after the calibration shots it took, and after. The learned onset, gravity, drag and cap are then held against the game's, so the fit is shown to recover the mechanism and not merely to land. The measured figures are in the commit that landed the learner and in the ledger rows the fixture files. It does not exercise the global projectile hooks themselves, which no loader runs headless, nor a homing or steered modded projectile, where the medians bound the damage a steer does to the fit but nothing measures it.
