# Behaviour diagnostics — the overlay and the record

Two independently configurable instruments answer why the companion acted: the visual inspector exposes retained decision and execution evidence, and the recorder preserves observations for an unnarrated session. `Companion/DiagnosticsConfiguration/` owns the native ModConfig switches. Recording opens on world entry only when enabled; disabling closes the stream immediately without clearing gameplay prediction state. A missing observation remains unknown rather than being reconstructed from intended action.

```
Diagnostics/
├─ CLAUDE.md
├─ DrawBrainOverlay.cs       selectable world layers and a scrollable raw/final decision score panel
├─ BrainInspectorSamples.cs bounded traces retained from actual aiming, movement and reflex computations, and the last second of brain cost the strip plots
├─ DescribeExecutionEvidence.cs the inspector's Execution lines and success-region boxes, read from retained brain state only
├─ RecordBrainTelemetry.cs   one tab-separated phase-labelled sample per companion tick, its actual elapsed wall time and a tile-window dump per failed plan or detected scenario
├─ RecordGodsEyeEvents.cs    sparse JSONL occurrence stream: stable spawn identities, shots, damage, pickups and native outcomes
├─ ObserveNativeCombatEvents.cs native spawn, projectile contact, damage and death hooks
├─ ObserveTerrainChanges.cs  bounded rolling terrain chunks around both actors, refreshed after edits
├─ ObserveTravelEpisodes.cs one whole journey against its proven ticks and the player's own, and every stop on a route with the reason attributed as it happens
├─ CaptureMovementScenario.cs the nine detectors that turn a playtest failure into a dump under their own reason; thresholds and cooldowns tune the instrument, not the brain
└─ DrawSessionMap.cs         one zoomed-out picture of the whole session written on world unload: everywhere the body went, everywhere it was asked to go, and per ask whether the distance ever closed
```

## A detector can only find a failure somebody imagined, so the report opens with a census

This is the correction of 2026-09-09 and it is the most important sentence in this folder. Every instrument here was a detector: it fires on a threshold a person chose, so a behaviour nobody thought to threshold produces no report at all, and zero findings is indistinguishable from zero coverage. In nine minutes the follower completed 1,227 walks, eight drops, no jumps and no fall-throughs; all four numbers were already in columns 85 to 90, no check asked the question, the session read clean, and the defect surfaced only because Caner mentioned in passing that he had watched it jump.

That is a coverage gap and not a false negative — a false negative needs a test that existed and said clean — so the answer is a different shape of instrument rather than a better threshold. `../Movement/MovementExecution/BehaviourCensus.cs` counts every category whether or not anything happened in it and attaches no verdict, so `Jump: planned 47, begun 1, completed 0` is a row nobody reads past where a detector's zero is silence. It is written beside the session on world unload and `../../../../Tools/SessionReport/` prints it above every finding.

The deeper form of the same problem is that every check here compares the brain against itself, so it can only ever verify internal consistency: behaviour that is derived correctly from the senses and is nonetheless wrong produces nothing. Competence needs a reference the brain did not produce, and the only one available in a singleplayer mod is the player's own trail — which this folder already writes into every window and which exactly one of the nine detectors reads. Widening that is the standing work, and the design's own pass line says why: if the player got through, the companion must.

## Every failure the player can feel becomes a block the tool replays

The pass line is a route the companion's own movement kit can perform; a player's accessory-assisted route requires evidence of which capability it needed. `ScenarioCapture.Watch` runs on every recorded tick and keeps four counters. Walking with the player, more than a leash of tiles behind for seconds, and no nearer than when the count began is a follow failure (a companion closing the gap at the player's pace is doing its job, and the Codex review of the first version showed the bare distance test firing every cooldown on an ordinary chase); a body that has not moved for seconds while the follower had a path is stuck, counted here and not on the navigator's own counter, which every replan resets; life lost within a second of a reflex approving a dodge, lava and fire excluded, is a hit through a dodge, which means the simulation the dodge was judged against was wrong; the player mining or chopping for seconds of activity, pauses of up to a second allowed because the ore-hit marker lives less than a second, while the matching action scored zero throughout, is a missed mode, which is the pickaxe icon never showing. Every duration is in game ticks, and every counter resets when a session opens, so a previous world's dodge cannot label the next companion's first scratch. Each fires at most once per cooldown and writes the same tile window the failed-plan dump writes, with the reason in the header, the start at the companion's feet and the goal at the navigator's goal or the player's feet. Every window, whichever path wrote it, carries a `trail` line: the player's feet tiles over the last while, oldest first, one per change of tile, kept on the player sense. The replay tool reads that line back and reports how many trail tiles fall inside the window and the first one the grid refuses, either not standable or outside the region the companion can reach from its start, so a failed follow reads as "the grid refuses tile X, which the player stood in" rather than as a red verdict.

The plan dump is the instrument for "why could it not get there": the navigator calls `DumpPlan` on every plan that returned nothing or only a partial path, and at most once every five seconds the writer appends the tile window spanning the start, the goal and the player's feet (padded, cut to a readable size on the start's side, because the first missing link is near the start; the player is inside it because "could it have reached the player" is the replay tool's second verdict, and a goal picked above a pit said nothing about a player six rows below the window) as one character per tile, written through `TextTileWorld.Glyph` in `../Movement/TerrainModel/` so the replay tool reads back exactly what was written: `#` solid, `=` platform, `_` half block, `\` and `/` floor slopes (solid below the line drawn), `<` and `>` ceiling slopes (solid on the side the bracket points away from), `~` water or honey, `L` lava, `o` air the body can stand in, `.` other air, with `S` start, `G` goal, `E` where a partial path ends, `N` the companion's feet and `P` the player's feet drawn over air tiles only, and a `markers` line under the header naming every one of those positions outright, because on a half block or a floor slope the feet tile is the supporting tile and a marker written there erased the support from the replay (2026-09-08 Codex review); the parser takes the line over the grid and still reads old dumps off the grid. The header line carries the tick, the reason, the expansions used and the window's bounds. It exists because the third run of 2026-09-08 showed 1,136 expansions on every plan for its last 3,468 ticks (about a minute at sixty a second) with the player over 200 tiles below, and nothing in the per-tick line says which tile the grid refused.

`RecordBrainTelemetry.cs` opens a versioned, tab-separated file on world load under the mod's source folder (`Main.SavePath/ModSources/AICompanion/Telemetry`, which is this repository; the folder is in `.gitignore` and `buildIgnore`). A two-line metadata preamble records the schema and UTC start instant, while each row carries an actual `Stopwatch` elapsed time rather than a wall time derived from ticks. Every row names three phases: the motor's observed body state at AI entry, the predicted state built from that exact observation and the applied controls, and the legacy `npc_px` sampled after AI helpers such as `StepUp` may have moved it. Chronology uses the entry observation for actual movement, never the helper-mutated compatibility pose. The record carries both bodies' precise positions, velocities, support shape and liquid state, the player's life/state/activity, the companion's applied controls and their source, and sparse hit events. The player event carries Terraria's final damage, source entity where one exists, direction and knockback; the companion event carries final damage, direction and knockback, and explicitly says that the NPC callback exposes no attacker source. A life fall without an event remains a life fall; the record does not invent a hit or knockback. Plan dumps pass the terrain's fall-through property to the text glyph so a sloped platform remains a sloped platform in replay. The support names are shapes (`platform`, `half-block`, floor or ceiling slope) rather than scenery labels, so the reader can infer an ascent or descent from a slope sequence but never guess that an arbitrary coordinate was a hill or staircase. The writer flushes once a second and closes on world or mod unload.

The TSV remains the continuous measurement. Its `-events.jsonl` sibling is the sparse causal trace: each native hook snapshots entity fields, a per-spawn identity, tick and Stopwatch time before the engine object can mutate or its slot can be reused. A UTC millisecond stem reserves the TSV with `FileMode.CreateNew`; a same-instant retry gains an attempt suffix, and its sidecars use that reserved stem, so a second load cannot overwrite the first run. Metadata is flushed at world entry even where no companion tick follows. Every line has a schema version and increasing sequence; a normal session has explicit start and end records. Write failures disable the stream and log an error; an absent end marker remains interrupted or active capture. Lifecycle occurrences name only callbacks this mod observed: world entry, first update, tag-load callback entry/return and save callback entry/return. `OnWorldLoad` precedes TagIO load, and returning from this ModSystem callback cannot prove Terraria completed the outer load or persisted the world; an unfinished stream is reduced coverage, never proof of a load failure. A shot records its final launch direction and intended impact, native projectile callbacks record terrain/enemy contact and death, and native damage records effective life removed. Pickups record the amount actually transferred before the engine clears the item. Decision snapshots carry raw/final scores, regroup pressure and estimated return time. A separate navigation snapshot records whether the brain actually executed on that tick, the search and attempt identities, completed steps, remaining route estimate, pending/expansion state, bounded progress reason, request and controls. It also preserves the valid follow objective and axis-specific gaps, recovery flight state, guard commitment, mine job state, target-evidence age and alternatives. This prevents an action preserved from a pre-death or skipped tick becoming fictional evidence of a cooldown or stall. Movement snapshots carry search stop, execution status, preparation result and the last physical rejection. That rejection is historical evidence, not a claim that the current executable move is rejected.

Terrain capture rotates through bounded chunks around both actors after the world tick. Each changed snapshot carries collision glyphs, platform semantics, exact liquid kind/amount, tile types and wall types. The queue limits work per tick and the remembered-chunk cache bounds memory; revisiting an evicted chunk writes another snapshot. Local sampling can lag a rapid edit or traversal, and uncaptured regions remain unknown. It neither dumps the whole world every tick nor stops recording after a session-wide event cap. The event header describes the coverage and binary encoding. Movement invalidation has its own owner and never depends on this recorder.

Reading it: upward velocity after grounded movement establishes take-off; a stable observed position despite a travel request establishes lack of progress. Search and execution status distinguish a proposed path from a validated move. A behavioural judgement uses the raw and final scores beside the changing player/threat state. Narration and a manual mark key are not required; the timestamped observations provide the chronology, with scenery and causal interpretations stated only where the recorded evidence supports them.

**Reading it by hand is no longer the way, and that is the point of the columns added on 2026-09-09.** `../../../../Tools/SessionReport/` takes a session and prints what is definitely wrong, what is probably wrong and what merely looks odd, addressing every column by name because the indices move whenever this file grows a fact. Five columns were added specifically so that reader could separate causes it otherwise had to guess at: `self_threat` is the companion's own danger beside the player's, which is what makes "it was hit in a tick it scored as perfectly safe" a definitive finding instead of an ambiguity; `press` is whether the motor was asking to fall through, which is the whole tell for the platform freeze; `engage` is what the hands are shooting at, independent of what the feet were told, so "it was following me and not attacking" is a row where that reads `-` beside a threat count; `exp_target` is what the chosen weapon expected against the target it actually fired at; and `fire` is *why* no projectile left the hands — `fired`, `cooldown`, `no-arc`, `no-target`, `hands-busy` — because a reload and a body standing in a place with no shot both read as `shot 0` and want opposite fixes.

The general rule those five follow: **record the rejected option and the reason for the negative, never only the outcome.** A record that says what happened supports one question, "what happened"; a record that says what else was on the table and why it lost supports the question anyone actually asks, which is "why that". The two expected-damage columns were the first of this shape and the fire reason is the second; the runner-up spot beside the chosen spot is the obvious third and is not written yet.

## Traps

Decision and navigation payloads retain engine-gravity, model-gravity, gravity-enabled and gravity-observation-tick from the motor's AI-entry observation. These distinguish a native/model environment mismatch from a different jump impulse. The source tick matters when recording retained state; the enabled flag matters during recovery. The recorder never runs another movement proof to produce these values.

The continuous safety fields carry a response identity, active flag, current kind/reason and last end reason. Navigation snapshots preserve the same owner alongside the shared escape target and phase. A safety response can exist with no ordinary activity and without a new chooser comparison. Its identity denotes one retained response, not a completed escape; release and the observed body state must be checked separately. Downing records explicit cancellation rather than leaving the previous response active.

Player intent records both vector axes, travel confidence, supporting sample count and local-work fraction. These describe the bounded observer's inference; velocity remains a separate physical observation. Confidence without samples is not evidence of a journey, and an inferred direction is not proof that a future meeting position is reachable.

Mining and chopping expose prepared remaining tool ticks and hits. Minus one means no estimate, not immediate completion. These values concern the next native tile outcome, excluding travel and collection, and retain the ordinary comparison's freshness: a safety tick does not refresh the suspended work estimate. They must be compared with actual tool outcomes before diagnosing inaccurate timing.

The score ledger and inspector include the applied reunion multiplier. Continuous rows retain accumulated observed separation, the last compared departure strength and delay-cost rate. Separation can update while safety skips selection; the latter two remain comparison evidence and use choice freshness. Reading all three as fresh utility output on a safety tick would invent a comparison that did not run.

`control-grant` records ownership transitions from the coordinator's completed grant. The TSV preserves every grant's identity, source tick, freshness, hand permission, requested owner and motor application count; the existing control source is the applied owner. Downed and recovery ticks can have fresh grants alongside stale ordinary choices. Available hands permit use but do not establish that a weapon fired. Requested and applied movement describe the AI phase before engine integration. `finalise_ms` measures motor application, hand resolution and outcome observation separately from navigation preparation, so a timing comparison must account for that boundary.

`activity-state` snapshots the primary owner's activity ID, phase, interruption reason and last termination. The recorder consumes the owner and never performs entry, exit or suspension. Tool effects join their native attempt to both comparison and activity IDs. Decision and navigation snapshots also carry the primary activity identity and phase, so recovery movement beside a suspended mining purpose cannot be read as ordinary mining execution. Actor/session identity remains necessary for these joins. Executing describes the adapter's current phase, not proof that a route succeeded or a useful native effect occurred.

Each recorded factor ledger includes the evaluator's error. Invalid candidate values or overflowing products produce zero selectable value with a named reason; the retained raw value remains evidence of the bad input. A zero from invalid evaluation must not be interpreted as a deliberate low preference.

The factor payload and decision tooltip also retain each queried attack method's source tick, request kind, destination, reason and bounded position alternatives. A method rejection keeps positive raw utility beside zero selectable value; the field distinguishes wanting to protect the player from having established a useful intervention. Empty method evidence means no query was recorded for that candidate. The inspector and recorder consume the retained ledger rather than running position selection again.

Every actual attack-method query also emits a `method-assessment` occurrence while recording is enabled, including repeated rejection without an activity-label change. It names the upcoming comparison and explicitly precedes activation; a missing later comparison remains missing. The native event target identity and gameplay observer generation are separate fields because their counters have different owners. Admission establishes only the bounded position query's result, with travel and native effects still unobserved. Periodic decision snapshots cannot replace these occurrences because a short-lived rejection can fall between snapshots.

Decision snapshots include every purpose family's nominated child and its already-evaluated value. An empty family writes child none and zero value. Recording reads LastNominations rather than rerunning selection; these are retained results with the decision's existing freshness metadata, not another comparison.

`brain_fresh` records that the coordinator ran; `choice_fresh` records that its chooser completed a comparison. Recovery and reflex paths may make the first true and the second false. `choice_id` and `choice_tick` retain the last comparison's identity and source time, with zero identity and minus-one tick before any comparison. Sparse decision freshness uses the chooser, while navigation freshness still describes brain execution. Tool effects carry the comparison identity that selected the executing action. Recording an unchanged winner does not establish a newly evaluated choice, and a comparison ID is not an activity or physical attempt ID.

`tool-effect` records preserve actor spawn generation, tool-local attempt identity, the activity attempt open when Execute struck (`activity-attempt-id`, zero with none open), source tick, target coordinate and native before/after tile and hit-table snapshots. The tool-local `attempt=` counts the tool instance's own swings and is never an activity attempt; the striking activity reads the owner's open attempt at the strike rather than the recorder inferring it from the row, because an attempt can open, strike and close inside one tick. Their amount is the observed positive damage increment only for a damage outcome; removal clears native damage bookkeeping and must not become negative damage. Material/frame changes remain distinct from productive damage or removal. Item yield remains explicitly unobserved by this event; pickups are separate evidence. Recording consumes these snapshots and never performs another tool query or mutation.

Schema 0.31.0 replaces the light scalar with what the torch decision actually reads. `dark_near` and `dark_ahead` are the share of measured open air that reads dark around the body and at the player's predicted feet, `torch_reason` is the answer that decided the hand this tick, and `light_region` is the dark region the lighting activity nominated as `tile,count` or `-`. `light_samples` and `light_read_tick` are the old ambient pair renamed: a zero brightness with zero samples reads as the unmeasured state it is rather than as a measured dark place, and the read tick is when the mod sampled, never when the lighting engine computed the tiles. Both shares write `-` rather than a zero when nothing was measured, because no dark air and nobody looked are opposite facts and a zero says the first. The same release renames `reach_n` and `returnable_n` to `reach_any` and `reach_two_way`, after the reach sense that now owns both floods, and adds `reach_complete` beside them: without it a small region and an unfinished one read alike, and "the tile is not in the set" means the opposite thing in each. The navigation-state occurrence carries the same `reach-complete`, so a capture can say whether a flood had settled on a tick that chose no destination.

Schema 0.32.0 appends `lighting_sites` and `lighting_sites_asked`, and declares `torch_reason` textual — which 0.31.0 wrote and never declared, so every one of its rows read as a column that failed to parse as a number. **The `# text_columns=` preamble is a hand-maintained string sitting beside the header builder, so every new textual column is two places to remember and the second is the one that gets forgotten.** SessionReport unions its own known-textual set with whatever a capture declares, so a capture already written is read correctly rather than only the next one; the declaration is still maintained, because the reader's set is a backstop and not the record.

`lighting_sites` is what lighting's last discovery search put to the approach query and what the reach sense answered, as `x,y=reachable|unreachable|not-yet-known` pairs, and `lighting_sites_asked` is how many it asked. They exist because the offer string alone cannot separate a starved search from a search over a genuinely sealed screen: the 2026-09-14 capture read "the question was not finished" on 79% of its rows with nothing to say whether three sites had been asked or three hundred. The ledger stops at twelve entries and the count does not, so the two together read as "the first few of this many" rather than presenting a sample as the whole search.

`terrain_revision` is appended after them, with the schema again left where it is. It is how many terrain edits the world has announced since it loaded, as a running count, so a reader takes differences: the edit *rate* is what decides how often retained search work is discarded, and the record held no column that could say what it was. The 2026-09-14 session was briefly read as carrying 426 edits, which were its `terrain-snapshot` records — captures on a cadence rather than edits — and that mistake is only avoidable once the count is in the row.

`transient_lights` is appended before them at the end of the 0.31.0 row, with that schema left where it was because nothing before it moved and every reader addresses columns by name. It counts the lights somebody was carrying inside the light field's window this tick — the player's torch, a pet, a mining helmet, whatever a mod adds — each of which the field discounted rather than reading as the room's. It earns a column because the failure it describes is otherwise invisible in a capture: a companion walking a passage that looks lit beside a torch-carrying player, placing nothing, is indistinguishable from a companion with nothing to do, and the count is the one number that separates them.

Schema 0.20.0 adds attempt and offer evidence. `activity_attempt_id` is the open attempt or zero; the `attempt_end_*` columns retain the latest concluded attempt's identity, activity identity, activity, family, status, cause, attribution, credited productive effects and its start and end ticks, and keep that history rather than re-reporting it as fresh each row. The `attempt-outcome` occurrence's channel is the status alone, or `Status:Attribution` wherever an attribution applies, so a reader grouping by channel keeps a shared or unattributed completion apart from the companion's own. One `<activity>_offer` column per registered activity carries `Eligibility:reason` from the retained score board, or `not-compared` when the activity was absent from it; those names are appended to the text-column declaration from the same registration list as the header, so the two cannot disagree. The sparse stream gains one `attempt-outcome` occurrence per concluded attempt, written through a cursor so two conclusions in one tick both appear, and control grants now carry the attempt identity they were issued under. The decision board's factor entries include `offer:Eligibility/reason`. An Interrupted attempt is an observation about ownership, never a verdict that the work was a bad choice.

Schema 0.21.0 adds `<family>_prepared`, `<family>_deferred` and `<family>_prepare_ms` for each purpose family, retained from the last completed comparison like the score board and reading -1 before any, and the decision board's `queries:<Family>=prepared:n,deferred:m,ms:x` entries. A deferred child's offer column reads `Deferred:family-preparation-allowance-spent`. Deferral is a scheduling fact about that comparison, never evidence that the activity's opportunity was absent.

Schema 0.22.0 appends reunion's meeting place to the end of the row: `meeting_reason` (priced ahead, priced at the player's position, retained, retained while undecided, undecided, no reachable place, player not travelling, not reuniting), `meeting_anchor`, the player's and the companion's priced ticks to it (-1 when unpriced), and how many candidates were considered and priced. The companion's ticks are measured from where its flood was rooted. Schema 0.23.0 appends `meeting_flood`: whether that flood is advancing, exhausted or capped at its node limit, and after the `@` how many ticks ago it was rooted, which is how old the companion's ticks behind a decision are — an unfinished flood keeps its root while the body stays in its returnable region, so that age is not bounded by the re-root cadence. A `not-reuniting` reason means the other columns describe no current destination; the place is released whenever keeping company leaves reunion.

Schema 0.24.0 ends the row with the navigator's movement-failure evidence, after the meeting columns: `nav_failure` is the class explaining why the held goal is not being delivered (`None` while it is), `nav_failure_reason` the evidence that produced it, `nav_failure_search_id` and `nav_failure_attempt_id` join it to the route query and traversal attempt it came from, `nav_attempt_ending` is how the last attempt ended, and `nav_attempts_completed`, `_failed`, `_preempted` and `_cancelled` are running tallies. The three text fields are appended to the text-column declaration. The failure is sticky until delivery or a new goal, so a row repeats a verdict until something supersedes it; count changes of `nav_failure_attempt_id` or `nav_failure_search_id`, never rows. The sparse movement-state record carries the same `failure`, `failure-reason` and `attempt-ending` fields and is written whenever the verdict changes.

Schema 0.30.0 joins two branches that numbered their schema changes independently, and no played capture carries either branch's numbers. The evidence branch's 0.25.0 to 0.29.0, described after the combat columns below, are the numbers SessionReport's version gates use; the combat branch spent 0.25.0 and 0.26.0 on the columns in this paragraph and the next, which the reader addresses by name. From 0.30.0 the row ends with the combat identities, after the navigator's columns, each written as `slot:generation` or `-`. `pursuit_target`, `pursuit_value`, `pursuit_access_ticks` and `pursuit_evidence` are the hunt's retained choice, its arsenal-valued delayed attack, its reposition wait and every candidate it examined (`slot:generation:verdict:wait:value`, pipe-separated); `aim_target` is the hands' target on that tick; `landed_hit_target`, `landed_hit_aimed`, `landed_hit_damage`, `landed_hit_tick` and `landed_hits` are the last companion shot the game reported striking something, the target that shot was aimed at, the effective damage and when, and a running count. The three identities are recorded apart because none is evidence for another: a shot at the visible enemy is not pursuit progress against the hidden one, and a hit is not the target the shot was aimed at. The landed-hit fields retain the last hit with its own tick rather than reporting it as fresh each row. `guard_removal_ticks` and `guard_usefulness` are protection's removal estimate and value share (-1 when no weapon can damage the threat), and `top_threat_effective_player` and `top_threat_effective_companion` are what the most urgent threat's hit costs each body after defence. Last come `encounter_intensity`, `encounter_source` (`boss`, `event:<name>`, `observed-pressure` or `none`), `encounter_recognised` (1 only for a native boss or event fact) and `encounter_pressure_ticks`, the observation the evaluator charged optional work against on that tick. `encounter_pressure_ticks` counts consecutive ticks on which the spawn-slot weight of reachable hostiles exceeded the game's own spawn cap; rows recorded before that rule counted ticks with a fixed number of reachable hostile records, so the column's values are not comparable across that change although its name and position are.

`guard_access_ticks` follows `encounter_pressure_ticks` (the combat branch's 0.26.0): the firing access protection's share counted for the prepared threat — `0.0` for a shot from here, the travel estimate to the nearest reachable sighted tile after moving — and `-1` when nothing was counted, which covers an unsettled reachable region, a proven absence of any firing position and a threat no weapon can damage. Those three are told apart by `guard_usefulness` beside it: a proven absence is the only one whose share is zero, and an undamageable threat also reads `-1` in `guard_removal_ticks`.
Schema 0.25.0 changes no TSV column: it appends `activity-attempt-id` to the `tool-effect` channel, after `activity-id`. The schema line versions the capture as a pair, so a reader seeing 0.25.0 knows every strike in the sidecar names its attempt, and a reader seeing an older schema knows none does and joins strikes through rows instead.

Schema 0.26.0 again changes no TSV column. The `attempt-outcome` detail ends with `claimed-yield-type` and `claimed-yield-quantity`: the item type and quantity the activity's own conclusion claims reached the cargo, both zero when it claims none. Collection is the only claimant — its Complete and Partial conclusions claim what the cargo's transfer ledger received from the drop the attempt walked to — and an interruption never claims, because the owner concludes it without asking the activity. The `pickup` detail ends with `collection-attempt-id`: the collection attempt open when the item touched was that attempt's own drop, asked at the contact pickup before the transfer runs, and zero for every other pickup. Every accepted transfer goes through that one contact site, so the pickups naming an attempt are everything its claim could have read.

Schema 0.27.0 ends the row with the positioner's success region, which from 0.30.0 follows the combat columns above: `region_kind` (none, follow-comfort, tool-reach, firing-position, meeting-place, undeclared), `region_revision` (the destination revision, which changes whenever the held destination does), `region_tick` and `region_terrain` (when and under which terrain revision it was admitted), `region_anchor_px`, `region_player_px` and `region_comfort` (the follow references and comfort extents, `-` for other kinds), `region_work_tile` and `region_reach` (a tool stand's tile and reach, `-` otherwise), and `region_arrival`. The last is the recorder's verdict: `-` unless the navigator reports Arrived on a tick the ordinary branch requested travel, because any other owner leaves the status from an earlier move; otherwise `inside` or `outside` from the region's own arithmetic on the entry body's feet, or `undeclared` when the kind carries no geometry. The seven text fields are appended to the text-column declaration. Count changes of `region_revision`, never rows: one destination repeats across every row that holds it.

Schema 0.28.0 changes no TSV column; it adds provenance and closure around the rows. The preamble gains `# source_revision=<revision>;tree=<clean|dirty|unknown>`, read from assembly metadata that `AICompanion.csproj`'s `StampSourceRevision` target stamps from git at build time — `unknown` when that build could not ask git, never anything inferred from the folder — and `# config=character;…`, the six per-character preferences and the two diagnostics switches the session starts under. A change to any of them mid-session is a `configuration` occurrence with the same payload, detected by comparing a value every row without allocating. Every normal closure ends the file with `# end=<reason>;rows=<n>`: world-unload, mod-unload, recording-disabled, superseded-by-world-load, or fixture-close from EngineReplay's direct calls. Only `Close(reason)` writes it; a failed row write disposes the stream without it, so a capture lacking the line was interrupted. An assembly built from a dirty tree records the revision it started from and says dirty, which is the honest limit of a build-time stamp.

Schema 0.29.0 ends the row with what recording costs and what the capture did not keep, as running totals: `record_ms` is the previous row's `Record` cost (a row cannot time its own write, so a session's first row carries `-`), `events_written` counts records handed to the event writer, `events_dropped` counts occurrences refused because a failed write had stopped that writer, `events_coalesced` counts cosmetic terrain contacts folded into summary records, and `terrain_evictions` counts chunks the terrain snapshot memory forgot. The end marker restates the closing totals. A drop is counted at every occurrence producer's guard, not only inside the writer, because most producers return before writing when the stream is inactive; before a session opens and after a normal close nothing is recording, so an occurrence offered then is not counted as lost. Terrain snapshots are outside the drop count: `RecordTerrainChunks` checks whether the stream is active every tick before it captures anything, so counting there would count ticks rather than lost evidence, and a stopped stream shows instead as `terrain-snapshot` records ceasing.

Schema 0.31.0 ends the row with `stops_per_minute` and `route_speed_mean`, running values over the ticks the body has spent on its own route, both `-1` until it has spent one — a rate over no travel is unmeasured rather than zero. Beside them the sidecar gains two occurrences from `ObserveTravelEpisodes.cs`. A `route-episode` is one whole journey — one continuous stretch of wanting one kind of place, the same episode the census counts — carrying its request kind, whether it was reached or abandoned, the sum of the proven ticks of the steps that finished inside it, the ticks it actually took — the span between its two tick stamps less the ticks the body spent downed, which are carried beside it — the straight-line and path distances in tiles, the mean observed speed, and the ticks the *player's* own recorded trail took between the same two ends, or `-` where the trail never covered both. That last figure is the only reference in this folder the brain did not produce, and it is the design's pass line applied to time rather than to terrain. A `stop` is a body on its own route under a walking pace for three ticks or more, with its reason attributed from retained state at the moment it happens — `during-replan`, `airborne-no-sideways-speed`, `brake-before-from-rest-move`, `inside-walk-step`, `other`, asked in that order — and the evidence that produced the reason written beside it, so a reader can disagree with the attribution without re-deriving the state behind it.

**Why a journey needs its own measurement at all, when the edge columns already time every move.** A move can complete inside its proven ticks every single time while the journey takes four times as long, because the time goes into the gaps between the moves rather than into the moves — and an instrument that only ever times the moves reports that session as clean. The episode is the unit the player actually feels.

**`brake-before-from-rest-move` is written to be deleted.** It exists because a move proven from a standing start requires the body at rest before it begins, so the ticks spent arriving at rest are that move's own cost rather than a fault. When the momentum graph lands and a move can be proven from the speed the body already carries, the reason becomes false rather than merely unused, and it is removed along with the `FromRest` read behind it.

**The observer derives its episodes rather than reading the census's.** `BehaviourCensus` keeps its open episode in private statics and exposes no accessor, so the boundaries are mirrored from the same retained facts the census's own callers act on: the recorded control owner (`travel` and `seeking-destination` open or continue an episode, `hold` ends one, and every safety, recovery and downed owner leaves it open) and the navigator's arrival.

**Where the census's boundary rule may be mirrored and where it may not.** Keeping an episode open through a dodge, a recovery flight or a death is right for both instruments, because an ask does not stop being one ask when something else takes the body for a moment. What may not be carried across is what those ticks then *mean*: the census counts asks, so a tick inside one costs it nothing, while this instrument divides distance by ticks and reports a duration, so every tick it holds is charged to the follower. A downing runs into the hundreds of ticks — longer than most journeys — so a death charged as travel would be the loudest slow journey in every report, from the one instrument built to say whether the follower is slow. Downed ticks are therefore counted separately, removed from the reported duration and from the distance, and written beside it; the wall clock stays recoverable from the two tick stamps, so nothing is lost by removing them. The general rule for anything built beside the census: a boundary that is right for a count is not automatically right for a duration, and the reason has to be re-derived rather than borrowed. The census's "places asked for, and whether the body got there" table is therefore a free consistency check on the episode counts — if the two disagree, one of them has drifted from the brain.

## The preamble says which world and which kit, because neither can be inferred afterwards

Two lines were added beside `# source_revision=`, and both exist to stop a false positive that nothing downstream could catch.

`# world=` carries the world id, its name, a stable hash of its unique id and generation seed, and its size. Without it, replaying an old capture against a world he has since mined through reads as a regression in everything: the route is the same, the terrain is not, and every unreachable answer is charged to the brain. **The hash is FNV-1a written out by hand**, because `string.GetHashCode()` is randomised per process on .NET Core — a hash taken from it differs between two captures of the same world and agrees with nothing, including itself tomorrow. A world whose file data is not yet attached records `unknown` rather than a hash of nothing, since an invented identity makes two worlds compare as one.

`# capabilities=` carries both bodies' movement kits: the companion's from `MovementCapabilities` itself rather than a list of flags copied out of it, so an ability added to that record appears here by having been added there, and the player's mount, wings, dash and rocket boots read from `Player`. **Recorded, never inferred**, and that is a ruling rather than a convenience: a harness that watches where the player went and decides which ability must have taken them there is a heuristic with its own false positives running underneath the thing being measured, and the question it feeds is exactly the one that matters — a place the player reached by wings is not evidence the companion failed to reach it, and a place they walked to is.

The companion's side is the *declared* kit and not a live read: the header is written at world entry, before any companion exists to ask, and the navigator's own capabilities are an instance property with no instance yet. That is exact while mastery is unbuilt and every companion runs the basic kit. **The moment a per-character kit can differ from the declaration, this line has to move from the header to the row**, which is also where the verification plan wants the player's flags — it asks for them per tick, and a header records only what was true at world entry.

## What a capture keeps, and what it forgets

Every capture's preamble carries `# retention=`, generated from the constants that bound each store rather than restated, so a reader tells an absence the recorder never kept from one that did not happen without opening the code. Rows are written on every companion AI tick while recording is on, and the occurrence stream writes every occurrence it is offered, bounded by nothing but the session; what is bounded is:

- terrain snapshots: `RecordTerrainChunks` captures a bounded number of chunks per tick around both actors and remembers a bounded set of last-written chunks. Memory exists only to suppress a capture identical to the last one written, so an eviction re-writes a chunk later rather than losing it.
- attempt outcomes: `OwnCurrentActivity` keeps a bounded list of recent conclusions, which the recorder drains each row by an attempt-id cursor, so the bound limits how many conclusions one tick can hold, not how many a session records.
- transfers: `CompanionInventory` keeps a bounded ring of recent accepted transfers, which is why a collection claim can only undercount.
- cosmetic contacts: harmless non-damaging projectile contacts are folded into a summary record after a bounded count.
- the inspector: `BrainInspectorSamples` keeps a bounded queue of aim and movement traces and a ring of recent brain-cost ticks for drawing, and nothing of either reaches the capture.
- the session map: `SessionMap` remembers a bounded number of tiles for its closing artefact.
- plan dumps and flushing: route plans are dumped at a fixed tick cadence, and rows since the last flush are what an interrupted capture loses.

The `mine_end_*` columns retain the last ended mining job's identity, source tick, reason and original bounded vein counts: present, changed, missing and unobserved. Unique sites with companion-attributed native removals are separate from missing world tiles. An absent conclusion uses job zero, tick minus one and unknown counts; a retained conclusion keeps its own time rather than becoming a fresh completion every row. Decision and navigation payloads carry the same gameplay-owned snapshot. A cleared observed set proves neither full-vein coverage nor item collection.

Session reservation owns the TSV handle immediately, before sidecar or capture initialisation can fail. The open-failure path disposes that reserved stream, closes any event capture and clears its paths; dropping the writer reference alone leaks the handle until collection. A recorded initialisation failure describes capture availability, never the surrounding world's load outcome.

Navigation evidence includes shared query and attempt identities, fresh/stale decisions, control source, pending local search, retained controls, breathing target and remaining breath. Position evidence keeps the best evaluated alternatives with final score and shot feasibility; target evidence keeps considered entity generations with expected effective damage and urgency. Each has its own evaluation tick because a held choice may outlive its last rescore. Unexamined candidates remain unexamined; these bounded alternatives do not enumerate the entire search tree. The HTML reader exposes the complete retained payload beside sampled continuous state.

- `ModPlayer.OnHurt` exposes resolved damage before subtracting health. Its event records the current life as the pre-state and labels the computed remaining life as expected; it must not fabricate an observed post-state. `PostHurt` alone omits fatal hits. The next continuous observation establishes actual resulting life.

- **A telemetry row has three named phases.** `observed_left` through `observed_mobility` are captured at AI entry before the brain writes helpers; `predicted_*` is the motor's same-body result after it receives the selected controls and before helpers; `npc_px` remains an after-helper compatibility value. `sample_phase` spells this out as `observed_before_ai;request_after_ai;npc_px_after_helpers`. A reader uses the observed fields for actual motion and never treats `npc_px` as an unmodified engine pose.
- **`moved` is blind to anything our own AI phase does to the position, and that is what hid the platform freeze for three attempts.** `oldPosition = position` is assigned inside `Collision_MoveWhileDry` immediately before `position += velocity`, so `moved` spans the engine's own move and `Collision_MoveSlopesAndStairFall` and nothing else. Our brain, our motor and `Collision.StepUp` all run before that assignment, so a write they make to `npc.position` is invisible here by construction — which is exactly what the motor's step-up was doing, lifting the body back onto the platform it was descending. The `pinned` column is the instrument that catches this class: a real velocity beside no displacement is impossible for a body the engine is integrating, so any run of it names an AI-phase writer without needing to know which one.
- **`vel_cut` does not measure what its name says, and the comment that claimed it did was wrong.** `oldVelocity = velocity` is assigned in `UpdateCollision` *after* our AI and before `ApplyTileCollision`, so `vel_cut` is the difference between two consecutive ticks' post-AI velocities, not what the collision removed. It happens to equal the collision's cut on ticks where the motor leaves `velocity.Y` alone — which it does throughout a descent, so the freeze reading was valid — and it is confounded in general rather than broken. Read it as "how the velocity changed across the tick boundary", not as "what collision took off".
- **A column's meaning is established from the writer before any arithmetic is argued from it.** `npc_px` writes `npc.Bottom`, whose X is the body's *centre*; read as the box's left edge it moves every clearance calculation ten pixels and produced a confident, wrong diagnosis of the platform freeze on 2026-09-09 — that the body was too wide for a shaft it fits with three pixels to spare.
- **A detector's "how long ago" is a subtraction, and a sentinel of `long.MinValue` makes it overflow.** The dodge detector compared `tick - lastDodgeTick` against its memory window with that sentinel, the subtraction wrapped a signed long to a large negative number, and the negative passed the `<=` test: every hit in a session where no reflex had ever fired was dumped as a hit through a dodge, with an age of −9,223,372,036,854,773,868 ticks printed in the window header. An age test needs the event itself to have happened, checked as its own clause, never encoded in a sentinel value. Found on 2026-09-09 by `../../../../Tools/SessionReport/` printing the window reasons; the detector had been fabricating scenarios since it was written.
- **An instrument that reports a defect it invented is worse than one that reports none**, because the invented one gets diagnosed. Every detector here is a claim about the game, so it is held to the bar the brain is held to, and a detector whose output looks impossible is a bug in this folder until proved otherwise.
- **A column inserted in the middle shifts every index after it**, and this file inserts columns often. Nothing reads it by position: `../../../../Tools/SessionReport/Session.cs` addresses by header name for that reason, and a hand-rolled `awk -F'\t' '{print $57}'` is wrong within a week of being written — which it was, on 2026-09-09, reporting a mean distance of three and a half thousand tiles.
