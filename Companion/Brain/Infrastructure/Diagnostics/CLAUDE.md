# Behaviour diagnostics — the overlay and the record

Two independently configurable instruments answer why the companion acted: the visual inspector exposes retained decision and execution evidence, and the recorder preserves observations for an unnarrated session. `Companion/DiagnosticsConfiguration/` owns the native ModConfig switches. The inspector, every overlay layer and telemetry default on so a playtest can watch the brain and still writes a capture. World-enter turns the panel and all layers on while the inspector config is enabled. Recording opens on world entry only when enabled; disabling closes the stream immediately without clearing gameplay prediction state. A missing observation remains unknown rather than being reconstructed from intended action. The clearance layer tints the combined wall-and-enemy heat parking and routes both read.

```
Diagnostics/
├─ CLAUDE.md
├─ DrawBrainOverlay.cs       selectable world layers and a scrollable raw/final decision score panel
├─ BrainInspectorSamples.cs bounded traces retained from actual aiming, movement and reflex computations, and the last second of brain cost the strip plots
├─ DescribeExecutionEvidence.cs the inspector's Execution lines and success-region boxes, read from retained brain state only
├─ RecordBrainTelemetry.cs   one tab-separated phase-labelled sample per companion tick, its actual elapsed wall time and a tile-window dump per failed plan or detected scenario
├─ MeasureFrameCost.cs       the whole game update, its draws counted apart from it, and this mod's own two drawing sections
├─ RecordGodsEyeEvents.cs    sparse JSONL occurrence stream: stable spawn identities, shots, damage, pickups, native outcomes and each activity's candidate funnel when it changes
├─ ObserveNativeCombatEvents.cs native spawn, projectile contact, player/direct and projectile damage, and death hooks; each physical strike enters Observation's gameplay receipt queue once per local bracket
├─ ObserveTerrainChanges.cs  bounded rolling terrain chunks around both actors, refreshed after edits
├─ ObserveTravelEpisodes.cs one whole journey against its proven ticks and the player's own, and every stop on a route with the reason attributed as it happens
├─ CaptureMovementScenario.cs the six detectors that turn a playtest failure into a dump under their own reason; thresholds and cooldowns tune the instrument, not the brain
├─ CaptureDecisionSnapshot.cs the values-only identity a producer attaches to an occurrence; a mutable brain, a Terraria entity or a live collection may never cross this boundary
├─ ReadCourseWorthPerActivity.cs the one place any surface asks what the course thought each registered activity's work was worth; the recorder's columns, the overlay's decision panel and the inspector's Execution tab all read it
├─ RecordCourseTrace.cs      the typed course records: observation ordinals, scalar-only payload fields and the occurrence shapes short of a whole decision snapshot, and the seam the decision audit hangs on
├─ AuditDecisionContracts.cs six contracts every published decision must keep, and the coalesced `contract-violation` occurrence written when one breaks
├─ ReadLiveCourseForAudit.cs the one file on either side of the headless-compile boundary that names `Selection/`: the audit's census and target facts, installed as its source
├─ QueueDiagnosticRecords.cs the bounded ordered transport: required, optional and gap partitions, charged by their complete retained text
├─ FlushDiagnosticRecords.cs the only owner of both sinks, through open, write, flush and disposal
└─ DrawSessionMap.cs         one zoomed-out picture of the whole session written on world unload: everywhere the body went, everywhere it was asked to go, and per ask whether the distance ever closed
```

Retained-course evidence begins at telemetry schema 0.41.0. Course occurrences cross the diagnostic boundary as immutable data-only contexts, scalar payloads and decision snapshots. The snapshot manifest compares every expected fact key, version and digest with the captured value; an aggregate digest also binds the context, model, scheduler and random state. The reader recognises only supported payload versions. These checks establish complete captured inputs, not an executed replay or a proof that the producer declared every read. A missing, evicted, mismatched or historical field is partial coverage, never an empty decision.

`QueueDiagnosticRecords` keeps required, optional and gap partitions bounded, charges the complete retained text and holds each reservation until its write resolves. `FlushDiagnosticRecords` alone owns both sinks through open, write, flush and disposal. A reader fence waits for queued records without adding a gameplay-thread disk write. A timed-out close retains worker ownership and marks the recording incomplete; if a clean terminal line was already being written, the worker appends an authoritative incomplete terminal line after that write resolves. The reader uses the last terminal status and verifies its row count before certifying input coverage. Injected sinks exercise held terminal writes and faults without involving Terraria or relying on a slow filesystem.

**0.45.0 adds the `contract-violation` occurrence, which is the first thing this folder writes that says a decision was wrong rather than only what it was.** Six independent readings of the 22 September 2026 capture each reached the same finding by hand: the census admitted three combat targets and four collect targets as `usable`, and the ordering stage in the same decision, against the same frozen observation, refused every order built from them because the target fact was not `Observed`. Each record carries the violation kind, a prose detail naming the numbers it read, a signature, the frozen observation's ordinal, and two counts. **The contracts are keyed on the observation ordinal rather than on the tick, and that is load-bearing**: `Admitted`, `LastRefusals` and `Last` all persist while a retained course is carried, so a per-tick audit would report one contradiction five hundred times. The transitions — a course released within a tick of publishing, and a bound activity changing while the decision is unsettled — are per event, because they are about the boundary rather than the decision. **Both of those read records the producer writes on an *unsettled* decision, and a rule that waited for a settled one would never fire in play while passing every hand-built fixture row.** `DecideCourseEachTick` releases inside the tick that finds its next use invalid and starts a decision on that same tick; a decision that does not settle in one tick returns companionship, and `Companionship` calls `Trace` like any other outcome, so the record carrying `release-reason` is usually unsettled. The activity-exit contract keys on the *transition into* an unsettled decision rather than on every unsettled tick, because `lastActivity` advances on every recorded decision — which is why the 22 September capture's 212 one-tick combat attempts are 212 firings rather than one per tick of each. **Output is coalesced on the evidence signature, so the true bound is one record per kind per sixty ticks *per signature*, and a kind whose signature moves writes on the tick it moves**, which is the same rule the candidate funnel already uses; a session that breaks a contract on most of its ticks would otherwise fill the sidecar's two-megabyte optional partition and mark the whole capture incomplete, losing the evidence the tripwire exists to keep, and `occurrences-since-last` and `occurrences-total` ride on every record so the count survives the coalescing. **That bound is only worth the sentence if a signature is stable while the thing it describes is, and two of them were not.** `census-admitted-binder-refused` keyed on the live usable count and `empty-course-beside-usable-work` on the whole usable summary with its numbers in it, so a contradiction standing while the census drifted by one wrote a record a tick — defeating the coalescing at the only moment it is needed. Both key on *which* domains contradicted themselves and how, never on how many candidates each admitted, and the counts stay in the detail. The two that remain deliberately coarse say so at their site: `fact-count-above-bound` keys on the fact count in 256s and `decide-overran-allowance` on the cost in 8 ms steps, because a growing overrun is a different finding from a steady one and the bucket is how it becomes visible without a record a tick. Two of the six carry a declared bound rather than a contradiction and say so at their constant: `MaximumFactsPerDecision` is 512 and **mirrors nothing yet** — there is no declared fact budget anywhere in `Selection/`, so once the course brain names one this constant mirrors it, and until then it is taken from the 22 September capture, where a decision carried a median 149 facts over its first 200 ticks and 1,548 over ticks 1,801–2,000 with about sixty of them accounted for by the census; and `DecideCeilingMilliseconds` reads `Weights.TotalPlanningMilliseconds` plus `Weights.RouteSearchMilliseconds`, the tick's allowance plus the largest atomic slice a query already inside its own deadline may finish, both read rather than copied so retuning either moves the ceiling. The same version adds `target-evidence` and `target-evidence-age` to the `course-decision` payload, appended by `RecordCourseTrace` rather than built in `Selection/`: a refused order records the count of its refusals and never the evidence the binder read, and the evidence is only reachable while the frozen observation is in hand. The age is ticks since **this recorder** last saw that fact key `Observed`, which is the only source for it — a `DecisionFact` carries a version and a digest and no observation time — and it is `-1` for a key never seen observed this session, which is a different fact from zero and is the distinction a reader of the column has to be able to make.

**The same two names come back as TSV columns, and they do not mean what they meant before 0.40.0.** `target_evidence` and `target_evidence_age` were the arsenal's own bounded shortlist of rejected weapon-target pairs — `slot:generation:0:0:weapon=N:reason` — and went with the weapon block's rename; a SessionReport check has been a named skip on every capture for the five schema versions since, while the shortlist it parsed was written nowhere. They are the course's now, per domain, as `<domain>=<fact key>:<evidence>:<observed>/<total>` and `<domain>=<ticks>`, with a dash for a decision that contradicted nothing — never for an absence of evidence. **The fact key carries two colons of its own**, being a `FactKey` rendered `kind:identity:generation`, so a reader splitting the entry left to right finds five fields where the format names three and must parse from the right-hand end instead. A capture from before 0.40.0 carries both names holding the arsenal's format, which is why the check reading them names a 0.45.0-only witness column it never reads: a column whose meaning moves under its own name is the one case the append convention cannot carry, and a witness is how a reader refuses to guess which meaning it has.

**The same version ends the row with a frame ledger, because the half of the frame outside the brain had no column at all.** On the 22 September capture the brain was 10.17 ms of a 25.30 ms median interval, so it was both the largest single contributor and less than half the problem, and subtracting it and the recorder left about 14 ms a frame unattributed between the engine, the inspector's own drawing — switched on for that whole session — and everything else; every independent reading of that capture had to end its frame-rate paragraph saying so. `frame_ms`, `draws`, `overlay_ms`, `inspector_ms` and `engine_ms` close the row, and `MeasureFrameCost` is their producer. Two things about them are load-bearing and a reader who assumes otherwise will get a number wrong.

**An update is not a frame.** The engine runs a fixed timestep and catches up by running two updates back to back with no draw between them — the 6.30 ms minimum gap in that capture is one — so `frame_ms` is update-to-update and `draws` is how many draws happened inside the interval. Frames a second is derived from the draw count and updates a second from the interval, and a figure named "frames per second" taken from the gaps is updates a second wearing the wrong name. The draw is counted at the top of the draw callback before any early return, so a session with the inspector switched off still reports its draws.

**Every timing column here declares its regime, and the five are not all on one clock or one tick.** `frame_ms`, `overlay_ms` and `inspector_ms` are the ledger's own `Stopwatch`, started once and never restarted inside a session, so they are subtractable from each other. The interval is anchored at `PostUpdateEverything`, because `ModSystem` on this tModLoader has no `PreUpdateEverything`; the consequence is a phase rather than a bias — **a row written inside update N sees the interval that closed at the end of update N−1**, so `frame_ms` describes the update before the row's own. Everything subtracted from it is that same update's: `brain_ms` is taken from the *previous* row and `record_ms` is already the previous row's cost, because a row cannot contain the time spent writing itself. `engine_ms` is therefore a remainder and never a measurement of the engine — Terraria's own update, the health notch, the profile card, any other mod's drawing and any cost of ours nothing times all sit inside it — and a negative value is written as it comes out rather than clamped, because a clamp turns an instrument fault into a plausible zero. None of these is comparable with a figure taken any other way, which is this project's standing rule and binds here as hard as anywhere. Beside them the sidecar gains a `frame-overrun` occurrence, written **per window of thirty ticks rather than per overrunning frame**: 84% of that capture's frames overran, so an occurrence each would be two thousand records carrying one fact, and the window carries how many overran and the whole split of the worst of them. The session-wide share is read off the `frame_ms` column instead, where every row has one.

**0.45.0 also stops two things in a capture from lying about the session that wrote them, and both are the folder's own trap rather than new defects.** The `# config=` preamble line was written by `WriteMetadata` at `OnWorldLoad`, which is before the character's saved preferences are loaded, so every value in it was a default — the 22 September capture's header read `chopping=Opportunistic` beside a tick-1 `configuration` occurrence and 3,781 census admissions both saying `Mimic`, and nothing in the file said which to believe. The line is written from the **first recorded row** now, which is the first moment the preferences the brain is deciding with exist, and it is still a preamble line because the header itself is written on that same row. **A session that recorded no row still writes one**, from `Close`, because the first version of this simply moved the line and a zero-row capture then carried no `# config=` at all — which reads as a capture from before 0.28.0 rather than as a session that opened and recorded nothing. One consequence binds anything reading a capture's occurrences: **a preference set before the first row is the session's baseline and writes no `configuration` occurrence**, where a preference moved after it still does, so a fixture that changes a preference right after `OnWorldLoad` is changing the baseline rather than witnessing a change. And `task_order` and `task_order_runner_up` read `Chooser.LastTaskOrder`, which is `OrderNearbyTasks`' permutation scoring, retired with the family chooser on `0bb2c8a` — so **every row of every capture since that commit wrote a dash in both** and a reader asking a capture what order it considered got nothing. `task_order` is the published course's bound steps in the order it will perform them, by purpose, and `-` for an empty course. `task_order_runner_up` **cannot be filled and writes the reason instead of a dash**, because a dash there is exactly what the dead column already wrote: `SearchCourseOrders` retains `Best` and `BestValue` and no second-best order, the search pruning as it goes, so filling it needs an accessor on that class for the runner-up projection whose first step differs from the best one's — and that file belongs to the course rather than to this folder. Both names and both positions are unchanged; the meanings moved, which is the one change the append convention cannot carry and is the reason this version exists.

Two shapes in that audit exist because their absence was measured while building it, and both generalise past this occurrence. **A coalesced record's totals are the totals as at the last record written, so the tail is lost unless something flushes it**: three hundred contradictions coalesced every sixty ticks closed at a recorded 241, a number nobody reading the capture could have told from the truth, so the recorder's own `Close` calls `AuditDecisionContracts.Flush` before the event stream shuts and each kind that has moved since its last record files one more carrying the closing total. And **the audit takes its census and its target facts through a delegate rather than reading the brain**, because `RecordCourseTrace` is on `Tools/EngineReplay`'s explicit second-compile list and calls it: reaching for `CompanionNPC.Instance.Brain.Course` from a file on that list would drag the whole of `Selection/` and `Activities/` across a boundary the headless suite cannot compile. `ReadLiveCourseForAudit` installs the real source from the recorder's `Load`, and a fixture replaces it to drive one decision with no world.

**That delegate costs the seam its witness, and getting the witness right took two goes.** Every tripwire row calls `AuditDecisionContracts.Audit` directly, because that is the only way to hand it a scene — so deleting the `Observe` call from `RecordCourseTrace.Record` left all of them green, and a count of audits proved nothing while nothing drove the count through the hook. Two things witness it now and they cover different failures. `the recorder's own seam drives the audit end to end` installs a source, goes in through `Record`, and reads a `contract-violation` record and the appended `target-evidence` back out of the sidecar; it is the only row here that exercises the hook, and removing the hook reddens it. **The installer cannot be witnessed headlessly at all** — `ReadLiveCourseForAudit` is not on EngineReplay's compile list, which is the whole point of it, so a tree where `Install` is never called compiles and passes every row. Its witness is the capture: `decisions-audited` and `audit-observations-read` ride out on the `# closing=` line, and the session reader's `did the decision audit run on the decisions this capture holds` grades the two zeroes apart — decisions recorded with nothing audited is the hook gone, and everything audited with nothing read is the installer never run, which silently reduces six contracts to the two transitions that read the payload alone. They are two counts rather than one because `Audited` increments at the top of the audit before the source is consulted, so a broken installer reports every decision audited and would look healthy under one number.

**The audit's own ordering is part of what it costs, and it was wrong in both places when it landed.** The hook ran before the event stream's `Accepting()` gate, so a session with recording off audited everything; and the source was invoked before the observation-ordinal short-circuit, so a carried course paid a full walk of the frozen observation on every tick to reach a line that returns immediately. Both are the other way round now — recording is checked first, the source is passed rather than invoked, and the refusal tally is parsed below the short-circuit for the same reason — and the row `a tick carrying a course reads the observation nowhere and allocates nothing` measures both orderings in one process: **200 source reads and 4,272 bytes per carried tick before, 1 read and 0.0 bytes after.** The general property, for anything else hung off a per-tick recorder: a diagnostic keyed on something coarser than the tick has to *read* at that coarseness too, or the key saves only the output.

Three older reading traps survive the bump. `follow_dx` and `follow_dy` kept their names and positions across 0.25.0 and changed what they measure, from offsets to the player's feet to offsets to the intent region's centre. `ObserveTravelEpisodes.cs` keeps its `travel-committed` arm, which for the orb marks a release the navigator is honouring rather than a jump in flight. And `terrain_revision` is written from `../Movement/TerrainModel/`'s recorded-edit ring rather than from anything this folder owns.

## A detector can only find a failure somebody imagined, so the report opens with a census

This is the correction of 2026-09-09 and it is the most important sentence in this folder. Every instrument here was a detector: it fires on a threshold a person chose, so a behaviour nobody thought to threshold produces no report at all, and zero findings is indistinguishable from zero coverage. In nine minutes the follower completed 1,227 walks, eight drops, no jumps and no fall-throughs; all four numbers were already in columns 85 to 90, no check asked the question, the session read clean, and the defect surfaced only because Caner mentioned in passing that he had watched it jump.

That is a coverage gap and not a false negative — a false negative needs a test that existed and said clean — so the answer is a different shape of instrument rather than a better threshold. `../Movement/Steering/BehaviourCensus.cs` counts every category whether or not anything happened in it and attaches no verdict, so `asked for 40 places, reached 11` is a row nobody reads past where a detector's zero is silence. It is written beside the session on world unload and `../../../../Tools/SessionReport/` prints it above every finding.

The deeper form of the same problem is that every check here compares the brain against itself, so it can only ever verify internal consistency: behaviour that is derived correctly from the senses and is nonetheless wrong produces nothing. Competence needs a reference the brain did not produce, and the only one available in a singleplayer mod is the player's own trail — which this folder already writes into every window and which exactly one of the nine detectors reads. Widening that is the standing work, and the design's own pass line says why: if the player got through, the companion must.

## Every failure the player can feel becomes a block the tool replays

The pass line is a route the orb can fly; a player's dash-assisted route requires evidence of that capability. `ScenarioCapture.Watch` runs on every recorded tick and keeps its counters. Following the player, more than a leash of tiles behind for seconds, and no nearer than when the count began is a follow failure (a companion closing the gap at the player's pace is doing its job, and the Codex review of the first version showed the bare distance test firing every cooldown on an ordinary chase); a body that has not moved for seconds while the follower had a route is stuck, counted here and not on the navigator's own counter, which every replan resets; life lost within a second of a reflex approving a dodge, lava and fire excluded, is a hit through a dodge, which means the prediction the dodge was judged against was wrong; the player mining or chopping for seconds of activity, pauses of up to a second allowed because the ore-hit marker lives less than a second, while the matching action scored zero throughout, is a missed mode, which is the pickaxe icon never showing. The walker's three detectors about jumps, mislandings and step faults went with the walker. Every duration is in game ticks, and every counter resets when a session opens, so a previous world's dodge cannot label the next companion's first scratch. Each fires at most once per cooldown and writes the same tile window the failed-plan dump writes, with the reason in the header, the start at the companion's centre and the goal at the navigator's goal or the player's feet. Every window, whichever path wrote it, carries a `trail` line: the player's feet tiles over the last while, oldest first, one per change of tile, kept on the player sense. The replay tool reads that line back and reports how many trail tiles fall inside the window and the first one the grid refuses, either not standable or outside the region the companion can reach from its start, so a failed follow reads as "the grid refuses tile X, which the player stood in" rather than as a red verdict.

The plan dump is the instrument for "why could it not get there": the navigator calls `DumpPlan` on every plan that returned nothing or only a partial path, and at most once every five seconds the writer appends the tile window spanning the start, the goal and the player's feet (padded, cut to a readable size on the start's side, because the first missing link is near the start; the player is inside it because "could it have reached the player" is the replay tool's second verdict, and a goal picked above a pit said nothing about a player six rows below the window) as one character per tile, written through `TextTileWorld.Glyph` in `../Movement/TerrainModel/` so the replay tool reads back exactly what was written: `#` solid, `=` platform, `_` half block, `\` and `/` floor slopes (solid below the line drawn), `<` and `>` ceiling slopes (solid on the side the bracket points away from), `~` water or honey, `L` lava, `.` air, with `S` start, `G` goal, `N` the companion's tile and `P` the player's feet drawn over air tiles only, a `markers` line under the header naming every one of those positions outright, because on a half block or a floor slope a marker written on the grid erased the support from the replay (2026-09-08 Codex review), and the orb's exact centre as the header's `orb cx,cy`; the parser takes the line over the grid and still reads old dumps off the grid. The header line carries the tick, the reason, the expansions used and the window's bounds. It exists because the third run of 2026-09-08 showed 1,136 expansions on every plan for its last 3,468 ticks (about a minute at sixty a second) with the player over 200 tiles below, and nothing in the per-tick line says which tile the grid refused.

`RecordBrainTelemetry.cs` opens a versioned, tab-separated file on world load under the mod's source folder (`Main.SavePath/ModSources/AICompanion/Telemetry`, which is this repository; the folder is in `.gitignore` and `buildIgnore`). A two-line metadata preamble records the schema and UTC start instant, while each row carries an actual `Stopwatch` elapsed time rather than a wall time derived from ticks. Every row samples the body once, after the motor has run the contact and before the engine adds the velocity, and `sample_phase` says so; there is one body and no helper that moves it, so the walker's three phases are gone. The record carries both bodies' precise positions, velocities and liquid state, the orb's wall contact and clearance, the player's life/state/activity, the companion's desired velocity beside its applied controls and their source, and sparse hit events. The player event carries Terraria's final damage, source entity where one exists, direction and knockback; the companion event carries final damage, direction and knockback, and explicitly says that the NPC callback exposes no attacker source. A life fall without an event remains a life fall; the record does not invent a hit or knockback. Plan dumps pass the terrain's fall-through property to the text glyph so a sloped platform remains a sloped platform in replay. The writer flushes once a second and closes on world or mod unload.

The TSV remains the continuous measurement. Its `-events.jsonl` sibling is the sparse causal trace: each native hook snapshots entity fields, a per-spawn identity, tick and Stopwatch time before the engine object can mutate or its slot can be reused. A UTC millisecond stem reserves the TSV with `FileMode.CreateNew`; a same-instant retry gains an attempt suffix, and its sidecars use that reserved stem, so a second load cannot overwrite the first run. Metadata is flushed at world entry even where no companion tick follows. Every line has a schema version and increasing sequence; a normal session has explicit start and end records. Write failures disable the stream and log an error; an absent end marker remains interrupted or active capture. Lifecycle occurrences name only callbacks this mod observed: world entry, first update, tag-load callback entry/return and save callback entry/return. `OnWorldLoad` precedes TagIO load, and returning from this ModSystem callback cannot prove Terraria completed the outer load or persisted the world; an unfinished stream is reduced coverage, never proof of a load failure. A shot records its final launch direction and intended impact, native projectile callbacks record terrain/enemy contact and death, and native damage records effective life removed. Pickups record the amount actually transferred before the engine clears the item. Decision snapshots carry raw/final scores, regroup pressure and estimated return time. A separate navigation snapshot records whether the brain actually executed on that tick, the search and attempt identities, completed steps, remaining route estimate, pending/expansion state, bounded progress reason, request and controls. It also preserves the valid follow objective and axis-specific gaps, recovery flight state, guard commitment, mine job state, target-evidence age and alternatives. This prevents an action preserved from a pre-death or skipped tick becoming fictional evidence of a cooldown or stall. Movement snapshots carry search stop, execution status, preparation result and the last physical rejection. That rejection is historical evidence, not a claim that the current executable move is rejected.

Terrain capture rotates through bounded chunks around both actors after the world tick. Each changed snapshot carries collision glyphs, platform semantics, exact liquid kind/amount, tile types and wall types. The queue limits work per tick and the remembered-chunk cache bounds memory; revisiting an evicted chunk writes another snapshot. Local sampling can lag a rapid edit or traversal, and uncaptured regions remain unknown. It neither dumps the whole world every tick nor stops recording after a session-wide event cap. The event header describes the coverage and binary encoding. Movement invalidation has its own owner and never depends on this recorder.

Reading it: a velocity beside a `pinned` count establishes a body held against terrain; a stable position despite a travel request establishes lack of progress; a `desired_vel` the `npc_vel` never approaches names the motor or the contact rather than the brain. Search and execution status distinguish a proposed route from one being flown. A behavioural judgement uses the raw and final scores beside the changing player/threat state. Narration and a manual mark key are not required; the timestamped observations provide the chronology, with scenery and causal interpretations stated only where the recorded evidence supports them.

**Reading it by hand is no longer the way, and that is the point of the columns added on 2026-09-09.** `../../../../Tools/SessionReport/` takes a session and prints what is definitely wrong, what is probably wrong and what merely looks odd, addressing every column by name because the indices move whenever this file grows a fact. Five columns were added specifically so that reader could separate causes it otherwise had to guess at: `self_threat` is the companion's own danger beside the player's, which is what makes "it was hit in a tick it scored as perfectly safe" a definitive finding instead of an ambiguity; `press` is whether the motor was asking to fall through, which is the whole tell for the platform freeze; `engage` is what the hands are shooting at, independent of what the feet were told, so "it was following me and not attacking" is a row where that reads `-` beside a threat count; `exp_target` is what the chosen weapon expected against the target it actually fired at; and `fire` is *why* no projectile left the hands — `fired`, `cooldown`, `no-arc`, `no-target`, `hands-busy` — because a reload and a body standing in a place with no shot both read as `shot 0` and want opposite fixes.

The general rule those five follow: **record the rejected option and the reason for the negative, never only the outcome.** A record that says what happened supports one question, "what happened"; a record that says what else was on the table and why it lost supports the question anyone actually asks, which is "why that". The two expected-damage columns were the first of this shape and the fire reason is the second; the runner-up spot beside the chosen spot is the obvious third and is not written yet. The candidate funnel is the rule at the scale of a whole search: every candidate a preparation refused, the stage that refused it and the numbers it read, beside the one it offered.

## Traps

**A diagnostic that reads a retired producer fails silently, and a system with nothing to report looks exactly the same.** This is the folder's own version of the census correction above and it is the lesson of 21 September. A frozen float is a legal float, an empty list parses, and `0.00` is a number — so nothing went red, nothing threw, and four surfaces reported constants for a whole day. The recorder, the overlay panel, the Execution tab and the decision occurrence were each reading `Chooser.*` after the course took the tick. The symptom would have appeared as a report on the next capture somebody opened, which is the worst place to find it. Two consequences bind anything added here: a surface reads its producer through one shared reader rather than reaching for a static itself, and **the check for a stranded producer asks what the old procedure *wrote*, not only what it returned.** `VerifyTheCourseOwnsTheTick.TheLegacyChooserIsOffThePath` proved the chooser had stopped deciding and could never have caught any of this, because it only ever looked at the return value. The sharpest instance is `4e7d094`: `Chooser.Choose` did two jobs and only one of them was choosing — its first thirty lines observed companionship — so taking `Choose` off the tick stranded the return estimate, the delay cost, the departure reading and the regroup urgency while the apart-tick count, reached directly, kept running. **A decision procedure that also observes leaves its observations stranded when something replaces the decision.**

The continuous safety fields — a response identity, active flag, kind, reason and last end — and the navigation snapshot's escape target and phase went in schema 0.36.0 with environmental escape, the takeover they described. A capture written before carries them, and a reader reads them there by name; a capture written after has no column for them, which is an absence of the response rather than an absence of evidence.

Player intent records both vector axes, travel confidence, supporting sample count and local-work fraction. These describe the bounded observer's inference; velocity remains a separate physical observation. Confidence without samples is not evidence of a journey, and an inferred direction is not proof that a future meeting position is reachable.

Mining and chopping expose prepared remaining tool ticks and hits. Minus one means no estimate, not immediate completion. These values concern the next native tile outcome, excluding travel and collection, and retain the ordinary comparison's freshness: a recovery or downed tick does not refresh the suspended work estimate. They must be compared with actual tool outcomes before diagnosing inaccurate timing.

The score ledger and inspector include the applied reunion multiplier. Continuous rows retain accumulated observed separation, the last compared departure strength and delay-cost rate — all of which are observed by `Chooser.ObserveCompanionship`, which the tick calls directly, rather than as a side effect of a comparison; between `0bb2c8a` and `4e7d094` they were reached only through `Choose`, so `reunion_delay_cost_per_tick` and `reunion_departure` sat at their defaults while `reunion_apart_ticks` climbed beside them, and a capture written in that window carries one live number and two constants. Separation can update while recovery skips selection; the latter two remain comparison evidence and use choice freshness. Reading all three as fresh utility output on a recovery tick would invent a comparison that did not run.

`control-grant` records ownership transitions from the coordinator's completed grant. The TSV preserves every grant's identity, source tick, freshness, hand permission, requested owner and motor application count; the existing control source is the applied owner. Downed and recovery ticks can have fresh grants alongside stale ordinary choices. Available hands permit use but do not establish that a weapon fired. Requested and applied movement describe the AI phase before engine integration. `finalise_ms` measures motor application, hand resolution and outcome observation separately from navigation preparation, so a timing comparison must account for that boundary.

`activity-state` snapshots the primary owner's activity ID, phase, interruption reason and last termination. The recorder consumes the owner and never performs entry, exit or suspension. Tool effects join their native attempt to both comparison and activity IDs. Decision and navigation snapshots also carry the primary activity identity and phase, so recovery movement beside a suspended mining purpose cannot be read as ordinary mining execution. Actor/session identity remains necessary for these joins. Executing describes the adapter's current phase, not proof that a route succeeded or a useful native effect occurred.

Each recorded factor ledger includes the evaluator's error. Invalid candidate values or overflowing products produce zero selectable value with a named reason; the retained raw value remains evidence of the bad input. A zero from invalid evaluation must not be interpreted as a deliberate low preference.

The factor payload and decision tooltip also retain each queried attack method's source tick, request kind, destination, reason and bounded position alternatives. A method rejection keeps positive raw utility beside zero selectable value; the field distinguishes wanting to protect the player from having established a useful intervention. Empty method evidence means no query was recorded for that candidate. The inspector and recorder consume the retained ledger rather than running position selection again.

Every actual attack-method query also emits a `method-assessment` occurrence while recording is enabled, including repeated rejection without an activity-label change. It names the upcoming comparison and explicitly precedes activation; a missing later comparison remains missing. The native event target identity and gameplay observer generation are separate fields because their counters have different owners. Admission establishes only the bounded position query's result, with travel and native effects still unobserved. Periodic decision snapshots cannot replace these occurrences because a short-lived rejection can fall between snapshots.

Decision snapshots carried every purpose family's nominated child and its already-evaluated value until 0.42.0, read from `Chooser.LastNominations`; under the course brain that list is always empty and the alternatives are the four `course*` entries above. Recording still reads retained results with the decision's existing freshness metadata rather than rerunning anything.

`brain_fresh` records that the coordinator ran; `choice_fresh` records that a comparison completed. Recovery and reflex paths may make the first true and the second false. `choice_id` and `choice_tick` retain the last decision's identity and source time from `DecideCourseEachTick`, with zero identity and minus-one tick before any decision, and the identity advances once per decision *reached* rather than once per tick a retained course is carried. Tool effects carry the comparison identity that selected the executing action. Recording an unchanged winner does not establish a newly evaluated choice, and a comparison ID is not an activity or physical attempt ID.

`tool-effect` records preserve actor spawn generation, tool-local attempt identity, the activity attempt open when Execute struck (`activity-attempt-id`, zero with none open), source tick, target coordinate and native before/after tile and hit-table snapshots. The tool-local `attempt=` counts the tool instance's own swings and is never an activity attempt; the striking activity reads the owner's open attempt at the strike rather than the recorder inferring it from the row, because an attempt can open, strike and close inside one tick. Their amount is the observed positive damage increment only for a damage outcome; removal clears native damage bookkeeping and must not become negative damage. Material/frame changes remain distinct from productive damage or removal. Item yield remains explicitly unobserved by this event; pickups are separate evidence. Recording consumes these snapshots and never performs another tool query or mutation.

Schema 0.31.0 ends the row with `stops_per_minute` and `route_speed_mean`, running values over the ticks the body has spent on its own route, both `-1` until it has spent one — a rate over no travel is unmeasured rather than zero. Beside them the sidecar gains two occurrences from `ObserveTravelEpisodes.cs`. A `route-episode` is one whole journey — one continuous stretch of wanting one kind of place, the same episode the census counts — carrying its request kind, whether it was reached or abandoned, the sum of the proven ticks of the steps that finished inside it, the ticks it actually took — the span between its two tick stamps less the ticks the body spent downed, which are carried beside it — the straight-line and path distances in tiles, the mean observed speed, and the ticks the *player's* own recorded trail took between the same two ends, or `-` where the trail never covered both. That last figure is the only reference in this folder the brain did not produce, and it is the design's pass line applied to time rather than to terrain. A `stop` is a body on its own route under a crawling pace for three ticks or more, with its reason attributed from retained state at the moment it happens — `during-replan`, `against-wall`, `other`, asked in that order — and the evidence that produced the reason written beside it, so a reader can disagree with the attribution without re-deriving the state behind it. The walker's four reasons about airborne ticks, rest before a proven move and the inside of a walk step went with its moves; an orb on a route either is replanning, is pressed into a wall by the contact, or has stopped for a reason the observer cannot name, and `other` is kept so that the unnamed case is counted rather than hidden.

**Why a journey needs its own measurement at all, when the route columns already say where the body is on it.** A body can make progress on every segment while the journey takes four times as long, because the time goes into the gaps — the replans and the wall contacts — rather than into the flight, and an instrument that only ever reads the route index reports that session as clean. The episode is the unit the player actually feels.

**The observer closes the companion it watched, not the one the recorder finds at unload.** The recorder's close hands it the live lookup, which searches the NPC table; a journey still open when the world closes is the last one a session has, and whenever that search found nothing it was dropped without a record — a fixture that killed and revived the body mid-journey and then unloaded reported no journey at all. The observer keeps the companion its last watch saw and closes that one when it is handed nothing.

**The observer derives its episodes rather than reading the census's.** `BehaviourCensus` keeps its open episode in private statics and exposes no accessor, so the boundaries are mirrored from the same retained facts the census's own callers act on: the recorded control owner (`travel` and `seeking-destination` open or continue an episode, `hold` ends one, `accompany` ends one as reached, `travel-committed` continues the one already open, and every recovery and downed owner leaves it open) and the navigator's arrival. `accompany` ends a journey as reached whatever the navigator says, because it is the owner that moves the body about the player's region once the body is inside it: being inside is what a journey toward him is for, and a route to a place inside the region is dropped the moment the body gets there rather than flown to its end.

**`travel-committed` exists because a source name that lumps two states together destroys the measurement built on it.** The brain named the control source `hold` while the navigator was still steering a move it had committed to and would not abandon, so the observer read the ordinary end-of-journey signal and closed the episode mid-air: the end feet were in the air, and the landing's ticks and its edge report left every travel figure the instrument produces. The source is `travel-committed` for as long as a release is pending, the episode stays open across it, and the census's ask stays open too, so a journey walked to its landing under a held release reads as reached rather than abandoned. The general form, for anything else that ever reads a control source: an owner string is an input to instruments that were written before that owner existed, so a new state folded into an old name is a silent change to every one of them.

The rate columns are narrower than the episodes beside them, deliberately and worth knowing before comparing the two: `stops_per_minute` and `route_speed_mean` accumulate only on a plain `travel` owner with the navigator executable or partial, so the committed-release ticks an episode holds are not in their denominator.

**Where the census's boundary rule may be mirrored and where it may not.** Keeping an episode open through a dodge, a recovery flight or a death is right for both instruments, because an ask does not stop being one ask when something else takes the body for a moment. What may not be carried across is what those ticks then *mean*: the census counts asks, so a tick inside one costs it nothing, while this instrument divides distance by ticks and reports a duration, so every tick it holds is charged to the follower. A downing runs into the hundreds of ticks — longer than most journeys — so a death charged as travel would be the loudest slow journey in every report, from the one instrument built to say whether the follower is slow. Downed ticks are therefore counted separately, removed from the reported duration and from the distance, and written beside it; the wall clock stays recoverable from the two tick stamps, so nothing is lost by removing them. The general rule for anything built beside the census: a boundary that is right for a count is not automatically right for a duration, and the reason has to be re-derived rather than borrowed. The census's "places asked for, and whether the body got there" table is therefore a free consistency check on the episode counts — if the two disagree, one of them has drifted from the brain.

## The preamble says which world and which kit, because neither can be inferred afterwards

Two lines were added beside `# source_revision=`, and both exist to stop a false positive that nothing downstream could catch.

`# world=` carries the world id, its name, a stable hash of its unique id and generation seed, and its size. Without it, replaying an old capture against a world he has since mined through reads as a regression in everything: the route is the same, the terrain is not, and every unreachable answer is charged to the brain. **The hash is FNV-1a written out by hand**, because `string.GetHashCode()` is randomised per process on .NET Core — a hash taken from it differs between two captures of the same world and agrees with nothing, including itself tomorrow. A world whose file data is not yet attached records `unknown` rather than a hash of nothing, since an invented identity makes two worlds compare as one.

`# capabilities=` carries both bodies' movement kits: the companion's from `MovementCapabilities` itself rather than a list of flags copied out of it, so an ability added to that record appears here by having been added there, and the player's mount, wings, dash and rocket boots read from `Player`. **Recorded, never inferred**, and that is a ruling rather than a convenience: a harness that watches where the player went and decides which ability must have taken them there is a heuristic with its own false positives running underneath the thing being measured, and the question it feeds is exactly the one that matters — a place the player reached by wings is not evidence the companion failed to reach it, and a place they walked to is.

The companion's side is the *declared* kit and not a live read: the header is written at world entry, before any companion exists to ask, and the navigator's own capabilities are an instance property with no instance yet. That is exact while mastery is unbuilt and every companion runs the basic kit. **The moment a per-character kit can differ from the declaration, this line has to move from the header to the row**, which is also where the verification plan wants the player's flags — it asks for them per tick, and a header records only what was true at world entry.

## What a capture keeps, and what it forgets

Every capture's preamble carries `# retention=`, generated from the constants that bound each store rather than restated, so a reader tells an absence the recorder never kept from one that did not happen without opening the code. Rows are written on every companion AI tick while recording is on, and the occurrence stream writes every occurrence it is offered, bounded by nothing but the session; what is bounded is:

- terrain snapshots: `RecordTerrainChunks` captures a bounded number of chunks per tick around both actors and remembers a bounded set of last-written chunks. Memory exists only to suppress a capture identical to the last one written, so an eviction re-writes a chunk later rather than losing it.
- attempt outcomes: `OwnCurrentActivity` keeps a bounded list of recent conclusions, which the recorder drains each row by an attempt-id cursor, so the bound limits how many conclusions one tick can hold, not how many a session records.
- transfers: `CompanionInventory` keeps a bounded ring of recent accepted transfers, which is why a collection claim can only undercount.
- cosmetic contacts: harmless non-damaging projectile contacts are folded into a summary record after a bounded count.
- the inspector: `BrainInspectorSamples` keeps a bounded queue of aim and movement traces and a ring of recent brain-cost ticks for drawing, and nothing of either reaches the capture.
- the session map: `SessionMap` remembers a bounded number of tiles for its closing artefact.
- plan dumps and flushing: route plans are dumped at a fixed tick cadence, and rows since the last flush are what an interrupted capture loses.

The `mine_end_*` columns retain the last ended mining job's identity, source tick, reason and original bounded vein counts: present, changed, missing and unobserved. Unique sites with companion-attributed native removals are separate from missing world tiles. An absent conclusion uses job zero, tick minus one and unknown counts; a retained conclusion keeps its own time rather than becoming a fresh completion every row. Decision and navigation payloads carry the same gameplay-owned snapshot. A cleared observed set proves neither full-vein coverage nor item collection.

Session reservation owns the TSV handle immediately, before sidecar or capture initialisation can fail. The open-failure path disposes that reserved stream, closes any event capture and clears its paths; dropping the writer reference alone leaks the handle until collection. A recorded initialisation failure describes capture availability, never the surrounding world's load outcome.

Navigation evidence includes shared query and attempt identities, fresh/stale decisions, control source and pending search state. Position evidence keeps the best evaluated alternatives with final score and shot feasibility; target evidence keeps considered entity generations with expected effective damage and urgency. Each has its own evaluation tick because a held choice may outlive its last rescore. Unexamined candidates remain unexamined; these bounded alternatives do not enumerate the entire search tree. The HTML reader exposes the complete retained payload beside sampled continuous state.

- `ModPlayer.OnHurt` exposes resolved damage before subtracting health. Its event records the current life as the pre-state and labels the computed remaining life as expected; it must not fabricate an observed post-state. `PostHurt` alone omits fatal hits. The next continuous observation establishes actual resulting life.

- **A telemetry row has one phase and says which.** `sample_phase` reads `in_ai_after_contact_before_engine_move`: the body is sampled after the motor has resolved the contact and written the velocity, and before the engine adds it. `npc_px` is therefore the centre the engine will move from, and `npc_vel` is the displacement it will add. The walker's three phases existed because the engine's own collision ran after the AI and the record had to hold both sides; the orb's contact runs inside the AI, so one sample is the whole tick.
- **`moved` spans the engine's own position update and nothing else.** `oldPosition = position` is assigned inside the engine immediately before `position += velocity`, so a write the motor makes to `npc.position` directly — the recovery ejection, the downed sink — is invisible to `moved` by construction. The `pinned` column is the instrument for the class: a velocity beside no displacement is impossible for a body the engine only integrates, so any run of it names an AI-phase writer without needing to know which one.
- **A column's meaning is established from the writer before any arithmetic is argued from it.** `npc_px` writes `npc.Center` now, where the walker's wrote `npc.Bottom`; a reader carrying the walker's feet arithmetic over an orb capture is off by a body radius on every clearance it computes, which is the same class as the wrong shaft-width diagnosis of 2026-09-09.
- **A detector's "how long ago" is a subtraction, and a sentinel of `long.MinValue` makes it overflow.** The dodge detector compared `tick - lastDodgeTick` against its memory window with that sentinel, the subtraction wrapped a signed long to a large negative number, and the negative passed the `<=` test: every hit in a session where no reflex had ever fired was dumped as a hit through a dodge, with an age of −9,223,372,036,854,773,868 ticks printed in the window header. An age test needs the event itself to have happened, checked as its own clause, never encoded in a sentinel value. Found on 2026-09-09 by `../../../../Tools/SessionReport/` printing the window reasons; the detector had been fabricating scenarios since it was written.
- **An instrument that reports a defect it invented is worse than one that reports none**, because the invented one gets diagnosed. Every detector here is a claim about the game, so it is held to the bar the brain is held to, and a detector whose output looks impossible is a bug in this folder until proved otherwise.
- **A column inserted in the middle shifts every index after it**, and this file inserts columns often. Nothing reads it by position: `../../../../Tools/SessionReport/Session.cs` addresses by header name for that reason, and a hand-rolled `awk -F'\t' '{print $57}'` is wrong within a week of being written — which it was, on 2026-09-09, reporting a mean distance of three and a half thousand tiles.

`DrawBrainOverlay.cs` registers the inspector key, default left square bracket. `PlayerIntegration/HandleCompanionInput.cs` honours only the saved Mod Controls binding. A saved key that FNA cannot report must be rebound; changing the registered default cannot migrate it, and there are no hidden raw-key overrides. The key opens a native-coloured menu with World layers and Decisions tabs. The menu and the drawings are independent: closing the menu dismisses only the panel and the selected layers keep drawing, because the panel covers the thing it describes and a layer you can only see while a menu is open is a layer you cannot watch. "Show world drawings" is the master off switch, and the diagnostics config forces both off. Input is consumed before player item use. Rows scroll at small effective viewports, and each choice explains the evidence it draws.

World layers include observed enemy bodies and velocities, already-computed terrain-constrained enemy forecasts, the projectile observer's linear forecasts, committed route steps, evaluated standing alternatives, actual sampled aiming traces, evaluated local movement alternatives, passive-body danger and separate hand/work/player attention targets.

Three layers say what the companion means to do rather than what it looked at, and they ship on in the live mod rather than behind a developer switch, so they are held to the bar of a surface a player sees. **Where following wants it** (bit 10) draws the player's intent region itself, because the follow box *is* that region now: a solid box at the region's own centre and half-size — led, filtered, clamped and grown — tinted from calm to warning by the region's `Pull` at the companion's feet, so a body drifting toward an edge is visible before it is a complaint, with a line from the player's feet to that centre and a ring at the leading edge wherever the lead is non-zero, and a label carrying whether the sense reads travelling or still, the pull and the resting-settled tick count. The request's own anchor is a small dashed box beside it, so the gap between what the sense offers and what the request named is the picture. Nothing here builds a `FollowPlayerObjective`: the drawing reads `Senses.Intent.Region`, which is the same object every consumer reads, so it cannot disagree with the brain by construction rather than by mirroring its inputs. Beside them the calm distance band as two faint rings, and the retained meeting place as a white ring carrying its reason and both bodies' priced ticks — drawn ahead of the with-player guard, so a meeting place stays visible on a tick following is not asking for. Those rings are two different kinds of circle and the file keeps two helpers so they cannot be confused: the calm band's radii are real world distances and scale with the zoom, while the meeting marker and the drop diamonds are fixed screen sizes so a place forty tiles away stays findable. A marker drawn at a world radius with its label offset by the same constant separates from that label as the view zooms, which is a class of defect invisible at zoom one. It is deliberately a different picture from the success-region layer, which draws what an earlier tick *accepted*: the gap between the two is the question whenever following looks wrong. **What it senses** (bit 11) draws the player-activity arrow — the player sense's own confidence-weighted continuation a fixed number of ticks out, labelled with that confidence and its supporting sample count, and it is a different fact from the intent region bit 10 draws: this is where the player looks like they are going, that is the box the follower measures itself against — and each drop as a diamond whose fill is its reach state — filled where the positioner's last flood holds the drop's feet tile, filled in the warning colour where the flood finished without it, and hollow where the flood has not settled, because an unfinished flood is not evidence that a drop is unreachable. Bit 12 was **Safety response**, which labelled the escape and drew a line to the tile being escaped to; it went with the escape, and the bit is retired rather than reused, so a saved layer mask from before cannot switch on whatever would have taken its place. **Cost of thinking** (bit 13) plots the last second of `DecideMs + PositionMs + NavigateMs` against a ceiling that never moves. Appending a layer means a bit, a menu entry, a description and an arm in the index-to-toggle switch, and that switch names every index explicitly with a false default rather than falling through to the last layer: a default arm holding a real toggle maps the next appended bit onto an existing layer instead of onto its own, silently, and the menu then shows two entries that flip together. **Clearance field** (bit 14) tints every free tile within a screen's reach of the body by how far it is from a wall, reading `ClearanceField.Shared` and computing nothing — it is the picture of what the search's edge cost prices, so a route hugging a red tile is a route the cost failed to move. The route layer draws the navigator's smoothed route as it stands and the lookahead point the steering law is aiming at in gold, which is a steering target rather than a waypoint. Bits above are open for the candidate grid by rejection reason and the reach flood tint.

The cost strip reads the brain's three per-tick laps and never `flood_ms`, which is two separate errors avoided: `Positioner.LastFloodMs` is sticky, so summing it per column draws one flood as a sixty-wide plateau and destroys the spike the fixed ceiling exists to show, and the flood runs inside `Positioner.Resolve` inside the `PositionMs` lap, so adding it to a navigate-and-decide sum counts it twice. The sample is taken once per game tick in `PostUpdateEverything` rather than while drawing, because drawing runs per frame and a per-frame sample turns one column per tick into one column per frame the moment the machine drops below sixty — which would make the strip read as a slower brain on a slower machine. **A zero column is a tick the brain did not run on, never a cheap tick**: a downed companion or a frame Terraria skipped records zero rather than repeating the last value, because a repeat draws a plateau that never happened, and the two are told apart only by knowing this. The decision panel reads `ReadCourseWorthPerActivity` and shows, per activity, what the course thought its work was worth: the outline is the nominal total of the best order that domain leads, the fill is what survives that order's harm and companionship costs, and gold is the activity running. Its hover text was rewritten rather than kept when the course took the tick, because the old line multiplied five chooser factors that no longer exist and keeping its wording would have been a sentence about a brain nobody runs. Optional captures attach to real solver calls; drawing never asks a planner or aimer to compute another answer. Each retained trace has a short lifetime and a fixed queue bound. Route lines connect steps rather than claiming to draw their full physical trajectories; evaluated candidates are not guaranteed routes, and a simulated hit is not a fired projectile. Private future enemy AI decisions remain unknown.

A third tab, Execution, shows what happened to the choice rather than how it was scored, one short line each from `DescribeExecutionEvidence`:

- **What the course weighed.** Per activity, from `ReadCourseWorthPerActivity`: the nominal worth of the best order its domains lead, what survives that order's costs, and the census admission in the three values every bounded search in this tree answers in. It read the family chooser's nominees until `19022bd` and printed "no comparison has run yet" for a whole session.
- **Admitted region.** The success region the held destination was admitted against, with its destination revision.
- **Control.** The control requested beside the control applied, marked where the owners differ, with hand, phase and attempt.
- **Attempt.** The open attempt beside the latest conclusion's status, attribution, cause, effects and claimed yield.

The "Where the purpose succeeds" world layer, bit 9 of the saved layers, draws the retained region's boxes: the follow comfort box around the admission references, or a tool hover's reach box with its tile and hover marked. Both read retained state only.

**A drawing placed by arithmetic rather than by a world coordinate is placed wrong until it has been driven over every screen the game supports.** The cost strip's first version sat under the panel where it fitted and at the screen's bottom-left otherwise, which reads as obviously correct and is not: at 800x600 with interface scale two the panel is 276 logical pixels tall on a 300-pixel screen, so the fallback fired and put the strip entirely inside the panel, which is drawn afterwards — an invisible strip on the smallest resolution the game offers. `StripBounds` is therefore pure arithmetic on the two screen extents, tries three candidate slots in order and takes the first inside the screen and clear of the open panel, and returns a zero width as its honest fourth answer when none clears, which the caller draws as nothing. Anything else placed this way is checked the same way: extract the function with `sed`, diff the copy against the source, and drive it over both interface scales from 800x600 to 3840x2160 with the panel open and closed.

The same discipline applies *inside* a drawing, not only to where the drawing sits, and the strip's second defect was there: its numeric reading was drawn in the top-right corner of a plot that occupies the whole strip, so the newest columns — the right-hand ones, and the only reason the strip is on screen — were painted underneath it. The plot now ends where the reading's gutter begins, the gutter is the measured text plus a gap and is clamped to half the strip so a narrow screen keeps a plot rather than losing it, and the harness drives that arithmetic over a range of text widths because the font cannot be measured with no graphics device. The general shape: whenever one part of a drawing is positioned from another part's size, the sizes are computed before either is drawn and the harness is given both.

The offscreen renderer checks three things. The source of both drawing files names no positioner, planner, aimer or reach call. Box edges agree with the region's own test and the reach arithmetic. The Execution page and a drawn reach box land where their geometry says.

**Every one of the fourteen layers defaults on, including the master switch**, so a fresh character sees all of them and turning one off is the standing choice the save carries; only the chooser panel itself starts closed, deliberately, because it covers the drawings it describes. Sample capture follows the drawings rather than the menu: gating it on the panel being open stopped a layer recording the moment the panel closed, so the evidence for every tick in between was never taken and reopening showed an empty layer. `MagicPixel` is an atlas texture: line drawing must select one source pixel before scaling to length and thickness, or its atlas dimensions multiply every line into a screen-sized shape. The native renderer's pixel regression checks this primitive directly. Retained aiming samples use the same engine tick as their expiry comparison.

Continuous rows declare their text columns and include navigator status, position reason, stalled state, the liquid the body touches, the wall normal, the desired velocity beside the applied control, attack value, projected kills/harm, cooldown and hunt progress. Decision events preserve the actual chooser multipliers. Shot events link their sequence estimate to projectile generation and subsequent contacts. Terrain snapshots retain active/actuated/solid flags, slopes, half blocks, material and wall IDs, tile frames and exact liquid amounts/types; glyphs remain a readable view rather than the only replay state.

The HUD and inspector read the coordinator's movement-progress state independently of whether a torch fills the hand. A continuously unsatisfied travel request with little net displacement and no retained-route advancement is labelled "not making progress"; it is not automatically diagnosed as a missing route. Behaviour churn cannot reset that observation, and movement away from the player can still advance a valid detour.

Follow-gap and one-way-held-out captures read the actual reunion/protection request kind. Keeping company can also hold and hover beside the player, so its activity label alone cannot establish an outstanding reunion request. Recording retains the activity and requested method separately.

The continuous collection_method field distinguishes a known-drop offer from potential pot contents under the same collecting activity. It is retained activity evidence, read with choice freshness and suspension state; it does not report that a pot was broken or its contents picked up.
