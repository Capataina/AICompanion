# Position selection — turn an intent into a useful place

`ChooseUsefulPosition.cs` turns a behaviour’s `PositionRequest` into a standable feet position. It owns candidate scoring; shared movement owns whether a route can be planned and the companion motor owns applying the resulting controls.

```
Position/
├─ CLAUDE.md                 this guide
├─ PositionRequest.cs        request kinds and their anchor, target, jump intent, meeting-place flag and a tool stand's work tile
├─ DeclareSuccessRegion.cs   the region a held destination was admitted against, as a pure containment test
├─ DescribePositionOffer.cs  bounded destination evidence for activity admission
├─ ChooseMeetingPlace.cs     reunion's place on the player's apparent journey, priced by the companion's own routes
├─ FollowPlayerObjective.cs  generic follow destination region, meeting-place lead and arrival proof
└─ ChooseUsefulPosition.cs   candidate flood, scoring, incumbent hold and temporary bans
```

The resolver samples standable tiles around the request anchor, prefers the shared movement region when one reaches the request, scores cheap factors first, and asks projectile aiming only about its strongest firing candidates.

Attack activities expose their captured position request before activation. PrepareOffer refines a nominated method through this same resolver. A usable destination retains the resolved state for ordinary execution; a rejected method restores the previous activity's held destination and its explanation. Shared reach-search work survives rejection, so yielding an unavailable attack cannot prevent the query from making progress. Candidate queries do not advance the temporary-ban clock as extra executed ticks. The returned evidence describes the source tick, destination, reason and bounded tested alternatives, not native attack damage or guaranteed arrival-time access.

Hunting and guarding require an active target and a weapon profile. Their chosen destination must have a solved trajectory; a refused solve remains recorded with zero value and cannot win as a low-scoring fallback. When the bounded shortlist establishes no usable destination, the request remains unresolved. That result does not prove that no shot exists elsewhere. A later opening can restore a candidate. Losing the target or weapon invalidates the held destination immediately; a changed weapon profile forces reconsideration rather than borrowing the old profile's proof. A terrain revision discards held position evidence and refreshes the shared reachable region before reuse. Ordinary target motion still follows the rescore cadence, so a solved candidate is evidence at evaluation time, not a guarantee of a shot after travel.

Courtesy is a score factor, not a movement writer. `PlayerSense.Interference` is the player's footprint (a solid block or wall aimed at the companion's body, or the stretch of a one-body-tall passage the player is walking down), and a follow spot whose body would overlap it keeps `CourtesyOccupancyShare` of its score. A factor rather than a veto: among useful spots one out of the player's way wins, and the only usable spot stays usable. A priced meeting place that overlaps the footprint is not taken as the destination, so ordinary scoring chooses instead, because a meeting place on a travelling player's journey inside a passage is exactly where the player is walking. A new footprint forces one rescore rather than waiting out the cadence. Guard and line-of-fire spots do not read it: protection is not priced against courtesy. What makes a resting companion ask this resolver at all is `KeepCompany`'s yield, described in `../../Activities/NearbyAssistance/CLAUDE.md`.

PredictedExposureAt supplies the geometric enemy-exposure query used both by destination scoring and shared combat-spacing search. It measures proximity and forecast overlap, not calibrated health loss. SharedSafety searches actual body states independently of attack destinations; no retreat request competes inside this resolver. The movement controller remains responsible for actual travel and collision validation.

**The cheap pass decides who pays for a solve, so whatever the cheap pass cannot see, the chosen spot cannot have.** Only a handful of candidates are ever solved — a fixed count and a millisecond budget, whichever binds first — so a cheap ranking carrying no line-of-fire information selects that handful on band, danger and openness alone, and the winner is then whichever blind spot happened to rank well. The cheap pass therefore carries a straight-ray sight test to the target. It ranks and never vetoes, because a straight ray is a lower bound on an arcing projectile: a candidate the ray fails keeps a reduced rank, can still reach the shortlist, and is refused only by a real solve.

The thing being shot at is excluded from the approach-clearance factor. It is already priced by the destination's danger and by the standoff factor that owns how close to it the companion should stand; counting it a third time as an obstacle on the way made every spot on its far side lose most of its score, which during a hunt is most of the useful ground. The score combines the request’s band, sight, danger, openness, travel bias and, where relevant, a feasible shot. A place outside an exhausted reachable region is absent rather than merely worse; a missing tile in a still-expanding region is unknown, so a partial route may begin toward a useful goal before the flood proves otherwise.

`FollowPlayerObjective.cs` is now a view over the player's intent region (`../Observation/ObservePlayerIntentRegion.cs`) rather than a box it computes itself, plus the two things a region cannot know: which anchor this request is aiming at, and whether the body has been grounded inside long enough for arrival to mean anything. Every consumer starts from the sense's own objective and refines it with `At(anchor)`, so there is one region and one settled streak in the brain instead of nine constructions that could disagree.

Acceptance is the region less the navigator's stopping radius, on both axes; a point on a nearby but different floor cannot satisfy following merely because its circular distance is small. The anchor is a preference rather than a second acceptance box — `Preference(feet)` falls off from it across the region's own width — because when it widened acceptance by a growth factor of its own, a far-off meeting place enlarged the arrival test it was supposed to be aiming inside, and the body could satisfy following by standing anywhere along a line to a place it had not reached. Completion additionally requires the settled streak, so an airborne tick cannot read as an arrival; the region's own paragraph in the Observation folder file carries why. The incumbent is admitted only when it still belongs to the region, so hysteresis retains a route's semantic destination rather than an arbitrary old floor.

## A held destination carries the region it was admitted against

The navigator's `Arrived` says the body stands near a point; it cannot say whether the point served the request. So every resolve that sets the destination also publishes `Region`, a `SuccessRegion` snapshot of what that destination was admitted to achieve, and `ChosenRevision`, which advances whenever `Chosen` takes a different value, including clearing it. The snapshot keeps the admission's own references, because judging an arrival against the world some ticks later would blame the destination for the player moving.

- A follow destination from `Best` records the intent region's centre and half-extents at admission, with the anchor kept beside them as evidence of what the request aimed at rather than as a second box. Every non-null answer from `Best` either passed acceptance against those references (the incumbent included) or is the partial-progress-candidate returned when no candidate was accepted, declaring no region because it is certain only that the reachable spot is not there yet. Acceptance reserves the navigator's arrival radius inside the comfort box, so an arrival outside both boxes names a destination that was never admitted (or a partial candidate with no box to satisfy).
- An exact request carrying `WorkTile` records the stand, the tile and the reach, and its region is the tool's native reach box: the arithmetic half of `FindToolAccess.InReach`. The line to an exposed face is not part of the region, because it reads live tiles and is not interval-shaped between the poses a stand was proven at. Only a standing proof sets `WorkTile`; a hop take-off is by construction a pose that does not reach, so declaring its tile would make every ceiling-ore walk an arrival outside its region.
- An attack destination declares a firing position with no box, since its arc belongs to a moving target. A priced meeting place, a roam and an exact request without a work tile declare no geometry.

The refinement query restores the revision and region with the rest of the held state, after restoring `Chosen`, so a rejected query leaves the held destination's identity exactly as it found it. The recorder writes the snapshot every row, and SessionReport judges claimed arrivals against it.

## Reunion meets the player on their journey, priced by the companion's own routes

`ChooseMeetingPlace` answers where reunion aims while the player is travelling. A point extrapolated from the player's velocity was the earlier answer, and it failed exactly where routes differ: the region around a point a few tiles ahead of the player can be nearer through a ledge behind than through a gap ahead, so the companion turned back and chased a route it could have met. Candidates are the standable tiles nearest the player's line of travel at a ladder of horizons, snapped within a window smaller than a floor plus a body so they stay on the player's apparent floor, plus the place already chosen while it still lies ahead on that journey. Each is priced from a returnable flood rooted at the companion's feet. An unfinished flood keeps growing while the body stays inside the region it has already proven it can return from, and is replaced only when the terrain changes or the body leaves that region; a finished flood is re-rooted once its root is older than the re-root cadence. Restarting an unfinished flood on the cadence was the first version, and it meant a flood needing more than one cadence never finished, so a cave-sized reunion never got a priced place. Arriving before the player costs the wait until the player gets there; arriving after the player has passed costs the arrival plus the chase at the difference in pace, capped where the player is at least as fast; far horizons pay for low travel confidence. Along one shared floor every point ahead ties, which is why the uncertainty charge and the held place decide there.

The place is decided only on a finished flood, because a best-first flood's travel times are upper bounds until it stops, and deciding on those bounds priced a reconnection ahead as dearer than the long way round. The decision is taken on the tick the flood finishes even when its root is by then older than the cadence, because demanding a fresh root as well would leave a moving companion in a large cave re-flooding for ever; the root's age is recorded beside the decision instead. A held place survives unless a new one is cheaper by the switch margin, and stays held while a re-rooted flood is still expanding. A player working or paused in place has no journey and is met where they stand. With nothing priced yet, reunion aims at the player's travel continued a bounded distance, the anchor it used before meeting places existed: aiming at the player's current feet instead trailed a travelling player for as long as a large flood took to finish. A priced place travels as the request's anchor with `MeetingPlace` set, and the resolver walks to that exact tile rather than scoring a region around it, because a different tile in the region can have a different best route — scoring the region is what sent the companion back through the ledge after the price had chosen the gap. Dropping the flag is a changed request, so the dropped tile does not survive the rescore cadence as the destination. Arrival is still judged against the player's current body, and distant recovery still keys on the reunion request rather than on the place.

**The region's edge is the anchor and the priced place is a refinement of it, which is the reverse of how they used to stand.** A place is decided only on a finished flood, and the flood does not finish while the player moves: on the 2026-09-14 capture the priced place decided on 3 rows out of 22,473, so the fallback was the anchor in all but a rounding error of ticks and it ran a second, shorter lead of its own beside the one `../Observation/ObservePlayerIntentRegion.cs` already carries — two places in the brain both claiming to be where the player is going, disagreeing by the difference between their lead times. The fallback is now the region's leading edge, and the still-player home is the region's centre rather than the player's feet, so the filter's drift is not undone by aiming through it. A priced place the region does not contain loses to that leading edge while the player travels, because the pricing minimises the cost of meeting and the cheapest meeting with a walking player is the one that happens where he already is: on open ground the ladder reliably names a tile at about his feet, which is behind a region a lead has carried forward. Measured on the authored straight walk with the priced place as the anchor, the body trailed by a steady 42 px and was ahead on 1.9% of moving rows; with the region overruling it, ahead on 75.4%. The route-awareness the flood buys is kept exactly where it points somewhere the companion is going, which is the parallel-routes case it was built for and where the priced tile does lie inside the region.

It does not know where the player is going, only the journey their recent displacement describes. It prices only routes the returnable flood can prove, so a one-way shortcut is never a meeting route. Its times are the planner's step durations, which record a plain walk from pose to pose at the walk speed; a sum still omits acceleration at a route's start and at turns, so a priced time is an estimate rather than an arrival promise. `Tools/EngineReplay/Movement/VerifyResponsiveFollowing.cs` holds the parallel-route pair (a gap ahead against a cliff), the activity pairs (travel, torch placement, a brief reversal, sustained backtracking), a flood that must keep its progress while every call arrives past the cadence with the body moved, and a dropped meeting place that must not remain the destination.

One-way drops are refused by default. The exception is for following the player down: it opens only when the raw region actually reaches the player, which separates a player below a drop from a player sealed behind a wall. A stuck route temporarily bans its chosen tile so the next answer is a different place rather than the same unwalkable claim.

**The floods themselves live in `../Observation/ObserveReach.cs` now, not here.** This resolver drives their cadence, because the lava and one-way rules are set per request and a flood run at the top of the tick would answer under the previous tick's rule, and it keeps its whole public surface — `ReachComplete`, `Reaches`, `IsReturnable`, `ReachCount`, `ReturnableCount`, `ChosenReturnable`, `PlayerOnlyOneWay`, `LastFloodMs` — as one-line delegations, because the brain tick, firing-position admission, keeping company, the telemetry and eleven fixtures all read them by those names. What changed is that everyone else can now ask the same region the same questions instead of running a route search per candidate.

**On a player-only-one-way tick this resolver scores against a different set from the one every other consumer reads, and the two completenesses are named apart for that reason.** When the player stands beyond a drop the two-way flood cannot hold, scoring opens the raw region so the companion can follow him down; `ReachComplete` here therefore reports `ScoredComplete`, the exhaustion of whatever set is being scored, because it is paired with a membership test on that same set. Everyone asking whether the body can go somewhere *and come home* reads `Reachable`, which answers from the two-way set and rests on `Complete`, the two-way flood's own exhaustion. Keeping one flag for both is not a tidiness question: it lets a tile the two-way flood has simply not reached yet be graded proven-absent on the strength of the raw flood having finished, and a proven absence is now remembered for the no-return wait, so the mistake would persist rather than correct itself on the next rescore.

A WithPlayer request that accepts no candidate returns the reachable standable tile that most reduces the follow objective's horizontal-plus-vertical gap, with `ChoiceReason` `partial-progress-candidate`, rather than nothing. Following is the one request that must always produce somewhere to go: its acceptance is a region around the player, so a companion outside that region with no candidate inside it used to answer nothing at all and leave the navigator with no proven destination. The gap is measured on the objective's own two axes rather than as straight-line distance, because that is what the objective is satisfied by — a tile on another floor can be nearer as the crow flies and further from being with the player. Other request kinds keep the null: there is no partial credit for a firing position that cannot fire.

`IsReturnable` exposes membership of the returnable region, the one flooded with no-way-back edges refused, and refreshes the flood on its own cadence first. Keeping company's local method reads it to choose stroll goals during its decide phase, before this tick's `Resolve`, often while its request is a hold that would not refresh the flood itself; it therefore reads a region at most a rescore old, the same staleness the positioner tolerates. It answers the returnable region even when the player stands only beyond a drop and scoring has opened the raw one, because a stroll never follows the player down. An unfinished flood answers false beyond its frontier, so a caller reading false as "not here" stays inside what has been proven.

## Traps

Destination acceptance must include the navigator's stopping slack. A candidate on the comfort boundary can be valid while the body stops just outside it, leaving an Arrived navigator and an unsatisfied follow objective forever. Follow candidates leave that slack on both axes and sample each tile so a narrow valid landing is not skipped by a coarse lattice. If no candidate exists, the unresolved follow intent reaches the shared state search rather than becoming a Hold request.

The returnable and unrestricted regions retain frontier work across rescores. A moved start reuses them only after generated connections establish travel in both directions to the previous root; a short drop cannot inherit the upper ledge's reachable region merely because it is close. World revision and lava-policy changes invalidate both. The incumbent's exact tile is reconsidered alongside newly sampled candidates, so shifting a sampling lattice does not silently remove a still-useful follow destination. Candidate counts and known travel ticks feed diagnostics and the chooser's excursion cost.

- Candidate danger and approach clearance test hostile geometry directly. An enemy unable to reach either actor's current position can still occupy the place the companion is considering entering.
- Do not resolve a route here. Position selection states where the companion would be useful; `CoordinateMovement` decides how its body gets there.
- The current chosen tile gets a small incumbent preference. Removing it turns equal candidates into repeated replans.
- A weapon trajectory solve is expensive; apply it only after the cheap scoring pass. The cheap pass must still rank on something correlated with a shot, or the budget is spent on whichever candidates won the unrelated factors.
- The reachable region is flooded incrementally across rescores, so a single `Resolve` call sees a region only a few tiles wide. A test or diagnostic that resolves once is measuring flood budget, not scoring.
- Terraria's `Collision.CanHitLine` tests three tile rows at each step rather than one, so a gap exactly as wide as the opening reads as blocked. Geometry built to have a sight line needs slack on both sides of it.
- CandidateEvidence retains the strongest evaluated alternatives, their final scores and whether each solved a shot, stamped with the rescore tick. It exposes the decision to the recorder without making diagnostics rerun spatial queries.
- A retained region's membership can transfer within a proven two-way component; its root-relative travel times cannot. EstimatedTravelTicks takes the live origin and returns unknown after that origin moves, leaving the chooser's current geometric and active-route estimates in charge.
