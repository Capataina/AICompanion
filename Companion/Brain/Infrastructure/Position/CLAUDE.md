# Position selection — turn an intent into a useful place

`ChooseUsefulPosition.cs` turns a behaviour's `PositionRequest` into a hover point the orb can be at. It owns candidate scoring; shared movement owns whether a route can be planned and the companion motor owns applying the resulting controls.

```
Position/
├─ CLAUDE.md                 this guide
├─ PositionRequest.cs        request kinds and their anchor, target, meeting-place flag and a tool hover's work tile
├─ DeclareSuccessRegion.cs   the region a held destination was admitted against, as a pure containment test
├─ DescribePositionOffer.cs  bounded destination evidence for activity admission
├─ ChooseMeetingPlace.cs     reunion's place on the player's apparent journey, priced by the companion's own routes
├─ FollowPlayerObjective.cs  a view over the player's intent region, plus this request's anchor, whether the body was inside last tick and whether it can reach him inside the region
└─ ChooseUsefulPosition.cs   candidate sampling, scoring, the retention rule, the refusal memory and temporary bans
```

## Candidates are free corners near the anchor, under a ceiling

The resolver's candidates are the usable corners of the free-space graph within a radius of the corner nearest the request anchor — a corner is usable when the contact circle fits there, which is the same test the search uses for its nodes, so every candidate is a place the navigator can plan to by construction. A candidate above a ceiling a fixed number of tiles over the player's feet is refused before scoring: the body can fly anywhere free, and without that rule a follow spot on open ground drifts to the top of the sky because nothing there is in anyone's way. The ceiling is a positioning rule and never a wall the body meets; the flood and the contact know nothing about it. Reach is the sense's own answer about the corner, and a candidate the flood has finished without is absent while one it has not reached is unknown. The cheap factors are scored first — the request's band around the anchor, danger, openness, which for an orb is the clearance field's value at the candidate rising to full at a couple of tiles, a hover-height factor that prefers a spot at or above the player's head to one at his feet, courtesy, travel bias — and projectile aiming is asked only about the strongest firing candidates. An exact request is answered by the anchor itself when the circle fits there and the flood has not proven it unreachable — an unfinished flood accepts it, a finished flood without its corner refuses it — and otherwise by the nearest hoverable point, preferring one the flood holds.

Attack activities expose their captured position request before activation. PrepareOffer refines a nominated method through this same resolver. A usable destination retains the resolved state for ordinary execution; a rejected method restores the previous activity's held destination and its explanation. Shared reach-search work survives rejection, so yielding an unavailable attack cannot prevent the query from making progress. Candidate queries do not advance the temporary-ban clock as extra executed ticks. The returned evidence describes the source tick, destination, reason and bounded tested alternatives, not native attack damage or guaranteed arrival-time access.

Hunting and guarding require an active target and a weapon profile. Their chosen destination must have a solved trajectory; a refused solve remains recorded with zero value and cannot win as a low-scoring fallback. A later opening can restore a candidate.

**A bounded search that ran out is not a proven absence, and the two answers are named apart so nothing downstream can confuse them.** The shortlist is cut by a solve count or a millisecond budget, whichever binds first, and a pass that stopped early has established nothing about the candidates it never reached. Such a pass reports an unfinished search and the chooser classifies the offer unresolved, which leaves the activity able to win a later comparison; only a pass that solved or already remembered a refusal for every candidate reports the proven absence the chooser may brand on. The names live in one place beside the offer type, because the chooser has to ask the question without knowing how the search works. Reading a cut as a fact about the world is what let a hunt be vetoed by its own budget: on the 2026-09-14 capture hunt read known-unusable on 303 of 624 decisions, and half of its incumbent losses carried that reason, so keeping company took the body by default.

**An unfinished answer must be able to finish, so a refusal is remembered and a sweep resumes.** A shortlist sorted the same way every rescore cuts in the same place every rescore, so without memory an unfinished answer in a scene nobody is changing would be permanent and the stand past the cut would never be asked about. A stand a real solve refused is therefore remembered against the target's generation, the terrain revision and where the body was standing, does not consume the next pass's budget, and counts toward exhaustion.

**Which refusals survive the body moving is decided by what each one was about, and reusing a refusal is what lets a pass walk deeper than its own budget.** A stand with no arc is a fact about the line between the stand and the target, and the body's position is not in that question, so it holds from anywhere. A stand refused because the shot would close before the body could walk there is a fact about the trip, so it holds only from where that trip started and is re-asked once the body has moved, which is when the answer can differ. Scoping both kinds to the body was what stopped a moving companion's search from ever finishing, and not by the route it looks like: a remembered refusal is skipped *without spending a solve*, so when the body moved and nothing was skipped, every solve in the budget went to the top of the shortlist and the pass never reached the stands below it, however many passes ran. The count was never the binding constraint — the skipping was.

**Whether a verdict may be reused and whether the sweep has finished are two questions, and only the first is about the body.** Exhaustion asks whether the stands around this target have been asked, so it is keyed on the stand and the target and never on where the body was standing at the time. What that costs is worth naming: a stand refused for a trip from somewhere else counts as asked, so a sweep can complete on a refusal a shorter walk might now beat. It is not lost, because the stand is re-solved as soon as the budget reaches it from the new position; what the exhaustion flag claims is that every stand has an answer under this terrain and this target's position, and that is true. The contract for it walks the body across the memory's own stride while a sealed enemy's sweep runs, and the stationary contract beside it stays green under the defect — which is why the walking one had to exist.

**The memory evicts one target at a time rather than flushing.** A cap is needed because the memory is keyed per target and a long fight produces targets; what it must not be is a reset of the one structure that makes a bounded search terminate. Clearing everything at the cap restarted every sweep in progress at once, at a moment decided by an unrelated target's entries arriving — so a blood moon with several unshootable enemies in it sent every target back to its first stand. The target that has been in the memory longest loses its entries, and never the one being refused right now, since that would throw away the entry about to be written along with the current sweep's progress. The consequence is stated rather than hidden: where the only target in the memory is the one being refused, nothing is evicted and its entries pass the cap, because the alternative is restarting the sweep that is running. The growth is bounded by the distinct stands around one target under one terrain revision, and a revision change throws the whole memory away, so what a long fight against a mobile boss accumulates is dead marks for positions it has left rather than unbounded entries. No fixture reaches the eviction path at all — every scene here holds one target and a few dozen stands against a cap of several hundred — so the eviction rule is argued rather than measured.

**Every refusal is kept, and the first version of this memory that kept only some of them could not finish.** A stand refused because the shot would close before the body could walk there is a fact about that trip rather than about the place, so keeping it looks wrong and excluding it was tried: what that produced is a block of trip-refused stands at the top of the ranking re-solved on every pass for ever, nothing beneath them ever reached, and the search permanently undecided — breaking the exact property the memory exists to provide. The property to hold is therefore that **an unfinished search must be able to finish, which needs every answered candidate to stay answered**, and the trip's dependence on the body is expressed by scoping the memory to where the body stands rather than by forgetting the refusal. "Where the body stands" is a coarse bucket and not its exact tile, for the reason the firing query keys its own cache in the same strides: the exact tile changes on almost every tick a walking body is asked, and a hunt is challenged while another activity moves the body, so an exact key would discard the last few passes' work on each of them and the search would never exhaust while the body moved.

The memory is thrown away whenever the terrain revision changes, rather than compared against at each lookup, because a rebuilt world returns the revision to a value the memory has already seen and a stale refusal whose stamp happens to match writes off a stand nothing ever solved against. Losing the target or weapon invalidates the held destination immediately; a change in the weapons in hand forces reconsideration rather than borrowing the old weapons' proof, while a different weapon choice among the same weapons does not, since every stand is priced by all of them. A terrain revision discards held position evidence and refreshes the shared reachable region before reuse.

**A stand's shot is solved against where the target will be when the body gets there, not where it is while the choosing happens.** A destination is chosen now and stood on a walk later, so a shot proved against the target's current centre is a proof about a creature that will have moved; the companion was sent to places chosen for slimes that had left. The solve therefore asks at the candidate's own estimated trip, and then across a window past it whose length is the trip's own, bounded above so a walk across the world is not asked for an arc that holds indefinitely. The sampling is every interval up to that window and then the window's own end whenever the interval does not land on it, which is the clause the rule was missing: a loop beginning at one whole interval samples a trip shorter than an interval **nowhere**, so a short walk to a stand was accepted on the arrival solve alone — and a short walk is still a walk the shot has to survive. The same clause covers every window the interval does not divide, where sampling only the multiples leaves the last stretch unasked. The rule also used to read the trip backwards, taking it as a *requirement* the arc had to hold for after arrival rather than as the window to measure, so the trip only chose between one sample and two while a long walk was held to a middling walk's window. The refusal carries the window it measured, so the reason can be read back against the trip it was about. The offset is a tick count rather than a displacement because the forecast is terrain-constrained, and a straight-line shift equals it only while the motion is straight. Where the forecast carries too little measured confidence to be evidence — no error samples yet, or a trip long enough that the measured continuation has decayed past the floor — the current position is asked instead and the recorded reason says which was used, because refusing a stand on an unmeasured guess is worse than the staleness it replaces. The consequence worth knowing: on a long walk the forecast is not confident, so a far stand is still judged on where the target is now, and this rule bites on short and middling trips.

Courtesy on the follow path is a refusal, and it lives where the body moves. `PlayerSense.Interference` is the player's footprint (a solid block or wall aimed at the companion's body, or the stretch of a one-body-tall passage the player is walking down). Outside the region, the nearest place back inside passes over any place whose body would overlap it; inside the region, the accompanying motion refuses those tiles as places to move to (`../Movement/Steering/CLAUDE.md`). A priced meeting place that overlaps the footprint is not taken as the destination, because a meeting place on a travelling player's journey inside a passage is exactly where the player is walking. A new footprint forces one rescore rather than waiting out the cadence, and that force is spent by whoever resolves first. **The refinement query restores it along with everything else it touched, and it is the one piece of that state whose consumption is not idempotent.** Resolve spends the force by stamping the footprint as seen, so a rejected query that put back the rescore counter but not the stamp ate the force on behalf of whoever asked next. The paired contract that held this for keeping company was deleted on 15 September 2026, because its scene stood the body on a follow destination it held, and a body on a destination is inside the region, where nothing is held; the restore stays, since a rejected query must not spend a force it did not ask for. Guard and line-of-fire spots do not read the footprint: protection is not priced against courtesy.

PredictedExposureAt supplies the geometric enemy-exposure query destination scoring prices. It measures proximity and forecast overlap, not calibrated health loss. Scoring is now the only place exposure acts: the combat-spacing search that also read it, and took the body to lower it, was deleted on 15 September 2026, so a spot beside an enemy is worth less and nothing takes the body away from it. No retreat request competes inside this resolver. The movement controller remains responsible for actual travel and collision validation.

**The cheap pass decides who pays for a solve, so whatever the cheap pass cannot see, the chosen spot cannot have.** Only a handful of candidates are ever solved — a fixed count and a millisecond budget, whichever binds first — so a cheap ranking carrying no line-of-fire information selects that handful on band, danger and openness alone, and the winner is then whichever blind spot happened to rank well. The cheap pass therefore carries a straight-ray sight test to the target. It ranks and never vetoes, because a straight ray is a lower bound on an arcing projectile: a candidate the ray fails keeps a reduced rank, can still reach the shortlist, and is refused only by a real solve.

The thing being shot at is excluded from the approach-clearance factor. It is already priced by the destination's danger and by the standoff factor that owns how close to it the companion should stand; counting it a third time as an obstacle on the way made every spot on its far side lose most of its score, which during a hunt is most of the useful ground. The score combines the request's band, sight, danger, openness, hover height, travel bias and, where relevant, a feasible shot. A place outside an exhausted reachable region is absent rather than merely worse; a missing corner in a still-expanding region is unknown, and an unknown is not offered.

**A firing stand is priced by the best attack any weapon in hand could make from it, not by the weapon the arsenal chose last.** A stand is solved at arrival against every handed weapon's flight model in turn, and the first that solves gives the stand its shot; with none, the refusal kept is a trip refusal if any weapon gave one, because a stand refused only for the length of the walk may open from closer. Each weapon asked counts as a solve against the shortlist's budget, so two weapons do not silently double a pass's cost. That accounting is paid in depth: on 15 September 2026 the sealed-enemy offer fixture took 114 passes to prove the absence against 100 at `530b16d`, where a stand cost one solve whatever was in hand. The arsenal solves behind `FiringStandShare` are not counted against the solve count, so in play only the millisecond budget bounds them, and that cost has not been measured under production allowances. A solved stand's `fire` factor is then `FiringStandShare`: the arsenal's value of the best attack any weapon makes from the stand against the target as it will be on arrival, as a share of `IdealShotValue` — the best those weapons could do with no geometry in the way — lifted onto a floor so a stand with a poor shot is still a stand. Both values come from the arsenal's own evaluator on learned forecasts (`../WeaponKnowledge/CLAUDE.md`), so everything a shot is worth is in the stand's price without a factor of its own: the push charge when the shot knocks the target into the player or back at the stand, a pierce through a crowd, a debuff the other weapon set up, and what the learner has seen each weapon achieve. The value is priced at the arrival offset like the solve, and against the other hostiles where they are now. A request resolved for a bare NPC that is not the companion has no arsenal, solves with the profile it was handed and keeps its whole share.

This replaced a side preference, and the property that killed it is worth keeping: **a stand priced by one weapon's push ignores the other weapon.** The preference read the settled push of whichever weapon the arsenal had chosen last and kept less of a stand's score where that push pointed at the player or the stand. When the last choice was the weaker weapon — made for a different target, or from where the body happened to hover — a stand from which the stronger weapon's push went away from the player tied with, or lost to, a stand from which only the weaker weapon was safe; at the unfixed code the fixture's two stands scored identically. What forces a rescore changed with it: a change in the weapons in hand does, read as the gear's signature, and a different weapon choice does not, because nothing about a stand's price depends on it.

`FollowPlayerObjective.cs` is now a view over the player's intent region (`../Observation/ObservePlayerIntentRegion.cs`) rather than a box it computes itself, plus the three things a region cannot know: which anchor this request is aiming at, whether the body was inside the region on the tick before, and whether the body has a way to the player that stays inside the region. Every consumer starts from the sense's own objective and refines it with `At(anchor)`, so there is one region and one inside latch in the brain instead of nine constructions that could disagree. The last two are facts about the body the sense saw, not about a point, and every caller of `IsSatisfied` passes the live body. Being with the player is having that way to him and being inside the region, or within the settle radius beyond its edge for a body that was inside on the tick before; the way back in for a body that is not is the nearest usable inside corner the sense has not proven cut off from the player, so a body cut off by a wall is sent round to his side rather than to the corner it already hovers at; there is no rest to wait out and no line of sight asked, and the Observation folder file carries why.

Acceptance is the region less the navigator's settle radius, on both axes — the arrival radius plus the hover's drift, because an arrived orb drifts around its spot rather than stopping on it, and a spot accepted against the arrival radius alone would be hovered around partly outside the region that admitted it; a point on a nearby but different floor cannot satisfy following merely because its circular distance is small. The anchor is evidence of what the request aimed at and never a second acceptance box, because when it widened acceptance by a growth factor of its own, a far-off meeting place enlarged the arrival test it was supposed to be aiming inside, and the body could satisfy following by standing anywhere along a line to a place it had not reached; a scoring preference toward the anchor was written and deleted unconsumed, since the anchor alone already put the companion ahead on two thirds of a straight walk's moving rows without it. That measurement was taken where the follow anchor fell back to the region's leading edge, which went on 15 September 2026, so the figure describes a system that no longer exists. The incumbent is admitted only when it still belongs to the region, so hysteresis retains a route's semantic destination rather than an arbitrary old floor.

## A chosen destination is kept until it stops belonging to its own region

Retention is a rule rather than a preference. Between rescores the destination simply holds, as it always did. At a rescore the destination already held is tested against the region it was admitted to serve, and if it still belongs there it is kept and nothing else is searched at all: no lattice is resampled, no candidate is scored, and the revision does not advance, so one revision names one journey. A replacement happens only where that test fails, which is the only moment a different place is actually needed.

What "still belongs" means is each kind's own. A follow destination is one the objective accepts now, against the player's current feet and the current anchor. A firing stand has no box by design, because its arc belongs to a moving target, so its membership is the arc: the target must not have drifted past a hold slack, and the arc is then re-proved by a single solve taken before any shortlist budget is spent — which is what makes a stand that still works immune to a solve count or a millisecond limit running out. Every kind additionally needs its corner usable, un-banned and not proven outside the reachable region, because a place the body cannot get to is not a place however well it once served the purpose.

**Both follow kinds also release a destination that stands in the player's own way, and that clause is what keeps courtesy alive under retention.** A new interference footprint forces a rescore rather than waiting out the cadence, and the follow objective is about being near the player and says nothing about being in the way — so a retention that asked only the objective turned that forced rescore into a rescore that retains, which is no rescore at all, and the companion held the tile the player was walking down. Releasing is not a veto: courtesy is priced as a share in the scored pass that follows, so the same spot is chosen again where it is the only usable one.

This replaces a multiplier. The destination used to be resampled from a lattice on every rescore with the incumbent given a small bonus and a pixel slack to defend itself with, and two free corners a few pixels apart score within noise of each other, so the named spot wandered continuously under requests that had not changed — the success-region revision moved 1,017 times in 22,473 rows of the 2026-09-14 capture, once every twenty-two ticks, while journeys asked to be with the player were reached 13 times against 209 abandoned. A bonus is the wrong instrument for this because it is a tiebreak in a competition that should not have been held. The incumbent no longer enters the scored pass at all, and the bonus and slack constants are dead.

One consequence to expect rather than debug: a destination inside a pocket the reach flood has not yet proven unreachable is legitimately retained while the flood grows, because an unknown is not an absence. It is released on the rescore after the flood settles.

## A held destination carries the region it was admitted against

The navigator's `Arrived` says the body stands near a point; it cannot say whether the point served the request. So every resolve that sets the destination also publishes `Region`, a `SuccessRegion` snapshot of what that destination was admitted to achieve, and `ChosenRevision`, which advances whenever `Chosen` takes a different value, including clearing it. The snapshot keeps the admission's own references, because judging an arrival against the world some ticks later would blame the destination for the player moving.

- A follow destination from `Best` records the intent region's centre and half-extents at admission, with the anchor kept beside them as evidence of what the request aimed at rather than as a second box. **There is one box, because there is one region**: the recorded kind is `follow-comfort` and the containment test reads the snapshotted centre alone, so an arrival beside a far-off meeting place is outside the region it was admitted to, which it always was. Acceptance reserves the navigator's arrival radius inside that box, so an arrival outside it names a destination that was never admitted. Every non-null answer from `Best` passed acceptance against those references; when nothing was accepted the answer is null and the follow intent goes to movement as a travel intention with no chosen place.
- An exact request carrying `WorkTile` records the hover, the tile and the reach, and its region is the tool's native reach box: the arithmetic half of `FindToolAccess.InReach`. The line to an exposed face is not part of the region, because it reads live tiles and is not interval-shaped between the points a hover was proven at.
- An attack destination declares a firing position with no box, since its arc belongs to a moving target. A priced meeting place, a roam and an exact request without a work tile declare no geometry.

The refinement query restores the revision and region with the rest of the held state, after restoring `Chosen`, so a rejected query leaves the held destination's identity exactly as it found it. The recorder writes the snapshot every row, and SessionReport judges claimed arrivals against it.

## Reunion meets the player on their journey, priced by the companion's own routes

`ChooseMeetingPlace` answers where reunion aims while the player is travelling. A point extrapolated from the player's velocity was the earlier answer, and it failed exactly where routes differ: the region around a point a few tiles ahead of the player can be nearer through a ledge behind than through a gap ahead, so the companion turned back and chased a route it could have met. Candidates are the hoverable cells nearest the player's line of travel at a ladder of horizons, snapped within a window smaller than a floor plus a body so they stay on the player's apparent floor, plus the place already chosen while it still lies ahead on that journey. Each is priced from a flood rooted at the companion's centre. An unfinished flood keeps growing while the body stays inside the region it has already proven it can return from, and is replaced only when the terrain changes or the body leaves that region; a finished flood is re-rooted once its root is older than the re-root cadence. Restarting an unfinished flood on the cadence was the first version, and it meant a flood needing more than one cadence never finished, so a cave-sized reunion never got a priced place. Arriving before the player costs the wait until the player gets there; arriving after the player has passed costs the arrival plus the chase at the difference in pace, capped where the player is at least as fast; far horizons pay for low travel confidence. Along one shared floor every point ahead ties, which is why the uncertainty charge and the held place decide there.

The place is decided only on a finished flood, because a best-first flood's travel times are upper bounds until it stops, and deciding on those bounds priced a reconnection ahead as dearer than the long way round. The decision is taken on the tick the flood finishes even when its root is by then older than the cadence, because demanding a fresh root as well would leave a moving companion in a large cave re-flooding for ever; the root's age is recorded beside the decision instead. A held place survives unless a new one is cheaper by the switch margin, and stays held while a re-rooted flood is still expanding. A player working or paused in place has no journey and is met where they stand. With nothing priced yet, reunion aims at the nearest place inside the player's region to the body, because rejoining means being inside the region again and inside it the companion moves about with the region rather than aiming anywhere in it. A priced place travels as the request's anchor with `MeetingPlace` set, and the resolver walks to that exact tile rather than scoring a region around it, because a different tile in the region can have a different best route — scoring the region is what sent the companion back through the ledge after the price had chosen the gap. Dropping the flag is a changed request, so the dropped tile does not survive the rescore cadence as the destination. Arrival is still judged against the player's current body, and distant recovery still keys on the reunion request rather than on the place.

**The region is the anchor and the priced place is a refinement of it, and a place inside the region is only the way back in.** A place is decided only on a finished flood, and the flood does not finish while the player moves: on the 2026-09-14 capture the priced place decided on 3 rows out of 22,473, so the fallback was the anchor in all but a rounding error of ticks and it ran a second, shorter lead of its own beside the one `../Observation/ObservePlayerIntentRegion.cs` already carries — two places in the brain both claiming to be where the player is going, disagreeing by their lead times. The fallback became the region's leading edge, and on 15 September 2026 the nearest place inside the region to the body; the still-player home is the region's centre rather than the player's feet, so the filter's drift is not undone by aiming through it. A priced place the region does not contain loses to that nearest place while the player travels. **Being ahead of a travelling player stopped being the meeting place's job the same day.** It was, measured on the authored straight walk: with the priced place as the anchor the body trailed by a steady 42 px and was ahead on 1.9% of moving rows, and with the leading edge overruling it, ahead on 75.4%. But that overruling needed the priced tile at the player's feet to lie outside a region led off him, and a region that always holds the player contains his feet, so the cheapest meeting — where he already is — is inside it and stands. Once the body is inside, it moves about the region, and `Movement/Steering/CLAUDE.md`'s accompanying motion keeps a travelling player's companion level or ahead by closing the rear of the box while it leads. The route-awareness the flood buys is kept exactly where it points somewhere the companion is going, which is the parallel-routes case it was built for.

It does not know where the player is going, only the journey their recent displacement describes. It prices only routes the flood can prove. Its times are the flood's costs at the orb's pace, which omit acceleration at a route's start and at turns, so a priced time is an estimate rather than an arrival promise. `Tools/EngineReplay/Movement/VerifyResponsiveFollowing.cs` holds the parallel-route pair (a gap ahead against a cliff), the activity pairs (travel, torch placement, a brief reversal, sustained backtracking), a flood that must keep its progress while every call arrives past the cadence with the body moved, and a dropped meeting place that must not remain the destination.

A stuck route temporarily bans its chosen spot so the next answer is a different place rather than the same unflyable claim; the ban is keyed by the spot's tile.

**The flood itself lives in `../Observation/ObserveReach.cs` now, not here.** This resolver drives its cadence, because the flood's unit of time is the rescore and a flood driven from the top of every tick would never finish under a hold, and it keeps its whole public surface — `ReachComplete`, `Reaches`, `IsReturnable`, `ReachCount`, `ReturnableCount`, `ChosenReturnable`, `PlayerOnlyOneWay`, `EstimatedTravelTicks`, `LastFloodMs` — as one-line delegations, because the brain tick, firing-position admission, keeping company, the telemetry and the fixtures all read them by those names. The walker's two floods are one for the orb, so the returnable and any-way counts are the same number and `PlayerOnlyOneWay` is always false; the names stay so that nobody was renamed for no behaviour, and the walker's reason for keeping two completenesses apart is recorded in the Observation guide for whoever adds a second flood.

**A WithPlayer request that accepts no candidate answers nothing, and nothing is what movement wants.** The walker returned a partial-progress tile — the reachable tile that most reduced the objective's gap — because a follow request that admitted no candidate still had to produce somewhere to go and the walker's state search could not aim at the player directly. It was a destination that could be arrived at without achieving anything, it had its own success-region kind and its own defects (it once named the tile the body was already standing on and froze a 328-row stretch of the 2026-09-14 capture), and it is deleted rather than kept: the null answer hands the follow intent to `CoordinateMovement.SeekDestination`, which aims the navigator at the anchor itself until the objective's own arrival test says the body is there. Other request kinds keep the null too: there is no partial credit for a firing position that cannot fire.

`IsReturnable` exposes membership of the region and refreshes the flood on its own cadence first. Keeping company's stroll used to read it during its decide phase, and that query was, by accident, the only thing that grew the flood while company held; the stroll is gone and the `Hold` branch of `Resolve` refreshes the flood itself, so no consumer depends on asking a question to keep the region alive. An unfinished flood answers false beyond its frontier, so a caller reading false as "not here" stays inside what has been proven.

## Planned work — `ChooseUsefulPosition.cs` has outgrown one reading

Measured at `31e6775`, before the orb rewrite: 893 lines against a folder median of 86, which is 10.4x and the widest outlier in the project. The rewrite deleted the partial-progress region and shortened the candidate loop, so the line numbers below no longer hold, and the boundary argument does. It is one `Positioner` class holding six concerns that barely touch each other, and the split is recorded rather than performed **because it is code, and a droid documents behaviour rather than changing it**. Nothing here is urgent: the file is correct, and this is a readability plan for whoever next opens it with a reason to.

The boundary a split should follow is the last row and then the fifth, in that order, because those are the two that carry no instance state: the scoring factors are already ten `private static` functions taking their whole world as arguments, and the firing arc owns its two dictionaries and nothing else touches them. `ScoreSpot` and its nine helpers move to a `ScoreStandingSpot.cs` as a static class; the arc work moves to a `ProveShotFromStand.cs` holding the refusal memory as an instance the resolver owns. The remaining regions are genuinely one object and should stay together — splitting `Resolve` from `Best` would put the region declaration and the search that feeds it in two files.

The evidence that the threshold is crossed is the outlier ratio above plus the fact that six of the seven regions are separately describable in one line, which is the tell that a reader seeking one of them reads past five. The reference surface a later session must sweep first: `Positioner` appears in 28 files with 138 member references, and `PredictedExposureAt` alone had 10 call sites at that measurement, one of them the combat-spacing search deleted since, so the public surface must not move even where the code behind it does — every extraction keeps its entry point on `Positioner` as a delegation, for exactly the reason the reach delegations above were kept.

## Traps

Destination acceptance must include the navigator's settle radius. A candidate on the comfort boundary can be valid while the body hovers partly outside it, leaving an Arrived navigator and an unsatisfied follow objective forever. Follow candidates leave that slack on both axes. If no candidate exists, the unresolved follow intent reaches movement as a travel intention aimed at the anchor rather than becoming a Hold request.

The region retains frontier work across rescores and is re-rooted when the body has moved away from its root. The region and its invalidation belong to `../Observation/ObserveReach.cs` now; what this resolver still owns is its cadence, and discarding its own held position evidence when the terrain revision moves. Nothing here reconsiders the incumbent tile among the sampled candidates: retention is settled before any sampling happens, so a shifted lattice cannot remove a still-useful follow destination because no lattice is built while one is held. Candidate counts and known travel ticks feed diagnostics and the chooser's excursion cost.

- Candidate danger and approach clearance test hostile geometry directly. An enemy unable to reach either actor's current position can still occupy the place the companion is considering entering.
- Do not resolve a route here. Position selection states where the companion would be useful; `CoordinateMovement` decides how its body gets there.
- Raising the solve count or the millisecond budget is an instance fix and the class survives at the new bound: whatever the numbers are, a shortlist can exceed them, and the question is only whether the cut is reported as a cut. The same holds for the chooser's commitment bonus, which cannot hold an offer whose own validity has died.
- A weapon trajectory solve is expensive; apply it only after the cheap scoring pass. The cheap pass must still rank on something correlated with a shot, or the budget is spent on whichever candidates won the unrelated factors.
- The reachable region is flooded incrementally across rescores, so a single `Resolve` call sees a region only a few tiles wide. A test or diagnostic that resolves once is measuring flood budget, not scoring.
- The hover ceiling is `Weights.HoverCeilingTiles` above the player's feet and it is applied to candidates only; a route the navigator plans to an accepted candidate may pass above it, and the flood ignores it entirely.
- Terraria's `Collision.CanHitLine` tests three tile rows at each step rather than one, so a gap exactly as wide as the opening reads as blocked. Geometry built to have a sight line needs slack on both sides of it.
- CandidateEvidence retains the strongest evaluated alternatives, their final scores and whether each solved a shot, stamped with the rescore tick. It exposes the decision to the recorder without making diagnostics rerun spatial queries.
- A retained region's membership survives the body moving inside it; its root-relative travel times do not. EstimatedTravelTicks takes the live origin and returns unknown after that origin moves, leaving the chooser's current geometric and active-route estimates in charge.
