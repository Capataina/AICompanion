# One allowance is borrowed by every decision consumer

These game-free owners separate computation limits from gameplay preference. A native frame owns one `DecisionWorkBudget`; nested discovery, repair, binding, combat and route work borrow it. Operation counts make forced cuts reproducible while a monotonic deadline bounds production slices. Mandatory engine observation and motor work remain separately measured. A deadline can be passed by up to fifteen further *checks* under the real clock — checks, not operations, because one check can be a multi-operation `TrySpend` or an `Exhausted` poll followed by unmetered work — which is observable in `OverrunMilliseconds` rather than claimed impossible. The searches that do coarse work (route, reach flood, course travel) read the clock themselves every expansion, so they stay prompt. The clock is read on every sixteenth check because a read per check was 8.4% of the brain thread on the 22 September capture's replay, and once a check sees the deadline passed it stays passed. An injected fixture clock is read on every check, so a row that cuts at an exact tick of its clock still does. Measured 23 September 2026 within one replay basin, the whole-brain p99 fell by 1.5 to 2.3 ms with the stride in, so the overrun has not shown up as a cost.

**The tick's millisecond figure is read through `TickAllowance.Milliseconds` and nowhere else, so one override moves the whole decision.** In the game it is exactly `Weights.TotalPlanningMilliseconds`, because `OverrideMilliseconds` is null and nothing in the mod sets it; the world run's budget curve sets it to ask what a smaller allowance costs in behaviour. It needs no second seam because every nested slice — the route search's 8 ms, the reach flood's 2 ms, the meeting and player-side floods' 1 ms — takes the earlier of its own deadline and the tick's through `LimitPlanningWork.Deadline`, and spends through the tick's budget, so a 2 ms tick caps all of them without any being edited. The one other reader of the tunable is `AuditDecisionContracts.DecideCeilingMilliseconds`, which only decides when a `decide-overran-allowance` record is written and still reads the tunable, so under an override that record is judged against the production ceiling. `Tools/EngineReplay/DecisionMaking/VerifyTheTickAllowanceOverride.cs` fails on any new production reader that bypasses the seam. An override that is not a positive finite figure is refused rather than clamped.

**`Cut` is sticky and is a property of the allowance, never of the caller that reads it.** It is set the first time any consumer is refused an operation and stays set until that allowance is gone, which is what makes `FirstCutSubsystem` meaningful and what makes a late read of `Cut` answer a wider question than it looks like it asks: not "was I cut" but "was anything cut this tick". A consumer that treats its own late `budget.Cut` as evidence about its own work is reading another subsystem's exhaustion as its own refusal. Combat's plan re-pricing did exactly that and released valid committed fights for a day because of it; the rule that came out of it lives in `../../../Activities/Combat/Planning/CLAUDE.md`, and the general form is that an exhausted bound is a third answer rather than a negative.

**One allowance is installed per owner, and an owner restores what it found.** `Begin` refuses to replace a standing allowance, which is what stops a nested planner minting a second deadline after exhausting the first. `Own` is the other half and is what an actual owner uses: it saves the standing allowance, installs its own, and gives the previous one back on the way out. `Brain.Tick` is the only production owner and finds nothing standing, so `Own` is exactly the `Begin`/`End` pair it always had; the reason it exists is that a harness installs an ambient allowance per case so a fixture entering below the tick can borrow one at all, and a fixture that drives a whole tick must not destroy it. `Restart` is the blunter form — end whatever stands and install this — for a per-case reset, which has nothing to restore.

**A budget bounds a decision, not a round of it.** A consumer that answers what is queued, resumes and collects the next requests has completed one exchange rather than spent its allowance, so a caller loops on `budget.Exhausted` rather than calling once — `DecideCourseEachTick.Advance` is the live instance, and `../CLAUDE.md` carries the 30-of-120 measurement that found it. The loop terminates on the budget rather than on progress, deliberately: a round that answers nothing is a legitimate state whenever a request stays queued against a full store, and terminating on "no progress" would abandon a decision whose queue was merely full.

A cache capacity bounds storage, not the identities the brain may ever discover. Sources continue past evicted entries, and rescan finite stable sets. Current and in-flight work is pinned. A fully pinned cache refuses admission visibly rather than evicting the live prefix. A new frame is not a new cursor epoch. No consumer can silently buy a second production deadline after exhausting the first.

**A store shared between producers evicts group-aware, and the property is the reason rather than the rule.** `DecisionStorage` takes an optional grouping and a group count; `GroupFloor` is `Capacity / groups`, every group is guaranteed that floor, and only what a group holds *above* its floor competes on recency. A group that has produced nothing still has its floor waiting, which is what stops the order sources happen to run in from deciding who exists — sizing the floor to who has arrived is the same defect wearing a smaller hat, because the first arrival claims everything. With every group at or under its floor and the store still full, admission falls back to plain recency, so a capacity that cannot cover every floor still accepts work rather than refusing it; a store passing no grouping keeps plain recency throughout. The durable property, earned on 21 September 2026 when six opportunity domains shared one store of sixty-four and lighting's 138 sites evicted mining's, chopping's and collection's one candidate each: **a shared bounded resource divided by recency alone is a preference for the loudest producer, and every producer needs a floor it cannot be pushed below.** It is written here rather than at discovery so that any other bounded store shared between producers inherits it; `../Opportunities/CLAUDE.md` carries the scene that found it.

## Current state — 2026-09-21

These owners are live under the course brain: `Brain.Tick` opens exactly one allowance around the whole tick and every nested discovery, repair, binding, combat and route consumer borrows it. They still do not establish whole-brain budget coverage or live performance, and two things are worth knowing before reading a timing off them. The engine suite lifts the millisecond allowances on every case, so a planning deadline in a fixture cannot depend on how busy the machine was; and a case measured inside the whole harness runs about 2.3× slower per operation than the same case standalone, which the root guide's traps section carries — so a cost figure taken in-suite is not comparable to one taken alone.
