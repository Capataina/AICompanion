# Behaviour selection — a course decides, and the utility scorer beside it is retired but compiled

**`DecideCourseEachTick.cs` is what the brain tick calls**, since `0bb2c8a` on 21 September 2026. It owns the whole decision: freeze one observation through `AssembleCourseSnapshot`, discover across all six domains through `DiscoverOpportunities`, search bounded orders through `SearchCourseOrders` priced by `ForecastCourseConsequences`, and publish through `RetainCourse` a course whose next step the tick carries out via `ExecuteCourseBinding`. A retained course whose next use still validates is carried with no search at all — that is the retention the design is named for, and it is checked against a freshly frozen observation every tick because the question is whether the next native call would succeed *now*.

Four shapes in it exist because their absence was measured. A decision is frozen for its own life rather than for one tick, since a travel query can cost more than a tick's leftover allowance and re-observing every tick discarded and restarted one for ever. Publication reads the model-extended snapshot, because answers are appended as they complete and `ReadyToPublish` refuses a proposal naming a fact its snapshot lacks. A course with no steps is released as finished, because an empty order is a legal winning course that a next-step check never sees. And **a tick spends its whole remaining allowance on the decision rather than one round of it.**

That fourth one is the cheapest to get wrong and the hardest to see. `RetainCourseModelQueries.ContinueSearch` is deliberately one round — answer the models already queued, extend the frozen catalogue, resume the search, collect what the search newly asked for — and the tick called it once, so a search that discovered a *new* model request during that round waited a whole tick for it however much allowance was left unspent. Binding a site is what produces its travel query, so that is the ordinary case rather than a corner. **The budget is the bound, not the round**: a decision spans ticks because a query genuinely outlasts an allowance, never because the tick stopped asking after one exchange. Measured on the scene whose entire premise is that mining keeps the body, mining held it for 30 ticks of 120 and holds 109 now; its sibling went 41 to 109. The companion was not choosing badly, it was spending three-quarters of its time with no decision made — **and a slow decision is indistinguishable from a decision against the work from outside**, because every tick with no published course falls through to companionship, which is the right fallback and is also the disguise. The funnel is what separates them, and it read `decision=deciding` rather than `no-order-worth-taking`; anyone reaching for the comparison weights on that evidence would have been tuning a system that had not finished thinking. `DecideCourseEachTick.Advance` loops on the budget rather than on progress, deliberately, because `ContinueSearch` leaves a request queued when the model store is at capacity and a round that answers nothing is a legitimate state.

**A fifth shape landed on 22 September 2026, and it is the one the first four made necessary.** Those four all make a decision span ticks more often and more deliberately, and until now every tick inside one asked for companionship — which the tick then *selects*, exiting whatever was running. For combat that exit releases the committed plan with `activity-exited`, the course's accepted use goes absent, the course is released, and a decision starts. `DecideCourseEachTick.Deciding` is the plan's tick-order step 5 built at last: an unsettled tick carries combat's own continuation — the committed plan's stand, or the opener a cut search proved — and falls to companionship only when there is nothing to continue. It rides on `CourseDecision.Request` rather than a fabricated `StepBinding`, because a step id no course ever published would join a recorded effect to a course that does not contain it. `Companion/Brain/Activities/Combat/CLAUDE.md` owns the continuation itself and the measurement behind the fixture that guards it.

What it deliberately does not do: **opportunistic replacement mid-course**. `RetainCourse.Consider` requires the incumbent reprojected from the current observation before two futures can be compared, and that reprojection is unbuilt, so a valid course is kept and no rival is weighed against it. The companion finishes what it started and picks again at the end.

## The objective prices harm to the player, and the term standing in for defending him has outlived two deletion conditions

**Both holes that made defending the player unreachable closed on 21 September 2026, and the behaviour is still only half there.** The player is a contact actor now, with his own motion track captured by `CapturePlayerMotion` and extended by the same law a hostile's is, and each hostile is projected against him as well as against the companion — a separate projection rather than a copy, because native melee geometry is victim-dependent. And a course's own predicted kills truncate the threats they remove, for both bodies, read off the course's effects where a combat step names its target as a `HostileLife` need whose delta carries the life left. `censusComplete` is the census's own answer rather than a pin, and the projection's tail is the forecast's honest verdict.

What it buys is real and narrower than it sounds: a course that kills a threat **before it reaches anybody** is now worth the harm it prevents, which is the interception behaviour.

**Its cost was first quoted from the wrong scene, and the honest figure is on a crowd.** `--brain-cost`'s seeded scene has no hostiles at all, so the term the change added — a second contact geometry per hostile — runs zero times in it, and the 1.952 → 1.966 ms first published was the change measured where it does nothing. Single-variable on `--crowd-cost`, four hostiles including a boss, with the player actor forced off and on: decide mean 22.099 → 22.689 ms, p95 54.943 → 55.247, max 823.761 → 814.852. Six tenths of a millisecond of mean, nothing at the maximum. That scene also exposed a pre-existing cost that has nothing to do with this change and is far larger than it — `AIC-445`.

**A threat that keeps hitting is priced too, as of the evening of 21 September 2026, and the behaviour still does not follow from it.** `ForecastContactHarm` used to stop an actor's scan at first contact; it now resumes after the game's own immunity window and stops only when accumulated hits exhaust that actor's life. `Courses/CLAUDE.md` owns that mechanism, its window table and why knockback's absence bounds rather than tilts the comparison. What the successor model bought is real and smaller than it sounds: idling now reads harm 0.6184 where any course that moves the body reads 0.9082, so the model does discriminate — but combat and mining carry the same 0.9082 to four decimals, which can only mean **the course's own predicted kill never lands inside the harm horizon**, so the truncation that is supposed to pay for defending never fires. The whole of combat's advantage on that scene is a useful-effects margin of 0.0067.

So `CourseComparisonEpisode.RelevanceFor` is still what produces the behaviour, and it was measured rather than assumed: with its urgency half removed, combat takes 0 of 120 ticks where it takes 109 with it. Its deletion condition was rewritten to name the gap that is actually open — delete it when a course's own kill truncates the harm it prevents inside the horizon — and the investigation it points at is the kill rather than the hits. `AIC-439` carries the table; the numbers and the chain to read first are in `Courses/CLAUDE.md`.

**Combat wins that row on `useful`, not on harm**, and the row's green belongs to the scene being repaired rather than to the mechanism: a `HostileLife` need's worth is damage over life, so replacing the 400-life override with a real zombie's own numbers raises combat's useful about ninefold. An independent review disabled kill truncation entirely and the row was unchanged at 109 of 120, which is the single-variable run that settles the attribution — **and is the same absence `2cbd503` measured that evening from the other side**, where combat and mining carried identical harm to four decimals. A truncation that changes nothing when removed and a harm column that does not separate two courses are one fact reached twice: the course's own predicted kill never lands inside the harm horizon.

The measurement that opened this, on `danger lifts combat over work` at 0.33.2, with a zombie beside a player at 40 life and ten real decisions inside the 120 ticks:

**That scene could not have witnessed the preference under any brain, and the reason is worth more than the numbers.** Its zombie stood exactly where it was placed — enemy AI does not run headless — forty-eight pixels from a player whose boxes close at nineteen, so no contact forecast however correct could predict a hit, and combat pricing near zero was the comparison being right. It walks at him by hand now, and carries the game's own life and damage rather than the 400 and 20 it overrode them with, because a hostile the companion cannot kill inside the horizon takes its hit whatever the companion does.

**Three scoring hypotheses were tested against this row and all three were wrong, so they are recorded as eliminations rather than left to be retried.** Combat does not score below mining because of a missing bonus — a derived-from-nothing constant moved the outcome by nothing. Combat orders are not refused during projection — the refusals are twelve `native-target-unresolved` and eight `assistance-target-unresolved`, none of them combat's. A fourth reading, that no comparison happens at all because the course is retained, was contradicted by the per-tick decision tally: `course-retained` on 110 ticks and `course-published` on 10, so ten real searches ran at urgency 0.757 with combat admitted and mining won every one.

The property that survives all four, and the one to check before anyone writes a fifth patch here: **a preference the objective has no term for cannot be recovered by tuning the terms it does have.** The term exists now, and the property held on the way in as well as on the way out — deleting `RelevanceFor` was proposed twice on 21 September 2026 on the strength of a term that had just landed, and both times the measurement refused it. The first time the harm term priced one hit while the thing it stood in for was a threat that keeps hitting; the second time the hits were priced and the *kill* was the term that never fired. Each time the honest move was to rewrite the deletion condition to name the newly-visible gap rather than to remove the stand-in, because a stand-in removed before its replacement demonstrably produces the behaviour is a behaviour deleted.

One method caveat belongs with those numbers, because the prints invite the mistake: **a harm column taken with the term and one taken without it are not comparable term-for-term.** With combat winning 109 ticks the companion moves and fights, so the world at the sampled decision is a different world. The single-variable claim is the tick count and nothing else in the table.

**Hunting outranks work by being eligible, never by outscoring it, so a discount of hunt against work would change nothing.** Across the 2026-09-11 session, mining won 1,368 of the 1,734 ticks where it had a live target and torch placement won the other 366; hunting won none of them. Hunting seeks an attack opportunity. Useful investigation belongs to lighting, collecting or other concrete work, while idle local movement belongs to keeping company in the agreed final structure. Hunting does not require an independent exploration purpose to justify its existence.

Regroup time uses available route-step durations and the positioner's observed travel estimate as well as straight-line distance. The course has no raw-then-protection split — a need's worth is already multiplied by `CourseComparisonEpisode.RelevanceFor` — so the row asserts two things instead: worth is invariant under a threat to the *companion*, and falls under a threat to the *player*. Two further premises changed vocabulary rather than strictness, and the reason binds anyone writing a row against a course: **course nominals are signed net values rather than the chooser's 0..1 utilities**, so "this excursion has a real opportunity" is now whether the course priced an order led by that work — chopping and lighting both lead at negative worth in a calm scene while being perfectly healthy opportunities, and `raw > 0` would have failed on them — and the offer column reads the course's three-valued census admission rather than the retired eligibility enum. `Courses/CLAUDE.md` carries the four scenes' numbers and the arithmetic row that proves the fall is charged once rather than squared. Interruptible work pays the time until it can yield, rather than the duration of its entire job, against the threat horizon. Unknown intervention is conservative; the companion remains autonomous while the player is dead, with player-protection pressure removed.

A boss fight or world event reaches optional work through the same protection factor and nowhere else. `ActivityComparisonContext.EncounterIntensity` carries the observation's intensity, and a non-combat excursion — mining, chopping, collecting, lighting — pays `1 − max(urgency, intensity)`, while a candidate marked `ServesEncounter` (the chooser marks the Combat family) pays urgency alone. The maximum rather than the product is the point: a boss is also a threat that raises urgency and crowd pressure is the threats themselves, so multiplying the two would charge one danger twice. Keeping company is not an excursion and is untouched, which is deliberate rather than an omission, because nomination selects only positive values and zeroing every non-combat child would leave a quiet blood-moon lull with no activity at all; the README's wider encounter leash belongs to position and movement, not to this factor. ## A job is worth what it delivers per time, and close jobs are compared as orders

**Every job somewhere pays for the time it takes, travel included, and a job nearly done is worth nearly all of its value.** Beside the protection, commitment, horizon and reunion factors, a task candidate — one with a target that is neither following nor serving the player directly — is multiplied by `window / (window + ticks until done)`, the window in `BehaviourWeights.cs`. That one term is what the owner asked for on 15 September 2026 as "commitment that grows as the job nears done": an enemy at a fifth of its life has a short kill time left and so scores higher than the same enemy fresh, with no bonus for having started, and a long job is never scored at zero for being long. The ticks come from `CompanionAction.TaskTicks`, which defaults to the activity's forecast and which the combat stance extends with an optimistic kill time on its hunt side, because the hunt forecast deliberately counts only the flight the threat horizon was tuned against. Keeping company and the guard side declare `ServesPlayerDirectly` and pay none of this, because their worth is where the player is.

**A trip job that reports no trip is scored as instant, and that is the defect the time term was built on.** The lighting activity inherited a zero forecast from the base class, so a torch trip of 218 ticks outbid a slime hunt that honestly reported its flight and was discounted for it; the nearby-work base now reports its own trip, and `VerifyPreparedActivities` refuses any excursion that inherits the zero rather than trusting the next activity to remember.

**A job somewhere pays for keeping the companion apart whether or not it is an excursion, and it pays once.** A hunt that can shoot from where the orb hovers is not an excursion for the threat horizon, and it used to pay no separation either, so on 15 September 2026 it held the orb over a surface zombie while the player dropped four hundred pixels into a cave. The separation is `Chooser.Separation`: one minus keeping company's own slope, uncapped, read at the job's stand against the player's region carried along his observed travel for the job's duration, at a distance that is the gap beyond the region plus the route home's detour. The detour (`Chooser.RouteDetour`) is how much longer the reach flood's way from the stand to the player runs than the straight line: the flood is rooted at the body, so its costs to the player and to the stand are two distances from one point and their difference, taken either way round, bounds the route between them from below — taken one way only it read zero with the body beside the player, which is where a job is chosen from while the companion keeps him company; `VerifyRouteHomeFromEitherEnd` reads it from both ends, where the two readings differ by the flood's directional pricing (each edge is priced by the clearance at its far corner, so the way out and the way back through one corridor cost differently) and by nothing else — and a mining stand, being a solid ore tile, is priced at the nearest corner the orb fits at, or from the body when the flood holds no such corner. So a stand inside the region pays nothing whatever its route, a stand beyond it pays by the gap and the detour, a stand the player's travel will leave at fly-home distance leaves the job worth nothing, and a player leaving makes a long job pay more than a quick one. **Neither an unknown route nor an absent one reads as free.** While the flood has not reached the player the detour is zero and the cost is the straight-line gap, a floor the real route can only exceed; a player's tile a finished flood proves absent reads a detour of twice the straight line, which is the disc's own guarantee that no route to a place it proved absent is shorter than about three times its straight line. The owner ruled on 15 September 2026 that the one cost reads the route home; with the straight line, a mine on a near and a far way up was worth the same (0.453 and 0.453 at 1.5 px a tick), and with the detour 0.255 against 0.116. The flood prices clearance, so even a direct way home reads some detour, which is why the near route's worth fell too. **The cap is rejoining's and not the job's, and capping both was built and measured wrong.** With the separation capped at half, the surface-zombie scene built from the live region geometry kept the hunt at 0.198 against rejoining's 0.076 once the player had dropped four hundred pixels — rejoining's 0.38 cut to a fifth by the discount on following while useful work exists — so a fight up where the player used to be still won. The two rulings are about different things: rejoining never on its own outweighs a job genuinely worth doing is about what rejoining is worth, and the pull taking all of the companion's attention at the distance where it would lose him is about how far is far, so a job reads the slope to its end. The stand is the site for an excursion and the body for a job done from where it hovers. It replaced a reunion factor priced from a per-tick delay cost, and the replacement is a consolidation rather than a retuning: the delay cost and the rectangle were two separation costs, and the delay cost measured the wrong thing — on that play a slime hunt beside an idle player lost 0.36 raw to 0.02 final because a long return estimate was charged at a departure speed of a tenth of a pixel. `AssessReunionCost` still computes it because the recorder writes its three columns; nothing decides on it. `PreparedActivity.PlayerFit` is the work allowance carried forward the same way, and a job the player's heading takes beyond the started-job radius is worth nothing, which is policy — the radius is a preference — rather than a second separation.

**A fight that still serves him is not leaving him.** `ServesPlayerDirectly` keeps reunion at 1 and skips the excursion protection discount, even when the path to the stand is longer than a local trip. Capture `2026-09-18_16-35-26-353` dumped combat at raw 1.02 to final 0.00 on the next tick because a 10-tick stand became a 227-tick FireFrom and reunion multiplied the fight to nothing; company 0.05 took the body. The surface-zombie drop is still `ServesPlayerDirectly` false, so it still zeros once he has gone. `VerifyPreparedActivities` holds both.

A second mechanism for the zombie was built and torn out. The same capture shows the discount on following during useful work turning a reunion worth 0.80 into 0.16, so the discount was lifted, first whenever the companion was outside the player's region and then whenever he was also travelling away. Both went red on `VerifyOreWork`'s proven-job row, whose scene is a player walking away from ore thirty tiles off — the small job on the walk that Expected Behaviour's 4:00 scene asks the companion to take — so the discount is unconditional and the separation alone makes the zombie hunt lose once the player has gone.

**The flat floor keeping company offers inside the region, and the time term, were both judged against the capture and left as they are.** On the rows where a hunt lost to that floor the target was an off-screen zombie shot from where the orb hovered: raw 0.11 is the off-screen nearness floor on hunting's worth, and the recorded factor list in the capture's events names the cutters as the threat horizon at 0.50 and the delay-cost reunion factor at 0.69, with the time term the smaller third at about 0.66 — a far target losing to doing nothing, which is correct. The slime hunts that lost beside an idle player lost to keeping company's rejoin value at the old full cap, to the delay charge, or because the hunt's own stand query found no firing position; none of those is the floor or the time term, and the first two are what this folder and keeping company changed.

**Close jobs are compared as orders, and ordering decides which of them runs, never whether one of them runs.** `OrderNearbyTasks` takes every task within a share of the best task's score, tries every order of them, and scores an order by the worth each job delivers and when, the first job at its own task time and each later one after the straight flight from where the previous job happened. The first job of the best order takes the best single task score and the rest are placed below it, so keeping company and protection are compared against exactly what they were compared against before. The objective is worth delivered early rather than the least total time, because every order does the same work and the least total time cannot tell killing the slime on the way from killing it on the way back. Only the first step runs; the order is worked out again every comparison and nothing is kept. There is deliberately no separate switching margin: flying toward the chosen job shortens its time and lengthens the others', and the incumbent's commitment is already inside its worth, which the fixture's two-even-jobs row holds by counting lead changes while the body flies. Travel between jobs is a straight line over the body's pace, so an order through terrain is scored a little optimistically. The recorder writes the chosen order and the best order that starts differently as `task_order` and `task_order_runner_up`. Capture `2026-09-18_18-57-09-481` is the live negative: `task_order` read `collect>place-torches` while the body sat between the two Exact destinations swapping every fifteen ticks, because collect raw collapsed and the order is discarded. The unfinished discussion of keeping the order, freezing leftover with identity, and applying the same comparison inside combat and on the way home is `research/Historical Evidence/Archived Brain Proposals/04 Plan a Course of Action and Follow It.md`; nothing of it is built, it is not an implementation plan, and keep-the-job membership on collect and torch is not that discussion.

## Preparation is scheduled per family, so one expensive family cannot spend the tick

`ScheduleOpportunityQueries` decides which activities prepare in a comparison. Families run in enum order; inside each, the incumbent and every non-excursion child (protection and keeping company) always prepare, then optional children prepare in a rotating order. The first optional child always runs, and each optional child runs inside a `LimitPlanningWork.Narrow` deadline set to what remains of the family's share (defined in `BehaviourWeights.cs`); further optional children run only while that share is unspent. Because every nested route, reach and approach query reads the shared deadline, the share bounds an optional child's whole preparation, not just whether its siblings start. Mandatory children deliberately run under the tick's total allowance instead: narrowing the incumbent would turn an approach the body is already walking into the same undecided answer every tick. That was acute while tool-approach queries were fresh bounded A* searches per candidate pose; they read the reach sense now, so the undecided answer comes from an unsettled flood rather than a spent budget, and the reason to leave the incumbent unnarrowed is unchanged. A skipped child is written to the board as `Deferred` with zero value and the reason `family-preparation-allowance-spent`; its retained discovery is neither compared nor allowed to win. The family's next comparison starts from the first child it deferred, so a family of n optional children prepares each at least once every n comparisons. `Queries.LastFamilies` carries prepared and deferred counts and milliseconds per family for the recorder and harness.

This is scheduling, not preference: deferral never lowers a prepared value, and protection, keeping company and the running activity cannot be starved by it. The share exists so optional siblings cannot pile their own searches onto a tick: the brain-cost scene measured a single incumbent mining approach recomputation spending the whole total planning allowance, and anything prepared after it on that tick would have found nothing left. That incumbent cost was a property of the approach query itself, not of scheduling, and it was fixed there twice: the tool-access query asks nearest poses first and stops at the first reachable one, which brought the same scene's worst decide tick to about 4 ms, and it then stopped searching for routes at all and reads the reach sense instead. With `LimitPlanningWork.Unbounded` set nothing is deferred, which keeps offline fixtures deterministic; a fixture that tests the rotation sets the chooser's share to zero, which deterministically prepares exactly one optional child per family.

## The urgency ladder, and why an action topping out at one can never interrupt anything

Candidate preparation and shared comparison are separate calls. The chooser invokes each adapter's Prepare once, then reads its captured raw value, forecast and classification through context-free methods. `EvaluatePreparedActivities` accepts only immutable scalar records, reads no live activity or world object and never invokes discovery. It returns all factors and an explicit error for invalid or non-finite values. Positive-infinite threat horizon means no observed deadline. Repeated or reordered evaluation preserves each candidate's value. Preparation still owns mutable discovery caches; execution ownership is handled separately below.

Incumbent commitment is deliberately independent of the shared body-stall flag. A replacement activity would otherwise inherit its predecessor's physical failure before attempting any movement. Activity-owned remaining effort and outcomes must replace that coarse commitment; body-level stalling alone cannot decide that a job was a bad choice.

Prepare is required for every adapter rather than an optional hook. Score and ForecastTicks receive no world context. All ordinary candidates obey that boundary. Keeping company combines rejoining the player's region and moving about inside it in one candidate. Avoiding a hit is movement's evade layer, which is neither a candidate nor a takeover, and nothing takes the body to escape a liquid, because every liquid is air to it.

Zero and invalid candidates produce no family nomination. If all families are empty, selection returns no activity and releases the former one; the coordinator holds ordinary movement while independent hands and the earlier recovery and downed paths retain their existing contracts. Positive candidates use one tie key at both levels: their stable registration index. Group order and duplicated identical candidates cannot change the result. The flat selector exists only as a test reference.

Each prepared candidate also captures its target binding. Before activation, the chooser verifies that the adapter still names that identity, that an NPC remains alive/active in the same spawn generation, or that an item still has quantity and retains its type. A rejected candidate keeps its original raw value and named rejection in the ledger but loses selectable value. Families nominate again from the remaining prepared board without discovery. Shared opportunity costs are recomputed from captured values so a rejected work offer cannot keep suppressing companionship. Each rejection removes one candidate, bounding reconsideration by the board size.

Item activation also verifies that the captured object still occupies its world slot, naming a replaced slot separately from exhausted quantity or a changed type. Same-type replacement cannot inherit the old offer. This binding check does not establish native tile permission, a useful arrival pose, or a new generation when an arbitrary mod mutates the same item object in place without a lifecycle signal. Those remain the activity-specific offer and native interaction contracts; non-entity identities currently use the adapter's existing equality semantics. It also does not make an unresolved route proven merely because its target still exists.

Before comparing the captured identity, activation also reads the activity's side-effect-free prepared-target rejection. Tile work uses this to distinguish a vanished tile from changed material, preserving the original raw value and letting families nominate another prepared candidate. Coordinate/material binding cannot detect an unobserved same-material replacement and does not certify tool permission or access.

Gathering's prepared validation also rejects a disabled work policy. ## A bound course step is translated, never executed a second time
