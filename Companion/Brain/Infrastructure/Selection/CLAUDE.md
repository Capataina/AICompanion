# Behaviour selection — a course decides, the activities perform, and nothing else in here chooses anything

**`DecideCourseEachTick.cs` is what the brain tick calls**, since `0bb2c8a` on 21 September 2026. It owns the whole decision: freeze one observation through `AssembleCourseSnapshot`, discover across all six domains through `DiscoverOpportunities`, search bounded orders through `SearchCourseOrders` priced by `ForecastCourseConsequences`, and publish through `RetainCourse` a course whose next step the tick carries out via `ExecuteCourseBinding`. A retained course whose next use still validates is carried with no search at all — that is the retention the design is named for, and it is checked against a freshly frozen observation every tick because the question is whether the next native call would succeed *now*.

Four shapes in it exist because their absence was measured. A decision is frozen for its own life rather than for one tick, since a travel query can cost more than a tick's leftover allowance and re-observing every tick discarded and restarted one for ever. Publication reads the model-extended snapshot, because answers are appended as they complete and `ReadyToPublish` refuses a proposal naming a fact its snapshot lacks. A course with no steps is released as finished, because an empty order is a legal winning course that a next-step check never sees. And **a tick spends its whole remaining allowance on the decision rather than one round of it.**

That fourth one is the cheapest to get wrong and the hardest to see. `RetainCourseModelQueries.ContinueSearch` is deliberately one round — answer the models already queued, extend the frozen catalogue, resume the search, collect what the search newly asked for — and the tick called it once, so a search that discovered a *new* model request during that round waited a whole tick for it however much allowance was left unspent. Binding a site is what produces its travel query, so that is the ordinary case rather than a corner. **The budget is the bound, not the round**: a decision spans ticks because a query genuinely outlasts an allowance, never because the tick stopped asking after one exchange. Measured on the scene whose entire premise is that mining keeps the body, mining held it for 30 ticks of 120 and holds 109 now; its sibling went 41 to 109. The companion was not choosing badly, it was spending three-quarters of its time with no decision made — **and a slow decision is indistinguishable from a decision against the work from outside**, because every tick with no published course falls through to companionship, which is the right fallback and is also the disguise. The funnel is what separates them, and it read `decision=deciding` rather than `no-order-worth-taking`; anyone reaching for the comparison weights on that evidence would have been tuning a system that had not finished thinking. `DecideCourseEachTick.Advance` loops on the budget rather than on progress, deliberately, because `ContinueSearch` leaves a request queued when the model store is at capacity and a round that answers nothing is a legitimate state.

**A fifth shape landed on 22 September 2026, and it is the one the first four made necessary.** Those four all make a decision span ticks more often and more deliberately, and until now every tick inside one asked for companionship — which the tick then *selects*, exiting whatever was running. For combat that exit releases the committed plan with `activity-exited`, the course's accepted use goes absent, the course is released, and a decision starts. `DecideCourseEachTick.Deciding` is the plan's tick-order step 5 built at last: an unsettled tick carries combat's own continuation — the committed plan's stand, or the opener a cut search proved — and falls to companionship only when there is nothing to continue. It rides on `CourseDecision.Request` rather than a fabricated `StepBinding`, because a step id no course ever published would join a recorded effect to a course that does not contain it. `Companion/Brain/Activities/Combat/CLAUDE.md` owns the continuation itself and the measurement behind the fixture that guards it.

What it deliberately does not do: **opportunistic replacement mid-course**. `RetainCourse.Consider` requires the incumbent reprojected from the current observation before two futures can be compared, and that reprojection is unbuilt, so a valid course is kept and no rival is weighed against it. The companion finishes what it started and picks again at the end.

## The objective prices harm to the player, and the term standing in for defending him has outlived two deletion conditions

**Both holes that made defending the player unreachable closed on 21 September 2026, and the behaviour is still only half there.** The player is a contact actor now, with his own motion track captured by `CapturePlayerMotion` and extended by the same law a hostile's is, and each hostile is projected against him as well as against the companion — a separate projection rather than a copy, because native melee geometry is victim-dependent. And a course's own predicted kills truncate the threats they remove, for both bodies, read off the course's effects where a combat step names its target as a `HostileLife` need whose delta carries the life left. `censusComplete` is the census's own answer rather than a pin, and the projection's tail is the forecast's honest verdict.

What it buys is real and narrower than it sounds: a course that kills a threat **before it reaches anybody** is now worth the harm it prevents, which is the interception behaviour.

**Its cost was first quoted from the wrong scene, and the honest figure is on a crowd.** `--brain-cost`'s seeded scene has no hostiles at all, so the term the change added — a second contact geometry per hostile — runs zero times in it, and the 1.952 → 1.966 ms first published was the change measured where it does nothing. Single-variable on `--crowd-cost`, four hostiles including a boss, with the player actor forced off and on: decide mean 22.099 → 22.689 ms, p95 54.943 → 55.247, max 823.761 → 814.852. Six tenths of a millisecond of mean, nothing at the maximum. That scene also exposed a pre-existing cost that has nothing to do with this change and is far larger than it — `AIC-445`.

**A threat that keeps hitting is priced too, as of the evening of 21 September 2026, and the behaviour still does not follow from it.** `ForecastContactHarm` used to stop an actor's scan at first contact; it now resumes after the game's own immunity window and stops only when accumulated hits exhaust that actor's life. `Courses/CLAUDE.md` owns that mechanism, its window table and why knockback's absence bounds rather than tilts the comparison. What the successor model bought is real and smaller than it sounds: idling now reads harm 0.6184 where any course that moves the body reads 0.9082, so the model does discriminate — but combat and mining carry the same 0.9082 to four decimals, which can only mean **the course's own predicted kill never lands inside the harm horizon**, so the truncation that is supposed to pay for defending never fires. The whole of combat's advantage on that scene is a useful-effects margin of 0.0067.

So `CourseComparisonEpisode.RelevanceFor` is still what produces the behaviour, and it was measured rather than assumed: with its urgency half removed, combat takes 0 of 120 ticks where it takes 109 with it. Its deletion condition was rewritten to name the gap that is actually open — delete it when a course's own kill truncates the harm it prevents inside the horizon — and the investigation it points at is the kill rather than the hits. `AIC-439` carries the table; the numbers and the chain to read first are in `Courses/CLAUDE.md`.

**Combat wins that row on `useful`, not on harm**, and the row's green belongs to the scene being repaired rather than to the mechanism: a `HostileLife` need's worth is damage over life, so replacing the 400-life override with a real zombie's own numbers raises combat's useful about ninefold. An independent review disabled kill truncation entirely and the row was unchanged at 109 of 120, which is the single-variable run that settles the attribution — **and is the same absence `2cbd503` measured that evening from the other side**, where combat and mining carried identical harm to four decimals. A truncation that changes nothing when removed and a harm column that does not separate two courses are one fact reached twice: the course's own predicted kill never lands inside the harm horizon.

The measurement that opened this, on `danger lifts combat over work` at 0.33.2, with a zombie beside a player at 40 life and ten real decisions inside the 120 ticks:

The property that survives all four, and the one to check before anyone writes a fifth patch here: **a preference the objective has no term for cannot be recovered by tuning the terms it does have.** The term exists now, and the property held on the way in as well as on the way out — deleting `RelevanceFor` was proposed twice on 21 September 2026 on the strength of a term that had just landed, and both times the measurement refused it. The first time the harm term priced one hit while the thing it stood in for was a threat that keeps hitting; the second time the hits were priced and the *kill* was the term that never fired. ## One activity holds the body, and an attempt is one continuous stretch of executing it

`OwnCurrentActivity` is the sole holder of the current executor, and the tick delegates to it rather than keeping another current-action field: `ExecuteCourseBinding` names the activity a published step performs and the owner selects it. It identifies consecutive purposes by the activity's own identity and, where that identity is an NPC, its spawn generation; other identities keep their value or reference semantics, which does not certify an item slot's generation or revalidate an offer. Selecting a different purpose changes the activity id even when the executor is unchanged. A suspended purpose keeps its id if freshly selected again, and replacement ends it without calling its physical release twice. The owner retains the current purpose and the last termination summary, never a stack of intentions.

**Purpose and attempt are separate, and the attempt is the finer of the two.** Each continuous stretch of execution opens one attempt with its own identity and calls the activity's `BeginAttempt`; reselecting the executing purpose keeps it open, suspension closes it as Interrupted with the suspension reason, and replacement or an empty selection closes it with the replaced activity's own `ConcludeAttempt` before Exit runs. Attempt identities come from one process-wide counter rather than one per owner, so a respawned companion's new brain cannot reuse identities a recorder cursor has already written this session. Resuming a suspended purpose therefore keeps the activity identity and opens a fresh attempt with no inherited effects. A bounded list of recent outcomes survives, so two conclusions inside one tick — a replacement, then a recovery interruption — both reach the recorder. An attempt identity is not a route attempt: the navigator keeps its own.

**Productive effects are credited only through `RecordWork`, and only while an attempt is open**, so an effect observed outside execution belongs to nobody's purpose. That method also remembers *where* the work happened, for a bounded time and radius, which is what `CompanionAction` asks when a collect target turns up beside a vein the companion has just broken: a drop near a site it has just worked is its own mess rather than a fresh errand. The two are one event seen twice, which is why they are one call — every caller records a site for an observed productive *native* effect, which is exactly the thing that credits the attempt.

Repeated selection of the same executing purpose is not a lifecycle transition: the transient selection stage inside a tick must not refresh the changed-at timestamp or flood the sparse recorder with fictional transitions. The owner also delivers post-control outcome observations only while the ordinary activity is executing, so recovery flight can keep observing body progress while that activity is suspended without the elapsed ticks consuming its failure budget; resumption restores ordinary observation, so an interrupted unproductive pursuit can still expire once it gets control back. Recovery and downing suspend explicitly through this owner: suspension invokes the release hook once and preserves the purpose's admission identity, reselection re-enters before execution, and moving to another executor releases the previous admission. **A suspension reason is not a failed route and not proof that choosing the work was wrong.**

The selected job earns continuation by identity on every winning tick, including consecutive targets inside one behaviour. A moving enemy or drop keeps its identity as its position changes; a different entity must pass acquisition again. Mining retains a vein id, chopping a trunk coordinate and material, and small world interactions a target tile.

## Companionship is observed every tick, and nothing decides on what it costs

**A distance measured in this tree is to the player's intent region, and which point of it depends on the question.** The region is a sense (`../Observation/ObservePlayerIntentRegion.cs`, which owns why it exists, how it leads and why the player sits in its bottom third). A radius — the active-activity allowance — measures to the region's `Heading`, his centre carried by the lead, because a radius anchored where he stands shrinks the ground in front of him as he walks into it, and one anchored on the box's centre would lean a third of the box upward. A separation — the regroup urgency — measures to the rectangle, on the gap beyond its edge, because the owner ruled every pull is measured from the region and is zero anywhere inside it. The route-home estimate prices the flight to the box's centre, where the companion lives.

`ObserveCompanionship.Observe` runs from the brain tick, before the early recovery branch, and produces three things. `Reunion` accumulates observed separation through `AssessReunionCost`, which charges only intervals it actually observed — a duplicate observation of one tick is not charged twice and an unobserved gap is not charged at all, because missing observations do not prove continued separation. `EstimatedReturnTicks` is the larger of the straight line and the *priced* route home, and the route is priced to the plain tile at the region's centre rather than to the player's feet, because feet floor onto the solid floor row that no flood of free space ever holds — priced to that, the estimate was null and the straight line silently won every tick, in play as well as in the fixture. `RegroupUrgency` reads the gap beyond the region: zero inside it whatever the player is doing and whatever a route round a thin wall would cost, and outside it the larger of a separation term — the gap plus how far his departure will add over the free return time, against the full regroup distance — and a travel term from the estimated return and any stall, ramped in over a region's half-size beyond the edge so a companion just outside with a long estimate is not treated as lost. Only that third value is read by anything that decides: `KeepCompany.CalculateReunionValue` raises rejoining with it. The delay cost is written by the recorder and read by nobody.

The moving-away component consumes the observation layer's confidence-weighted travel estimate rather than instantaneous velocity, so repeated local motion cannot imply a sustained departing journey because its current leg points away.

**This is a separate class rather than a method on whatever decides, and the reason is the ten hours on 21 September 2026 when it was not.** All of it but the apart-tick count lived inside `Chooser.Choose`. `CoordinateBrainTick` called the apart-tick observation directly, so the count kept running; `Reunion.Evaluate`, the return estimate and `RegroupUrgency` were reachable only through `Choose`, which `0bb2c8a` took off the tick at 08:21. So the observation ran and the evaluation did not — the recorder wrote one live number beside two frozen defaults while `KeepCompany` read a regroup urgency of exactly zero — until `4e7d094` diagnosed it at 18:42 the same day and called the observation from the tick. Nothing went red for those ten hours, because a frozen float is a legal float, and this file asserted the opposite in a sentence nobody could falsify by reading it.

**`AIC-419` the next day relocated this out of the deleted class and revived nothing**, which is worth saying plainly because the deletion is the conspicuous commit and the fix is not: every one of these numbers was already live at the branch point, and a reader crediting the extraction with reviving them would date the defect a day late and miss that the row guarding it is older than the extraction. The general property is `4e7d094`'s rather than the extraction's: **a decision procedure that also observes strands its observations when something replaces the decision**, and the check for it asks what the old procedure *wrote*, not only what it returned. `VerifyTheCourseOwnsTheTick`'s companionship row is that check, it arrived with `4e7d094`, and it survives the move proved by mutation — removing the tick's call to `Companionship.Observe` reddens it with `a body far from the player still estimates no time to return; ticks=0`.

## A bound course step is translated, never executed a second time

`ExecuteCourseBinding` maps one published `StepBinding` onto the two things this tick already carries out: the activity that performs the purpose, and the `PositionRequest` that says where the body is asked to be. It is a pure translation on purpose. The course owner decides *what* to do; the activities below still perform it, the positioner still resolves the point, and the motor is still the only writer to the live NPC — a parallel execution path for course work would give the companion two ways to mine and no single answer about what it is doing, which is the post-grant second chooser the plan names for deletion arriving by the back door.

**Who performs a domain is read from the activities, since `04f9df2` on 23 September 2026.** Each activity declares the domains it performs in `CompanionAction.CourseDomains`, and the map is built from `RegisterActivities.All()`, throwing if two activities declare one domain. It used to be a table written out here, and a written table is how pots came to be discovered and bound with no performer. The vocabularies do differ, which is why the map exists at all — combat's domain is `combat` with the purpose `fire`, lighting's is `light-target` with the activity `place-torches` — but the activity is the one place that says what it performs. An undeclared domain throws in `ActivityFor` and is refused before enumeration by the search, since a silent default would route new work to whichever activity sorted first. A pot is collection work since 23 September 2026 — `collect-target` with the purpose `break-pot` — so collection performs a bound pot like a bound drop; one in reach is also broken in passing, through a one-step binding the course accepts (`DecideCourseEachTick.AcceptIncidental`, which reads optional work's relevance from the same comparison-episode inputs a decision does).

A firing stand keeps `RequestKind.FireFrom` rather than becoming an Exact hover at the same point, because the combat stance's own admission and its rock fallback live behind that kind. Tile work carries its work tile so the positioner applies the tool-reach proof that admitted the pose; a drop does not, because pickup is by contact and naming a tile would demand a proof nothing needs.

**That work tile is read from the opportunity's own identity and never from the pose**, in the two shapes the tree mints — `tile:x,y` from the light and pot captures, `tile:material:x,y` from gathering, whose material makes a replaced tile a different opportunity — and an unrecognised shape throws rather than guessing. The pose is where the *body* goes, which for every tile domain is a hover beside the work and therefore air: deriving the tile from it declared a tool-stand success region over nothing, measured on the mining evidence scene as a recorded work tile of 24,59 with `has-tile=False` where the scene's only copper was 25,59. A work site and a body destination are two different quantities, and a field carrying one cannot be asked for the other — the same confusion the torch census had, one layer further on. An empty course asks `WithPlayer`, never Hold, so "the course found nothing worth doing" cannot look identical to "the course told the body to freeze".

**A step the performing hand refuses reaches the course, since 23 September 2026, and is not ordered again until something the hand's check reads has changed.** Every hand revalidates its bound target against the live world at the native call and refuses by name without substituting, but until that date the refusal stopped at the attempt record: the course's own next-use check reads the frozen observation, so a target the observation still described as workable was bound again on the next tick and refused again — 111 invalid attempts in 115 ticks on the lane B review's silent-edit scene. An activity now sets `CompanionAction.StepRefusal` through `RefuseStep`, the tick hands it to `DecideCourseEachTick.PerformerRefused`, and the course is released, a decision in flight is dropped because it may be about to publish the same step, and the opportunity is withheld from the search. **What ends the withholding is chosen, not the census fact's revision, and that was measured wrong first**: an ore fact carries a stand computed from the body's position, so a released body drifting republished the vein every few ticks and a hand refusing every step was handed it 13 times in 120. It holds until an announced edit lands within tool reach of the target, the companion's gear, cargo or the player's policies change (read by an observer of its own, because the decision's observer feeds the model owner's travel invalidation), or `Weights.NearbyWorkNoReturnRetryTicks` passes, the wait every proven site refusal in this tree already keeps and the backstop for a change nothing announces. Every production refusal mirrors a census check on purpose, so the channel is the backstop for drift between two copies of one ladder rather than a path any scene exercises today; `Tools/EngineReplay/Gathering/VerifyGatheringActsOnTheBinding.cs` reaches it through the real tick with a stand-in miner that refuses everything — handed once in 120 ticks, 120 times with the tick's forwarding removed — and requires the vein ordered again once an announced edit grows it. Combat does not set it: its activation refusals mean the binding names an older plan, which the next-use check already retires, not that the target cannot be fought.

`Tools/EngineReplay/DecisionMaking/VerifyCourseBindingExecution.cs` holds it, and its load-bearing row checks every purpose the five sources mint against the activity names `RegisterActivities` actually registers — because a purpose with no executor is not an error anyone would see: the course would bind the work, publish it, and nothing would happen, which reads in play as a companion that decided to do something and then stood there.
