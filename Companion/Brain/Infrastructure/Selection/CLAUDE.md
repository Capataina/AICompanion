# Behaviour selection — a course decides, and the utility scorer beside it is retired but compiled

**`DecideCourseEachTick.cs` is what the brain tick calls**, since `0bb2c8a` on 21 September 2026. It owns the whole decision: freeze one observation through `AssembleCourseSnapshot`, discover across all six domains through `DiscoverOpportunities`, search bounded orders through `SearchCourseOrders` priced by `ForecastCourseConsequences`, and publish through `RetainCourse` a course whose next step the tick carries out via `ExecuteCourseBinding`. A retained course whose next use still validates is carried with no search at all — that is the retention the design is named for, and it is checked against a freshly frozen observation every tick because the question is whether the next native call would succeed *now*.

Three shapes in it exist because their absence was measured. A decision is frozen for its own life rather than for one tick, since a travel query can cost more than a tick's leftover allowance and re-observing every tick discarded and restarted one for ever. Publication reads the model-extended snapshot, because answers are appended as they complete and `ReadyToPublish` refuses a proposal naming a fact its snapshot lacks. And a course with no steps is released as finished, because an empty order is a legal winning course that a next-step check never sees.

What it deliberately does not do: **opportunistic replacement mid-course**. `RetainCourse.Consider` requires the incumbent reprojected from the current observation before two futures can be compared, and that reprojection is unbuilt, so a valid course is kept and no rival is weighed against it. The companion finishes what it started and picks again at the end.

## The objective cannot express "defend him", because no course prices harm to the player

**Every course in existence prices the player's harm at exactly zero, so no comparison between two courses can ever prefer the one that defends him.** `ForecastCourseConsequences` hands `ForecastContactHarm` a companion actor and no player actor at all: the player's contact geometry is passed as an explicit unsupported empty, because nothing models his path. This is deliberate and correct as far as it goes — an empty *supported* geometry would read as "the player is never touched", which is the lie — but the consequence is that the one quantity that would make a threat on the player worth anything contributes nothing to any candidate.

Behind it sits a second hole, and either alone is enough to keep the behaviour unreachable: `ForecastContactHarm` does not apply projected enemy kills, so a course that kills the zombie and a course that mines beside it price the *same* future player harm. Both must land before a threatened player can lift combat off a vein through the objective rather than through a multiplier.

The measurement, on `danger lifts combat over work` at 0.33.2, with a zombie beside a player at 40 life and ten real decisions inside the 120 ticks:

```
mine-target  0.1774   useful 0.1774  harm 0.0000  gap 0.0000   ← already × 0.243 for urgency
combat       0.0020   useful 0.0020  harm 0.0000  gap 0.0000
(idle)       0.0000
```

`harm 0.0000` on every row with no `harm-uncertain` entry beside it means `course.Harm` was *empty* rather than unresolved — no hits predicted for anybody, not hits that could not be priced. That distinction is the whole finding and it is invisible without the unknowns printed beside the total.

**Three scoring hypotheses were tested against this row and all three were wrong, so they are recorded as eliminations rather than left to be retried.** Combat does not score below mining because of a missing bonus — a derived-from-nothing constant moved the outcome by nothing. Combat orders are not refused during projection — the refusals are twelve `native-target-unresolved` and eight `assistance-target-unresolved`, none of them combat's. Combat is not filtered out before enumeration by being admitted `Unresolved` — admission reads `combat:1ok/0?/0x`. A fourth reading, that no comparison happens at all because the course is retained, was contradicted by the per-tick decision tally: `course-retained` on 110 ticks and `course-published` on 10, so ten real searches ran at urgency 0.757 with combat admitted and mining won every one.

The property that survives all four, and the one to check before anyone writes a fifth patch here: **a preference the objective has no term for cannot be recovered by tuning the terms it does have.** `CourseComparisonEpisode.RelevanceFor` is the multiplier currently standing in for the missing quantity, and its own body carries the condition under which it is deleted.

**Everything below this line describes the family chooser, which is compiled and no longer on the tick's path.** It survives for the activity list it holds and for `OwnCurrentActivity`, and the plan's migration table retires the rest of it once the course brain has been played — kept until then so a defect found in play can still be attributed to one brain or the other. Thirteen behaviour rows that held under it do not hold under the course, and `AIC-437` adjudicates them one at a time; until that closes, the measurements in this file describe a decision procedure the game no longer runs.

`ChooseBehaviour.cs` prepares every activity and evaluates its captured value through the shared factor machinery. Gathering, Combat and NearbyAssistance each nominate their highest positive-value child; the parent compares those concrete nominations without another weight or commitment bonus. `LastScores` and `LastNominations` retain the child ledger and family results for diagnostics. Selection returns the chosen executor; that executor supplies a PositionRequest to position selection. The chooser neither selects a tile nor controls the body.

**Hunting outranks work by being eligible, never by outscoring it, so a discount of hunt against work would change nothing.** Across the 2026-09-11 session, mining won 1,368 of the 1,734 ticks where it had a live target and torch placement won the other 366; hunting won none of them. Hunting reached 32.2% of that session because it was eligible three-quarters of the time and everything better was absent. The lever is therefore whatever makes useful work *eligible* — an ore whose approach the search could not decide, a target admitted with no way to shoot it — and never the distance between two ceilings. Lowering hunt's constant buys the appearance of a priority ruling and restores the behaviour in the next crowded cave, which is why these measurements are recorded here instead.

Hunting seeks an attack opportunity. Useful investigation belongs to lighting, collecting or other concrete work, while idle local movement belongs to keeping company in the agreed final structure. Hunting does not require an independent exploration purpose to justify its existence.

Regroup time uses available route-step durations and the positioner's observed travel estimate as well as straight-line distance. Excursions are discounted by protection urgency, and that is the only place the player's danger reaches optional work: urgency already compares each threat's arrival with the companion's intervention time, so an activity that also multiplied its own raw value by player danger charged one threat twice and could not tell a shot it can take from across the room from one it cannot. Raw values read the companion's own danger where it matters to that activity (hunting's own-skin term) and never the player's. `VerifyCompanionActivities` holds the four matched scenes — nobody, the player, the companion, both threatened — that pin this, with a real opportunity for every excursion in each scene so no raw value can hide a danger term behind a zero. Their own acquisition and continuation bounds determine whether distance permits the job. Charging them again for leaving the short follow-comfort band would defeat that permission. Interruptible work pays the time until it can yield, rather than the duration of its entire job, against the threat horizon. Unknown intervention is conservative; the companion remains autonomous while the player is dead, with player-protection pressure removed.

A boss fight or world event reaches optional work through the same protection factor and nowhere else. `ActivityComparisonContext.EncounterIntensity` carries the observation's intensity, and a non-combat excursion — mining, chopping, collecting, lighting — pays `1 − max(urgency, intensity)`, while a candidate marked `ServesEncounter` (the chooser marks the Combat family) pays urgency alone. The maximum rather than the product is the point: a boss is also a threat that raises urgency and crowd pressure is the threats themselves, so multiplying the two would charge one danger twice. Keeping company is not an excursion and is untouched, which is deliberate rather than an omission, because nomination selects only positive values and zeroing every non-combat child would leave a quiet blood-moon lull with no activity at all; the README's wider encounter leash belongs to position and movement, not to this factor. Stranded companions pay neither term. `VerifyEncounterContext` pins the arithmetic and a live mining scene that stops only when the event reaches the player.

## A job is worth what it delivers per time, and close jobs are compared as orders

**Every job somewhere pays for the time it takes, travel included, and a job nearly done is worth nearly all of its value.** Beside the protection, commitment, horizon and reunion factors, a task candidate — one with a target that is neither following nor serving the player directly — is multiplied by `window / (window + ticks until done)`, the window in `BehaviourWeights.cs`. That one term is what the owner asked for on 15 September 2026 as "commitment that grows as the job nears done": an enemy at a fifth of its life has a short kill time left and so scores higher than the same enemy fresh, with no bonus for having started, and a long job is never scored at zero for being long. The ticks come from `CompanionAction.TaskTicks`, which defaults to the activity's forecast and which the combat stance extends with an optimistic kill time on its hunt side, because the hunt forecast deliberately counts only the flight the threat horizon was tuned against. Keeping company and the guard side declare `ServesPlayerDirectly` and pay none of this, because their worth is where the player is.

**A trip job that reports no trip is scored as instant, and that is the defect the time term was built on.** The lighting activity inherited a zero forecast from the base class, so a torch trip of 218 ticks outbid a slime hunt that honestly reported its flight and was discounted for it; the nearby-work base now reports its own trip, and `VerifyPreparedActivities` refuses any excursion that inherits the zero rather than trusting the next activity to remember.

**A job somewhere pays for keeping the companion apart whether or not it is an excursion, and it pays once.** A hunt that can shoot from where the orb hovers is not an excursion for the threat horizon, and it used to pay no separation either, so on 15 September 2026 it held the orb over a surface zombie while the player dropped four hundred pixels into a cave. The separation is `Chooser.Separation`: one minus keeping company's own slope, uncapped, read at the job's stand against the player's region carried along his observed travel for the job's duration, at a distance that is the gap beyond the region plus the route home's detour. The detour (`Chooser.RouteDetour`) is how much longer the reach flood's way from the stand to the player runs than the straight line: the flood is rooted at the body, so its costs to the player and to the stand are two distances from one point and their difference, taken either way round, bounds the route between them from below — taken one way only it read zero with the body beside the player, which is where a job is chosen from while the companion keeps him company; `VerifyRouteHomeFromEitherEnd` reads it from both ends, where the two readings differ by the flood's directional pricing (each edge is priced by the clearance at its far corner, so the way out and the way back through one corridor cost differently) and by nothing else — and a mining stand, being a solid ore tile, is priced at the nearest corner the orb fits at, or from the body when the flood holds no such corner. So a stand inside the region pays nothing whatever its route, a stand beyond it pays by the gap and the detour, a stand the player's travel will leave at fly-home distance leaves the job worth nothing, and a player leaving makes a long job pay more than a quick one. **Neither an unknown route nor an absent one reads as free.** While the flood has not reached the player the detour is zero and the cost is the straight-line gap, a floor the real route can only exceed; a player's tile a finished flood proves absent reads a detour of twice the straight line, which is the disc's own guarantee that no route to a place it proved absent is shorter than about three times its straight line. The owner ruled on 15 September 2026 that the one cost reads the route home; with the straight line, a mine on a near and a far way up was worth the same (0.453 and 0.453 at 1.5 px a tick), and with the detour 0.255 against 0.116. The flood prices clearance, so even a direct way home reads some detour, which is why the near route's worth fell too. **The cap is rejoining's and not the job's, and capping both was built and measured wrong.** With the separation capped at half, the surface-zombie scene built from the live region geometry kept the hunt at 0.198 against rejoining's 0.076 once the player had dropped four hundred pixels — rejoining's 0.38 cut to a fifth by the discount on following while useful work exists — so a fight up where the player used to be still won. The two rulings are about different things: rejoining never on its own outweighs a job genuinely worth doing is about what rejoining is worth, and the pull taking all of the companion's attention at the distance where it would lose him is about how far is far, so a job reads the slope to its end. The stand is the site for an excursion and the body for a job done from where it hovers. It replaced a reunion factor priced from a per-tick delay cost, and the replacement is a consolidation rather than a retuning: the delay cost and the rectangle were two separation costs, and the delay cost measured the wrong thing — on that play a slime hunt beside an idle player lost 0.36 raw to 0.02 final because a long return estimate was charged at a departure speed of a tenth of a pixel. `AssessReunionCost` still computes it because the recorder writes its three columns; nothing decides on it. `PreparedActivity.PlayerFit` is the work allowance carried forward the same way, and a job the player's heading takes beyond the started-job radius is worth nothing, which is policy — the radius is a preference — rather than a second separation.

**A fight that still serves him is not leaving him.** `ServesPlayerDirectly` keeps reunion at 1 and skips the excursion protection discount, even when the path to the stand is longer than a local trip. Capture `2026-09-18_16-35-26-353` dumped combat at raw 1.02 to final 0.00 on the next tick because a 10-tick stand became a 227-tick FireFrom and reunion multiplied the fight to nothing; company 0.05 took the body. The surface-zombie drop is still `ServesPlayerDirectly` false, so it still zeros once he has gone. `VerifyPreparedActivities` holds both.

A second mechanism for the zombie was built and torn out. The same capture shows the discount on following during useful work turning a reunion worth 0.80 into 0.16, so the discount was lifted, first whenever the companion was outside the player's region and then whenever he was also travelling away. Both went red on `VerifyOreWork`'s proven-job row, whose scene is a player walking away from ore thirty tiles off — the small job on the walk that Expected Behaviour's 4:00 scene asks the companion to take — so the discount is unconditional and the separation alone makes the zombie hunt lose once the player has gone.

**The flat floor keeping company offers inside the region, and the time term, were both judged against the capture and left as they are.** On the rows where a hunt lost to that floor the target was an off-screen zombie shot from where the orb hovered: raw 0.11 is the off-screen nearness floor on hunting's worth, and the recorded factor list in the capture's events names the cutters as the threat horizon at 0.50 and the delay-cost reunion factor at 0.69, with the time term the smaller third at about 0.66 — a far target losing to doing nothing, which is correct. The slime hunts that lost beside an idle player lost to keeping company's rejoin value at the old full cap, to the delay charge, or because the hunt's own stand query found no firing position; none of those is the floor or the time term, and the first two are what this folder and keeping company changed.

**Close jobs are compared as orders, and ordering decides which of them runs, never whether one of them runs.** `OrderNearbyTasks` takes every task within a share of the best task's score, tries every order of them, and scores an order by the worth each job delivers and when, the first job at its own task time and each later one after the straight flight from where the previous job happened. The first job of the best order takes the best single task score and the rest are placed below it, so keeping company and protection are compared against exactly what they were compared against before. The objective is worth delivered early rather than the least total time, because every order does the same work and the least total time cannot tell killing the slime on the way from killing it on the way back. Only the first step runs; the order is worked out again every comparison and nothing is kept. There is deliberately no separate switching margin: flying toward the chosen job shortens its time and lengthens the others', and the incumbent's commitment is already inside its worth, which the fixture's two-even-jobs row holds by counting lead changes while the body flies. Travel between jobs is a straight line over the body's pace, so an order through terrain is scored a little optimistically. The recorder writes the chosen order and the best order that starts differently as `task_order` and `task_order_runner_up`. Capture `2026-09-18_18-57-09-481` is the live negative: `task_order` read `collect>place-torches` while the body sat between the two Exact destinations swapping every fifteen ticks, because collect raw collapsed and the order is discarded. The unfinished discussion of keeping the order, freezing leftover with identity, and applying the same comparison inside combat and on the way home is `research/Historical Evidence/Archived Brain Proposals/04 Plan a Course of Action and Follow It.md`; nothing of it is built, it is not an implementation plan, and keep-the-job membership on collect and torch is not that discussion.

## Preparation is scheduled per family, so one expensive family cannot spend the tick

`ScheduleOpportunityQueries` decides which activities prepare in a comparison. Families run in enum order; inside each, the incumbent and every non-excursion child (protection and keeping company) always prepare, then optional children prepare in a rotating order. The first optional child always runs, and each optional child runs inside a `LimitPlanningWork.Narrow` deadline set to what remains of the family's share (defined in `BehaviourWeights.cs`); further optional children run only while that share is unspent. Because every nested route, reach and approach query reads the shared deadline, the share bounds an optional child's whole preparation, not just whether its siblings start. Mandatory children deliberately run under the tick's total allowance instead: narrowing the incumbent would turn an approach the body is already walking into the same undecided answer every tick. That was acute while tool-approach queries were fresh bounded A* searches per candidate pose; they read the reach sense now, so the undecided answer comes from an unsettled flood rather than a spent budget, and the reason to leave the incumbent unnarrowed is unchanged. A skipped child is written to the board as `Deferred` with zero value and the reason `family-preparation-allowance-spent`; its retained discovery is neither compared nor allowed to win. The family's next comparison starts from the first child it deferred, so a family of n optional children prepares each at least once every n comparisons. `Queries.LastFamilies` carries prepared and deferred counts and milliseconds per family for the recorder and harness.

This is scheduling, not preference: deferral never lowers a prepared value, and protection, keeping company and the running activity cannot be starved by it. The share exists so optional siblings cannot pile their own searches onto a tick: the brain-cost scene measured a single incumbent mining approach recomputation spending the whole total planning allowance, and anything prepared after it on that tick would have found nothing left. That incumbent cost was a property of the approach query itself, not of scheduling, and it was fixed there twice: the tool-access query asks nearest poses first and stops at the first reachable one, which brought the same scene's worst decide tick to about 4 ms, and it then stopped searching for routes at all and reads the reach sense instead. With `LimitPlanningWork.Unbounded` set nothing is deferred, which keeps offline fixtures deterministic; a fixture that tests the rotation sets the chooser's share to zero, which deterministically prepares exactly one optional child per family.

## The urgency ladder, and why an action topping out at one can never interrupt anything

Candidate preparation and shared comparison are separate calls. The chooser invokes each adapter's Prepare once, then reads its captured raw value, forecast and classification through context-free methods. `EvaluatePreparedActivities` accepts only immutable scalar records, reads no live activity or world object and never invokes discovery. It returns all factors and an explicit error for invalid or non-finite values. Positive-infinite threat horizon means no observed deadline. Repeated or reordered evaluation preserves each candidate's value. Preparation still owns mutable discovery caches; execution ownership is handled separately below.

Incumbent commitment is deliberately independent of the shared body-stall flag. A replacement activity would otherwise inherit its predecessor's physical failure before attempting any movement. Activity-owned remaining effort and outcomes must replace that coarse commitment; body-level stalling alone cannot decide that a job was a bad choice.

Prepare is required for every adapter rather than an optional hook. Score and ForecastTicks receive no world context. All ordinary candidates obey that boundary. Keeping company combines rejoining the player's region and moving about inside it in one candidate. Avoiding a hit is movement's evade layer, which is neither a candidate nor a takeover, and nothing takes the body to escape a liquid, because every liquid is air to it.

Zero and invalid candidates produce no family nomination. If all families are empty, selection returns no activity and releases the former one; the coordinator holds ordinary movement while independent hands and the earlier recovery and downed paths retain their existing contracts. Positive candidates use one tie key at both levels: their stable registration index. Group order and duplicated identical candidates cannot change the result. The flat selector exists only as a test reference.

Each prepared candidate also captures its target binding. Before activation, the chooser verifies that the adapter still names that identity, that an NPC remains alive/active in the same spawn generation, or that an item still has quantity and retains its type. A rejected candidate keeps its original raw value and named rejection in the ledger but loses selectable value. Families nominate again from the remaining prepared board without discovery. Shared opportunity costs are recomputed from captured values so a rejected work offer cannot keep suppressing companionship. Each rejection removes one candidate, bounding reconsideration by the board size.

Item activation also verifies that the captured object still occupies its world slot, naming a replaced slot separately from exhausted quantity or a changed type. Same-type replacement cannot inherit the old offer. This binding check does not establish native tile permission, a useful arrival pose, or a new generation when an arbitrary mod mutates the same item object in place without a lifecycle signal. Those remain the activity-specific offer and native interaction contracts; non-entity identities currently use the adapter's existing equality semantics. It also does not make an unresolved route proven merely because its target still exists.

Before comparing the captured identity, activation also reads the activity's side-effect-free prepared-target rejection. Tile work uses this to distinguish a vanished tile from changed material, preserving the original raw value and letting families nominate another prepared candidate. Coordinate/material binding cannot detect an unobserved same-material replacement and does not certify tool permission or access.

Gathering's prepared validation also rejects a disabled work policy. This is live permission evidence, separate from the previously captured utility value: an unchanged ore or tree cannot keep an offer selectable after its work permission is revoked. Native mutation permission and execution cleanup remain with the activity and tool.

After identity validation, a nominated attack activity exposes its captured PositionRequest for lazy method admission through the existing positioner. **That query answers in three values, not two, and the chooser has to keep them apart.** A destination makes the offer `Usable`. No destination is `KnownUnusable` only when the reason says the search answered; where the reason says the bounded search was *cut* — the solve count or the millisecond budget ended it with candidates still unasked — the offer is `Unresolved` instead. Either way the candidate loses this comparison and is re-nominated from, with a method-specific reason, keeping its raw desirability and tested alternatives; what the third value buys is that an unresolved activity may still carry a positive raw value into a later comparison without that being a defect, where a known-unusable one may not. Reading a cut as an answer is what let a hunt be vetoed by its own solve count and the companion flip to keeping company on the tick the budget expired. `../Position/CLAUDE.md` owns why the search reports a cut apart from an answer and how it makes an unfinished search able to finish. The families nominate again from the prepared board; this does not rerun opportunity discovery or lower the perceived threat. Failed method admission is bounded evidence, never a permanent unreachable judgement. A later comparison can refine the method again or admit an opened shot. The score ledger retains method evidence for accepted and rejected queries; candidates never queried have no invented method evidence.

Mine/chop belong to Gathering; the one fighting stance belongs to Combat; collection, torches and keeping company belong to NearbyAssistance. Keeping company prepares the greater value of rejoining, capped and zero inside the region, and its wander floor, then pays the shared useful-work discount once, while which method runs follows where the body is; those methods do not receive separate incumbent bonuses or activity identities. Collection compares its known-drop and potential-content offers using the same ComparisonContext and evaluator, then publishes the chosen raw offer for parent evaluation. The parent does not multiply an already-discounted method score. Movement's evade layer bends every one of them away from a predicted hit without being nominated. Richer unresolved-method eligibility remains separate offer-contract work.

`OwnCurrentActivity` is the sole holder of the current executor. The chooser delegates activation to it rather than maintaining another current-action field. The owner identifies consecutive purposes using the adapter's activity identity and the NPC spawn generation where that identity is an NPC. Other adapter identities retain their existing value/reference semantics; this does not certify item-slot generation or full offer revalidation. Selecting a different purpose changes the activity ID even when the executor is unchanged. A suspended purpose keeps its ID if freshly selected again; replacement ends it without calling its physical release twice. The owner retains only the current purpose and the last termination summary, not a stack of intentions.

The owner also separates purpose from attempt. Each continuous stretch of execution opens one attempt with its own identity when execution begins and calls the activity's `BeginAttempt`; reselecting the executing purpose keeps it open, suspension closes it as Interrupted with the suspension reason, and replacement or an empty selection closes it with the replaced activity's own `ConcludeAttempt` before Exit runs. Attempt identities come from one process-wide counter rather than one per owner, so a respawned companion's new brain cannot reuse identities a recorder cursor has already written this session. Resuming a suspended purpose therefore keeps the activity identity and opens a fresh attempt with no inherited effects. Productive effects are credited only through `Chooser.RecordWork`, which every caller invokes for an observed productive native effect, and only while an attempt is open, so an effect outside execution belongs to nobody. A bounded list of recent outcomes survives so two conclusions inside one tick — a replacement then a recovery interruption — both reach the recorder. Attempt identity is not a route attempt: the navigator keeps its own.

Prepared candidates carry the activity's offer eligibility into evaluation, and the evaluator returns `value-without-eligible-offer` for positive value beside anything but a usable or unresolved offer. Method admission refines the ledger row it queries: an established destination makes the offer usable for that comparison, an answered absence makes it known-unusable with the method reason, a cut search makes it unresolved, and none of the three blacklists the target for later comparisons.

Repeated selection of the same executing purpose does not create a lifecycle transition. The transient selection stage inside a tick must not refresh its changed-at timestamp or flood the sparse recorder with fictional transitions. Comparison freshness remains separately measurable.

The activity owner also delivers post-control outcome observations only while the ordinary activity is executing. Recovery flight can keep observing body progress while that activity is suspended; the resulting elapsed ticks must not consume its failure budget. Resumption restores ordinary observation, so an interrupted unproductive pursuit can still expire after it actually gets control back.

Recovery and downing explicitly suspend through this owner. Suspension invokes the adapter's release hook once and preserves the remaining purpose's admission identity; reselection prepares opportunities again and re-enters before execution. Moving to another executor releases the previous admission. The legacy discovery caches still have their own retained target data and are not an independent execution owner. A suspension reason is not a failed route or proof that choosing the work was wrong.

Completed comparisons carry an increasing evaluation identity and their engine tick. The coordinator separately records whether selection ran in its current invocation: an early recovery or downed return can update the brain without refreshing the choice. Retained scores keep their original identity and source time. These comparison identities describe decisions, not ongoing job identity or route attempts.

An attack-method query records an occurrence against the upcoming comparison identity before activation. This preserves rejected alternatives even when the chosen activity stays unchanged between periodic decision snapshots. Its pre-activation phase must not be interpreted as proof that the comparison completed or the activity executed.

Each retained score carries every multiplier its final carries — protection, incumbent commitment, horizon, reunion, the time a job takes, the player's fit, useful work, and the rescale the ordering of close jobs applies after the product — so the recorded factors multiply to the recorded final, which `Tools/EngineReplay/Assistance/VerifyCandidateFunnel.cs` asserts for a task both on the retained score and on the factor list as written. Sparse decision events expose those factors beside raw and final utility; recording consumes the evaluated factors rather than preparing another board, which could acquire or release jobs.

**A distance this file measures is to the player's intent region, and which point of it depends on the question.** The region is a sense (`../Observation/ObservePlayerIntentRegion.cs`, which owns why it exists, how it leads and why the player sits in its bottom third). A radius — the active-activity allowance, `PlayerFit`'s projection — measures to the region's `Heading`, his centre carried by the lead, because a radius anchored where he stands shrinks the ground in front of him as he walks into it, and one anchored on the box's centre would lean a third of the box upward. A separation — the regroup urgency, a job's separation share — measures to the rectangle, on the gap beyond its edge, because the owner ruled every pull is measured from the region and is zero anywhere inside it. The route-home estimate prices the flight to the box's centre, where the companion lives.

Regrouping raises keeping company's rejoin value rather than introducing a behaviour. `CalculateRegroupUrgency` reads the gap beyond the region: zero inside it whatever the player is doing and whatever a route round a thin wall would cost, and outside it the larger of a separation term — the gap plus how far the player's departure will add over the free return time, against the full regroup distance — and a travel term from the estimated return and any stall, ramped in over a region's half-size beyond the edge so a companion just outside with a long estimate is not treated as lost. A viable targeted excursion discounts ordinary companionship inside its permitted activity envelope; protection is unaffected. `IsExcursion` defaults on, with protection and keeping company opting out. `RegroupUrgency` and `EstimatedReturnTicks` remain available to diagnostics.

The moving-away component consumes the observation layer's confidence-weighted travel estimate rather than instantaneous velocity. Repeated local motion therefore cannot imply a sustained departing journey solely because its current leg points away.

`AssessReunionCost` still observes separation after senses, even when recovery skips ordinary comparison, and still evaluates a per-tick delay cost from observed departure, return duration and accumulated separation. Nothing decides on that cost any more; the recorder writes it, with its departure and apart-tick inputs, and the columns are kept because a recorder's columns are only ever appended. A reader of those columns is reading a diagnostic, not a factor.

The selected job earns continuation by identity on every winning tick, including consecutive targets inside one behaviour. A moving enemy or drop keeps its identity as its position changes; a different entity must pass acquisition again. Mining retains a vein id, chopping a trunk coordinate and material, and small world interactions a target tile. Successful work also records a short-lived collection site so the resulting drops can be gathered under the same ongoing allowance. The shared bounds check both the companion and its target against the player.

An ordinary action scores a product of considerations in 0..1, and the chooser multiplies the incumbent by `Weights.Commitment`. So a running action does not sit at its own score, it sits above it, and an action that has to *interrupt* one has to clear that product rather than merely reach the top of the band. An action whose raw expression cannot exceed 1 therefore loses to a committed ordinary action by construction, on every tick, at every input — not narrowly and not on a weight. Guard was that action, which is why a following companion stood holding a torch with the player's danger reading full, and why turning the danger term up was never going to move it.

`Weights.GuardUrgency` can clear a committed ordinary action, including its incumbent bonus. Its value lives in `BehaviourWeights.cs`. New ordinary activities still use comparable current value rather than a fixed ordering among family names.

`Tools/EngineReplay/Movement/VerifyFollowRecoveryAndProtection.cs` exercises immediate guard entry against committed following and continued protection through a small retreat. `VerifyLiquidsAreAir.cs` beside it holds that no liquid starts any response. Those checks establish their inputs rather than every combination of threat and action factors.

Combat, gathering and nearby-assistance activities live in their respective PurposeFamilies folders. Each explicitly declares its purpose family. A new activity must supply that declaration and a preparation implementation; folder names alone do not determine selection. The data flow is observation → preparation → shared evaluation → family nominations → parent choice → activity execution → position selection → shared movement → motor. Movement outcomes return as observed facts rather than a lower stage changing the parent's selected purpose.
## A bound course step is translated, never executed a second time

`ExecuteCourseBinding` maps one published `StepBinding` onto the two things this tick already carries out: the activity that performs the purpose, and the `PositionRequest` that says where the body is asked to be. It is a pure translation on purpose. The course owner decides *what* to do; the activities below still perform it, the positioner still resolves the point, and the motor is still the only writer to the live NPC — a parallel execution path for course work would give the companion two ways to mine and no single answer about what it is doing, which is the post-grant second chooser the plan names for deletion arriving by the back door.

The purpose-to-activity mapping is written out rather than derived from the domain string, because the two vocabularies genuinely differ and each difference is a decision: combat mints opportunities per *use* with the purpose `fire` while its activity is `combat`, and lighting's domain is `light-target` while its activity is `place-torches`. An unmapped purpose throws instead of falling back, since a silent default would route new work to whichever activity sorted first. A pot names no activity at all and returns empty — it is broken in passing by whatever activity is already travelling, through the shared nearby-work adapter — and a caller reads that as "no activity change" rather than a refusal.

A firing stand keeps `RequestKind.FireFrom` rather than becoming an Exact hover at the same point, because the combat stance's own admission and its rock fallback live behind that kind. Tile work carries its work tile so the positioner applies the tool-reach proof that admitted the pose; a drop does not, because pickup is by contact and naming a tile would demand a proof nothing needs. An empty course asks `WithPlayer`, never Hold, so "the course found nothing worth doing" cannot look identical to "the course told the body to freeze".

`Tools/EngineReplay/DecisionMaking/VerifyCourseBindingExecution.cs` holds it, and its load-bearing row checks every purpose the five sources mint against the activity names the chooser actually registers — because a purpose with no executor is not an error anyone would see: the course would bind the work, publish it, and nothing would happen, which reads in play as a companion that decided to do something and then stood there.

# Planned course ownership

The [selected implementation plan](<../../../../research/proposal/Implement the Retained Course Brain.md>) owns the future replacement of family nomination, discarded local ordering and private commitment rules. Selection will contain Opportunities, Courses and Computation with one owner of the current course; `OwnCurrentActivity` keeps the sole live attempt lifecycle. The central outcome policy and its contrasting fixtures decide relative work; producers report facts, and no domain installs a private exchange rate. This is planned work, not a description of the current implementation below.
