# Torches — held light and permanent lighting

```
Torch/
├─ CLAUDE.md
├─ TorchBearer.cs             decides lit and shown, then drives held item, light and map reveal
├─ RecommendTorchPlacement.cs Smart Cursor's own torch step, asked about one tile, about the tile his cursor would offer, and through a cheap attachment filter
├─ PlaceTorches.cs            native placement of the companion's own torches: the handed kind chosen, nothing used up, the edit announced
└─ RememberCompanionTorches.cs the world's memory of which torches are the companion's and which spots the player cleared, saved with the world
```

`Lit` comes from the light field asked twice: how much of the measured open air reads dark around the body, and how much does at the player's predicted feet. Either above the raise share lights the torch; both below the lower share puts it out.

The two halves treat an unread neighbourhood differently, and that asymmetry is the rule rather than an oversight. Raising asks whether anybody saw dark air, so one measured dark answer carries it and a silent one is skipped. Lowering asks whether there is no dark air anywhere the companion is about to be, which nobody can answer about a place nobody read, so a silent query blocks it. Letting a silent query merely abstain is the same defect as reading its share as a zero, one step further out: a companion deep in an unlit cave the engine has not computed, beside a player whose predicted feet are out in daylight, has one bright answer and one silence, and every rule that lets the bright one carry alone puts the torch out in the dark. The cost of erring the other way is a torch held a few seconds in a room already lit. The heading half is what makes the README's 6:00 scene work — torch up while crossing a bright surface toward a dark hole, because the dark is ahead rather than underfoot — and a mean brightness could express neither half, since one number for a whole window is wrong about both the lit room and the dark wing beside it. Hysteresis and a minimum hold still damp it, and the field reads the world's own light — the engine's scan of the tiles, taken before its blur merges anything a body carries — so the reading cannot be made by the thing it decides, nor by a light that walks away. The companion's torch is not a special case in that: it reaches the engine through `Lighting.AddLight` exactly as the player's torch, a light pet or a mod's lantern does, and the scan the sense reads is taken before any of them is merged. `Reason` names which of those answers decided the hand, for the telemetry. `Shown` additionally requires a free hand and a dry body, following vanilla's ordinary-torch wet restriction. The NPC clears last tick's hand claim and calls `Hide` before resolving this tick, so a previous torch cannot veto its own fallback. Light, map reveal and held item follow that one answer; downed state never shows the torch.

Permanent placement is optional work offered by `../../../Activities/NearbyAssistance/LightUsefulArea.cs`, and the rule it follows is the owner's: wherever the player's own smart cursor could place a torch in the dark, the companion may place one too. The game's `SmartCursorHelper.Step_Torch` is the one home of that rule — an empty or cuttable tile, dry for an ordinary torch, a wall behind or an attachable block beside or below, and no torch within the game's own spacing — and this folder asks it three ways without ever reading or writing the local player's cursor. `Accepts` hands it a reach box one tile wide with the mouse on that tile, which is the decision for a candidate. `PlayerCursorTorch` hands it the player's own reach box, built exactly as `SmartCursorLookup` builds it, with the mouse at his centre, which is the tile his cursor would offer him and the reference the record judges lighting against. `MayAccept` is not the step at all but a necessary condition drawn from its attachment test — a wall behind, or a tile on the left, the right or below — so the lighting search can skip the step on the empty tiles of a screen that could never hold a torch. It is a filter and never a decision: it passes tiles the step refuses (a half brick or a slope beneath, a torch within spacing) and never refuses one the step accepts, and every tile it passes is still put to the step before anything is placed. The step's private context, argument array and scratch list are one of each and reused, because a search asks about hundreds of tiles a preparation and a fresh boxed context per tile was the loudest allocation in the tick. The shared target list is restored in `finally`, and a changed private engine contract fails explicitly rather than silently inventing recommendations. That filter-versus-decision line was already written here and was crossed anyway, which is why it is worth saying twice: the light census was built on `MayAccept` and published its passes as though they were sites, so the companion chained onto tiles the step would have refused for spacing and sat idle for 1798 ticks in front of work it had offered itself. `3eaa891` made the census ask `Accepts` — the question the placer actually answers — and `Activities/NearbyAssistance/CLAUDE.md` carries what that cost and what it measured. The general shape, for whoever adds the next cheap prefilter beside an expensive decision: a predicate whose contract is *passes everything the decision would accept, and more* is safe only where every pass is still put to the decision, so the moment a caller treats its output as a result the contract silently becomes a lie.

The companion's torches are its own and placing one uses up nothing, by the owner's ruling of 15 September 2026: lighting a cave is its job, and a job that stops when a stack runs out reads as the companion giving up, so there is no supply to check and no stage that refuses for the lack of one. A torch item still decides which torch goes in — the first in the companion's bag, then the player's inventory, else an ordinary torch — so a player carrying bone or coloured torches sees those; the item is read for its tile and style and never debited.

**A free torch never becomes an item, and a torch the player takes down stays down.** `RememberCompanionTorches.cs` keeps the tiles holding a torch the companion placed, and its tile hook sets `noItem` when one is killed, whoever kills it — the game drops a broken torch's own item otherwise, and a single rare torch handed over became an endless supply. When one is removed by anything but the companion's own hit (its pickaxe taking the block a torch stood on), the spot is refused: no companion torch within the game's own torch spacing of it, as though his removal were a torch still standing there, because the place is still dark and a refusal of the spot alone puts the same torch one tile over at the next search. The refusal ends when the spot holds a tile again — his next decision about the place — and both memories are saved with the world, because a refusal forgotten at a reload re-places the torch he removed. The companion's placement deliberately calls no `TileLoader.PlaceInWorld`: that hook's contract is a player placing an item from his inventory, and a mod reading it would credit or charge him for the companion's free torch. Work credit for a torch tile is paid once per world by `../../../../Progression/CreditWork.cs`. Native `WorldGen.PlaceTile` remains the placement authority, and a placement counts only when the resulting tile is that torch. The companion adds stricter limits to Smart Cursor: no replacing occupied terrain, no liquid placement, and no edits inside a protected bed room. These restrictions intentionally exclude water torches until underwater placement is part of the designed work kit.

Native placement establishes a resulting torch tile, not that a useful dark region became brighter. The lighting activity offers placement only on a tile whose own light `../../Observation/ObserveLight.cs` reads dark — the active engine computed the tile, and the world's own light there is known to be below the same dark level the held torch reads, so carrying and placing light agree. Light somebody is carrying is not in that reading at all, so a tile under a torch reads by the world's own light like any other (`Observation/CLAUDE.md` carries the rule and the three guesses that preceded it). A tile daylight reaches is never a site at all, by the engine's own surface rule, so the companion does not light the ground outside at night. The placer announces each torch it places to the terrain edit record by hand, because `WorldGen.PlaceTile` runs no tile hook and every retained search that read the tile — the reach flood, a route, the clearance field — is discarded only on an announced edit. A tile the engine never computed is unread and refused with a lit one; a zero returned outside a native light buffer never stands in for a dark placement opportunity. A placement's later benefit is still not re-measured and attributed: the next preparation simply reads the tiles it lit as lit, and the game's spacing refuses the rest.

**The attachment filter may only ever be wider than the step.** Narrowing it removes tiles from the lighting search without any refusal being recorded, because a tile the filter drops is never a candidate; `Tools/EngineReplay/Assistance/VerifyCandidateFunnel.cs` sweeps every attachment the step distinguishes and fails on a tile the step accepts and the filter refuses, and planting the filter back without its wall term is how that row is known to discriminate.

## Traps

- **Nothing here publishes a fact the light sense reads any more, and the trap that stood here is the reason to keep it that way.** The sense used to ask the bearer whether its torch was out, so that it could discount it; `Shown` was cleared at the top of `CompanionNPC.AI` and recomputed after `Brain.Tick`, so the answer the sense read was false on every live tick, the torch's own glow was credited to the room, and in a dark cave the torch dropped, the field read dark, the minimum hold expired and it came back — a three-second cycle that every fixture passed, because each set `Shown` by hand and painted a glow instead of reading the live order. The sense reads the engine's own scan of the world now and asks this folder nothing, so the ordering cannot be got wrong; a future fact published from here for another system to read inherits the whole trap.
- **A place nobody read is not the same as a place being bright.** Asymmetric read logic between raise and lower: raising asks "is there any measured dark air", so one measured dark answer carries it and a silent one (a place the engine has not computed) is skipped; lowering asks "is there no dark air", which requires an answer to every place, and a silent query cannot be answered, so it blocks the lower. If you let a silent query merely abstain from the lower check, that is the same defect as reading it as a zero — the companion is next to unmeasured bright area and unmeasured dark cave, sees bright, and puts out a torch in the dark because the dark's silence was not a reading of "nothing dark".
