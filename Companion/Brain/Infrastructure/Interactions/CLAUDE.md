# World interactions — game abilities chosen elsewhere

This folder performs reliable closed-set abilities using Terraria’s own mechanics where possible. It never selects a behaviour, chooses a place, plans movement or writes the NPC body. Gathering activities in `../../Activities/Gathering/` and nearby interaction methods decide when to request these abilities; the brain supplies the position and hand permission.

```
Interactions/
├─ CLAUDE.md             this guide
├─ RenderTileCracks.cs   companion-owned tile damage presentation
├─ ObserveTileToolEffect.cs native tool-call snapshots and observed damage/removal outcomes
├─ EstimateRemainingToolWork.cs next tile completion from native damage, owned progress and cooldown
├─ FindToolAccess.cs     shared tile reach, exposed-face access and the reachable hover beside a tile; the reach box alone as pure arithmetic for success regions
├─ BindTileTarget.cs     captured coordinate/material availability without discovery or mutation
├─ Chopping/             tree detection and axe damage
├─ Mining/               ore discovery and the game pickaxe path
├─ Doors/                open or close a route obstruction
├─ Torch/                free-hand light and supplied permanent torch placement
└─ WorldProtection/      bed-room protection for autonomous edits
```

Tools read the player’s held-item numbers but do not invoke the player item-use pipeline. That boundary is why a companion ability works consistently while preserving player upgrades that are explicitly read. A terrain-changing interaction invalidates shared movement’s cached terrain facts.

Three of these abilities are also where the companion's work earns experience, because they are the only place the edit is observed to have happened: the miner credits an ore tile that is gone after its `PickTile`, the chopper a trunk bottom that is gone after its killing `KillTile`, and the torch placer a torch that stands after its `PlaceTile`. Each calls `../../../Progression/CreditWork.cs` after the native call and never decides anything about the credit itself; a swing that only cracks a tile, or a placement the game refused, credits nothing.

Mining, chopping, pot collection and torch work use one tool-access query, `FindToolAccess.Approach`. It first tests the body's own centre against the current player-derived reach box and a native tile walk to an exposed target face. The walk is the game's `Collision.CanHit`, not its wider `CanHitLine` beam, because a swing reaches into a one-tile notch that a beam three tiles wide refuses; it still refuses any walk that enters a solid tile. Current access needs no route. Otherwise it enumerates every hoverable cell inside the reach box of the tile whose hover point is in reach of it, ranks them by distance to the tile's centre, and asks the reach sense about each nearest first, stopping at the first Yes; only when no cell is reachable does every cell get asked, because Unknown versus No needs all the answers. The hover point returned is inside the cell, at the usable corner nearest its centre, so the point the activity requests is one the navigator can plan to. This query proves access under the observed geometry, not native permission or future completion. Execution checks actual access again because terrain, range or the body can change after preparation. Reach is measured from the orb's centre, which is where its eye and its tools are; `EyeHeight` is zero and stays as a name for the callers that read it.

**It asks the reach sense, and it may not do anything else.** A cell is reachable when the flood in `../Observation/ObserveReach.cs` holds any corner of it — a hash lookup against a region one flood has already computed for every corner in it — and the sense's three answers are the query's three: in the region is Yes, an unfinished flood is Unknown, a flood that ran out of region is No. The walker's query was a fresh bounded A* per pose, and the cost was not the problem. The problem was the verdict: a bounded search answers Unknown when it runs out of expansions, an Unknown cannot be remembered without writing a site off on evidence that does not exist, and a caller rationing itself to a few poses a search therefore asked the same nearest poses for ever. On the 2026-09-14 capture that arrangement produced "the question was not finished" on 79% of lighting's offers, with a settled flood beside more than half of them holding the answer. `Tools/check-navigation-boundary.sh` refuses any route search under this folder or `../../Activities`, so the rule is inherited by whatever is written next rather than remembered.

The sense arrives as a parameter rather than through an ambient static, deliberately: the finder is static and has no context of its own, and an unset ambient would have answered silently in exactly the fixtures that had never flooded. Making the compiler name every call site was the point.

There is one query and no second one. The walker needed a hop for a tile no standing pose reached — a take-off pose, a simulated jump, an admission rule about arrival speed that refused narrow tops as a known limitation — because standing reach was bounded by where the body could stand. An orb hovers anywhere free, so a tile with an exposed face inside a free cell is reached by the same scan as any other, ceiling ore included, and a tile with no exposed face inside any free cell is known-unusable rather than a case for another proof.

Reach is the player's, read live from `Player.tileRangeX/Y`, so accessories, buffs and held items change it mid-job. The tool is not the player's: since the gear merge of 15 September 2026 the pickaxe and the axe are whatever sits in the companion's own tool slots (`../../../Inventory/CompanionGear.cs`), read through `TileMiner.PickaxeFor` and `TileChopper.AxeFor`, and an empty slot is an air item with no power, so the work is offered as known-unusable rather than dug with a tool the companion was never handed. The player's held tool is never borrowed, which is the four-slot ruling. `FindToolAccess.Reach` is that value as one revision, and every activity that retains something derived from access keys it on that revision: a held hover, an approach deferral and a "nothing reachable" search result. Without the key, a smaller reach kept flying to or holding a cell it could no longer swing from, and a larger one waited out a search cadence or a deferral for work it could already reach. Tool power is the same kind of evidence and is keyed the same way where it is retained (mining's pick power). The companion's own pace is a multiple of the player's run speed and acceleration, read by the motor every tick rather than keyed here, because nothing retained about access depends on it.

Prepared mining and chopping also bind their tile's coordinate and material. Availability validation detects disappearance or material replacement without discovering another target. This is distinct from tool access and native use permission; a replacement may be perfectly usable yet require a fresh offer. An unobserved remove-and-replace cycle with identical material is not a new generation this binding can identify.

Tool execution preserves immutable before/after observations around the native call. A requested swing, increased damage in that tool's hit table, tile removal and changed material/frame are separate outcomes. The hit table belongs to the hitter, so its damage is not shared world health; removal alone does not establish item yield. Tool instances retain their latest observation with its tick and monotonically increasing attempt number, and cooldown rejection creates no observation. Consumers join attempt identity with actor, tool and session rather than treating a retained result as fresh each tick.

RemainingToolWork estimates the next useful tile completion under unchanged native permission, damage and cadence. It includes the outstanding tool cooldown once, then the intervals between remaining hits. It excludes travel, subsequent targets and drops. Incapable or unavailable work returns no estimate rather than a fabricated finite duration. Partial damage from another hitter is not transferred between native tables; removed world tiles are shared evidence and disappear from subsequent opportunity preparation regardless of who removed them.
