# Route planning — representative nodes propose moves for actual-state validation

What a node is, how edges are generated from what the body can do, the search over them, the path it returns and the bounded reachability questions the senses ask. The mechanism at depth for poses, drops lowered row by row and jump profiles simulated is in `../MovementExecution/CLAUDE.md` and `../BodySimulation/CLAUDE.md`; this file is the map and the seams. The edge cache and the box it is dropped in are *not* deferred to those files — they are `AStar.cs` in this folder and they are described below.

```
RoutePlanning/
├─ CLAUDE.md
├─ ContinueRouteSearch.cs retained frontier, yielded edge work and directed reachability while a route is being walked
├─ RememberExecutedRoutes.cs executed connections, compatibility fingerprints, directed suffix composition and defensive world-save serialisation
├─ NavGrid.cs        what a node is: a feet tile where BodyPhysics.Stand finds a pose and no lava is in the column; block, support, liquid, submerged-head, lava-count and platform-underfoot tests over the world interface; the open span beside a lip (its columns and its centre) that a drop is planned across and steered along; FeetWorld, which is the pose's own point (off-centre in a tight column, partway down a slope); and the jump box and drop limits
├─ AStar.cs          synchronous replay search, terrain edge cache, shared traversal pricing and bounded directed-return probes; the live retained search uses the same edges and costs, while Region remains the offline reachability diagnostic
├─ NavPath.cs        the steps, first first, each carrying its own landing tile as `Tile` and the tile it leaves from as `From` (which defaults to the world origin and is read by nothing spatial — see the traps), its move kind (walk, jump, drop, fall-through), what realises it (a jump's velocity scale and the take-off its arc was proven from, a descent's steer line), how many ticks it was proven to take and the mobility state at its landing; whether the path is partial; and NavNode, what the search and the flood are keyed by: a feet tile and the body's mobility state on arriving there, so two states on one tile are two nodes and a move that spends a counter (an air jump, a dash) plans as an ordinary edge without a second search; every counter is zero today, so a node is its tile, and the public seams (Find from a tile to a tile, Region returning tiles, the edge cache's invalidation box) stay in tiles
└─ Reachability.cs   can a walker/flyer get from A to B; the walker answer is three-valued (the method is `WalkerReach` and the type it returns is `Reach`: a whole path, a region exhausted without arriving, or unknown — the budget ran out or an end has no standable tile) with one named rounding per caller on top, `WalkerCanReach` reading unknown as reachable and `WalkerProvenReach` reading it as no; the flyer flood is still a single bool that answers yes when its budget runs out; the walker search forces AllowOneWayDrops on around itself and puts it back, because an enemy takes any drop and the brain's flag is still whatever the last request set when the senses run; `RoundTrip` asks the same walker search both ways and walks a breath envelope over the two routes
```

The property behind the tri-state is that **an absence of evidence must not be recorded as evidence**. A bounded search that runs out has established nothing, and collapsing that into "reachable" is only correct for a caller that wants to be wrong in that direction. The threat sense does: a threat wrongly feared costs a little caution and one wrongly ignored costs the player a hit. Self-rescue is the same arithmetic inverted, because it *holds* the refuge it is handed, so an unknown accepted there is a commitment to walk at somewhere the body may never arrive with the breath running down. Neither rounding is the default and every call site names the one it wants, so a new caller chooses deliberately rather than inheriting whichever helper was nearest. This tri-state reachability is a trap the navigator addresses by forcing `AllowOneWayDrops` and restoring it around each walker search; the flyer flood has one caller and has not been split.

**Reaching a place is not a certificate of leaving it**, so `Reachability.RoundTrip` (and `MovementQueries.RoundTrip`) keeps the two answers apart: an outward walker search and a separate return search from the destination, each three-valued by the same rule as `WalkerReach`, with drops allowed both ways because the return search, not a one-way filter, is what judges whether a drop was one-way. A return search that runs out of budget is unknown and never a return. Breath is judged only over a trip whose both legs are whole routes: it walks the outward steps then the return steps, draining a step's proven duration whenever the head is under water at either end of the step and restoring duration times the recovery rate otherwise, capped at capacity. That rule's error has a stated direction — it over-charges a step that surfaces part way and honey or shimmer, and misses an arc that dips under water between two dry endpoints — and it adds no time at the destination, so a caller that means to work there adds the work itself. The envelope is three integers the caller builds from the character's own breathing rule, because this folder compiles without the game. The query's charge is only as good as the step durations it sums, which are the traversals' proofs, and a walk's proof now runs pose to pose at the speed the body actually walks. Measured against the native head's drowning test, the charge's slack is not spread evenly: on a flat flooded floor every charged step is one the head spends wholly under water, and the charge sits only just above native (a few percent over a trip walked out and back, from rest, with arrival included in the native count), while a route with wet-ended jumps or drops, such as a staircase out of a basin, charges each of those steps in full for a head that is under for part of it, which is where a wider margin comes from. So the rule's "errs high" is thin for walking alone, and anything that lengthens a walk under water beyond its proof — a current, a push, an entry against the walk direction — is not in the charge. Lighting and pot trips ask it for every remote working pose they would walk to (`Companion/Brain/Activities/NearbyAssistance/PerformNearbyWorldWork.cs`); mining and chopping approaches still ask `WalkerReach` outward only, and collection's known drops keep their own marginal-return check, so wiring each of those is that purpose's own change.

**A search's millisecond allowance is a static that the last planner to set it leaves behind.** `AStar.Find` turns `AStar.MsBudget` into a deadline at every top-level call, and the navigator's `Plan` sets that static to its own planning allowance and never restores it, so every later search in the tick that does not set it spends whatever the navigator last asked for. `RoundTrip` owns its budget: it holds the static at zero for both legs, so each leg is bounded by its expansion budget and by the shared `LimitPlanningWork` deadline the caller runs under (narrowed with `LimitPlanningWork.Narrow` where a caller wants less, as lighting and pot trips narrow each round trip to the navigator's per-search route allowance), and puts the caller's value back. Before it did, a round trip asked after a plan starved to one work unit answered unknown both ways for a pit it could walk. `WalkerReach` still inherits the leaked value, which in play is the navigator's planning allowance per search, and its callers in work approaches and collection get that bound without choosing it; restoring the static where `Plan` sets it would change that bound for all of them at once, which is why it has not been done inside a fix scoped to the round trip.

The seam that matters is the step: a path step carries everything the traversal needs to perform the move it proved (the kind, the tile it leaves from, the state a jump's arc was flown from or a descent's steer line, the ticks it took), so the follower never re-derives a move from the tiles. The search itself knows no move: it asks the traversals for a node's edges and prices what comes back.

**A step may carry only what its proof used, and the test is that every field names a state the body was actually in.** A jump's step used to carry the profile's nominal start speed — 3.5 for a running profile — beside an arc that had been flown at whatever the runway behind the take-off delivered, which on one tile of floor is about 1.7. Nothing about that is a rounding error: it is a second rule, living in a field, and every reader inherited it. It also shows how such a field survives, because it reads as a label rather than as a claim, and a label nobody treats as load-bearing is exactly what four separate consumers went on to compute with. `RememberExecutedRoutes` serialises steps into the world save, so its format moved 2 to 3 rather than reinterpreting the slot: the nominal and the proven speed are the same shape and only the version separates them.

**A route step is also read for a landing tile that the route memory keys on**, so the same rule applies in reverse: a remembered jump that reconstructs without the mark its run-up started at is a jump nobody can repeat, which is why the archive entry carries it.

SearchStopReason distinguishes a found route, exhausted graph, expansion limit, deadline and invalid start. The live navigator supplies its actual entry pose for the initial expansion, bypassing cached representative-pose edges there, and filters only first edges with applicable physical rejection evidence. Future edges still use representative poses and require execution-time validation. Edge enumeration yields between jump-profile proofs, including rejected candidates; retaining only completed expansions would repeatedly pay for an unfinished expensive node. Each advance reserves a bounded generator operation even when upstream planning spent the shared deadline. This is a soft time budget: one indivisible generator operation can finish after the clock expires. Return probes may establish a one-way edge only after exhaustion without a complete return route; a partial path does not prove a return. Limits are unknown and never enter the return-verdict cache or establish the brain's stranded state. The text-world graph being exhausted still does not prove the real world physically sealed: missing terrain metadata and missing movement abilities can close this model.

**A retained query is invalidated where the edit happened, not by a counter that moves for the whole world.** `TerrainChanges` keeps a bounded ring of the tiles behind its recent revisions beside the count, so a query asks whether any edit since it last looked landed somewhere it read. What a query has to defend is its *closed* set rather than its frontier — the frontier reads fresh edges, because the edge cache already dropped whatever the edit could have changed — so the sensitive region is the explored tiles inflated by exactly the box that cache is dropped in, `AStar.ScanReaches`, and that box lives in one place for that reason. A margin drawn from the body's own size is unsound here and not merely tight: a drop steers sideways for as long as the deepest fall takes, so an edit two dozen columns from a closed tile can change where that tile's drop lands.

**What a query depends on is a superset of what it reached, and the difference is route memory.** A remembered suffix is adopted by enqueueing the destination with the whole run of steps hung off it as one parent edge, so the goal is dequeued and returns Found with every tile in between never expanded and never reached. Under a world-global compare that cost nothing, because an edit anywhere invalidated everything; spatially it is a route priced from terrain that may since have changed, handed to the navigator as valid, and the failure would arrive downstream as the live proof refusing a step, which reads as a physical fault rather than as stale knowledge. So the query keeps a sensitivity set that is the reached tiles plus every adopted suffix's landings, while `Reached` stays exactly the expanded region, because the goal-less owners — the two reach floods and the meeting search — read it as a region and never have suffixes.

**A suffix is terrain the answer stands on from the moment it is snapshotted, not from the moment it is adopted, and the two need different guards because the ordinary check cannot see the first window at all.** The suffixes are copied out of route memory into a private dictionary when the query is born, each fingerprint-checked against the world at that instant. An edit landing on one of them before it is adopted retires the archive's own entry and never touches that copy — and the validity check walks the set of tiles the query has *read*, which does not yet hold the suffix, so it answers Unchanged and moves the query's own revision **past** the very edit that mattered. Every later check is then made against a revision after the only announcement that could have caught it, and the query hands back a remembered route across ground the game has said changed. So the query keeps the revision its snapshot was taken at, separate from the revision it was last found clean at, and a suffix is admitted only when nothing announced since the snapshot landed within `AStar.ScanReaches` of one of its landings or of the destination. That margin is deliberately the same one the noting commits to for those same tiles a line later: a narrower rule before adoption than after it would be two rules for one piece of terrain, and the wider one covers a jump's arc, whose apex sits inside the box above a take-off the expanding tile already carries.

The alternative was noting every candidate suffix's tiles at construction, when they are all known, and it is the worse trade for a reason that is about precision rather than cost. Its cost is bounded by the suffix count where this one is bounded by the *adopted* suffix's own length times the edits announced since the snapshot — which is nothing at all in the ordinary case, because the record returns on one integer compare when no edit has landed. Far more expensively, noting candidates makes the query sensitive to every remembered route to the goal including all the ones it never takes, so a dig on a route the query will not use throws the query away: the world-global restart this whole mechanism replaced, wearing a smaller box.

Two properties of the query's side of that are load-bearing. The check costs the number of edits announced since this query last asked — usually none, which returns on one integer compare — and each edit walked costs a four-compare box test and, only on a box hit, a scan of the dependency set, because the box around a corridor holds a great deal of world the corridor never read. And **a query advances its own revision on every clean answer**, so the record only ever has to cover the gap between two checks rather than the query's whole life; without that, a query surviving a window's worth of edits anywhere in the world falls off the back of the record and restarts on the global edit rate, which is the cadence this replaces wearing a different name. Invalidity is remembered, because a query can never become valid again. The record's own shape — why a ring of tiles rather than counters over a chunk grid, how far back it reaches, and why a lost window answers changed — is `../TerrainModel/CLAUDE.md`'s.

Not everything invalidated by an edit is boxed, and the exception is deliberate. `AStar.TileChanged` drops the per-tile edge cache inside `ScanReaches` and clears the one-way return verdicts **wholesale**, because a verdict is about the shape of a whole region: a tile dug deep inside a pocket changes whether a lip far outside any scan box still has a way back. A box drawn for edges is the wrong instrument for a claim about a region's exits.

**Two searches born together no longer live and die together, and an owner holding several must ask each one.** Under a world-global compare one counter gave one answer, so a pair created from the same tile at the same revision was always valid or always stale as a pair, and testing one stood in for both. Spatially they come apart the moment they explore different ground — which is by construction for any pair whose policies differ, since the more permissive search reaches ground the stricter one cannot — and an edit in that extra ground invalidates one alone. The consequence of testing only one is not a wasted search but a wrong answer: the untested search advances while invalid, expands nothing, and its stale reached region is then read as though it were still proven. The reach sense is the live instance of this and `../Observation/CLAUDE.md` carries it; the property belongs here because it is a property of retained queries, not of that sense.

**Asking *where* is opt-in, and the retained route search is still the only consumer that asks.** Everything else in the brain that retains work across ticks keys on the world-global counter and therefore still throws that work away for an edit anywhere in the loaded world. The list is produced by a `.Revision` grep over `Companion/Brain` rather than remembered, and every entry on it is a candidate for the same spatial treatment:

```
retained proofs        PlanLocalMovement's validated preparation prefix and its macro proof over the
                       remaining traversal — two separate caches, both on the global counter
                       SearchControlSequences' retained control sequences
rejection memories     Navigator.failedEntries, the entry-rejection memory, and beside it the
                       physical-verdict protection and the executed-traversal continuity check
position selection     ChooseUsefulPosition's discard of held position evidence, its success-region
                       stamps, and its per-target refusal memory
approach and deferral  MineOre's approach cache and hop take-off deferral, ChopTree's approach key,
                       PerformNearbyWorldWork's no-return store, the two combat firing memories,
                       ProtectCompanionHomes' home cache
shared and sensed      ReachEnvironmentalSafety's air-target and nearby-air searches,
                       ObserveThreats' per-hostile reachability memory
not caches             ReadGameTerrain's delegation, the motor's one-tick divergence reason, and the
                       telemetry column — these read the counter and retain nothing
```

**`ChooseUsefulPosition`'s discard is the next one worth doing, and it is the only entry that undoes a rule somewhere else.** It nulls the chosen spot and clears the refusal set on *any* world edit, which is exactly the destination retention the offer-validity work introduced: a player mining a screen away throws away a spot that is still perfectly good and still inside its own admitted region. Every other entry on the list costs a recomputation; this one costs a decision the brain had already made.

**The dependency set duplicates `Reached` for the three goal-less owners, and that is recorded rather than fixed.** A query with no goal holds no suffixes, so its dependency set and its reached set hold the same tiles and the second is pure duplication. Sharing one set is a one-line change and it is wrong: `Touch` adds to `Reached` and then calls `Note`, whose first act is `read.Add(tile)`, which on a shared set returns false for a tile already present and returns **before** the explored bounds are widened — so the pre-filter box would never grow past the start tile and every distant edit would be answered by a box the size of one tile. A `HashSet<Point>` slot is a hash code, a next index and two ints of payload, sixteen bytes, plus a bucket int, with capacity a prime at or above the count after doubling growth, so the duplicate costs roughly twenty to forty bytes per tile in the region. The fixture floor's own floods measured 46 tiles two-way and 93 scored on 2026-09-14, under two kilobytes, which is that geometry rather than a cave; the live figure is the capture's `reach_any` column and no capture carries it yet.

**The gain is unmeasured, and the direction it is bounded in is known.** The sensitive box is two dozen columns either side of the explored region and tens of rows below it, so a player mining anywhere near the companion is still inside it and still restarts everything: this cuts restarts from *distant* edits and does nothing for one mining inside the region, which is why the navigator's bound on how long a goal may wait for an answer is still needed and still applies. No before-and-after exists, because the capture the argument was built on carries no terrain-edit rate to compare against. The measurement is the next play's `terrain_revision` column read as differences between rows, joined against the reach sense's complete share.

The navigator continues its query while walking a usable partial route. A completed result joins only at the live feet tile while grounded and outside an active traversal; failure to join starts a fresh query immediately. Position selection uses two retained region searches, with and without one-way drops, rather than repeatedly calling the immediate `AStar.Region` tool described above. It may reuse a moved start only when generated directed connections establish both the old-to-new and new-to-old routes. Nearby coordinates alone cannot establish this across a ledge. A policy change replaces the query outright; a terrain edit replaces it only when it landed somewhere the query depends on, by the spatial rule above. Membership in an unfinished region is positive evidence, while absence is unknown.

An arbitrary open-frontier prefix is not sufficient evidence of useful detour progress. The 2026-09-10 experiment publishing such prefixes preserved 86 completed historical routes but changed 31 partial endpoints and 14 incomplete cases into 3 partial endpoints and 42 incomplete cases; it was removed. Retaining only the live frontier fixed stale-branch proposals but did not remove the repeated attempts at incomplete route ends. A complete route may still begin away from its destination, and the native C-turn fixture passes without this fallback. Revisit early detour publication only with a goal-level progress contract that prevents repeated exploration from undoing completed work.

Experience belongs to the world save through `TerrariaIntegration/TrackTerrainChanges`. The navigator records a completed traversal only when the observed motion remained continuous with its predicted successor and the terrain revision stayed fixed. An interrupted or externally displaced attempt teaches nothing. The archive accepts the ordinary movement kit only; richer ability-dependent learning is not implemented. Connections store the swept area's tile shape, platform state, liquid kind and amount fingerprint. Changed tiles invalidate intersecting connections; fingerprint validation also catches unannounced liquid changes. Physical execution failures retire the connection, while threat rejection is not permanent terrain evidence.

Reverse directed search composes known suffixes to the requested destination, allowing a new C-to-A search to join an executed A-to-B route. Alternatives are compared with the same complete current environmental price and returnability policy before pruning, then offered as ordinary frontier candidates. Every resulting traversal still passes live physical validation. Capacity, archive size, suffix depth and live query nodes are bounded in their owning classes; memory is a proposal cache, not a second movement authority. Exact destination tiles currently anchor suffix lookup, so a nearby but different requested destination need not reuse that memory.

**Putting the body's arrival speed in the node key routes better and costs about four times the planning time, and the cost is the node count rather than the pricing.** Built in full and measured on `Tools/Scenarios` on 2026-09-13, then left unshipped. A node became a feet tile plus an entry class — the speeds the motor actually produces at a column boundary while walking from rest, which are 0, 1.77, 2.52, 3.08 and 3.50 px/tick, signed by direction, so nine classes per tile — every traversal proved its edge from that class's velocity and recorded the class it landed in, edges were priced in their proven ticks instead of per kind, and the heuristic became ticks (`max(dx·16/WalkSpeed, dy·16/MaxFallSpeed)`, admissible because the body falls and travels sideways in the same ticks so a sum would overclaim). Against the same corpus at the same commit:

    per-route planner ms      p50    p90     p95     max      total
    tile nodes, per-kind cost  6.3  160.8   346.0   457.8    13258
    tile nodes, tick cost     13.2  171.9   380.0   497.5    16224
    entry-class nodes         23.2  615.8  1318.4  1704.4    51676

    per-route expansions       p50    p90     p95     max      total
    tile nodes, per-kind cost   86    312     763    1037     40229

The middle row is the attribution and is the part worth keeping: with the classes collapsed and everything else left in place, the expansion p95 and max are *identical* to the old graph's, so pricing edges in ticks and weakening the heuristic to stay admissible cost 1.22x in time and nothing in nodes opened. The classes are the remaining 3.18x. Per-node edge proving did not change, so the multiplier is close to the node multiplier, and it lands worst on the smallest routes — 4 expansions became 55, 5 became 78, 34 became 371, while the corpus's most expensive route only went 377 to 1495 — which says the search is opening class siblings of tiles where nothing needed them.

The planning half of it routes better: one block the old graph closed as model-sealed became a proven route, partial routes fell from 67 to 64, and no route that planned stopped planning. **The walking half is much worse, and that, rather than the cost, is what actually kills the design.** Over the same corpus the follow harness went from 89 routes walked, 43 to a partial plan's end and 1 not, to 76 walked, 38 partial and 20 not, with mislandings rising from 8 to 40. The planner proves better routes and the body then fails to walk them.

The mechanism is the quantisation itself, and it is worth stating precisely because it is not a tuning miss. A class stands for one speed; a body arriving at a tile has a real speed somewhere inside that class's band. The edge out of that node is proven from the class's nominal speed and performed at the body's real one, and a descent carries that difference through every tick it falls. From the trace: a body arrives at 3486,477 at 1.40 to 1.68 px/tick, which files as the rest class because the next class up is 1.77, and the drop out of it was proven from a standstill; over the drop's eighteen ticks the real body travels some twenty-five pixels further than the proof did and lands a tile and a half away from the tile the step promised. Rounding the other way does not help, it only turns overshoot into undershoot. So the requirement is that the class gap times the longest steered move stay inside the arrival slack, and with descents of eighteen to fifty ticks against a ten-pixel slack that means a gap well under half a pixel per tick — roughly twice as many classes again, on a graph already costing 3.9x. **Soundness needs a finer quantisation than performance can afford, and that is a property of the design rather than of this implementation of it.**

Three properties survive the attempt and constrain whatever tries again. **A speed class map must be a chain the body climbs, never a bucket it can round back into**: quantising an arrival to the nearest of a coarse set files the 2.52 the body really has back into the 1.77 class it just left, and the graph then never leaves the slow class while the body accelerates away from it, which is the from-rest system with more nodes. That is what forces the class list to be the ramp's own samples and so forces the node count. **Every tile must stay reachable in its rest class or the graph silently loses moves**, because a descent is proven from the speed at the lip; the walk therefore has to offer a second, braking edge onto the same landing, which doubles the walk edges out of every node. And **route memory keyed on the exact class reuses almost nothing**: a remembered move is only offered to a body arriving at the speed it was recorded at, and a body approaching the same tile from a different distance is at a different speed, so `VerifyMovementContracts`'s C-to-A-then-remembered-A-to-B fixture goes red on reuse while staying correct. Keying it loosely is not the escape — that hands a move made at a run to a body that has stopped, which is the mislanding c4bc752 already bought once.

The full experiment, its ramp probe and the rest of the stops investigation it belongs to are in [research/Navigation Research/Momentum, Stops and the One-Mover Design.md](<../../../../../research/Navigation Research/Momentum, Stops and the One-Mover Design.md>), which is where the derivation lives; this section keeps the properties because they constrain anyone editing route planning, who has no reason to be reading research first. **The code was not thrown away** — it is kept on the branch `experiment/entry-speed-graph` at `355787b`, so a revival re-runs the measurement rather than rebuilding the graph to take it.

A class-aware heuristic is available and is worth having if any of this is revived — from class k the least ticks to cover a horizontal distance is the full-throttle continuation of the ramp, exact on flat ground and admissible everywhere, where the tick heuristic used here is exact only for a body already at the walk speed and loose for one at rest — but it addresses the cost and not the mislandings, so it is not the thing to try first. The blocker is that a discrete class cannot carry a speed accurately enough for a steered move to land where its proof did. Anything reviving this has to answer that before it is worth planning with: either the move is re-proved from the body's exact velocity at the moment it starts, which is what `PlanLocalMovement` already does and what the graph was supposed to make unnecessary, or the graph stops trying to promise a landing tile for a speed-sensitive move at all.

**Deriving the jump generator's horizontal scan from the body's flight instead of from `JumpGapTiles` plans better and costs 2.5x the planning time, and it was built, measured on 2026-09-14 and left unshipped.** `NavGrid.JumpGapTiles`, 4 at that measurement and a tunable a later session may move, has a comment saying a running jump clears four tiles, which the arithmetic beside it contradicts: a full jump at 8.5 px/tick under gravity 0.3 is airborne about fifty-seven ticks returning to its own row, and fifty-seven ticks at the walk speed is over twelve tiles. So the replacement bounded `dx` per landing row by the airtime of the tallest jump up to the moment it crosses that row on the way down, times the walk speed, plus a tile — a sound upper bound, ranging from nine tiles for a seven-row rise to fourteen for a landing two rows below. Against `Tools/Scenarios` with `--follow`, the same command both sides:

    per-route planner ms      p50    p90     p95      max     corpus total
    JumpGapTiles = 4          5.6  147.8   325.4    430.4          12,271
    derived from the flight  15.7  382.4   826.1  1,091.9          29,659

    routes                  walked  partial  never   windows planned  model-closed
    JumpGapTiles = 4            89       42      2               107            33
    derived from the flight     91       38      4               119            21

It routes better and it is not close on cost: p95 is 2.54x against a declared line of 1.5x, and twelve windows the old bound left model-closed now plan. Mislandings went 5 to 6.

**The bound cannot be tightened, and the reason is the part to keep.** The obvious refinement is to bound the launch speed by what the floor behind the take-off can actually deliver, so a take-off with no runway gets a narrow scan. That is unsound: a jump launched at rest still crosses ground, because the in-air steering accelerates the body toward its landing over the whole flight. Measured on the 09:28 capture's window #3, every jump proven out of 3393,614 carries `launchVx 0.00` and they reach five tiles across. A runway-derived bound would have dropped all of them.

The pair that prompted this is worth recording too, because it answers a different question than expected. The player's recorded trail crosses from 3393,614 to 3387,609, six across and five up, and no such edge exists. Under the derived bound that pair *is* proposed — eleven tiles of reach at a five-row rise — and every profile is still refused by simulation, and `--edges 3393,614` lists nothing leftward at all. **So the generator's bound was never what refused that jump; the body cannot make it.** A gap in a route that follows the player's own trail is a capability difference rather than a generator bound, and reading "the planner proves no jump edge" as "the constant is too small" is the reading this measurement exists to prevent. `--compare-jump` now says which of the two it is, against the box's own dimensions, so the question does not need re-deriving.

What would make this shippable is a cheaper way to reject a far tile than proving arcs at it, since the cost is per candidate tile per profile and the reach widens the candidate set by about three times. Nothing here attempts one.

## Traps

- **Only a suffix's landing tiles are noted as dependencies, never a step's `From`, and that is not an oversight to tidy up.** `NavStep.From` defaults to `default`, which is the tile (0,0), so noting it would stretch the dependency box from the search region to the world origin and destroy the four-compare pre-filter for every query that ever reuses a remembered route. The landings chain anyway — a step's landing is the next step's departure, and the first departs from the tile being expanded, which is closed and already noted — so the run is covered. Anything extending what a query depends on checks the default of the field it is about to note.
- **`Reached` and the dependency set are two sets on purpose, and folding them together is the tempting wrong fix.** `Reached` is exactly the expanded region, because the goal-less owners — the two reach floods and the meeting search — read it as a region, and un-expanded ground folded into it would put tiles in a reachable region that nothing proved reachable. Those owners pass no goal, hold no suffixes, and their two sets are identical.

## Gaps and planned work

Splitting it would move the statics to a fifth file that every part then reaches into, which is one more indirection for no reader. Revisit only if a part gains state the others do not touch.
