# Route planning — representative nodes propose moves for actual-state validation

What a node is, how edges are generated from what the body can do, the search over them, the path it returns and the bounded reachability questions the senses ask. The mechanism at depth (poses, drops lowered row by row, jump profiles simulated, the edge cache and its invalidation box) is in `../CLAUDE.md`; this file is the map and the seams.

```
RoutePlanning/
├─ CLAUDE.md
├─ ContinueRouteSearch.cs retained frontier, yielded edge work and directed reachability while a route is being walked
├─ RememberExecutedRoutes.cs executed connections, compatibility fingerprints, directed suffix composition and defensive world-save serialisation
├─ NavGrid.cs        what a node is: a feet tile where BodyPhysics.Stand finds a pose and no lava is in the column; block, support, liquid, submerged-head, lava-count and platform-underfoot tests over the world interface; the open span beside a lip (its columns and its centre) that a drop is planned across and steered along; FeetWorld, which is the pose's own point (off-centre in a tight column, partway down a slope); and the jump box and drop limits
├─ AStar.cs          synchronous replay search, terrain edge cache, shared traversal pricing and bounded directed-return probes; the live retained search uses the same edges and costs, while Region remains the offline reachability diagnostic
├─ NavPath.cs        the steps, first first, each with its move kind (walk, jump, drop, fall-through), the tile it leaves from, what realises it (a jump's velocity scale and start speed, a descent's steer line), how many ticks it was proven to take and the mobility state at its landing; whether the path is partial; and NavNode, what the search and the flood are keyed by: a feet tile and the body's mobility state on arriving there, so two states on one tile are two nodes and a move that spends a counter (an air jump, a dash) plans as an ordinary edge without a second search; every counter is zero today, so a node is its tile, and the public seams (Find from a tile to a tile, Region returning tiles, the edge cache's invalidation box) stay in tiles
└─ Reachability.cs   can a walker/flyer get from A to B; the walker answer is three-valued (`WalkerReach`: a whole path, a region exhausted without arriving, or unknown — the budget ran out or an end has no standable tile) with one named rounding per caller on top, `WalkerCanReach` reading unknown as reachable and `WalkerProvenReach` reading it as no; the flyer flood is still a single bool that answers yes when its budget runs out; the walker search forces AllowOneWayDrops on around itself and puts it back, because an enemy takes any drop and the brain's flag is still whatever the last request set when the senses run; `RoundTrip` asks the same walker search both ways and walks a breath envelope over the two routes
```

The property behind the tri-state is that **an absence of evidence must not be recorded as evidence**. A bounded search that runs out has established nothing, and collapsing that into "reachable" is only correct for a caller that wants to be wrong in that direction. The threat sense does: a threat wrongly feared costs a little caution and one wrongly ignored costs the player a hit. Self-rescue is the same arithmetic inverted, because it *holds* the refuge it is handed, so an unknown accepted there is a commitment to walk at somewhere the body may never arrive with the breath running down. Neither rounding is the default and every call site names the one it wants, so a new caller chooses deliberately rather than inheriting whichever helper was nearest. `../CLAUDE.md` carries the trap and the session that bought it; the flyer flood has one caller and has not been split.

**Reaching a place is not a certificate of leaving it**, so `Reachability.RoundTrip` (and `MovementQueries.RoundTrip`) keeps the two answers apart: an outward walker search and a separate return search from the destination, each three-valued by the same rule as `WalkerReach`, with drops allowed both ways because the return search, not a one-way filter, is what judges whether a drop was one-way. A return search that runs out of budget is unknown and never a return. Breath is judged only over a trip whose both legs are whole routes: it walks the outward steps then the return steps, draining a step's proven duration whenever the head is under water at either end of the step and restoring duration times the recovery rate otherwise, capped at capacity. That rule's error has a stated direction — it over-charges a step that surfaces part way and honey or shimmer, and misses an arc that dips under water between two dry endpoints — and it adds no time at the destination, so a caller that means to work there adds the work itself. The envelope is three integers the caller builds from the character's own breathing rule, because this folder compiles without the game. The query's charge is only as good as the step durations it sums, which are the traversals' proofs, and a walk's proof now runs pose to pose at the speed the body actually walks. Measured against the native head's drowning test, the charge's slack is not spread evenly: on a flat flooded floor every charged step is one the head spends wholly under water, and the charge sits only just above native (a few percent over a trip walked out and back, from rest, with arrival included in the native count), while a route with wet-ended jumps or drops, such as a staircase out of a basin, charges each of those steps in full for a head that is under for part of it, which is where a wider margin comes from. So the rule's "errs high" is thin for walking alone, and anything that lengthens a walk under water beyond its proof — a current, a push, an entry against the walk direction — is not in the charge. Lighting and pot trips ask it for every remote working pose they would walk to (`PurposeFamilies/NearbyAssistance/PerformNearbyWorldWork.cs`); mining and chopping approaches still ask `WalkerReach` outward only, and collection's known drops keep their own marginal-return check, so wiring each of those is that purpose's own change.

**A search's millisecond allowance is a static that the last planner to set it leaves behind.** `AStar.Find` turns `AStar.MsBudget` into a deadline at every top-level call, and the navigator's `Plan` sets that static to its own planning allowance and never restores it, so every later search in the tick that does not set it spends whatever the navigator last asked for. `RoundTrip` owns its budget: it holds the static at zero for both legs, so each leg is bounded by its expansion budget and by the shared `LimitPlanningWork` deadline the caller runs under (narrowed with `LimitPlanningWork.Narrow` where a caller wants less, as lighting and pot trips narrow each round trip to the navigator's per-search route allowance), and puts the caller's value back. Before it did, a round trip asked after a plan starved to one work unit answered unknown both ways for a pit it could walk. `WalkerReach` still inherits the leaked value, which in play is the navigator's planning allowance per search, and its callers in work approaches and collection get that bound without choosing it; restoring the static where `Plan` sets it would change that bound for all of them at once, which is why it has not been done inside a fix scoped to the round trip.

The seam that matters is the step: a path step carries everything the traversal needs to perform the move it proved (the kind, the tile it leaves from, the profile or the steer line, the ticks it took), so the follower never re-derives a move from the tiles. The search itself knows no move: it asks the traversals for a node's edges and prices what comes back.

SearchStopReason distinguishes a found route, exhausted graph, expansion limit, deadline and invalid start. The live navigator supplies its actual entry pose for the initial expansion, bypassing cached representative-pose edges there, and filters only first edges with applicable physical rejection evidence. Future edges still use representative poses and require execution-time validation. Edge enumeration yields between jump-profile proofs, including rejected candidates; retaining only completed expansions would repeatedly pay for an unfinished expensive node. Each advance reserves a bounded generator operation even when upstream planning spent the shared deadline. This is a soft time budget: one indivisible generator operation can finish after the clock expires. Return probes may establish a one-way edge only after exhaustion without a complete return route; a partial path does not prove a return. Limits are unknown and never enter the return-verdict cache or establish the brain's stranded state. The text-world graph being exhausted still does not prove the real world physically sealed: missing terrain metadata and missing movement abilities can close this model.

The navigator continues its query while walking a usable partial route. A completed result joins only at the live feet tile while grounded and outside an active traversal; failure to join starts a fresh query immediately. Position selection uses two retained region searches, with and without one-way drops, rather than repeatedly calling the immediate `AStar.Region` tool described above. It may reuse a moved start only when generated directed connections establish both the old-to-new and new-to-old routes. Nearby coordinates alone cannot establish this across a ledge. Terrain revision and policy changes replace the query; membership in an unfinished region is positive evidence, while absence is unknown.

An arbitrary open-frontier prefix is not sufficient evidence of useful detour progress. The 2026-09-10 experiment publishing such prefixes preserved 86 completed historical routes but changed 31 partial endpoints and 14 incomplete cases into 3 partial endpoints and 42 incomplete cases; it was removed. Retaining only the live frontier fixed stale-branch proposals but did not remove the repeated attempts at incomplete route ends. A complete route may still begin away from its destination, and the native C-turn fixture passes without this fallback. Revisit early detour publication only with a goal-level progress contract that prevents repeated exploration from undoing completed work.

Experience belongs to the world save through `TerrariaIntegration/TrackTerrainChanges`. The navigator records a completed traversal only when the observed motion remained continuous with its predicted successor and the terrain revision stayed fixed. An interrupted or externally displaced attempt teaches nothing. The archive accepts the ordinary movement kit only; richer ability-dependent learning is not implemented. Connections store the swept area's tile shape, platform state, liquid kind and amount fingerprint. Changed tiles invalidate intersecting connections; fingerprint validation also catches unannounced liquid changes. Physical execution failures retire the connection, while threat rejection is not permanent terrain evidence.

Reverse directed search composes known suffixes to the requested destination, allowing a new C-to-A search to join an executed A-to-B route. Alternatives are compared with the same complete current environmental price and returnability policy before pruning, then offered as ordinary frontier candidates. Every resulting traversal still passes live physical validation. Capacity, archive size, suffix depth and live query nodes are bounded in their owning classes; memory is a proposal cache, not a second movement authority. Exact destination tiles currently anchor suffix lookup, so a nearby but different requested destination need not reuse that memory.

**Putting the body's arrival speed in the node key routes better and costs about four times the planning time, and the cost is the node count rather than the pricing.** Built in full and measured on `Tools/Scenarios` on 2026-09-13, then left unshipped. A node became a feet tile plus an entry class — the speeds the motor actually produces at a column boundary while walking from rest, which are 0, 1.77, 2.52, 3.08 and 3.50 px/tick, signed by direction, so nine classes per tile — every traversal proved its edge from that class's velocity and recorded the class it landed in, edges were priced in their proven ticks instead of per kind, and the heuristic became ticks (`max(dx·16/WalkSpeed, dy·16/MaxFallSpeed)`, admissible because the body falls and travels sideways in the same ticks so a sum would overclaim). Against the same corpus at the same commit:

    per-route planner ms      p50    p90     p95     max      total
    tile nodes, per-kind cost  6.3  160.8   346.0   457.8    13258
    tile nodes, tick cost     13.2  171.9   380.0   497.5    16224
    entry-class nodes         23.2  615.8  1318.4  1704.4    51676

    per-route expansions       p50    p90     p95     max      total
    tile nodes, per-kind cost   86    312     763    1037     40229
    tile nodes, tick cost      114    347     763    1037     46758
    entry-class nodes          358   1484    3754    4974    185657

The middle row is the attribution and is the part worth keeping: with the classes collapsed and everything else left in place, the expansion p95 and max are *identical* to the old graph's, so pricing edges in ticks and weakening the heuristic to stay admissible cost 1.22x in time and nothing in nodes opened. The classes are the remaining 3.18x. Per-node edge proving did not change, so the multiplier is close to the node multiplier, and it lands worst on the smallest routes — 4 expansions became 55, 5 became 78, 34 became 371, while the corpus's most expensive route only went 377 to 1495 — which says the search is opening class siblings of tiles where nothing needed them.

What it bought is real and is why this is recorded as a trade rather than a failure: one block the old graph closed as model-sealed became a proven route, partial routes fell from 67 to 64, and no route that passed stopped passing. The routes are genuinely better because a lip a body can only reach at speed is now provable.

Three properties survive the attempt and constrain whatever tries again. **A speed class map must be a chain the body climbs, never a bucket it can round back into**: quantising an arrival to the nearest of a coarse set files the 2.52 the body really has back into the 1.77 class it just left, and the graph then never leaves the slow class while the body accelerates away from it, which is the from-rest system with more nodes. That is what forces the class list to be the ramp's own samples and so forces the node count. **Every tile must stay reachable in its rest class or the graph silently loses moves**, because a descent is proven from the speed at the lip; the walk therefore has to offer a second, braking edge onto the same landing, which doubles the walk edges out of every node. And **route memory keyed on the exact class reuses almost nothing**: a remembered move is only offered to a body arriving at the speed it was recorded at, and a body approaching the same tile from a different distance is at a different speed, so `VerifyMovementContracts`'s C-to-A-then-remembered-A-to-B fixture goes red on reuse while staying correct. Keying it loosely is not the escape — that hands a move made at a run to a body that has stopped, which is the mislanding c4bc752 already bought once.

The first thing to try on a revival is a class-aware heuristic, and the ramp is what makes it possible: from class k the minimum ticks to cover a horizontal distance is the full-throttle continuation of that ramp, which is exact on flat ground and admissible everywhere because nothing accelerates faster than `StepVelocity` and no move carries more than the walk speed sideways. The tick heuristic used here is exact for a body already at the walk speed and badly loose for one at rest, which is precisely the sibling-opening described above. It would not touch the multiplier at branch points.

A stronger geometric heuristic is not a free speed improvement: the weighted-heuristic experiment regressed previously passing historical routes. Search priority retains the ordinary geometric estimate; remembered-route ties favour reuse without inflating that estimate. `Tools/NavReplay/VerifyMovementContracts.cs` exercises sliced completion, starvation prevention, directed reuse, actual executed learning, new-start composition, full-price pruning, external-displacement rejection and archive invalidation.
