# Shared movement system — one request surface, a game-free core, one motor

This subsystem turns a place the brain wants the orb to be into a velocity the motor applies. `CoordinateMovement` is the only request surface a brain stage may use and `MovementQueries` is their read-only geometry surface; the motor in `TerrariaIntegration/` is the only component that writes the NPC. Everything between the two — the contact, the free-space graph, the search, the route, the steering law — is game-free and compiles into `Tools/NavReplay` without Terraria, so the search the mod runs and the search a headless tool runs are one search rather than a copy.

```
Movement/
├─ CLAUDE.md                 this guide
├─ CoordinateMovement.cs     the request surface: MoveTo, HoverHere, Hold, SeekDestination, Accompany (with the floor its legs stay above), Evade
├─ MovementQueries.cs        the read-only geometry surface: the world, tile and corner questions, hover points
├─ LimitPlanningWork.cs      the shared millisecond allowance every search reads, and the switch that lifts it offline
├─ Contact/                  the circle-against-tiles contact, the one body the orb has
├─ FreeSpace/                what is free for the orb, the clearance field, the corner graph and the one resumable search
├─ Steering/                 the body's state and controls, the route, the steering law, the navigator, the pace and the census
├─ TerrainModel/             the game-free tile interface, the text world, and the record of where the world was edited
└─ TerrariaIntegration/      the live tile reader, the edit announcements and door detours, and the motor
```

## The body is an orb, and that decides everything below it

The companion is a twenty-pixel circle that flies: no gravity, no ground, no jump, no breath. The engine's tile collision is switched off for it, so the engine's whole contribution to its motion is adding the velocity to the position; the contact that keeps it out of walls is the mod's own (`Contact/`), and it runs in the motor after the tick's velocity has been chosen. There is therefore one body, and a headless tool that integrates a pose through the same contact is integrating the body the game has, which is the property the walker's two bodies never had and the reason its divergence had to be measured every tick.

What the body treats as a wall is one rule. The contact pushes out of solid tiles only: full blocks, half blocks and every slope, because slopes are full tiles to this body, with platforms passable and a closed door solid until the door interaction opens it. The planner's wall (`FreeSpace/OrbTerrain`) is the contact's wall and nothing more, because every liquid is air to this body: water, honey, lava and shimmer are never a wall to a search, never hurt it and never slow it. Until 15 September 2026 water and lava were walls to every search until a mastery immunity opened them and hurt on touch, and honey and shimmer slowed the body; the owner ruled the immunity built in, and the planner's wall and the contact's became the same rule.

## How a request becomes a velocity

A request takes one of three shapes:

- **A destination** (`MoveTo`) hands the navigator a goal point. The navigator owns one route at a time: it plans an A* over the corner graph from the corner under the body to the corner under the goal, smooths the corners by line of sight without losing the clearance the search paid for, and steers the body along the result every tick, re-planning when the goal moves materially, when the world is edited under the route, when the segment ahead is no longer clear, or when the body has stopped making progress. A search too large for one tick keeps its frontier; while it is unfinished the body follows the route it had, steers straight at the goal when the line is clear, and otherwise holds. A search that exhausts the free space without finding the goal is a proven absence, reported apart from unfinished — and every "not yet is not no" rule upstream stands on that distinction.
- **A travel intention with no chosen place** (`SeekDestination`) aims at the request's anchor itself until the caller's own arrival test says the body is there. It exists because a follow request that admits no candidate is still a request to be with the player, and the walker's substitute-tile fallback for that case was a destination that could be arrived at without achieving anything.
- **A hold** (`HoverHere`) releases the route and drifts around the place the hold began. The orb is never strictly standing still, whatever it is doing, so there is no request that asks the motor for nothing on the brain's behalf: a destination arrived at, a travel intention satisfied, an unreachable goal and a hold all end in the same hover (`Steering/HoverAroundSpot`). The anchor is taken once, where the hold began, and every request that moves the body for another purpose clears it — a hold anchored before a takeover floats the body straight back to where the takeover took it from — and an anchor the body has no clear straight line to is replaced by the body's own centre, because hovering toward a place behind a wall is a body pinned still against that wall. The navigator's wait anchor obeys the same two rules.

`Hold` is the one request that does ask for nothing, and it is for owners that take the body away from the brain — downing and recovery flight — never for anything the brain chooses. Each of these that ends a retained route says who ended it, so the navigator scores the attempt as completed, cancelled, pre-empted or failed rather than folding them together.

**Safety is a layer over whichever request produced the tick, not a fourth request.** After navigation the tick's controls go through `Evade`: the job's own flight — this tick's controls, then what the job's steering would ask from each simulated state, forecast from whichever request produced the tick without touching the navigator or the hover — is simulated forward through the contact against the reflex's predicted-collision predicate, and only if that flight would meet a hit are the controls bent — headings scored by context steering, danger masking and interest choosing, the job's own heading carrying the interest, a heading that goes nowhere scored as the stop, and a stop winning only when it is safer than every moving heading by more than the danger tolerance. Every request method records which producer ran and clears the last verdict, so `LastEvade` never describes an earlier tick's owner, and a request of a different kind from the last tick's releases the hover, so no motion's kept state outlives the request that produced it (`Steering/CLAUDE.md`). The job keeps its activity, its attempt and its hands, and the grant names the tick `evade`. The dodge used to be a request that took the body, beside a combat-spacing search that held it until enemy exposure fell; both suspended the job, and the second parked an unarmed orb beside a zombie for as long as the zombie stayed, because nothing it read ended when the player walked away. Environmental escape, the last response that took the body for its own safety, went when every liquid became air to the orb.

The motor (`TerrariaIntegration/`) then moves its own momentum toward the requested velocity under the law in `Steering/OrbPace` — the change across the heading capped by the turn authority, the change along it by a slower speed change — caps the speed, runs the contact on where that velocity would put the body — push-out along the closest-point normal, the velocity into the wall killed, the slide along it kept — reads which liquid the resolved position touches, and writes the resolved displacement as the NPC's velocity. Momentum is the motor's, not the NPC's: the displacement written after a push-out is not read back as the next tick's velocity, or a push-out would become a bounce. The pace the motor applies is the player's own maximum run speed and run acceleration, each times a multiple the body carries, published every tick to `Steering/OrbPace` so the steering eases and bends against the numbers that will be applied.

## Two things the shape guarantees, and one it does not

Any route with clearance is followable: the body is holonomic, every edge between two usable corners is swept-clear by construction, and the steering's bend cap is what makes that true in practice, so there is no proof step between planning and performing and no class of "the planner proved it and the body could not". The turn authority — the cap on the velocity change across the heading, so the turning radius at speed is the square of the speed over it — was raised until the corridor fixture's steered body kept clearance through a bend; a body that cannot turn inside a corridor's width scrapes its far wall however good the route was. **Turning and speeding up are two caps, and they were one.** A single acceleration capped the change in every direction, so the value a tight bend needed also snapped every departure to full speed in a fifth of a second, and the owner saw a body that moved in bursts. Splitting the change along and across the heading kept the bend and slowed the ramp; only a dodge may spend the turn authority along its heading (`Controls.Burst`), because getting out of the way is the one move that should look sudden.

What it does not guarantee is arrival on an unfinished search, and what it does guarantee instead is that the body never waits in place for one. While a route search runs, the body flies a partial route to the nearest place the search has already reached, which is Recast/Detour's partial path result, and a search that stops at its node limit leaves that route behind; the straight line to the goal is flown instead wherever it is clear. A search that ended without a route is kept for the goal it answered — a proven absence while the body stays inside what it explored, a node limit until the partial route has brought the body materially nearer — so the same question is not asked every tick, and a proven absence is published as such, which the brain bans the spot on so the positioner answers with another. The stuck strikes are untouched by any of this: they count windows without progress, and nothing else. A goal with no free corner beside it is planned to the free corner nearest it within a bounded search. **That bound was kept on 15 September 2026 when its deletion was on the table, because a buried goal still reaches the navigator.** The positioner answers a companion outside the player's region with a corner, which is never buried, but when no usable corner inside the region exists the brain hands the navigator the meeting place's destination instead, and for a player standing still that is the region's centre — a point above his head that underground can sit in cave rock. Deleting the bound would leave such a goal with no end to plan to and the body hovering outside the region. The first build hovered in place until a search came back and then asked again, and in the second play of 0.27.0 that held the orb in open air for two seconds at a time at eight to ten milliseconds of brain a tick; `Tools/NavReplay`'s navigator row replays the recorded plan dump and three built scenes of the same class. What survives is the cost: a goal in a pocket that a search cannot prove sealed within its node limit still costs a capped search per leg of progress. The reach sense in `../Observation/` does not have that problem, because its flood is a disc around the body that exhausts; a route search has a goal and no disc.

## Traps

- A retained search is thrown away only where the world changed under what it read: every search records the box it explored and asks the world's edit record whether an edit landed inside it. A caller that only compares `Revision` restarts on any edit anywhere. `TerrainModel/CLAUDE.md` owns the record.
- `LimitPlanningWork` is a soft deadline shared by every nested search in a tick; its `Unbounded` switch is the one place an offline harness removes machine load from a result, and the brain tick always begins a deadline, so two identical live ticks are not guaranteed to agree. Every headless instrument lifts it, and a fixture whose subject is the deadline says so in its recorded mode. The one exception is a replay of a recorded session, which keeps the deadlines and is handed the play's own answers to every question they ask (`Expired`, and `FreeSpaceSearch.Advance`'s per-expansion check) through `DecisionClock` in `../Selection/Computation/`; any new clock comparison in this folder asks there too. `LimitPlanningWork` has no comparison of its own outside that path: its `Spent(Stopwatch, double)` helper, which read a stopwatch directly and had no caller, was deleted on 25 September 2026 so the rule has no exception to find.
- The corner graph's cardinal and diagonal edges are clear by usability alone, and the swept-circle test is kept for the segments that theorem does not cover: the long segments of smoothing, and the joins from the body to its first corner and from the last corner to the goal. Running the swept test on every edge is what made a screen-sized flood cost eighteen milliseconds a slice.
- `MovementQueries.World` is plugged in by the mod at load and by every headless tool for its scene; asking before it is set throws by design rather than answering from an empty world.
