# Steering — a route, and the momentum that follows it

The navigator owns one route and one search at a time and turns them into a velocity every tick; the steering law is the pure function under it. Nothing here writes the body: the answer is a `Controls`, the velocity the motor should accelerate toward, and the motor is what applies it.

## The law

The body is steered at a point ahead of its projection on the route, which is what makes it lean into a turn and cut its inside rather than track every waypoint, and a body that has drifted off the route is steered at the lookahead from its projection, which pulls it back onto the route rather than back to where it left. The lookahead is a number of ticks of the body's own speed, clamped between a floor and a ceiling, so a fast body looks further down the route and curves into a bend early while a slow one does not cut a corner it is barely moving through. The speed asked for is the cap, lowered toward the goal so the body eases in rather than overshoots — the speed from which only a share of the speed change would bring it to rest over what is left, which is what makes the slowdown gradual rather than a late stop — and lowered into a bend in proportion to how sharply the route turns at the next waypoint within braking distance.

**The motor's law has two caps, one on each axis of the heading, and `OrbPace.Step` is the only place it is written.** The change in velocity across the heading is capped by the turn authority, so the turning radius at speed is the square of the speed over it; the change along the heading is capped by the speed change, which is several times smaller, so the body gathers speed and sheds it over most of a second while still turning tightly. Below a walking pace there is no heading to split along, and the whole change takes the speed-change cap. A dodge sets `Controls.Burst` and may spend the turn authority along its heading as well, which is the one move meant to look sudden. A body that cannot turn inside a corridor's width scrapes the far wall whatever the route says; the corridor fixture's minimum-clearance row is where that showed, the turn authority is what fixed it, and splitting the speed change off it is what stopped that fix from snapping every departure to full speed in twelve ticks. NavReplay's arrival row carries the ramp as a measure and a declared band, and every headless tool integrates this same function rather than a copy.

## A route is smoothed without giving back what the search paid for

Smoothing skips waypoints while the straight segment past them keeps the body clear of walls, from the front, using the contact's own swept test. It also refuses a skip where the chord, at the point standing in for a skipped waypoint, has less clearance than that waypoint had by more than the half tile the corner lattice cannot resolve, because the search paid for the middle of a corridor through its edge cost and a chord from one wall-hugging end of a corridor to the other is clear of walls while spending its whole length nearer them. The first smoother did exactly that and undid the search, and the corridor fixture's smoothed row is what refuses it now.

## Arrival, replanning, and what the navigator reports

Arrival is a radius around the goal, and staying arrived is a wider one. Inside the arrival radius the navigator drops its route and hovers around the goal; it keeps reading arrived until the body leaves the settle radius, which is the arrival radius plus the hover's own reach plus a margin, so a drifting body does not flicker between arrived and travelling. The settle radius, not the arrival radius, is what every region the positioner admits a destination against reserves, so a spot on a region's boundary cannot be hovered around outside it. `Tools/NavReplay`'s arrival row drives that tick by tick, holds it for a hundred and twenty ticks on one search, and requires the body never to sit still for the session reader's still threshold. A goal that drifts a little re-aims the last segment; one that moves materially cancels the attempt and plans afresh. A goal the search has proven unreachable, or one it has not finished, is hovered around a wait anchor taken where the body was when it stopped being able to go, never answered with nothing: nothing is a body standing still. **Every hover anchor dies with the request that made it and is never somewhere the body cannot fly straight to.** The wait anchor is dropped by a new goal, an interrupt and a clear, and replaced by the body's own centre whenever the swept line to it is blocked; the first build kept it across an interrupted wait, and the next unreachable goal pulled the body 230 px back toward the old place and held it at zero against the wall between. `Tools/NavReplay`'s anchors row carries a body across a wall mid-wait to hold that.

## Hovering is a pursuit of a wandering target, and both halves of that were learned by measuring

`HoverAroundSpot` is Reynolds' wander: a target circles the spot along a flattened ellipse, and the rate it circles at is itself a small random walk with a floor, so the path keeps turning and never parks, and it reverses direction now and then so it is not an orbit. Its seed is per instance, so a headless run is reproducible. Two things about it are properties rather than tuning, and each was found by the arrival row's trace rather than by reading.

- **The body pursues the target and does not seek it.** It asks for the target's own motion plus a correction toward it, capped under the settled threshold so a hovering body still reads as at rest. A seek asks for a speed proportional to the gap, and a body a few pixels from its target slowed to a tenth of a pixel a tick every time the target doubled back — a body standing still with extra steps.
- **A target mirrored off a wall reverses its direction of circling with it.** Reflecting a circular path reverses its angular velocity; mirroring only the offset bounced the target straight back into the ceiling it had just been reflected off. When no mirror is clear the target falls back to the opposite point, then half the offset, then the spot itself with the circling reversed.

The anchor is taken once, where the hover began, and not from the body each tick, because an anchor that moves with the body turns the drift into a slow wander away from where it arrived.

## Evading is a bend, and it runs only when the job's own flight would be hit

`EvadeWhileMoving` simulates the controls the job asked for through the contact for a short horizon against the reflex's predicted-collision predicate. If they stay safe it returns them untouched, which is almost every tick. If not, it scores a ring of headings at full speed plus the job's own heading and a stop, as context steering does: danger is how soon a heading is hit, every heading within a small tolerance of the least dangerous is eligible, and among those the one with the most interest wins — the job's heading carries interest, a stop carries negative interest, so the body only stops where stopping is safer than every heading that keeps it going by more than the danger tolerance. Water and lava the body is not immune to count as danger in that simulation exactly as a hit does, because the contact it runs pushes out of solid tiles only; the first build scored against the contact alone and flew a body squeezed between two shots twenty-five pixels into lava. The answer bursts, so it turns away at the turn authority rather than easing. It replaced a reflex that took the body and a spacing search that held it, both of which suspended whatever the body was doing; nothing here touches the route, the activity or the hands.

A route is re-planned when the world is edited under it (the route keeps the box it was planned over and asks the edit record), when the body's immunities no longer match the ones it was planned under, when the segment ahead is no longer clear for the body, or when net displacement over a window stays under a floor — two such windows on one goal are two strikes, and the brain hands the goal back to the positioner, which bans the spot for a while. The status names the difference between a route being flown, a search still pending with the body following the route it had or steering straight where the line is clear, a proven absence, and arrival; every attempt ends completed, cancelled, pre-empted or failed, and the census counts places asked for against places reached so the record reads "asked for forty places, reached eleven" rather than a minute of following as thousands of requests.

## Traps

- `OrbPace` is written by the motor every tick from the live player and defaults to the fallback pace in `Selection/BehaviourWeights.cs`; a headless tool that never writes it runs the body a plain player would produce, and a fixture that changes the pace mid-run changes the braking distance the law reads next tick.
- The lookahead is the point drawn gold on the overlay and written to the record, and it is a steering target and not a waypoint: a body steering at it is often not heading at any point on the route.
- An evade that fires every tick a threat is merely near is a body that never flies its job. The predicate is a predicted collision within the horizon, never proximity, and the job's controls are tested before any heading is scored, so a threat the job's own flight already avoids costs nothing.
- The hover's speed sits under the follow objective's settled threshold on purpose. Raising it past that makes every hovering companion read as still moving, arrival never settles, and keeping company flips back to reunion beside a player who has not moved.
