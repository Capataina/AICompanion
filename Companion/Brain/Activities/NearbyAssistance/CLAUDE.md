# Nearby assistance — lighting, collection and keeping company

Keeping company is one ordinary activity in NearbyAssistance. It offers positive value while the player is alive and competes with other useful work; it is not merely a fallback for an empty board.

```
NearbyAssistance/
├─ CLAUDE.md           activity purpose, method choice and evidence limits
├─ KeepCompany.cs      reunion, resting, nearby movement and sealed-pocket roaming
├─ CollectNearbyItems.cs known drops and uncertain pot-content opportunities
├─ LightUsefulArea.cs   supplied permanent lighting offered through native placement rules
└─ PerformNearbyWorldWork.cs shared pot/torch discovery, approach and interaction methods
```

Far reunion pull is capped at `KeepCompanyFarCap` (0.8) so a proven job further across the screen can still win; the hard leash at fly-home distance is not that cap.

Preparation compares the existing reunion urgency with the value of staying locally active. Execution requests WithPlayer when reunion wins, or rests and selects small nearby movements otherwise. A sealed pocket uses the existing bounded roaming/retry policy. These are methods of the same purpose and preserve its activity identity. Entry and reunion discard old local destinations, so resuming after an interruption cannot revive a stroll in the previous neighbourhood.

Every request passes through position selection and shared movement. Only an explicit reunion request can qualify for distant recovery; a local stroll near player coordinates cannot. Shared safety can interrupt any method, and compatible shooting and held light remain independent hands work.

Reunion asks `../../Infrastructure/Position/ChooseMeetingPlace.cs` for a place on the player's apparent journey priced by the companion's own routes and requests that exact tile; a paused or working player is met where they stand. Leaving the activity, or switching to the local method, releases the meeting place so its reason and flood never describe another activity's movement. Local movement either rests or strolls to a goal chosen for being a safe place to keep company. A pick traces the walk once each way from the feet across the calm band either side of the player, one standing tile per column, and keeps the walked tiles that sit within a few rows of the player's feet and are standable and not the rim of a drop (floor under their own column, and under each neighbouring column within one walk step of that floor), lie a short walk from the feet, are inside the positioner's returnable region (so never beyond a drop the body cannot climb back from), have no lava in any column the body can cover, keep the head out of liquid in each of those columns, carry predicted enemy exposure within a BehaviourWeights limit, and are a walk from the feet: every column between has a supported, hazard-free standing tile within one row of the column before. One of those is taken at random, and a pick that finds none rests. The body is wider than a tile, which is why the columns either side are read: a standable rim tile beside a lava pool puts part of the body over the lava. Three failures shaped the rim and walk rules. `BodyPhysics.Stand` accepts a body overhanging an edge, so a tile over a pit beside its wall reads as standable and was chosen as a goal. The first answer asked for floor on the same row under both neighbours, and it failed as a property: a same-row test cannot tell a one-row step from a twelve-row drop, so on a floor rising one row per column (blocks or either slope style) every tile was refused and an idle companion rested for a whole window with no goal. The rim is now defined by the walk's own step, one constant that the walk rule steps by and the rim rule calls anything deeper than: a neighbour one row up or down is ground a walk crosses, anything further down is a drop. And a goal that is safe and returnable can still lie beyond a gap, pool or drop that the route jumps, so the first version put the body in lava and in the drop on the way to safe far-side goals. A stroll is company, not a traversal, so it never needs a jump. A held goal lasts its timer unless the player moves away from it or it stops passing the same check, so it neither re-rolls every tick nor survives an enemy walking onto it. Resting is a choice with its own timer, not a failed search. There is no hop in place: the earlier method hopped at random while resting or strolling, which is movement for show, and the only jumps local movement now produces are the ones a route to a goal needs. It does not establish final contextual separation policy or explore safely in every terrain, and a route to a safe goal may still cross lava when the planner is allowed to price it. There is no separate exploration activity.

A companion in a proven sealed pocket uses local movement without forgetting reunion. The coordinator exposes that state on the action context and cycles local roaming with reunion retries using BehaviourWeights. Keeping company's local method requests Roam and its reunion method requests WithPlayer. An incomplete path is not proof of a sealed pocket, and a model-closed region is not proof of physical impossibility in Terraria.

Collection prepares a fitting known drop and a bounded pot-content opportunity, compares them with the same shared factors as the parent, and publishes the chosen raw value and forecast. Publishing an already-discounted value would charge the trip twice. Neither comparison repeats discovery. Known drops bind the item, type and captured position, revalidating world-slot membership, availability and cargo acceptance before movement, and execution holds for a tick when the drop has moved more than a tile since it was proven, so the next preparation proves it where it now lies instead of walking to where it was. Each fitting drop is asked about in distance order: a standable contact pose, the one nearest the companion where the body grown by the contact pickup's reach touches the item, and then whether that pose is in the two-way reach region. A standable tile beside a drop proves none of that, and the reach that mined an ore says nothing about the pit its drop fell into. Every drop is asked, because membership of a flood already run costs a hash lookup: a farther drop is never offered before a nearer one has been asked about, and that ordering now holds by construction rather than by a bound arranged to produce it. A pose the flood has not claimed yet publishes an unresolved offer at zero value.

**Three things went when the question stopped being a search, and the reason is one reason.** A per-drop verdict cache keyed on pose, terrain revision and a recheck interval; a cap on how many new reach questions one preparation could ask; and the marginal return, which refused a drop only where the companion could reach the player from where it stood and could not from the drop, so a companion already cut off was not called stranded by going one step further. A cache exists to make an expensive answer reusable and a bound exists to ration an expensive answer, and neither has anything to ration now. The marginal rule went for a different reason and it is a real loss rather than a simplification: two-way membership asks the absolute question — can the body come back at all — so a companion inside a sealed pocket refuses drops inside that pocket, which the marginal rule allowed. That is the ruling, and the sealed-pocket case is what it costs. The offer is valued for the quantity the cargo can take (the loot sense's value scaled by the share of the stack's value that fits). Its forecast is the straight-line distance to the contact pose over walking speed, the unit mining and chopping use, and not a sum of the route's walk-step durations: a drop behind a detour is forecast as near as one in the open, and a change to how walk steps are timed does not reach it. Pot contents are forecast the same way, from the pot. A detached object can remain active after its slot is replaced; it is no longer a world drop. A newly observed replacement receives its own object identity. An arbitrary same-object mutation without a lifecycle signal is not detected as a new generation.

Pot contents remain unknown until the native operation produces them. Their prior value and handling allowance live in BehaviourWeights; approach contributes a geometric estimate rather than a certified pickup/return duration. Unknown contents require empty cargo space conservatively, whereas a known drop may fit an existing player or bag stack. Native home protection covers the pot's footprint and is rechecked at the operation, along with pot policy and capacity. The current method uses native KillTile at interaction range; weapon-driven breaking and prediction of the resulting drop locations remain separate implementation obligations.

Pot discovery and approach reuse PerformNearbyWorldWork. Dropping a method candidate does not release the collection activity's admission; the activity owner handles that lifecycle. Actual known-drop pickup remains the NPC's independent contact operation and can occur while another behaviour is active. A completed worksite can widen the allowance for its known drops, without granting nearby unknown pots the same allowance. Breaking a pot does not certify any pickup or obligate a subsequent trip.

**Lighting works a dark area rather than visiting it.** It reads `../../Infrastructure/Observation/`'s light field for the dark regions in range, nearest first by the nearest dark air in each, and offers the tiles in and around them — around every dark sample rather than around one point of the region, because a torch needs a wall or floor to attach to and the one a large region touches can be at its far end. Every region in range contributes, not only the nearest, since a band of light across a cave splits the dark into a near half that may be sealed air and a far half full of surfaces, and stopping at the first would leave the companion standing in the dark beside a wall it could light. The executor then takes the first site whose access it can prove, as it does for pots.

**What keeps lighting from outranking proven work is the band, not an ordering fixture, and the band guarantees less than it first appears.** Lighting's value rises with the nominated region's dark-sample count to a capped ceiling and is multiplied when the player carries no light of his own, and the two constants are chosen so the lit ceiling is exactly the flat value lighting carried before it read a region at all, with only the unlit case rising above it and never to 1. That is what makes lighting an ordinary action: every ordinary raw value lives in 0..1, the running activity is multiplied by the commitment bonus, and an expression that cannot reach 1 therefore cannot interrupt a committed job at any input — which an earlier arithmetic reaching 1.12 could, and did not deserve to. What the band does not promise is that lighting loses to mining or chopping in any particular scene: those values are computed from the ore or trunk in front of the companion and are often well below lighting's ceiling, so a large dark cavern with the player carrying no light can rightly outbid a nearly worthless vein. The guarantee is about the ceiling and about interruption, and it is deliberately structural: an ordering fixture between two scene-dependent values would pin one arrangement and read as a rule.

After a torch is placed the activity keeps its job the way mining keeps a vein: `ContinueAfterInteraction` clears the search wait so the next preparation re-nominates from where the body now stands, and the ninety-tick wait applies only when a search found nothing. Before this, a search found the nearest site anywhere on the screen, placed one torch and handed the tick back, so a cave was lit a torch per visit with a walk back to the player between each.

Darkness is open air the engine has computed, never packed tiles, and that ruling lives in the light sense so both carrying and placing inherit it. A site is refused unless its own neighbourhood's mean brightness is below the dark level — a neighbourhood rather than the tile, because a torch lights an area and a dark tile pressed against a lit room needs none; sampled from the engine at a finer stride than the field's lattice, which cannot resolve a lit patch narrower than its own step. Unmeasured is refused alongside lit: a place nobody has read is not a proven dark place. The companion's own torch is discounted by the field, so a lit bubble around the feet does not hide a dark pocket beyond it. Underground or at night is no longer the test.

Its four refusals are separate facts and no longer share one name: no dark region in range and no region inside the work radius are absences of work; a region whose stand the flood has not claimed yet is an unanswered search (`dark-region-tile-not-yet-known-reachable`, unresolved at zero value, which is how the ruling that optional work does not start on an unanswered search reaches lighting); a region the two-way flood has proven it cannot come home from is `interaction-site-has-no-return`, the same name the round-trip proof used for the same fact; and a region with no tile the game's placer accepts is a geometry problem where the companion can stand. Lighting learns which of the last two a site was from `NoteApproach`, which the executor calls with the approach verdict for every site it walks past; a shared fifth name, `interaction-stand-not-yet-known-reachable`, is what a subclass with no opinion reports in the third case.

`Tools/EngineReplay/Assistance/VerifyLightAndReachSenses.cs` holds the acceptance row for the whole arrangement: a sealed cavity of dark air nineteen tiles from the companion and a reachable dark floor thirty-seven tiles away, where lighting must offer the far one on its first search with the offer reading `Usable/reachable-interaction`. It fails on the exact starvation signature when a three-site budget is planted back, which is how it is known to be discriminating rather than merely green. Its value scales with the nominated region's dark-sample count, capped so a cavern cannot outbid protecting the player, and rises when the player carries no light of his own. Enablement is the torch setting plus a torch in supply: a locally lit disc is not "already lit" for the method. Lighting reads native Smart Cursor recommendations through `../../Infrastructure/Interactions/Torch/` for attachment and spacing, and may request a ground jump proved to reach the interaction and land safely. Native placement and resource consumption retain their own owner. An interruption invalidates an interaction jump; resumption cannot reuse proof from a previous take-off pose.

Both lighting and pots reach a site above standing reach by a hop, in mining's order: the current pose, a jump from the current pose, a standing approach, and only on a standing No `FindToolAccess.HopApproach` from a take-off the region claims. The hop asks the region *before* it simulates the jump, which is the reverse of the order it used when the reach question was a bounded search: a pose the body cannot walk to is not a take-off however well it jumps, and the simulation is now the expensive half of the pair. A hop scan that answers No keeps the site out of discovery under the same terrain-revision wait as a trip with no return. Execution walks to the take-off under the ordinary progress window, and only on the take-off's own feet tile, on the ground and at rest, re-proves the jump from the live pose and jumps; its own flight is held for the proof's flight length and then ends as `interaction-jump-did-not-deliver`. A take-off that fails its proof at rest on its own tile is lost: the site is deferred and the attempt fails with `interaction-jump-lost-take-off`. The tile rather than a body width is the arrival test because the proof is made at the tile's centre, and a pot take-off proved there failed from one tile short, which a body-width tolerance read as arrived and declared lost before the walk reached it. `MineOre` still uses the body-width tolerance; the two are not yet one rule. Pot and torch operations recheck home protection at mutation rather than treating discovery permission as permanent.

The shared interaction executor classifies a found candidate as usable and asks its subclass why a refused method is refused: lighting separates the torch setting (a prohibition) from an empty supply (known-unusable), and overrides `SearchRefusal` to split the four exits above where a pot still answers `no-candidate-in-search-window`; pot collection separates the pot setting from a full bag.

Three hooks on the shared executor exist for a subclass that knows more than the executor does: `ContinueAfterInteraction` keeps a job after a productive interaction, `SearchRefusal` names why a search found nothing, and `NoteApproach` is handed the approach verdict for a site the search walked past, so lighting can say which of its four exits that was where pot collection has nothing to add. None of the three is opt-in machinery around a cheaper proof; the proof is the same for both methods now.

**The round trip and the reach sense are not two ways of asking, and only one of them survives.** Membership of the two-way region *is* what returnable means in this project — everywhere the body can go and come home from — so a stand the region claims needs nothing proved about it, and the pair of fresh route searches `MovementQueries.RoundTrip` ran per site proved it a second time at a hundred times the price. It carries one gap, named rather than hidden, and the gap now covers pot collection as well as lighting: the two-way region holds no breath envelope, so a trip across a flooded passage the round trip would have refused for breath can be offered. Collection publishes the classification of whichever method's raw value it publishes, and names a zero-valued known drop as usable rather than absent. One interaction is the whole job, so an attempt with a credited productive effect is complete and the companion's own — a placed torch without measured coverage, a broken pot with unobserved contents — while an abandoned approach, a lost jump take-off, an undelivered jump or a refused native call is a failed method and a target that stopped qualifying is invalid. Those give-ups are cleared when an attempt opens. A drop's attempt concludes from what the cargo actually received from that item object after the walk began, read from the inventory's transfer record, because a drop leaving the world is also what the player's pickup and despawning do: all of it received is the companion's own completion, part received with the rest gone is shared, gone with nothing received is invalid, and part received with the rest still lying there is partial. Terraria also merges two nearby drops of one type in the world, deactivating the one in the higher slot, and mined veins drop items close enough to merge; a walked-to drop that vanished with another drop of its type within the native merge distance of where it was last seen was absorbed rather than taken, so nothing received concludes as an attempt replaced with the items still lying there (`drop-merged-into-world-drop`) and part received as partial, and the absorbing drop is offered as its own attempt on the next preparation. The check follows the native distance but not its doubling during a flood of new items, and it cannot tell a merge from the player taking the drop while an unmerged drop of the same type lies that close, which native merging rarely leaves for long. Keeping company classifies as usable while the player lives and concludes every attempt as executed, never complete.

A lighting or pot trip is offered only where the companion can come back. The shared search asks sites nearest first by the method's own cost and stops at the first whose access is proven, and the access query answers from the two-way region, so "can come back" is what the answer already means. A proven refusal defers the site until the terrain changes or a BehaviourWeights wait passes, and names itself `interaction-site-has-no-return` — the round trip's own name for the same fact, kept because the fact did not change when the evidence for it did. A verdict of not-yet-known defers nothing and is re-asked, which is the asymmetry the next paragraph is about. The verdict is taken at discovery and not again during the walk; a terrain change that removes the way back after discovery is caught only by the access and candidate checks execution already makes.

**This folder was `MovementQueries.RoundTrip`'s last production caller, so the query is now dead code and is left standing deliberately.** Its only remaining consumers are three fixtures: two that use it as a scene premise, and `VerifyRoundTripEvidence`, which tests the query itself and therefore now asserts a contract no shipped code depends on. Deleting it belongs to whoever owns `../../Infrastructure/Movement`, and the decision is not obvious — the breath envelope it carries is the one thing the two-way region does not, so a future consumer that needs a breath-aware trip would want it back rather than rewritten. Recorded here rather than fixed here, so the next reader of either folder finds the fact instead of rediscovering it.

Two problems went with the round trip and are worth recording as solved rather than as absent. Its walker search ran under whichever one-way rule the previous tick's request left set, so it once certified a dark pit reached only by a drop after a reunion tick, the Exact request then refused to plan it, and the trip stalled until its progress window deferred it; the region is flooded under one rule of its own, so the verdict no longer depends on the last request. And it cost two fresh route searches per site, which is what every bound in this file was ever rationing.

The executor's rules are also asked incidentally, by `../../Infrastructure/Grants/ConsiderIncidentalInteractions.cs`, which holds its own library instances of both methods rather than the chooser's activities. `FindIncidentalTarget` runs the method's enablement and then its candidate check over the tiles within actual reach of the current pose, nearest first, and never touches discovery state or asks for a route; `PerformIncidental` calls the same native `Perform`, which rechecks permission at mutation, with a note that the interaction event's detail begins with (`incidental;during=<activity>;activity-id=<id>;credited-to=none;`). An incidental effect never reaches `RecordWork`, so no attempt counts it as productive.

**A bound belongs on the expensive call, not on the proof it happens to feed — and this loop has now learnt that twice, in opposite directions.** The first time, the site count was charged against the round-trip proof, because that was the expensive thing every candidate reached; opting lighting into the reach sense made that proof nearly free and, by charging the counter only on the route it no longer took, removed the loop's only bound. The second time, the count was moved onto the approach query and the reach sense was given to every method, which made the *reach* half of the approach free too — and left the count rationing a pose scan, which is what the approach still costs. Measured on the wholly dark floor, all three inside the default suite: a budget of three sites, 7.4 ms; no bound at all, 25.5 ms; the tick's own planning deadline, 13.4 ms. The general shape is the same both times: when a cheaper proof replaces a dearer one, find what the dearer one's bound was standing in front of, because a saving that deletes a limit is not a saving.

**The count was the wrong bound and is not coming back, however the cost moves again.** It rationed by an ordering, and rationing a nearest-first scan by three sites is exactly what let three sites hide every site behind them for 79% of the 2026-09-14 session. The deadline rations the same cost without an ordering, and a scan it cuts is reported Unresolved and retried in a rescore, which is what the sites past the cut actually are. The scan that builds the candidate list reads the same deadline for the same reason.

**An answer that has not arrived and an answer of "no" are different results, and a search over a growing flood is correct only if it treats them differently.** A stand the region proves unreachable is remembered, so the next search advances past it; a stand it has not claimed yet is re-asked, because deferring an unfinished flood writes a site off for the whole deferral on evidence that does not exist. Collapsing the two is what made the old bounded search worse than no bound at all — it spent every budget on the same nearest sites and the answer never arrived, where the unbounded version at least reached the truth eventually. And the reason for a proven refusal has to outlive the scan that found it: once a site is deferred the next scan never builds it, so an unnamed refusal decays from "the companion cannot come back from there", which is knowledge, into "the placer accepted nothing", which is an absence. The executor now supplies that name itself rather than leaving it to a subclass, because pot collection had no opinion and was getting the absence.

One measurement rule came out of the first round and still binds: a figure from the warmed default suite and a figure from a cold standalone run of the same fixture differ by more than tenfold on identical code, so every number above states which it is, and a before taken one way is never compared with an after taken the other.

**Any timing from this suite is only comparable to another timing taken the same way.** A fixture run alone through its own flag pays JIT compilation for the whole approach and route-search path on its first lighting preparation; the same fixture inside the default suite has had that paid by the dozens of fixtures before it, and the two differ by more than tenfold on the same code. Quote the harness beside every figure, and never compare a before taken one way with an after taken the other.

**A search that could not answer retries in a rescore; one that answered "nothing here" waits the full cadence.** The long wait exists so a fruitless search is not repeated every tick, and those are not the same result. The reach region is flooded incrementally and takes a hundred-odd ticks to settle after a world change, so the first searches after one refuse on "not yet known" — correctly, since optional work does not start on an unanswered search — while keeping company goes on walking the body. Waiting the full cadence then re-asks from wherever it has wandered: on the J08 shelf that was thirteen columns along, so the first answerable search picked a tile beside the shelf instead of its top, needed a take-off it could not hold, and closed `interaction-jump-lost-take-off` repeatedly. The property, which will be met again anywhere a per-candidate route search is replaced by a shared incremental one: an answer that has not arrived and an answer of "no" are different results, and a retry policy treating them alike converts a slower answer into a worse decision, because the scene moves while the evidence is gathered.

Candidate search and deferred-approach cleanup run during Prepare. Comparison reads captured values and targets without invoking native torch rules or repeating a jump proof. Execution retains validation and progress accounting; a comparison cannot count as another interaction attempt. The shared executor is not a fourth selectable activity or a separate movement writer.

Method discovery clears its own candidate without releasing the activity's continuation allowance. Collection can still have a valid known drop when pot discovery is disabled. The shared activity owner releases admission on replacement; failure of one method cannot erase another method's admitted purpose. Execution checks enabled policy and candidate validity before approach or mutation.

An ineligible-to-eligible transition refreshes discovery immediately. Preparation and execution share this eligibility observation: permission revoked after preparation must invalidate the same discovery state as permission revoked before preparation. Otherwise a supply or policy change can clear a target while its old deadline prevents fresh work. Unchanged eligibility retains bounded discovery cadence; per-target physical deferrals remain separate evidence.

Keeping company is the only activity that yields to the player's interference footprint (`PlayerSense.Interference`). When its resting body, or the goal of a stroll, overlaps the footprint, its local method issues an ordinary follow request anchored on the player instead of holding, and position selection's occupancy share picks a spot out of the way; reunion's own follow request reads the same share. Resting that overlaps nothing is untouched, and mining, chopping, lighting, collection, protection and shared safety never read the footprint, which is what "courtesy is priced against work and safety" means in code: a companion swinging a pick where the player is building keeps swinging. `Tools/EngineReplay/Assistance/VerifyCourtesy.cs` holds a block aimed at the companion (moves), a bow or torch aimed at it (identical trajectory to an empty hand) and a one-body-tall passage (never left standing in the player's way).

A change in the player's reach (`FindToolAccess.Reach`) is the other thing that refreshes discovery immediately. Everything the executor retains about a site was derived under the old reach: the held stand, the wait before the next search, the failed-approach deferrals, and the proven refusals kept until the terrain changes (no way back from the working pose that reach chose, and no take-off for a hop that reach needed). So the executor releases every one of those stores on the same comparison and searches on that preparation. Before this, a smaller reach walked lighting or pot work to a stand it could no longer swing from until the progress window deferred the site as a failed approach, and a larger reach waited out the cadence. The no-return store was added later with its own terrain-revision key and was not released, so a pot whose only pose at a small reach was on a pit floor stayed refused for the store's wait after a larger reach could work it from the rim. The property is that a capability is one key every retained refusal is released on: a capability added to one store's key and not the other's refuses under the old value. Terrain revision stays a key of the no-return store alone, because only route proofs depend on it. Known drops are picked up by body contact and do not read tool reach at all.
