# Nearby assistance — lighting, collection and keeping company

Keeping company is one ordinary activity in NearbyAssistance. It offers positive value while the player is alive and competes with other useful work; it is not merely a fallback for an empty board.

```
NearbyAssistance/
├─ CLAUDE.md           activity purpose, method choice and evidence limits
├─ KeepCompany.cs      reunion, resting, nearby movement and sealed-pocket roaming
├─ CollectNearbyItems.cs known drops and uncertain pot-content opportunities
├─ LightUsefulArea.cs   permanent lighting with the companion's own torches, offered through native placement rules
└─ PerformNearbyWorldWork.cs shared pot/torch discovery, approach and interaction methods
```

**Keeping company is the fallback for when nothing is worth doing, and its value is built so it cannot be anything else.** Hovering inside the player's region is worth the wander floor, which is what doing nothing is worth. Rejoining is worth the rejoin pull — zero anywhere inside the region, rising with the gap beyond its edge — or the regroup urgency, whichever is larger, and never more than the far cap in `Weights.KeepCompanyFarCap`; only the hard leash at fly-home distance, a separate step, takes everything. The owner set the cap at half the value a job can have after the third orb play of 15 September 2026, where keeping company was chosen on 60.3% of the session and a slime above an idle player went unhunted: the earlier rulings that day had put the cap at the full value, which let rejoining out-bid any job anywhere on screen. The same slope, uncapped, at a job's stand against the region carried along the player's travel, is the separation every job pays in `../../Infrastructure/Selection/CLAUDE.md`, so a job and rejoining are priced on one curve and can never disagree about how far is far; the cap is on what rejoining is worth, and a job is worth nothing where the curve reaches fly-home distance.

Every nearby interaction now reports its trip — the flight to the site and a moment to use it — through `PerformNearbyWorldWork.ForecastTicks`. It inherited zero until 15 September 2026, so lighting was compared as though a torch trip cost no time while every other activity paid for its own, which is how a slime hunt lost to a torch site six hundred pixels away.

**There is no pull anywhere inside the player's region, and none of the stand-ins for one survive.** The region is where the companion lives and moves about, so a pull inside it would only drag a busy body toward a centre nobody asked it to sit at. Three things used to raise one and each is gone for a reason that transfers. A central gradient — a pull that grew toward the region's edge while the player travelled — existed because the old box was a place a body arrived at and stopped, so a moving player had to be a reason to move; a body inside the region is now carried by the region's own velocity, which is the reason to move without being a reason to leave work. And two floors of 0.3, one while the body could not see the player and one while it sat in a passage he walked down, were hand-written proxies for distance: a companion behind a pillar inside his region is with him, and courtesy is answered where the companion's place in the region is chosen. Outside, the pull is measured on the gap beyond the rectangle's edge, which starts at zero exactly where the region ends, so the curve has no step at the edge by construction rather than by two halves agreeing; the regroup urgency beside it is zero inside the region for the same reason, whatever a route round a thin wall would cost. `Tools/EngineReplay/Assistance/VerifyCompanyIsTheFallback.cs` holds the fallback from the chooser's side — a slime above an idle player hunted, a slime past the new-job radius left, rejoining capped short of the leash, and the floor with no sight inside the region — and `VerifyResponsiveFollowing`'s curve row samples the pull flat inside, stepless out through the edge and capped.

**Which method is in force is decided by where the body is, and neither method can cut the other.** Outside the player's region keeping company rejoins it; inside it, the companion moves about the region with him; a stranded body roams its pocket unless rejoining is worth more than roaming. Both methods ask for the same kind of request — a follow, aimed at the meeting place outside and at the region's centre inside — so a change of method is not a change of request kind and cancels nothing in flight. That retired the machinery that stood here until 15 September 2026: a rescore's wait before any change of method, exemptions from it outside the region and inside the player's interference footprint, a settled-arrival test and a check that the region's centre had stopped sliding. Every piece of it existed because the two methods used to issue different kinds and a change of kind cut the body's move — a Hold one tick after a take-off at tick 8128 of the 2026-09-14 capture, and thirty-nine method changes in six hundred ticks at three times the player's speed on 15 September 2026, where each clause of the settle rule was found by `Tools/EngineReplay/Movement/VerifyResponsiveFollowing.cs`'s stopped-player row. The width the wait gave the region's edge now comes from the intent sense's inside latch (`../../Infrastructure/Observation/CLAUDE.md`), and the stopped-player row still holds its ceiling of one change.

**Every "near the player" radius here is measured to the intent region rather than to his body, and `../CLAUDE.md` owns that rule for all seven activities.** Two things are local to this folder. Lighting centres its own work radius on the region as well, which is a second read and not the same test as the shared radius: it decides which darkness is worth lighting, where the nearest-first ordering below still starts from the companion's own centre, because that is about which darkness it can fly to. And keeping company is the one activity that reads the region for more than a radius — its pull is measured on the gap beyond the region, above, and its method on whether the body is inside it. Threat, safety and protection readers deliberately keep reading the player's body, because danger to him is about where he is and not about where he is going.

Preparation prices rejoining against the wander floor for the offer's value, and the method follows the body's place, above. Execution asks for the meeting place outside the region and for the region's centre inside it, where the brain moves the body about the region rather than choosing a place in it (`../../Infrastructure/Movement/Steering/CLAUDE.md`). A sealed pocket uses the existing bounded roaming/retry policy. These are methods of the same purpose and preserve its activity identity; nothing is retained between them, and the meeting place is released the moment the method is not rejoining.

Every request passes through position selection and shared movement. Only an explicit reunion request can qualify for distant recovery; a local hold near player coordinates cannot. Recovery or downing can interrupt any method, a predicted hit only bends it, and compatible shooting and held light remain independent hands work.

Reunion asks `../../Infrastructure/Position/ChooseMeetingPlace.cs` for a place on the player's apparent journey priced by the companion's own routes and requests that exact point; a paused or working player is met where they stand. Leaving the activity, or switching to the inside method, releases the meeting place so its reason and flood never describe another activity's movement. **Inside the region the companion moves about it with the player, and neither the stroll nor the hold that came before it survives.** The stroll, deleted on 15 September 2026, picked a random safe cell near the player and flew there on a timer, alternating with resting still; the hold that replaced it the same day hovered about wherever company settled; and the owner's ruling from the third orb play that evening is that inside the region there is no place to be, only the region to move through, easing to the front, falling back, rising and dipping. Both earlier forms were a body that stopped somewhere, which is what the ruling refuses. Two things the stroll did by accident were kept on purpose, and both still stand. It grew the reach flood as a side effect, which was the only thing that created a companion's first flood; the positioner refreshes the flood while the companion accompanies the player, and the accompanying motion reads it. And its hazard test kept an idle companion out of lava; the accompanying target is refused wherever the circle cannot sweep to it or a finished flood proved it out of reach. Lava stopped being a hazard on 15 September 2026, when every liquid became air to the orb, and `Tools/EngineReplay/Assistance/VerifyCompanyLocalMotion.cs` now holds a lava pool and a submerging pool as space the body may use, with every destination a place it fits and can come back from. Courtesy lives in the same motion: the tiles the player is building on or walking down are refused as places to move to, where a request for a fresh spot near his feet stood until 15 September 2026. There is no separate exploration activity.

A companion in a proven sealed pocket uses local movement without forgetting reunion. The coordinator exposes that state on the action context and cycles local roaming with reunion retries using BehaviourWeights. Keeping company's local method requests Roam and its reunion method requests WithPlayer. An incomplete path is not proof of a sealed pocket, and a model-closed region is not proof of physical impossibility in Terraria.

Collection prepares a fitting known drop and a bounded pot-content opportunity, compares them with the same shared factors as the parent, and publishes the chosen raw value and forecast. Publishing an already-discounted value would charge the trip twice. Neither comparison repeats discovery. Known drops bind the item, type and captured landing, revalidating world-slot membership, availability and cargo acceptance before movement, and execution holds for a tick when that landing has moved more than a tile since it was proven, so the next preparation proves it where it will now lie instead of walking to where it was.

**A drop is priced where it will land, not where it hangs.** The contact pose used to be searched around the item's instantaneous bottom within a tile, so an item still in the air had no pose at all — 431 rows of the 2026-09-14 capture were refused as `drop-has-no-contact-pose` — and the moved-drop guard then held the body on every tick of every fall, because an item in free fall has always moved more than a tile since it was proven. Both are one mistake: an object in flight priced as an object at rest. The forecast steps the game's own arc from `Item.UpdateItem` and `MoveInWorld` — gravity per tick capped at a maximum fall speed, horizontal velocity damped and zeroed below a floor, and in liquid the game's own ladder of shimmer, then honey, then water, each with its own gravity and cap —

**A liquid slows a falling item twice over, and the second half is the one that decides landing X.** Besides the gravity and the cap, `Item.UpdateItem` keeps a separate wet velocity and steps the position by a *share* of the velocity whenever the item is wet — half in water, three eighths in shimmer, a quarter in honey — and it captures that share from the velocity as it stood at the top of the tick, before that tick's gravity. Taking the wet gravity and then stepping the whole velocity covers twice the ground in water and four times in honey. The landing *row* survives that error, because a drop falling in a pool still lands on the same floor, which is why a row asserting where a drop lands can be green while the forecast is badly wrong; landing X does not survive it, and X is what decides which tile the companion walks to. Whether the share applies at all is gated on the wet flag alone, as the game gates it, rather than on whichever liquid supplied the gravity.

**A contract for a forecast has to read the forecast at the moment it is forecasting, and a hold count cannot stand in for that.** The moved-drop guard holds the body whenever the item has moved more than a tile from where it was proven, so a forecast re-taken every tick and wrong by the same smooth amount every tick produces no holds at all — measured: the wrong arithmetic above scored zero holds on every liquid. The assertion is the offer's own target at release, which is the landing the drop was priced at and the only moment the whole remaining fall is being predicted rather than observed, compared against where the drop actually comes to rest. Comparing the pose after the drop has landed proves nothing, because by then there is nothing left to forecast.

**Honey is slow enough that an ordinary fall does not resolve inside the horizon.** At a quarter of its velocity and a third of water's cap, an item descends about a fifth as fast as it does in air, so a twelve-tile fall in honey exceeds the forecast's horizon and the offer reports `drop-landing-undecided`. That is the middle answer working rather than a defect, and it is worth knowing before an undecided honey drop is read as one.

The forecast holds the item's liquid flags for the whole fall, so a drop that *enters* liquid partway down is forecast dry; the tile world exposes water and lava but not honey or shimmer, so re-reading the liquid kind per step would need a query that folder does not have. Named here rather than hidden, because a wrong liquid is worse than a known-dry one.

The forecast lands the item on the top of the first supporting row its track enters, asking the tile world rather than raw solidity, since a slope is not the surface its solidity suggests. Every row the bottom crosses in a tick is tested, because at the capped fall speed an item covers most of a tile a tick and a sampled check would pass through a floor. A fall that has not resolved inside the forecast horizon is `drop-landing-undecided`, unresolved rather than absent, which is the same middle answer the senses give: a drop still falling past the horizon is going somewhere the companion should not be committing to. The forecast reads tiles and the item's own numbers and runs no route search, which the navigation boundary script enforces here as everywhere under `Activities`. Each fitting drop is asked about in distance order: a hoverable contact cell, the one nearest the companion where the body grown by the contact pickup's reach touches the item, and then whether that cell is in the reach region. A free cell beside a drop proves none of that, and the reach that mined an ore says nothing about the pool its drop fell into. Every drop is asked, because membership of a flood already run costs a hash lookup: a farther drop is never offered before a nearer one has been asked about, and that ordering now holds by construction rather than by a bound arranged to produce it. A pose the flood has not claimed yet publishes an unresolved offer at zero value.

**Three things went when the question stopped being a search, and the reason is one reason.** A per-drop verdict cache keyed on pose, terrain revision and a recheck interval; a cap on how many new reach questions one preparation could ask; and the marginal return, which refused a drop only where the companion could reach the player from where it stood and could not from the drop, so a companion already cut off was not called stranded by going one step further. A cache exists to make an expensive answer reusable and a bound exists to ration an expensive answer, and neither has anything to ration now. The marginal rule went for a different reason and it is a real loss rather than a simplification: two-way membership asks the absolute question — can the body come back at all — so a companion inside a sealed pocket refuses drops inside that pocket, which the marginal rule allowed. That is the ruling, and the sealed-pocket case is what it costs. The offer is valued for the quantity the cargo can take (the loot sense's value scaled by the share of the stack's value that fits). Its forecast is the straight-line distance to the contact cell over the orb's pace, the unit mining and chopping use, and not the route's own cost: a drop behind a detour is forecast as near as one in the open. Pot contents are forecast the same way, from the pot. A detached object can remain active after its slot is replaced; it is no longer a world drop. A newly observed replacement receives its own object identity. An arbitrary same-object mutation without a lifecycle signal is not detected as a new generation.

Pot contents remain unknown until the native operation produces them. Their prior value and handling allowance live in BehaviourWeights; approach contributes a geometric estimate rather than a certified pickup/return duration. Unknown contents require empty cargo space conservatively, whereas a known drop may fit an existing player or bag stack. Native home protection covers the pot's footprint and is rechecked at the operation, along with pot policy and capacity. The current method uses native KillTile at interaction range; weapon-driven breaking and prediction of the resulting drop locations remain separate implementation obligations.

Pot discovery and approach reuse PerformNearbyWorldWork. Dropping a method candidate does not release the collection activity's admission; the activity owner handles that lifecycle. Actual known-drop pickup remains the NPC's independent contact operation and can occur while another behaviour is active. A completed worksite can widen the allowance for its known drops, without granting nearby unknown pots the same allowance. Breaking a pot does not certify any pickup or obligate a subsequent trip.

**Lighting puts a torch wherever the player's own smart cursor could put one in the dark, inside the work area.** The search scans the work area — the player's intent region's centre out to the work radius each way — clipped to the tiles the engine computed light for. A tile survives the scan when it is empty and dry, the torch step's attachment filter passes it (`../../Infrastructure/Interactions/Torch/`), home protection allows it, no refusal of its stand still stands, and its own light reads dark through `LightSense.ReadForPlacement` — the world's own light there, which nothing anybody carries is part of, so a tile under a player's torch reads by the cave's light like any other, while an unread tile is refused under `light-unread` and counts toward the unanswered-search refusal rather than toward "no dark tile"; and never a tile daylight reaches, refused as `open-to-daylight` however dark the night has made it. Lit and unread tiles are refused in the scan and recorded there, because reading a tile's light is cheap and a lit tile must not take a place in the executor's order. The executor then walks the survivors nearest the body first, puts each to the game's own torch step, and takes the first whose stand the reach sense holds, as it does for pots. The light field's dark regions no longer choose sites; the nearest dark region inside the work area only prices the job and names it in the record.

**What keeps lighting from outranking proven work is the band, not an ordering fixture, and the band guarantees less than it first appears.** Lighting's value rises with the nominated region's dark-sample count to a capped ceiling and is multiplied when the player carries no light of his own, and the two constants are chosen so the lit ceiling is exactly the flat value lighting carried before it read a region at all, with only the unlit case rising above it and never to 1. That is what makes lighting an ordinary action: every ordinary raw value lives in 0..1, the running activity is multiplied by the commitment bonus, and an expression that cannot reach 1 therefore cannot interrupt a committed job at any input — which an earlier arithmetic reaching 1.12 could, and did not deserve to. What the band does not promise is that lighting loses to mining or chopping in any particular scene: those values are computed from the ore or trunk in front of the companion and are often well below lighting's ceiling, so a large dark cavern with the player carrying no light can rightly outbid a nearly worthless vein. The guarantee is about the ceiling and about interruption, and it is deliberately structural: an ordering fixture between two scene-dependent values would pin one arrangement and read as a rule.

After a torch is placed the activity keeps its job the way mining keeps a vein: `ContinueAfterInteraction` clears the search wait so the next preparation searches again from where the body now stands, and the ninety-tick wait applies only when a search found nothing. Before this, a search found the nearest site anywhere on the screen, placed one torch and handed the tick back, so a cave was lit a torch per visit with a walk back to the player between each.

**A tile is dark by its own light, not by its neighbourhood's.** Darkness is open air the engine has computed, never packed tiles, and that ruling lives in the light sense so carrying and placing inherit it. The engine's reading at a tile already carries whatever light reaches it through air and through rock, so asking the tile is asking what a person standing there sees. The neighbourhood mean this replaced counted the open air of a lit room behind a few tiles of rock and refused the dark passage on the other side of the wall, and could not see a dark passage narrower than its own stride. Light somebody is carrying is not in the reading at all, because the sense reads the engine's scan of the world's own light, taken before the blur merges what a body carries; unread is refused with lit, because a place nobody has read is not a proven dark place. Underground or at night is not the test.

**Every exit has a name, and every exit about a stand is the executor's.** The gathering scan names its own: no light measured in range and a scan the deadline cut are unanswered (Unresolved); no dark tile in range and no dark tile the torch step accepts are absences (NoOpportunity). The executor's are shared by every method, in the order of how much of the question is still open: a search the deadline cut before it asked every site is `interaction-search-cut-by-planning-deadline` (Unresolved); a stand the flood has not claimed while it is still growing is `interaction-stand-not-yet-known-reachable` (Unresolved, which is how the ruling that optional work does not start on an unanswered search reaches lighting); a stand whose every pose lies outside a finished flood's known radius is `interaction-stand-beyond-known-radius` (Deferred, because nothing that flood will do answers it, and it answers again when a different flood does); and a stand a finished flood refused is `interaction-site-has-no-return` (KnownUnusable), the same name the round-trip proof used for the same fact. A search whose gathering could not answer asked no stand at all, and says so before any earlier proof, which is about somewhere else.

`Tools/EngineReplay/Assistance/VerifyLightAndReachSenses.cs` holds the acceptance row for the whole arrangement: a sealed cavity of dark air nineteen tiles from the companion and a reachable dark floor thirty-seven tiles away, where lighting must offer the far one on its first search with the offer reading `Usable/reachable-interaction`. It fails on the exact starvation signature when a three-site budget is planted back, which is how it is known to be discriminating rather than merely green. `Tools/EngineReplay/Assistance/VerifyTorchPlacementRule.cs` holds the rule's own rows on light the engine itself blurs at the game's brightness — a dark room the player's cursor could light is offered and a torch placed there, the same room lit by a standing torch or in daylight with him holding one offers nothing, and each executor exit is named — every one of them red against the code before it or against a named mutation. Its value scales with the nominated region's dark-sample count, capped so a cavern cannot outbid protecting the player, and rises when the player carries no light of his own. Enablement is the torch setting plus a torch in supply: a locally lit disc is not "already lit" for the method. Lighting reads native Smart Cursor recommendations through `../../Infrastructure/Interactions/Torch/` for attachment and spacing, and asks for a hover beside the site through the shared tool-access query. Native placement and resource consumption retain their own owner.

Both lighting and pots reach a site the way mining does: `FindToolAccess.Approach` answers the body's own centre when it already reaches the tile, and otherwise the nearest hoverable cell within the player's reach box whose centre has a line to an exposed face and whose tile the reach sense claims. A site the flood has finished without is refused as `interaction-site-has-no-return` and kept out of discovery while the flood that refused it answers; a site outside a finished flood's known radius is set aside the same way under its own name; a site a still-growing flood has not reached yet is re-asked. There is no hop, no take-off and no proof between choosing the hover and flying to it, because any hover the flood holds is one the orb can fly to; the arrival test is the tool's own reach box, the same for every tile-tool activity. Pot and torch operations recheck home protection at mutation rather than treating discovery permission as permanent.

The shared interaction executor classifies a found candidate as usable and asks its subclass why a refused method is refused: lighting's only refusal of that kind is the torch setting (a prohibition), because its torches never run out, and it overrides `SearchRefusal` to name its gathering's exits where a pot still answers `no-candidate-in-search-window`; pot collection separates the pot setting from a full bag.

The hooks on the shared executor exist for a subclass that knows more than the executor does: `ContinueAfterInteraction` keeps a job after a productive interaction, `SearchRefusal` names why its gathering found nothing, `RefusingStage` names which of its candidate stages refused a tile (`Candidate` is that stage being null), `CandidateReadings` says what those stages read, and `SearchFunnel` hands the executor the funnel to fill. **The stand exits are not a subclass's to name.** The hook that let lighting name them, `NoteApproach`, was handed each site's verdict in turn and kept the last, so a stand beyond the known radius followed by a refused one reported the refused one's name, and a search the deadline cut reported that the flood had not settled while every site it had asked was proven.

**The reach sense is the only way of asking, and reachable and returnable are one set.** Membership of the region *is* what returnable means in this project — everywhere the body can go and come home from — and for an orb the flood over free space has no one-way edge, so a hover the region claims needs nothing proved about it. The walker's round trip, a pair of fresh route searches per site proving the same fact at a hundred times the price, and the breath envelope it carried are gone with the body, and every liquid is air to the orb, so a trip across a pool is a trip across open space to the region and needs no second proof. Collection publishes the classification of whichever method's raw value it publishes, and names a zero-valued known drop as usable rather than absent. One interaction is the whole job, so an attempt with a credited productive effect is complete and the companion's own — a placed torch without measured coverage, a broken pot with unobserved contents — while an abandoned approach or a refused native call is a failed method and a target that stopped qualifying is invalid. Those give-ups are cleared when an attempt opens. A drop's attempt concludes from what the cargo actually received from that item object after the walk began, read from the inventory's transfer record, because a drop leaving the world is also what the player's pickup and despawning do: all of it received is the companion's own completion, part received with the rest gone is shared, gone with nothing received is invalid, and part received with the rest still lying there is partial. Terraria also merges two nearby drops of one type in the world, deactivating the one in the higher slot, and mined veins drop items close enough to merge; a walked-to drop that vanished with another drop of its type within the native merge distance of where it was last seen was absorbed rather than taken, so nothing received concludes as an attempt replaced with the items still lying there (`drop-merged-into-world-drop`) and part received as partial, and the absorbing drop is offered as its own attempt on the next preparation. The check follows the native distance but not its doubling during a flood of new items, and it cannot tell a merge from the player taking the drop while an unmerged drop of the same type lies that close, which native merging rarely leaves for long. Keeping company classifies as usable while the player lives and concludes every attempt as executed, never complete.

A lighting or pot trip is offered only where the companion can come back. The shared search asks sites nearest first by the method's own cost and stops at the first whose access is proven, and the access query answers from the region, so "can come back" is what the answer already means. A proven refusal defers the site while the flood that proved it answers and a BehaviourWeights wait has not passed, and names itself `interaction-site-has-no-return` — the round trip's own name for the same fact, kept because the fact did not change when the evidence for it did. A verdict of not-yet-known defers nothing and is re-asked, which is the asymmetry the next paragraph is about. The verdict is taken at discovery and not again during the flight; a terrain change that removes the way back after discovery is caught only by the access and candidate checks execution already makes.

Two problems went with the round trip and are worth recording as solved rather than as absent. Its search ran under whichever rule the previous tick's request left set, so it once certified a dark pit the Exact request then refused to plan, and the trip stalled until its progress window deferred it; the region is flooded under one rule set once per tick, so the verdict no longer depends on the last request. And it cost two fresh route searches per site, which is what every bound in this file was ever rationing.

The executor's rules are also asked incidentally, by `../../Infrastructure/Grants/ConsiderIncidentalInteractions.cs`, which holds its own library instances of both methods rather than the chooser's activities. `FindIncidentalTarget` runs the method's enablement and then its candidate check over the tiles within actual reach of the current pose, nearest first, and never touches discovery state or asks for a route; `PerformIncidental` calls the same native `Perform`, which rechecks permission at mutation, with a note that the interaction event's detail begins with (`incidental;during=<activity>;activity-id=<id>;credited-to=none;`). An incidental effect never reaches `RecordWork`, so no attempt counts it as productive.

**A bound belongs on the expensive call, not on the proof it happens to feed — and this loop has now learnt that twice, in opposite directions.** The first time, the site count was charged against the round-trip proof, because that was the expensive thing every candidate reached; opting lighting into the reach sense made that proof nearly free and, by charging the counter only on the route it no longer took, removed the loop's only bound. The second time, the count was moved onto the approach query and the reach sense was given to every method, which made the *reach* half of the approach free too — and left the count rationing a pose scan, which is what the approach still costs. Measured on the wholly dark floor, all three inside the default suite: a budget of three sites, 7.4 ms; no bound at all, 25.5 ms; the tick's own planning deadline, 13.4 ms. The general shape is the same both times: when a cheaper proof replaces a dearer one, find what the dearer one's bound was standing in front of, because a saving that deletes a limit is not a saving.

**The count was the wrong bound and is not coming back, however the cost moves again.** It rationed by an ordering, and rationing a nearest-first scan by three sites is exactly what let three sites hide every site behind them for 79% of the 2026-09-14 session. The deadline rations the same cost without an ordering, and a scan it cuts is reported Unresolved under its own name and retried in a rescore, which is what the sites past the cut actually are, with every refusal proved before the cut remembered so the next search starts past them rather than on them. The scan that builds the candidate list reads the same deadline for the same reason.

**An answer that has not arrived and an answer of "no" are different results, and a search over a growing flood is correct only if it treats them differently.** A stand the region proves unreachable is remembered, so the next search advances past it; a stand it has not claimed yet is re-asked, because deferring an unfinished flood writes a site off for the whole deferral on evidence that does not exist. Collapsing the two is what made the old bounded search worse than no bound at all — it spent every budget on the same nearest sites and the answer never arrived, where the unbounded version at least reached the truth eventually. And the reason for a proven refusal has to outlive the scan that found it: once a site is deferred the next scan never builds it, so an unnamed refusal decays from "the companion cannot come back from there", which is knowledge, into "the placer accepted nothing", which is an absence. The executor now supplies that name itself rather than leaving it to a subclass, because pot collection had no opinion and was getting the absence.

**Any timing from this suite is only comparable to another timing taken the same way.** A fixture run alone through its own flag pays JIT compilation for the whole approach and route-search path on its first lighting preparation; the same fixture inside the default suite has had that paid by the dozens of fixtures before it, and the two differ by more than tenfold on the same code. So every number above states which harness it came from, and a before taken one way is never compared with an after taken the other. The rule came out of the first bounding round and has bound every measurement here since.

**A search that could not answer retries in a rescore; one that answered "nothing here" waits the full cadence.** The long wait exists so a fruitless search is not repeated every tick, and those are not the same result. The reach region is flooded incrementally and takes a hundred-odd ticks to settle after a world change, so the first searches after one refuse on "not yet known" — correctly, since optional work does not start on an unanswered search — while keeping company goes on moving the body. Waiting the full cadence then re-asks from wherever it has wandered: on the walker's J08 shelf that was thirteen columns along, so the first answerable search picked a tile beside the shelf instead of its top and closed a lost take-off repeatedly. That is the same distinction the search-advance rule above rests on, applied to the retry policy instead of to the scan: treating the two alike converts a slower answer into a worse decision, because the scene moves while the evidence is gathered. Expect to meet it again anywhere a per-candidate route search is replaced by a shared incremental one.

Candidate search and deferred-approach cleanup run during Prepare. Comparison reads captured values and targets without invoking native torch rules or asking the reach sense again. Execution retains validation and progress accounting; a comparison cannot count as another interaction attempt. The shared executor is not a fourth selectable activity or a separate movement writer.

Method discovery clears its own candidate without releasing the activity's continuation allowance. Collection can still have a valid known drop when pot discovery is disabled. The shared activity owner releases admission on replacement; failure of one method cannot erase another method's admitted purpose. Execution checks enabled policy and candidate validity before approach or mutation.

An ineligible-to-eligible transition refreshes discovery immediately. Preparation and execution share this eligibility observation: permission revoked after preparation must invalidate the same discovery state as permission revoked before preparation. Otherwise a supply or policy change can clear a target while its old deadline prevents fresh work. Unchanged eligibility retains bounded discovery cadence; per-target physical deferrals remain separate evidence.

Keeping company is the only activity that yields to the player's interference footprint (`PlayerSense.Interference`). Inside the region the accompanying motion refuses the footprint's tiles as places to move to, and a body already standing in them is sent to the nearest allowed place it can fly straight to; outside the region the way back in passes over them, and a priced meeting place inside them is not taken. Mining, chopping, lighting, collection, protection and the evade layer never read the footprint, which is what "courtesy is priced against work and safety" means in code: a companion swinging a pick where the player is building keeps swinging. `Tools/EngineReplay/Assistance/VerifyCourtesy.cs` holds a block aimed at the companion (moves), a bow or torch aimed at it (identical trajectory to an empty hand) and a one-body-tall passage (never left standing in the player's way).

A change in the player's reach (`FindToolAccess.Reach`) is the other thing that refreshes discovery immediately. Everything the executor retains about a site was derived under the old reach: the held hover, the wait before the next search, the failed-approach deferrals, and the proven refusals kept until the terrain changes (no way to the hover that reach chose). So the executor releases every one of those stores on the same comparison and searches on that preparation. Before this, a smaller reach flew lighting or pot work to a hover it could no longer swing from until the progress window deferred the site as a failed approach, and a larger reach waited out the cadence. The refused-stand store was added later with a key of its own and was not released, so a pot whose only pose at a small reach was on a pit floor stayed refused for the store's wait after a larger reach could work it from the rim. The property is that a capability is one key every retained refusal is released on: a capability added to one store's key and not the other's refuses under the old value. The refused-stand store is additionally keyed on the reach sense's flood generation, because a refusal is a fact about the flood that proved it. Known drops are picked up by body contact and do not read tool reach at all.

## A search remembers what it proved, and three ways it once forgot are properties now

**A store of proven refusals that empties itself whole past a size re-proves its nearest refusals for ever.** Under a floor of sealed dark pockets the nearest sites are all refusals; a store that dropped every one of them on reaching a fixed size had each search re-prove the same pockets until the planning deadline cut it, and the reachable site behind them was never asked (15 September 2026: 2,347 lighting rows asking between thirteen and sixty-four sites, every one unreachable). The store prunes entries that have stopped being true and never drops one that still is.

**A proof keyed on the world's terrain revision is thrown away by the companion's own work.** The revision moves on every edit anywhere in the loaded world, the torch the companion has just placed included, so each placement discarded every refusal and the next search began on the same pockets. A refusal is keyed on the flood that proved it: a finished flood does not grow, and an edit it read replaces it.

**A deadline cut reported under an unsettled stand's name hides a finished flood.** The cut used to set the same flag an unfinished flood sets, so 3,607 rows of that capture read `interaction-stand-not-yet-known-reachable` beside a complete flood whose every asked site was proven unreachable, and nothing in the record said the search had simply run out of time.

## What each preparation did with each candidate is a record

Lighting and collection each fill a `CandidateFunnel` (`../RecordCandidateFunnel.cs`) as they prepare: every candidate looked at is counted by the stage that refused it, the nearest few are kept with the stage they passed and the numbers each stage read, and the candidate that got furthest is kept whatever its distance. Lighting's stages are the torch supply, the tile's occupancy and protection, its light (unread or lit), the torch step, and the executor's stand exits; collection's are the activity allowance, cargo capacity, the landing forecast, the contact pose, and the approach verdict. The recorder writes each funnel's furthest stage as a column every row and the whole funnel as an occurrence when its outcome changes.

Lighting also explains one tile on demand, through the same stages and without touching discovery: `ExplainTile`. It asks it of the tile the player's own smart cursor would offer him, every rescore, so the record carries that tile, its light, and the stage lighting refuses it at — or that it passed every stage and simply was not the site chosen, which is a comparison lost rather than a rule.
