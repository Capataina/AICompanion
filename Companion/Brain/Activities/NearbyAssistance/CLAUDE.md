# Nearby assistance — lighting, collection and keeping company

Keeping company is one ordinary activity in NearbyAssistance. It offers positive value while the player is alive and competes with other useful work; it is not merely a fallback for an empty board.

```
NearbyAssistance/
├─ CLAUDE.md           activity purpose, method choice and evidence limits
├─ KeepCompany.cs      reunion, resting, nearby movement and sealed-pocket roaming
├─ CollectNearbyItems.cs known drops and uncertain pot-content opportunities
├─ LightUsefulArea.cs   supplied permanent lighting offered through native placement rules
└─ PerformNearbyWorldWork.cs shared pot/torch discovery, approach and interaction methods
```

Far reunion pull is capped below the value a proven job can reach, so work further across the screen can still win however far away the player has walked; the cap lives in `Weights.KeepCompanyFarCap`. The hard leash at fly-home distance is a separate step that is not subject to it.

**The pull is continuous from the player's intent region outward, and while he travels it is never exactly zero inside it.** A flat zero inside the old follow box was the whole of the never-overtakes defect: a moving player was not itself a reason to move, so the body coasted to whichever edge it entered by and keeping company scored its wander floor, where any rival offer beat it. Inside the region the pull scales with the region's own normalised distance and reaches the central-pull weight at the edge; outside, the slope rises over the same span it always did, and the two meet at the edge, so there is no step anywhere along the curve.

**Both halves of that curve measure to the region, and the outside half did not used to.** It was priced on the straight-line distance from the companion's body to the player's, which is a different quantity from the one the inside half uses and is already large at the region's own leading edge — a companion standing exactly where the region asks it to be is a lead plus a half-width away from the player. So the slope reported a demand at the one boundary where the inside gradient says there is nothing left to want, and a regime ternary picked between the two, which is where the jump was. The outside half is measured on the gap beyond the region's edge now; it is zero everywhere inside, so the ternary had nothing left to choose between and is gone with it, and the whole curve is one expression.

**The size of that step was the length of the lead, which is why no fixture had found it.** Subtracting the region's width from a body-to-body distance leaves exactly the lead, so at a walking pace the outside slope reached about 0.18 against a central pull of 0.25 and there was no step at all — planting the old rule back under a walking player leaves the contract green, measured. It takes a lead near seven hundred pixels before the slope overtakes the gradient, which is a running player, and a lead that long only exists at a play-sized clamp: the headless floor caps it at about a third of that and caps the defect with it. A contract for a curve therefore needs both a declared screen and a player moving at a speed the game's accessories actually produce, and the one in `Tools/EngineReplay/Movement/VerifyResponsiveFollowing.cs` asserts both premises before it reads the curve, so it fails rather than quietly weakens if either is removed.

**The central pull now has a row that dies without it, and the walk alone never did.** Deleting the constant left the straight-walk contract green for a long time because the lead alone puts the body ahead on a little over half the moving rows, so the pull was live with nothing measuring it. The row that measures it takes the lead's help away: the body is held at rest inside the region until arrival settles, which zeroes the regroup urgency and satisfies the objective, so the pull is the only reason to move left standing. With it the offer is a reunion; without it the wander floor is the largest thing left and the companion strolls beside a departing player. The walk's own pass line sits at the floor of the band the walk actually produces rather than at half, because half is below what the lead reaches unaided. The central pull sits above the wander floor, or a travelling player would be strolled beside rather than kept up with, and well below the far cap and any proven job's value, so walking with a moving player still loses to work worth stopping for. The two floors that are steps rather than slopes — losing sight of him, and standing in the passage he is walking down — are unchanged, because both are facts about the moment rather than distances.

**A method change is a change of request kind, and a change of request kind cancels whatever the body is doing, so it waits for the body to be at rest and for a rescore.** At tick 8128 of the 2026-09-14 capture the walker's flip from reunion to local turned a WithPlayer into a Hold one tick after a take-off and cut the jump; the orb has no jump to cut, but a Hold reaching the navigator mid-flight still turns a body that was going somewhere into a hover around wherever it happened to be, so the rule stands with "on the ground" read as "at rest". So a regime that wants the other method has to hold for a rescore and has to be asked for from rest. The wait guards a body that is already where it is meant to be, so it is exempted wherever the body is not. Outside the region a change takes effect at once, because a companion that has lost the player, or has just spawned, has no business waiting a rescore per moving tick before it may go after him. A body overlapping the player's interference footprint is exempt for the opposite reason: it is inside the region and still in his way, and the wait was what actually held it in the passage — measured, when suppressing the region's own lead was tried as the fix for the same symptom and the courtesy contract stayed red until this exemption went in instead. The activity's very first preparation, and its first after entering, adopt whatever the geometry asks for, since before the first method there is nothing in flight to protect.

**Every "near the player" radius here is measured to the intent region rather than to his body, and `../CLAUDE.md` owns that rule for all seven activities.** Two things are local to this folder. Lighting centres its own work radius on the region as well, which is a second read and not the same test as the shared radius: it decides which darkness is worth lighting, where the nearest-first ordering below still starts from the companion's own centre, because that is about which darkness it can fly to. And keeping company is the one activity that reads the region for more than a radius — its pull is the region's own gradient, above. Threat, safety and protection readers deliberately keep reading the player's body, because danger to him is about where he is and not about where he is going.

Preparation compares the existing reunion urgency with the value of staying locally active. Execution requests WithPlayer when reunion wins, or holds otherwise, and the body hovers on that hold. A sealed pocket uses the existing bounded roaming/retry policy. These are methods of the same purpose and preserve its activity identity. Nothing local is retained between methods: the hover's anchor belongs to movement and is dropped by the first request that moves the body anywhere else.

Every request passes through position selection and shared movement. Only an explicit reunion request can qualify for distant recovery; a local hold near player coordinates cannot. Shared safety can interrupt any method, and compatible shooting and held light remain independent hands work.

Reunion asks `../../Infrastructure/Position/ChooseMeetingPlace.cs` for a place on the player's apparent journey priced by the companion's own routes and requests that exact point; a paused or working player is met where they stand. Leaving the activity, or switching to the local method, releases the meeting place so its reason and flood never describe another activity's movement. **Local company is a hold, and the orb hovers on it; the stroll that used to sit here was deleted on 15 September 2026.** It picked a random safe cell near the player — inside his intent region, clear of liquid, inside the reach flood, under an exposure limit — and flew there on a timer, alternating with resting still. The owner then ruled that the orb is never strictly still, whatever it is doing, and a slow hover around wherever company settled is the motion a stroll was approximating with a stop between every flight. The stroll was also the one piece of the brain that manufactured movement for its own sake, with its own hazard and exposure tests duplicating what movement and the positioner already know, so it went rather than being tuned. Two things it did by accident had to be kept on purpose, and both went red when it went. It grew the reach flood as a side effect of asking whether its candidate cells were returnable, which was the only thing that ever created the first flood of a companion's life; a hold now refreshes the flood through the positioner itself, and `../../Infrastructure/Observation/CLAUDE.md` carries that circle. And its hazard test was what kept an idle companion out of lava; a hover drifts at most a tile around a spot the positioner or the body chose, its targets are kept on this side of every wall the planner knows including liquid, and `Tools/EngineReplay/Assistance/VerifyCompanyLocalMotion.cs` holds it over lava, a submerging pool and a one-way pit. There is no separate exploration activity.

A companion in a proven sealed pocket uses local movement without forgetting reunion. The coordinator exposes that state on the action context and cycles local roaming with reunion retries using BehaviourWeights. Keeping company's local method requests Roam and its reunion method requests WithPlayer. An incomplete path is not proof of a sealed pocket, and a model-closed region is not proof of physical impossibility in Terraria.

Collection prepares a fitting known drop and a bounded pot-content opportunity, compares them with the same shared factors as the parent, and publishes the chosen raw value and forecast. Publishing an already-discounted value would charge the trip twice. Neither comparison repeats discovery. Known drops bind the item, type and captured landing, revalidating world-slot membership, availability and cargo acceptance before movement, and execution holds for a tick when that landing has moved more than a tile since it was proven, so the next preparation proves it where it will now lie instead of walking to where it was.

**A drop is priced where it will land, not where it hangs.** The contact pose used to be searched around the item's instantaneous bottom within a tile, so an item still in the air had no pose at all — 431 rows of the 2026-09-14 capture were refused as `drop-has-no-contact-pose` — and the moved-drop guard then held the body on every tick of every fall, because an item in free fall has always moved more than a tile since it was proven. Both are one mistake: an object in flight priced as an object at rest. The forecast steps the game's own arc from `Item.UpdateItem` and `MoveInWorld` — gravity per tick capped at a maximum fall speed, horizontal velocity damped and zeroed below a floor, and in liquid the game's own ladder of shimmer, then honey, then water, each with its own gravity and cap —

**A liquid slows a falling item twice over, and the second half is the one that decides landing X.** Besides the gravity and the cap, `Item.UpdateItem` keeps a separate wet velocity and steps the position by a *share* of the velocity whenever the item is wet — half in water, three eighths in shimmer, a quarter in honey — and it captures that share from the velocity as it stood at the top of the tick, before that tick's gravity. Taking the wet gravity and then stepping the whole velocity covers twice the ground in water and four times in honey. The landing *row* survives that error, because a drop falling in a pool still lands on the same floor, which is why a row asserting where a drop lands can be green while the forecast is badly wrong; landing X does not survive it, and X is what decides which tile the companion walks to. Whether the share applies at all is gated on the wet flag alone, as the game gates it, rather than on whichever liquid supplied the gravity.

**A contract for a forecast has to read the forecast at the moment it is forecasting, and a hold count cannot stand in for that.** The moved-drop guard holds the body whenever the item has moved more than a tile from where it was proven, so a forecast re-taken every tick and wrong by the same smooth amount every tick produces no holds at all — measured: the wrong arithmetic above scored zero holds on every liquid. The assertion is the offer's own target at release, which is the landing the drop was priced at and the only moment the whole remaining fall is being predicted rather than observed, compared against where the drop actually comes to rest. Comparing the pose after the drop has landed proves nothing, because by then there is nothing left to forecast.

**Honey is slow enough that an ordinary fall does not resolve inside the horizon.** At a quarter of its velocity and a third of water's cap, an item descends about a fifth as fast as it does in air, so a twelve-tile fall in honey exceeds the forecast's horizon and the offer reports `drop-landing-undecided`. That is the middle answer working rather than a defect, and it is worth knowing before an undecided honey drop is read as one.

The forecast holds the item's liquid flags for the whole fall, so a drop that *enters* liquid partway down is forecast dry; the tile world exposes water and lava but not honey or shimmer, so re-reading the liquid kind per step would need a query that folder does not have. Named here rather than hidden, because a wrong liquid is worse than a known-dry one.

The forecast lands the item on the top of the first supporting row its track enters, asking the tile world rather than raw solidity, since a slope is not the surface its solidity suggests. Every row the bottom crosses in a tick is tested, because at the capped fall speed an item covers most of a tile a tick and a sampled check would pass through a floor. A fall that has not resolved inside the forecast horizon is `drop-landing-undecided`, unresolved rather than absent, which is the same middle answer the senses give: a drop still falling past the horizon is going somewhere the companion should not be committing to. The forecast reads tiles and the item's own numbers and runs no route search, which the navigation boundary script enforces here as everywhere under `Activities`. Each fitting drop is asked about in distance order: a hoverable contact cell, the one nearest the companion where the body grown by the contact pickup's reach touches the item, and then whether that cell is in the reach region. A free cell beside a drop proves none of that, and the reach that mined an ore says nothing about the pool its drop fell into. Every drop is asked, because membership of a flood already run costs a hash lookup: a farther drop is never offered before a nearer one has been asked about, and that ordering now holds by construction rather than by a bound arranged to produce it. A pose the flood has not claimed yet publishes an unresolved offer at zero value.

**Three things went when the question stopped being a search, and the reason is one reason.** A per-drop verdict cache keyed on pose, terrain revision and a recheck interval; a cap on how many new reach questions one preparation could ask; and the marginal return, which refused a drop only where the companion could reach the player from where it stood and could not from the drop, so a companion already cut off was not called stranded by going one step further. A cache exists to make an expensive answer reusable and a bound exists to ration an expensive answer, and neither has anything to ration now. The marginal rule went for a different reason and it is a real loss rather than a simplification: two-way membership asks the absolute question — can the body come back at all — so a companion inside a sealed pocket refuses drops inside that pocket, which the marginal rule allowed. That is the ruling, and the sealed-pocket case is what it costs. The offer is valued for the quantity the cargo can take (the loot sense's value scaled by the share of the stack's value that fits). Its forecast is the straight-line distance to the contact cell over the orb's pace, the unit mining and chopping use, and not the route's own cost: a drop behind a detour is forecast as near as one in the open. Pot contents are forecast the same way, from the pot. A detached object can remain active after its slot is replaced; it is no longer a world drop. A newly observed replacement receives its own object identity. An arbitrary same-object mutation without a lifecycle signal is not detected as a new generation.

Pot contents remain unknown until the native operation produces them. Their prior value and handling allowance live in BehaviourWeights; approach contributes a geometric estimate rather than a certified pickup/return duration. Unknown contents require empty cargo space conservatively, whereas a known drop may fit an existing player or bag stack. Native home protection covers the pot's footprint and is rechecked at the operation, along with pot policy and capacity. The current method uses native KillTile at interaction range; weapon-driven breaking and prediction of the resulting drop locations remain separate implementation obligations.

Pot discovery and approach reuse PerformNearbyWorldWork. Dropping a method candidate does not release the collection activity's admission; the activity owner handles that lifecycle. Actual known-drop pickup remains the NPC's independent contact operation and can occur while another behaviour is active. A completed worksite can widen the allowance for its known drops, without granting nearby unknown pots the same allowance. Breaking a pot does not certify any pickup or obligate a subsequent trip.

**Lighting works a dark area rather than visiting it.** It reads `../../Infrastructure/Observation/`'s light field for the dark regions in range, nearest first by the nearest dark air in each, and offers the tiles in and around them — around every dark sample rather than around one point of the region, because a torch needs a wall or floor to attach to and the one a large region touches can be at its far end. Every region in range contributes, not only the nearest, since a band of light across a cave splits the dark into a near half that may be sealed air and a far half full of surfaces, and stopping at the first would leave the companion standing in the dark beside a wall it could light. The executor then takes the first site whose access it can prove, as it does for pots.

**What keeps lighting from outranking proven work is the band, not an ordering fixture, and the band guarantees less than it first appears.** Lighting's value rises with the nominated region's dark-sample count to a capped ceiling and is multiplied when the player carries no light of his own, and the two constants are chosen so the lit ceiling is exactly the flat value lighting carried before it read a region at all, with only the unlit case rising above it and never to 1. That is what makes lighting an ordinary action: every ordinary raw value lives in 0..1, the running activity is multiplied by the commitment bonus, and an expression that cannot reach 1 therefore cannot interrupt a committed job at any input — which an earlier arithmetic reaching 1.12 could, and did not deserve to. What the band does not promise is that lighting loses to mining or chopping in any particular scene: those values are computed from the ore or trunk in front of the companion and are often well below lighting's ceiling, so a large dark cavern with the player carrying no light can rightly outbid a nearly worthless vein. The guarantee is about the ceiling and about interruption, and it is deliberately structural: an ordering fixture between two scene-dependent values would pin one arrangement and read as a rule.

After a torch is placed the activity keeps its job the way mining keeps a vein: `ContinueAfterInteraction` clears the search wait so the next preparation re-nominates from where the body now stands, and the ninety-tick wait applies only when a search found nothing. Before this, a search found the nearest site anywhere on the screen, placed one torch and handed the tick back, so a cave was lit a torch per visit with a walk back to the player between each.

Darkness is open air the engine has computed, never packed tiles, and that ruling lives in the light sense so both carrying and placing inherit it. A site is refused unless its own neighbourhood's mean brightness is below the dark level — a neighbourhood rather than the tile, because a torch lights an area and a dark tile pressed against a lit room needs none; sampled from the engine at a finer stride than the field's lattice, which cannot resolve a lit patch narrower than its own step. Unmeasured is refused alongside lit: a place nobody has read is not a proven dark place. The companion's own torch is discounted by the field, so a lit bubble around the feet does not hide a dark pocket beyond it. Underground or at night is no longer the test.

Its four refusals are separate facts and no longer share one name: no dark region in range and no region inside the work radius are absences of work; a region whose stand the flood has not claimed yet is an unanswered search (`dark-region-tile-not-yet-known-reachable`, unresolved at zero value, which is how the ruling that optional work does not start on an unanswered search reaches lighting); a region the two-way flood has proven it cannot come home from is `interaction-site-has-no-return`, the same name the round-trip proof used for the same fact; and a region with no tile the game's placer accepts is a geometry problem where the companion can stand. Lighting learns which of the last two a site was from `NoteApproach`, which the executor calls with the approach verdict for every site it walks past; a shared fifth name, `interaction-stand-not-yet-known-reachable`, is what a subclass with no opinion reports in the third case.

`Tools/EngineReplay/Assistance/VerifyLightAndReachSenses.cs` holds the acceptance row for the whole arrangement: a sealed cavity of dark air nineteen tiles from the companion and a reachable dark floor thirty-seven tiles away, where lighting must offer the far one on its first search with the offer reading `Usable/reachable-interaction`. It fails on the exact starvation signature when a three-site budget is planted back, which is how it is known to be discriminating rather than merely green. Its value scales with the nominated region's dark-sample count, capped so a cavern cannot outbid protecting the player, and rises when the player carries no light of his own. Enablement is the torch setting plus a torch in supply: a locally lit disc is not "already lit" for the method. Lighting reads native Smart Cursor recommendations through `../../Infrastructure/Interactions/Torch/` for attachment and spacing, and asks for a hover beside the site through the shared tool-access query. Native placement and resource consumption retain their own owner.

Both lighting and pots reach a site the way mining does: `FindToolAccess.Approach` answers the body's own centre when it already reaches the tile, and otherwise the nearest hoverable cell within the player's reach box whose centre has a line to an exposed face and whose tile the reach sense claims. A site the flood has finished without is refused as `interaction-site-has-no-return` and kept out of discovery under the terrain-revision wait; a site the flood has not reached yet is re-asked. There is no hop, no take-off and no proof between choosing the hover and flying to it, because any hover the flood holds is one the orb can fly to; the arrival test is the tool's own reach box, the same for every tile-tool activity. Pot and torch operations recheck home protection at mutation rather than treating discovery permission as permanent.

The shared interaction executor classifies a found candidate as usable and asks its subclass why a refused method is refused: lighting separates the torch setting (a prohibition) from an empty supply (known-unusable), and overrides `SearchRefusal` to split the four exits above where a pot still answers `no-candidate-in-search-window`; pot collection separates the pot setting from a full bag.

Three hooks on the shared executor exist for a subclass that knows more than the executor does: `ContinueAfterInteraction` keeps a job after a productive interaction, `SearchRefusal` names why a search found nothing, and `NoteApproach` is handed the approach verdict for a site the search walked past, so lighting can say which of its four exits that was where pot collection has nothing to add. None of the three is opt-in machinery around a cheaper proof; the proof is the same for both methods now.

**The reach sense is the only way of asking, and reachable and returnable are one set.** Membership of the region *is* what returnable means in this project — everywhere the body can go and come home from — and for an orb the flood over free space has no one-way edge, so a hover the region claims needs nothing proved about it. The walker's round trip, a pair of fresh route searches per site proving the same fact at a hundred times the price, and the breath envelope it carried are gone with the body: water and lava are walls to the flood until an immunity opens them, so a trip across a pool is refused by the region itself rather than by a second proof. Collection publishes the classification of whichever method's raw value it publishes, and names a zero-valued known drop as usable rather than absent. One interaction is the whole job, so an attempt with a credited productive effect is complete and the companion's own — a placed torch without measured coverage, a broken pot with unobserved contents — while an abandoned approach or a refused native call is a failed method and a target that stopped qualifying is invalid. Those give-ups are cleared when an attempt opens. A drop's attempt concludes from what the cargo actually received from that item object after the walk began, read from the inventory's transfer record, because a drop leaving the world is also what the player's pickup and despawning do: all of it received is the companion's own completion, part received with the rest gone is shared, gone with nothing received is invalid, and part received with the rest still lying there is partial. Terraria also merges two nearby drops of one type in the world, deactivating the one in the higher slot, and mined veins drop items close enough to merge; a walked-to drop that vanished with another drop of its type within the native merge distance of where it was last seen was absorbed rather than taken, so nothing received concludes as an attempt replaced with the items still lying there (`drop-merged-into-world-drop`) and part received as partial, and the absorbing drop is offered as its own attempt on the next preparation. The check follows the native distance but not its doubling during a flood of new items, and it cannot tell a merge from the player taking the drop while an unmerged drop of the same type lies that close, which native merging rarely leaves for long. Keeping company classifies as usable while the player lives and concludes every attempt as executed, never complete.

A lighting or pot trip is offered only where the companion can come back. The shared search asks sites nearest first by the method's own cost and stops at the first whose access is proven, and the access query answers from the region, so "can come back" is what the answer already means. A proven refusal defers the site until the terrain changes or a BehaviourWeights wait passes, and names itself `interaction-site-has-no-return` — the round trip's own name for the same fact, kept because the fact did not change when the evidence for it did. A verdict of not-yet-known defers nothing and is re-asked, which is the asymmetry the next paragraph is about. The verdict is taken at discovery and not again during the flight; a terrain change that removes the way back after discovery is caught only by the access and candidate checks execution already makes.

Two problems went with the round trip and are worth recording as solved rather than as absent. Its search ran under whichever rule the previous tick's request left set, so it once certified a dark pit the Exact request then refused to plan, and the trip stalled until its progress window deferred it; the region is flooded under one rule set once per tick, so the verdict no longer depends on the last request. And it cost two fresh route searches per site, which is what every bound in this file was ever rationing.

The executor's rules are also asked incidentally, by `../../Infrastructure/Grants/ConsiderIncidentalInteractions.cs`, which holds its own library instances of both methods rather than the chooser's activities. `FindIncidentalTarget` runs the method's enablement and then its candidate check over the tiles within actual reach of the current pose, nearest first, and never touches discovery state or asks for a route; `PerformIncidental` calls the same native `Perform`, which rechecks permission at mutation, with a note that the interaction event's detail begins with (`incidental;during=<activity>;activity-id=<id>;credited-to=none;`). An incidental effect never reaches `RecordWork`, so no attempt counts it as productive.

**A bound belongs on the expensive call, not on the proof it happens to feed — and this loop has now learnt that twice, in opposite directions.** The first time, the site count was charged against the round-trip proof, because that was the expensive thing every candidate reached; opting lighting into the reach sense made that proof nearly free and, by charging the counter only on the route it no longer took, removed the loop's only bound. The second time, the count was moved onto the approach query and the reach sense was given to every method, which made the *reach* half of the approach free too — and left the count rationing a pose scan, which is what the approach still costs. Measured on the wholly dark floor, all three inside the default suite: a budget of three sites, 7.4 ms; no bound at all, 25.5 ms; the tick's own planning deadline, 13.4 ms. The general shape is the same both times: when a cheaper proof replaces a dearer one, find what the dearer one's bound was standing in front of, because a saving that deletes a limit is not a saving.

**The count was the wrong bound and is not coming back, however the cost moves again.** It rationed by an ordering, and rationing a nearest-first scan by three sites is exactly what let three sites hide every site behind them for 79% of the 2026-09-14 session. The deadline rations the same cost without an ordering, and a scan it cuts is reported Unresolved and retried in a rescore, which is what the sites past the cut actually are. The scan that builds the candidate list reads the same deadline for the same reason.

**An answer that has not arrived and an answer of "no" are different results, and a search over a growing flood is correct only if it treats them differently.** A stand the region proves unreachable is remembered, so the next search advances past it; a stand it has not claimed yet is re-asked, because deferring an unfinished flood writes a site off for the whole deferral on evidence that does not exist. Collapsing the two is what made the old bounded search worse than no bound at all — it spent every budget on the same nearest sites and the answer never arrived, where the unbounded version at least reached the truth eventually. And the reason for a proven refusal has to outlive the scan that found it: once a site is deferred the next scan never builds it, so an unnamed refusal decays from "the companion cannot come back from there", which is knowledge, into "the placer accepted nothing", which is an absence. The executor now supplies that name itself rather than leaving it to a subclass, because pot collection had no opinion and was getting the absence.

**Any timing from this suite is only comparable to another timing taken the same way.** A fixture run alone through its own flag pays JIT compilation for the whole approach and route-search path on its first lighting preparation; the same fixture inside the default suite has had that paid by the dozens of fixtures before it, and the two differ by more than tenfold on the same code. So every number above states which harness it came from, and a before taken one way is never compared with an after taken the other. The rule came out of the first bounding round and has bound every measurement here since.

**A search that could not answer retries in a rescore; one that answered "nothing here" waits the full cadence.** The long wait exists so a fruitless search is not repeated every tick, and those are not the same result. The reach region is flooded incrementally and takes a hundred-odd ticks to settle after a world change, so the first searches after one refuse on "not yet known" — correctly, since optional work does not start on an unanswered search — while keeping company goes on moving the body. Waiting the full cadence then re-asks from wherever it has wandered: on the walker's J08 shelf that was thirteen columns along, so the first answerable search picked a tile beside the shelf instead of its top and closed a lost take-off repeatedly. That is the same distinction the search-advance rule above rests on, applied to the retry policy instead of to the scan: treating the two alike converts a slower answer into a worse decision, because the scene moves while the evidence is gathered. Expect to meet it again anywhere a per-candidate route search is replaced by a shared incremental one.

Candidate search and deferred-approach cleanup run during Prepare. Comparison reads captured values and targets without invoking native torch rules or asking the reach sense again. Execution retains validation and progress accounting; a comparison cannot count as another interaction attempt. The shared executor is not a fourth selectable activity or a separate movement writer.

Method discovery clears its own candidate without releasing the activity's continuation allowance. Collection can still have a valid known drop when pot discovery is disabled. The shared activity owner releases admission on replacement; failure of one method cannot erase another method's admitted purpose. Execution checks enabled policy and candidate validity before approach or mutation.

An ineligible-to-eligible transition refreshes discovery immediately. Preparation and execution share this eligibility observation: permission revoked after preparation must invalidate the same discovery state as permission revoked before preparation. Otherwise a supply or policy change can clear a target while its old deadline prevents fresh work. Unchanged eligibility retains bounded discovery cadence; per-target physical deferrals remain separate evidence.

Keeping company is the only activity that yields to the player's interference footprint (`PlayerSense.Interference`). When its hovering body overlaps the footprint, its local method issues an ordinary follow request anchored on the player instead of holding, and position selection's occupancy share picks a spot out of the way; reunion's own follow request reads the same share. Hovering that overlaps nothing is untouched, and mining, chopping, lighting, collection, protection and shared safety never read the footprint, which is what "courtesy is priced against work and safety" means in code: a companion swinging a pick where the player is building keeps swinging. `Tools/EngineReplay/Assistance/VerifyCourtesy.cs` holds a block aimed at the companion (moves), a bow or torch aimed at it (identical trajectory to an empty hand) and a one-body-tall passage (never left standing in the player's way).

A change in the player's reach (`FindToolAccess.Reach`) is the other thing that refreshes discovery immediately. Everything the executor retains about a site was derived under the old reach: the held hover, the wait before the next search, the failed-approach deferrals, and the proven refusals kept until the terrain changes (no way to the hover that reach chose). So the executor releases every one of those stores on the same comparison and searches on that preparation.
