# Gathering activities — mine a vein or chop a tree

Mining and chopping animate accepted native swings, but credit their retained worksite only when the observed tool outcome shows increased damage or removal. They publish that same immutable outcome to the recorder. A cooldown, refused effect or changed tile frame cannot manufacture productive work credit; the native interaction owns the observation and the behaviour consumes it.

Tool ownership spans cooldowns only while that work is executing. Exit and suspension clear the swinging phase independently of retaining a vein or tree; otherwise an interrupted job reports an occupied hand after recovery or downing has already taken control. The current activity grant determines whether the arsenal may use the released hand.

Execution rechecks the work policy before requesting travel or taking the tool. Turning mining or chopping off revokes a previously prepared candidate, clears its retained job and releases its admission. Native tool permission remains a separate mutation check; an earlier positive utility value is never permission to keep acting after the user disables the work.

Prepared-target validation also reports `work-disabled` before activation when that policy is off, and a disabled offer carries no selectable value while keeping its original desirability, so a reader of the record can still see what the work would have been worth. The execution check still owns retained-job cleanup and protects calls made after activation; admission and execution observe the same policy at their respective boundaries.

```
Gathering/
├─ CLAUDE.md              job ownership, preparation and native evidence limits
├─ MineOre.cs             retained ore-only vein work under the chosen policy
├─ ChopTree.cs            separate-tree preference and retained reachable trunk work
├─ GatheringCourseOpportunities.cs resumable native ore census and the pure frozen-fact mine/tree source
├─ CaptureTreeOpportunities.cs sliced native trunk census with axe progress, access and policy evidence,
│                              composed into the gathering capture rather than driven on its own
├─ BindGatheringOpportunity.cs exact native swing bindings, readiness and conditional work successors
└─ DescribeOreJobEnd.cs   original bounded vein state at job end, with separate removal attribution
```

Both activities declare PurposeFamily.Gathering, a reading label since the family chooser's deletion under `AIC-419` on 22 September 2026, and register once in `../../Infrastructure/Selection/RegisterActivities.cs` — the one list of activity instances a brain has, which is what `ChooseBehaviour.cs` was before it was deleted the same day. They use the common activity contract and the thin policy readers in `../WorkPolicies.cs`; persistent preferences remain owned by PlayerIntegration. Native tools remain in WorldInteractions, and recovery or downing can interrupt either activity without becoming a gathering child.

`GatheringOpportunityBinder` reads only captured facts and hypothetical state. Each binding names one native swing at an exact tile/material and pose with the captured tool identity, power and cadence. Wider vein totals remain the census denominator; only the bound tile's own remaining work can be credited by that use. Physical damage follows the native swing even when less useful credit remains: the objective caps reward, while the successor applies the complete native hit. Tool readiness is a separate absolute native tick fact, projected across uses, so the hand is reserved at the actual use rather than throughout an earlier cooldown. An immediate application of the captured closed tool law has a conditional model successor; a nominal travel forecast cannot supply a finite latest effect time, so a later use depending on its result remains unresolved. Validation preserves the accepted tile/tool/pose rather than substituting another candidate. Native execution still needs the latest policy, access and readiness checks at its own call boundary.

**The census sweeps the radius the admission rule admits, and one budget unit buys a block of cells rather than a cell.** Both were wrong together and each hid the other. The window was a constant per activity — `MineOre.SearchRadiusTiles` at 45 tiles, `ChopTree.SearchRadiusTiles` at 40 — against `CompanionAction.AllowsTarget`, which admits new work within `NewActivityRadius`, 1000 px at the standard distance preference — 62.5 tiles, which the census rounds *out* to a half-side of 63, so the window is 127 by 127; a vein between those two radii was invisible to the brain that would have admitted it, and the census reported it as `examined=0, exhausted=true`, which reads as *looked and found nothing* rather than *never looked there*. Measured 21 September 2026 on `VerifyOreWork`'s departure row: at 800 px separation the ore sits about 866 px from the player's heading, inside the allowance and outside the sweep, and the course chose keeping company beside a vein it could not see. The family chooser hid it for as long as it was the brain, because `MineOre.Prepare` runs a *second* `OreFinder.FindNearest` centred on the companion's own body; the census runs one, centred on the heading. The window is `CompanionPreferences.WorkCensusRadiusTiles` now, derived from the same allowance both censuses are judged against, so the two cannot disagree by construction — and at 1000 px the same row's veto is now the allowance saying `outside-allowance-or-protected` rather than an absence, which is strictly better evidence for the same behaviour.

Deriving it immediately cost too much, and that is the second half. The sweep charged one budget unit per cell, so its accounted cost was its *area* rather than its work — a cell holding no ore is a tile-type read, a cell holding one pays for a vein flood and a reach query per tile, and the two differ by orders of magnitude. Widening 8,281 cells to 16,129 doubled the charge and the census stopped finishing inside a decision: `--crowd-cost` put `decide`'s median at 11.3 ms against its own 12 ms allowance, which is a brain spending every tick of its thinking on a tile scan. A block of `CaptureGatheringOpportunities.ScanBlockCells` per unit and one vein or trunk per unit prices the two kinds of work apart: 8,281 units to 64 for ore, 6,561 to 64 for trunks, with the window nearly doubled in both.

**A moved body re-answers the sites already found; it does not sweep for them again.** A completed scan used to reopen whenever the body moved or a different flood began answering, because every admission in it was proved against that body and that flood. That is true of the *admissions* and false of the *discoveries* — where a vein or a trunk stands has nothing to do with where the companion is — so reopening answered the cheap question by re-paying for the expensive one, on most ticks, since the body moves on most ticks. Each census keeps its sites with the flood they came from (ore keeps its vein tiles, the trunk census keeps its bottom) and re-runs one shared `Admit` over them, resumably, under the same allowance; only a terrain edit inside **the window unioned with every tile the census actually holds**, a changed pick, axe, list or policy, or the window itself moving reopens the sweep. `Admit` is one function per census deliberately, asked by both the sweep and the re-answer, because two copies of an admission ladder drift into disagreeing about the same ore.

**A cross-family review of that re-answer landed three defects the same night, and each is a shape rather than an instance.**

*A vein is not bounded by the window that seeded it.* `OreFinder.Vein` floods a connected component with no spatial bound — only a 400-tile cap — so a cell inside the window's edge can seed a vein whose tiles lie well outside it. While the reopen test watched the swept rectangle alone, mining one of those outside tiles reopened nothing: the re-answer replayed the frozen tile list, `EstimateRemaining` answered null for a tile that is no longer ore, the admission's own `remainingKnown` test went false because it required *every* tile to answer, and the whole vein flipped to `native-remaining-unresolved` and stayed worthless until the window moved. (The all-or-nothing half of that is gone since 22 September 2026 — the paragraph below owns it — so this episode's mechanism survives only as the reopen rule.) The treadmill had been hiding it by re-censusing from live tiles constantly, which is the durable half: **retiring a wasteful mechanism exposes whatever it was accidentally covering, and the question to ask of any such retirement is what else it was doing.** The union above is exact rather than a guessed margin, because the tiles it adds are exactly what `Admit` re-reads — the vein's tiles for ore, the trunk's bottom for trees.

*Charging a block on `offset % ScanBlockCells == 0` is not the same as charging once per block.* A site sitting exactly on a block boundary took the block unit, advanced one cell, was refused its own site unit, and rewound — so the next capture charged the same block again for zero cells. At an allowance of exactly one operation that is a livelock rather than a slow path, and `VerifyTreeOpportunityCapture` drives the census with exactly that, deliberately; it passed only because no site in its scene lands on a multiple of the block size. A `blockPaidThrough` offset is what fixes it: **a block that is paid stays paid across captures**, so reaching a site with nothing left costs nothing to retry.

*The sweep is a square and the allowance is a disc, so widening one to bound the other published a fringe nothing can ever admit.* About 24% of the 127x127 window lies outside the 1000 px disc — roughly 3,857 cells that were swept, charged a site unit at discovery, charged a re-answer unit on every geometry round, serialised every tick, and published as facts `InNewActivityAllowance` refuses by construction. Worse, the census denominator that scales every site's worth summed over what was *observed* rather than over what was *admitted*, so refused corner ore was quietly deflating real work. Before the window was widened the box fitted inside the disc and the fringe was zero, so it is a shape error created by the fix rather than found by it. Cells outside the disc are skipped, which is free rather than accuracy traded for speed.

**The re-answer had no test anywhere, which is why a review found what a suite did not.** `G08 a moved body re-answers every held site and a one-operation sweep still advances` in `Tools/EngineReplay/Gathering/VerifyTreeOpportunityCapture.cs` is the row that holds it now, and it is worth knowing that only `InNewActivityAllowance` reads the companion's own position — so only it can witness a re-answer at all. Two earlier drafts of that row would have failed against correct code, which is the kind that gets "fixed" by loosening it: one asserted on a site's stand, which for a tree is a free cell beside it and the same point wherever the companion is, and one threw the reach flood away expecting `approach-not-yet`, which did not happen because the flood resettles and the trunks are genuinely still reachable.

Retained-course discovery observes individual ore components and tree bottoms before those activities select a winner. Tree scanning spends the shared allowance a block of cells at a time, one unit per trunk found, and deduplicates branches by native trunk identity. Its quantity is remaining native axe damage on the bottom, with tool, permission, access and player-work evidence carried beside it; height and predicted wood drops do not create extra reward. A partial rectangle publishes coverage only. Local edits, axe changes and observed axe effects reopen the census; a geometry change re-approaches the trunks already found instead of reopening the scan, and "local" means the tiles the census holds rather than the rectangle it swept. The common source treats missing coverage and unresolved target evidence as unresolved admission. **Since `0bb2c8a` on 21 September 2026 these are not adapters awaiting a migration: they are what the live brain reads.** The tick asks a course, the course discovers mining and chopping through exactly these two censuses, and `ExecuteCourseBinding` supplies the position request — so a census that publishes a wrong fact is a companion that does the wrong thing this tick, where until that commit it was an unread input.

Mimic triggers come from the tile damage watcher in `../../Infrastructure/Observation/`, which sees real axe and pickaxe hits through the game's KillTile hook. Opportunistic triggers discover nearby resources without requiring a player hit. Both read the player's held tool's numbers, with a basic tool fallback. The work itself is in `../../Infrastructure/Interactions/Chopping/` and `../../Infrastructure/Interactions/Mining/`.

**"Near the player" means near his intent region for both jobs, in every one of the four places it is asked.** The shared work radius in `../CompanionAction.cs` measures to the region, both course censuses sweep the region's heading at that same radius, and both activities' own discovery centres on it too — `MineOre` through `OreFinder.FindNearest` and `ChopTree` through `TreeFinder.FindNearest(context.Senses.Intent.Region.Heading, …)`, verified 21 September 2026. A note stood here claiming chopping still took the player's own centre as a second origin, left over from the 15 September intent-region change that in fact moved it with the rest; `../CLAUDE.md` owns the rule and carries the correction.

Mining separates its policy trigger from its retained job. Mimic starts only while the player has recently hit ore; opportunistic can discover ore near the player or companion. Once a job exists it holds the bounded same-type vein across guard and self-defence action switches, revalidates tile type, native pick damage, line of sight and approach on every resume, then relocates to the next reachable tile. A route query that proves no approach ends the reachable portion. A bounded `Unknown` is not a plan: mining publishes unresolved at value zero and holds, so gathering does not nominate, and companionship or combat can take the tick. The unanswered ore stays in discovery status so a later preparation from a new position — usually because the companion followed the player — can still prove a hover.

Ending a mining job snapshots its original bounded tile set before clearing eligible work. Present original material, changed material, missing tiles and unobserved coordinates remain distinct counts. Clearing an eligibility list cannot prove harvesting. The job separately counts unique tracked sites removed by its own accepted native tool calls; missing world tiles alone earn no such credit. The retained conclusion carries its original job identity and tick through later idle periods and new discovery. ObservedClear means every tracked coordinate was empty at that observation, not that the entire world vein was discovered or that drops were collected. The snapshot also counts ore of the job's type touching a tracked site that the job never tracked, because the vein flood is bounded and the player can place ore beside a vein after the job began; an attempt whose tracked sites are all empty while such ore remains ends partial as `tracked-portion-clear-vein-continues`, and only a vein with none left completes. Preparation drops vanished or transformed ore from the working set on the preparation that observes it, so the remaining-tile count is the ore still there and a job whose ore is all gone ends as `no eligible ore remains` rather than as lost permission. Equal remaining work receives an equal offer and forecast whoever removed the rest. Partial damage is not equal work: Terraria keeps a hit table per hitter, so a tile the player has half broken is still a whole tile to the companion's pick. Resumed targets retain the discovered material type rather than inheriting a placeholder type from a missing approach.

**The player's mining list decides which ore is work before any other question is asked about it.** `WorkPolicies.MinesOre` reads the list's mark and mode (`../../../PlayerIntegration/KeepMiningList.cs`), and mining applies it in two places that together cover every way an ore becomes work: as the first clause of the discovery predicate both policies search with, so a left ore is never a candidate and no approach or tool question is spent on it, and on a job already under way, which ends on the next preparation as `ore left by mining list` the way turning mining off ends it. The list's instance and revision sit in the approach key beside pick power and reach, because a changed mark is new evidence about which ores are work: an ore the player has just allowed is taken on that preparation rather than after the search cadence. When no allowed ore is near but a left one is, the offer is classified as a policy prohibition with that reason rather than as no ore found, and a weak pick against an allowed ore is still reported as the tool, because the naming pass keeps the list applied and asks about left ore only after it finds nothing.

An undecided ore approach is discovery, not a destination. The 13 September 0.22.44 session spent 1,583 ticks walking at one unreachable pocket because Unknown was scored as an investigation and any 32 px of movement reset the stall that was supposed to drop it. Optional work does not start on an unanswered search: the companion wall-reads ore through dirt, which a player would never have begun for. Following the player is what moves the body; the next mining search from the new origin may come back Yes.

The ore search retains the nearest unresolved tile separately from its proven target, so status can still read `approach unknown` while the selected action is something else. It never substitutes the nearest arbitrary ore for an unknown reported elsewhere, and it never publishes that unresolved tile as `ActivityTarget` or a movement request. Discovery filters native pick damage before ranking distance, so a weak-pick ore does not hide a farther usable one. A change of pick is new evidence about every ore, so it reopens discovery on the same preparation: a stronger pick starts work at once, and a weaker one reports the tool as unable to mine instead of reporting nothing found. The job exposes its id, policy, status, remaining-tile count and current target/stand for diagnostics. It does not target dirt or carve a route.

Ore in a ceiling is ordinary work for a body that hovers: the tool-access query ranks every free cell in reach of the tile, above and below it alike, so there is no second method, no take-off and no proof between choosing the hover and flying to it. What the query cannot reach is ore with no exposed face inside any free cell — a vein sealed in rock — and that ends the reachable portion as known-unusable rather than as an approach to attempt. The walker's hop, its lost-take-off deferral and the narrow-top limitation went with the body.

Chopping uses the same policy names through `TreeFinder` and `TileChopper`. Mimic retains the recent-hit window and avoids the player's active tree. Opportunistic discovery prefers admitted separate trunks across both search origins, then permits the player's trunk when no alternative remains. A change in the player's observed trunk refreshes discovery if it overlaps retained work; unrelated retained trees continue normally. Dropping a trunk the player has taken records `player-took-trunk`, so the attempt that left it ends partial with that cause rather than as a generic replacement. The player's axe contact ages under every policy, Disabled included, and starts expired, because a job begun opportunistically carries no player trigger: switching it to Mimic with no player contact releases it as `mimic-awaiting-player-tree-contact` instead of treating it as a swing the player just made, and a contact made just before chopping was disabled has aged by the time Mimic comes back rather than reading as a moment old. This is a local discovery preference, not a world reservation. Opportunistic work retains a trunk through protective interruptions, but releases one outside its allowance or inside a protected home. Failed or unfinished approaches are deferred briefly so one tree cannot permanently mask local work. Resource admission measures the trunk rather than whichever side supplies its standing point.

Both activities classify their offers from the discovery they already ran. A disabled or mimic-waiting policy is a prohibition; a proven working pose or reachable trunk is usable; an undecided approach is unresolved at value zero, so it cannot win; a pick or axe that cannot damage the target, a vein whose remaining tiles have no proven pose, or a trunk with no approach is known-unusable. Their attempt conclusions read evidence recorded during the attempt. Mining counts job conclusions in a sequence and reads one only if it was captured after the attempt opened, and records method setbacks (an invalidated prepared tile, an unproven approach that stopped making progress) where they happen rather than re-reading a discovery status the next preparation may have replaced. An observed-clear tracked vein or a fallen trunk completes an attempt only when that attempt credited productive strikes, and otherwise the attempt is invalid, because an external edit to the same coordinates changes remaining work without being companion production. The completion is the companion's own only when every tracked ore site was removed by its strikes, or its own strike was the one that removed the trunk; a vein or tree finished by the player after companion strikes is a shared completion.

Mining and chopping update discovery, approach caches and retry timers only during `Prepare`. They capture the resulting target, value and trip duration; Score and ForecastTicks read that result without advancing any timer or querying terrain. External ore removal is incorporated during preparation, not during repeated comparison. A missing prepared chopping candidate cannot execute a retained tree merely because the discovery cache still contains it.

Their prepared tile bindings carry coordinate and material through activation and execution. Disappearance or replacement rejects the old offer, releases the tool phase and requests fresh preparation; eligible replacement material may become a new job. Chopping's continuation identity includes material, so a different tree type at the same coordinate does not inherit its predecessor's activity. Mining keeps its bounded vein identity while its individual tile binding is refreshed within that job.

Relocating within a retained vein consumes its search cadence only while that vein remains active. If relocation ends the job, it preserves a requested fresh discovery; otherwise a material-change rejection can clear the old work and then accidentally postpone evaluating its replacement.

For an established target, the trip duration combines approach distance with the tool's estimate of its next native tile completion. Remaining hits and outstanding cooldown replace fixed work durations. The captured RemainingWork is nullable: missing or incapable work is not a finite completion promise. Mining's unresolved approach still carries its provisional discovery estimate and no native completion estimate; it must not be interpreted as proven work. Completing one ore does not commit the remainder of the vein or its drops.

Mining uses actual tool reach to decide whether to swing. A navigator's approximate arrival tolerance can leave the body outside that reach; returning Hold from that pose would prevent the movement needed to make mining possible. Course bindings retain the requested tool pose separately from the predicted travel endpoint, so the next hypothetical leg starts where the movement model arrived; absent endpoint evidence leaves binding unresolved. Endpoint evidence alone does not replace the native tool-reach check. Retained vein pruning uses the same actor-and-target distance contract as discovery, and a removed target cannot survive merely because other vein tiles remain.

Chopping uses that same shared tool-access query for discovery, retained approaches and actual strikes. Current usable reach replaces the cached hover with the body's own centre; otherwise the prepared trip includes a reachable hover. Execution cannot infer axe access from distance to that pose. Terrain or reach changes may invalidate the access independently of whether the tree still exists.

Both activities key what they retain about access on `FindToolAccess.Reach`, the player's reach as one revision, beside the terrain revision and (for mining) pick power. A reach change re-derives a held hover on the next preparation, forces discovery rather than waiting for the search cadence, and drops the deferrals made under the old reach, chopping's unreached trunks among them. Before mining carried reach in its approach key, a body holding its hover when reach shrank asked for that same point every tick and never swung; a reach that grew waited out the cadence for ore it could already reach, and a trunk refused at a smaller reach stayed refused for its whole deferral. `Tools/EngineReplay/Assistance/VerifyCapabilityRevision.cs` holds each of those as a pair differing only in reach.

## A tile with no remaining estimate is a proven fact, and calling it unknown cost a whole session's mining

`TileMiner.EstimateRemaining` answers null on five conditions and **every one of them is something observed**: the tile is outside the world, it is no longer ore, it sits in a protected companion home, the game refuses to kill it, or the handed pickaxe has no power against it. None of those is a bounded search running out, which is the only thing `unknown` means anywhere under this tree — and because optional work does not start on an unanswered search, an admission that reports a proven refusal as unknown leaves the vein permanently unstartable *and* permanently unrefusable. The census can look again for ever and the answer will not change, because there was never a question.

That is what the 22 September 2026 play recorded. Every ore read `native-remaining-unresolved` for all 2,340 ticks — 2,321 rows of the TSV carry the string, and the events sidecar's funnel reads `mine-target=usable:0,unknown:10,unusable:0` for the whole session, so ten to thirteen veins were discovered and not one was ever usable or ever written off. The capture cannot say which of the five it was, and that is the second half of the defect rather than a reporting detail: the admission ladder had already computed the exact refusal, and the override discarded it.

Three things changed together on 22 September and each closes a different half:

- **The target decides the admission, and the other tiles decide only the worth.** The old test required every tile of the vein to answer before any of them counted, so one dead cell in a four-hundred-tile flood zeroed the whole vein's remaining damage. `remaining` is now the sum over the tiles that answered, which can only understate and never overstate.
- **A refusal is named.** `WhyNoRemainingWork` asks the five conditions in `EstimateRemaining`'s own order and answers `tile-outside-the-world`, `tile-is-no-longer-ore`, `outside-allowance-or-protected`, `the-game-refuses-to-kill-this-tile` or `pickaxe-cannot-damage`. The game's own refusal is split from the pickaxe's deliberately, because handing the player a stronger pick would not change the first. The admission is then `unusable`, which is a verdict a census can reach and a reader can act on.
- **`native-remaining-unresolved` survives as the honest fallback and nothing else.** A pickaxe with a zero `useTime` refuses with no condition above it holding, and a fact claiming a proven refusal there would assert something nobody established. That one case stays `unknown`, which is what `unknown` is for.

The consumer moved with it. `GatheringOpportunitySource` decided whether a site publishes a need by comparing its *reason string* against `native-remaining-unresolved`; it reads `site.Work == null` now, which is exactly the quantity the string was standing in for. That was a second home for the admission ladder's vocabulary, and the day the string became five strings a provably undiggable site would have started publishing a need worth zero rather than no need at all — the same class of defect as the one above, one seam further on.

`Tools/EngineReplay/Gathering/VerifyVeinRemainingWork.cs` is the standing guard, and it is a pair: meteorite under a copper pickaxe is the refusal, copper under the same pickaxe is the control, because an implementation that answered `unusable` to everything would satisfy the first alone. **Only one of the five refusal names is pinned by a row** — the meteorite case reaches `pickaxe-cannot-damage`. `tile-outside-the-world`, `tile-is-no-longer-ore`, `outside-allowance-or-protected` and `the-game-refuses-to-kill-this-tile` are asserted nowhere; each is a plausible scene (a vein at the world edge, a tile mined out from under the census, a protected home, a tile the game itself refuses) that nobody has built a fixture for, so a typo or a swapped condition in any of the other four branches of `WhyNoRemainingWork` would pass this suite today.

## Two traps the ore capture paid for, both about *when* a published fact was true

**A capture's remembered refusal has to be keyed on the flood that proved it, and the ore capture was the one that was not.** `ReachSense.FloodGeneration` exists for exactly this and its own summary says so: a refusal proved from a finished flood stays true while that flood answers, because a finished flood does not grow. `CaptureTreeOpportunities` had keyed on it since it was written; `CaptureOres` restarted on a changed area, a terrain edit, a changed pick, a changed policy or a new miner attempt — **and a companion that is not mining moves none of those**. So an ore refused while the flood was young kept its refusal for ever. Measured 21 September 2026 (`f6681ac`): a companion beside its player with the region emptied and copper thirty-two tiles away published `mine-target:0ok/0?/1x(approach-unreachable)` and kept company for all nine hundred ticks, while a probe at the end of the run read `approach=Yes`. The class is wider than that scene — the same freeze holds a refusal made while the body was walled off, or under a flood that had not yet expanded past a corner, for as long as the companion declines to mine, which is exactly as long as the refusal lasts. The ore capture follows the tree capture's rule now: the geometry is compared only once the scan has finished, because a body that moves a tile every few ticks would otherwise erase a partial scan before it could ever publish.

**A stand the capture publishes is body-relative and is bound several ticks later.** The scan runs in budgeted slices and restarts whenever the miner's last attempt changes — that is, on every strike — so the stand recorded for a tile is computed from wherever the body happened to be when that tile's slice ran, which after a strike is mid-hover. `FindToolAccess.Approach` short-circuits on the body's own centre only when the body reaches the tile *at that instant*; a body drifted a few pixels falls through to the ranking branch and gets the hover point of a cell beside the work. That stand is then published as a durable fact about the ore. Traced on the maximum-reach scene (`4808965`): bound pose 320.0 at tick 99, 384.0 at tick 114 — four tiles in, toward ore it was already hitting — and 336.9 at tick 129, with 14.6 px of drift measured over three strikes, all of it toward the ore. The fix is not here: `ExecuteCourseBinding` re-derives the pose from the live body for tile work already in reach, because the search prices orders against a frozen observation and must not read the live world, while execution is exactly where a decided course becomes what the body is asked to do. The property to keep in this folder is the one that made it possible — **a capture may publish a fact about the world, and must not publish a fact about where the body was when it looked.**

## Gaps and planned work

- **The publication block re-serialises every site to JSON on every capture, for zero budget units.** It predates the block charging and is its own piece of work, so the unit arithmetic above prices the scan and not the write.
- **Natural tree growth and unhooked ore placement route through no terrain hook**, so neither reopens a completed census. The geometry treadmill was covering them by accident and nothing does now; a census that is complete stays complete through a world that grew a tree inside it.
- **The terrain edit ring holds 256 entries**, so a census sitting complete for thousands of ticks can be pushed off the back by edits anywhere in the world and forced into a full rescan. Safe, because the answer is "reopen", but it converts a per-body-move cost into a per-256-edits one and it fires hardest while the player is mining.
- **`VerifyGatheringCooperation`'s `W04` is the chopping twin of the departure question**, and `AIC-450` is what it waits on: at 800 px a fresh job is worth a negative number and is still chosen, because idling costs more, and whether the choice should flip there is a product call rather than a defect. Its chooser-driven arm went with `AIC-419` on 22 September 2026, so the question is now asked of the course alone. `G02` is a real and unexplained course finding filed as `AIC-449` — a trunk created mid-session is published `usable / observed-native-tree`, is in the live snapshot, is examined by its own source every slice, and never appears in `Course.Admitted` or `LastLeaders` across two hundred consecutive decisions. Its only witness was the chooser's, so closing it needs a course-side row written into `VerifyAdmittedOpportunitiesBind`.
- **The three course-capture files are a cluster candidate** (`BindGatheringOpportunity.cs`, `CaptureTreeOpportunities.cs`, `GatheringCourseOpportunities.cs`, 3 of 5 kinship signals on the 21 September survey). Moving them into a `Course/` subfolder is a code move, so it is recorded rather than done: the reference surface is this file's map, `../CLAUDE.md`'s map, `Tools/EngineReplay/Gathering/CLAUDE.md`, and every `using`, namespace and `live::`-qualified reference in the three files plus their four fixtures. `EngineReplay.csproj`'s per-file compile list is not in it — it names movement sources and nothing under `Activities/`, checked 21 September 2026.

## Current state — 22 September 2026

**A null remaining-work estimate is now a refusal by name rather than an open question, and that is what the 22 September play's mining exposed.** Every ore in that capture read `native-remaining-unresolved` for all 2,340 ticks, which made every vein permanently unstartable and permanently unrefusable at once — the section above owns the mechanism and the fix. `MineOre.PickaxeFor` still reads the gear slot's pickaxe rather than whatever the player holds, so that finding is about the census's admission ladder, not about which tool the companion swings.

**The course is the brain, and these two censuses are on its critical path.** What `MineOre` and `ChopTree` still own is the hand, the retained job and the native evidence; where the body goes is the bound step's.
