# Combat activities — protect the player or pursue an attack opportunity

```
Combat/
├─ CLAUDE.md
├─ ProtectPlayer.cs             positioning against a particular threat to the player
├─ PursueAttackOpportunity.cs    an attack opportunity worth approaching, with target identity and progress evidence
└─ ResolveFiringOpportunity.cs   whether and how soon a reachable position can shoot an enemy, one cached answer shared by both activities
```

Hunting requests a useful attack position; it does not fire a weapon. `Brain.Engage` resolves an independent target through the arsenal after movement, whenever the hand grant permits it. Keeping shooting inside selected combat activities would make it unavailable during travel or assistance. SharedSafety owns retreat and collision avoidance across activities; those responses do not require a hunting or guarding offer.

**A hunt target is admissible only if a standing position exists from which a weapon has a real forecast, the same `ForecastAttack` the hands fire from.** Asking only whether a shot exists from where the companion currently stands is the obvious version and is worse than no check at all, because it refuses exactly the enemies hunting exists to walk toward. The answer is graded: a shot from here, a shot after moving, nothing established yet, or a proven absence. FromHere and AfterMoving are plans. AfterMoving includes a solvable stand the flood has not yet claimed, so hunt can start walking toward that region without freezing the tile. Unknown is an unfinished search that has found no arc; it publishes unresolved at value zero. Collapsing Unknown into None would still be wrong. Only the proven absence is known-unusable. Hunt does not pick the pair the hands will fire; it steers toward the pose where that chooser's outcome value is highest.

That answer is also a factor in the score, because whether a shot is possible was absent from the product entirely: hunting something unhittable scored exactly as well as hunting something killable. A target already in reach is worth more than one that must be walked to, which is also what lets ordinary work outscore a hunt across the room.

Existence uses the arsenal forecast, not a straight ray. A ray can say yes while the bow hits the floor, which is how hunt stood still on a retained stand with `no-clear-trajectory`. A handful of nearest stands are solved per target so a crowd cannot spend the tick.

**The proven absence costs a completed sweep of every sampled stand, not the first handful of them.** The stands around a target are sorted by distance and only a few are solved per scan, so a scan that stopped at its cap and answered "no firing position" was reporting an exhausted budget as a fact about the world — and it was the same mistake the positioner's own shortlist made one layer up, reached by a different route. The scan now resumes where the last one stopped and wraps, so a sweep completes across successive scans; until it has been all the way round the answer is the undecided one, and only a completed sweep with nothing solved returns the absence. Two things follow. A sealed enemy reads undecided for a while before it reads impossible, so hunting does not pursue it and guarding is priced on removal alone rather than written off — which is correct, because until the sweep finishes nobody has established anything. And a target whose nearest stands are all blocked is now findable at all, where a scan that always restarted at the nearest could never reach the stand that works. Guarding's single self-closing branch rests entirely on that meaning of the absence.

**A firing stand is proved against where the target is forecast to be when the body gets there, not where it stands when the stand is chosen.** A stand was chosen now and stood on a walk later while its arc was proved against the target's current centre, so a hunt could walk to a place whose shot had already closed. The solve is taken at the candidate's own estimated trip and then at samples across a short window past it, and a stand whose shot holds for less than the trip is refused (`shot-window-shorter-than-trip`). The mechanism belongs to `../../Infrastructure/Position/` and to the aimer, which takes a tick offset rather than a displacement, because the forecast is terrain-constrained and a straight-line shift equals it only while the motion is straight. One limitation is worth stating rather than discovering: where the forecast carries too little measured confidence — no error samples, or a trip long enough that the measured continuation has decayed past the floor — the target's current position is asked instead and the evidence records `clear-arc-unforecast`, so **on a long walk a stand is still judged on where the target is now**, and this rule bites on short and middling trips.

**The positioner's own shortlist running out is a third result too, and a hunt or a guard is unresolved rather than vetoed when it does.** A null destination used to be classified known-unusable whichever way the search ended, so the tick a solve count or a millisecond budget expired vetoed the offer and keeping company won by default — the ceiling-slime flip. A pass that exhausted every candidate still reports the proven absence and may close the offer; a pass that stopped early reports an unfinished search. This is the same rule as the stand sweep below, one layer up, and `../CLAUDE.md` owns the vocabulary both follow. An admitted firing stand is then retained while its arc still solves rather than being resampled: the target must not have drifted past a hold slack, and the arc is re-proved by a single solve taken before any shortlist budget is spent, which is what makes a stand that still works immune to the budget running out.

**An undecided candidate outranks a refused one when the family reports why it offered nothing.** Both can appear in one pass over the threat list, and reporting the refusal brands the whole offer known-unusable on the strength of a different enemy's settled answer while an enemy whose sweep has not finished is a question still open.

**Which admissible enemy a hunt walks toward is priced by what the hands could make of it once there, not by which one is nearest.** The hands already shoot the best target reachable from where the companion stands, so the only question pursuit answers is where the feet go. Up to a few candidates are examined in the threat list's order; each with a firing position is valued through the arsenal's `EstimateDelayedAttackValue`, which runs the arsenal's own outcome evaluator on a sequence whose first shot waits for the reposition — no wait for a shot from here, the positioner's travel estimate to the nearest reachable sighted tile after moving — with the arsenal's retained from-here attacks as the continuation. A reposition is therefore charged for the arrows it delays. The consequences follow from the arsenal's policy rather than from a rule about health bars: a short step to remove a hard-hitting enemy wins, the same enemy behind a long walk loses to the arrows that walk would delay, and finishing a nearly dead harmless enemy is not worth an arrow into a healthy one. Accepting the first admissible candidate, which is what this replaced, sent the feet to the nearest enemy while the hands shot a better one. When every examined value is zero — nothing lands inside the evaluation window — the first admissible candidate is kept, so a distant enemy still worth approaching is not refused for being distant. `PursuitEvidence` lists every examined candidate's verdict, wait and value; `PursuitValue` and `PursuitAccessTicks` describe the chosen one. The estimate ignores the hands' shots during the walk and assumes the arc at the stand, so a borderline choice can err toward staying; it is a comparison of current values, not a plan for a kill order.

Hunting remains opportunistic through the shared activity envelope and independent self-danger. `AllowsTarget` admits useful work farther away than ordinary following and retains an active job within its wider envelope; the selected distance profile owns that boundary rather than the screen edge. Its own skin, `1 − CompanionDanger` with a floor, stops it walking toward a fight it is already losing. Reading only player danger makes straying look safer precisely when the companion is exposed, so both bodies' danger remain separate inputs.

Hunt discovery and its firing/deferred-target caches update in `Prepare`. The candidate captures its NPC generation, position, utility and forecast before comparison. Repeated Score, ForecastTicks and ActivityTarget reads therefore cannot retarget or observe a different enemy position. A nominated hunt then submits its captured positioning request to the common method-admission boundary; the coarse discovery estimate alone cannot activate an attack with no established useful destination. Execution rechecks attackability and generation before requesting the captured firing approach; a disappeared or replaced hostile cannot use the old offer. Neither admission nor coarse sight establishes a guaranteed projectile hit.

The weapon itself is picked and the shot solved in `../../../Weapons/Arsenal.cs` through `../../Infrastructure/Aiming/`. The weapons are equipment, not behaviour, which is why they are not in the brain. Both combat activities declare PurposeFamily.Combat and are registered once in `../../Infrastructure/Selection/ChooseBehaviour.cs`. Their shared comparison and lifecycle contracts come from `../CompanionAction.cs`; this folder owns neither a second chooser nor a movement controller.

**Hunting withholds one-way permission without evidence of an acceptable reunion.** The movement policy grants that permission for reunion, guarding and self-rescue, but not a hunt. A short observed player trail does not establish that the player crossed a particular directed boundary or that the companion can return. The live geometric one-way check feeds telemetry rather than granting a hunt permission. A hostile below the companion therefore needs a reachable firing opportunity; its presence alone cannot justify entering a sealed pocket.

Hunting considers threats to either actor; personal danger still discounts its score, so recognising a nearby hostile is not permission to abandon companionship. SharedSafety separately assesses the companion's need for space, without changing player-protection urgency.

Hunting observes engagement progress after the hands step. Damage to the same pursued enemy generation, a newly fired shot aimed at that enemy or meaningful body displacement renews the attempt. Shooting an independently selected enemy and waiting through a weapon cooldown do not renew pursuit. A stationary hunt producing neither movement nor relevant attacks defers every target generation it pursued during that stretch, for a bounded retry interval. Changed body, target or terrain reopens it immediately. A relevant shot establishes an attack attempt, not a hit; body displacement remains an approach proxy rather than proof of improved access.

**Changing target is not progress, and a progress test that says otherwise cannot fire at all.** Enemies in a crowd score within noise of each other, so the selected target alternates every few ticks; a stall counter that resets on a new identity therefore resets continuously and its window never closes. The deferral must also cover the whole set aimed at during the stall rather than the one selected when the window happened to close, because deferring a single member of an alternating pair buys one more window and nothing else. Because target choice now values several candidates and keeps the best, the set also takes in every admissible candidate a preparation examined: a steady choice among enemies the hunt cannot progress against would otherwise defer them one window at a time, which is the same defect with the alternation removed. A life comparison is only meaningful while the identity holds; across two different enemies it compares unrelated numbers.

Hunting classifies a shot from here or a reachable firing position as usable, an unsettled reachable region as unresolved at value zero, a proven absence of firing positions or a no-progress deferral as known-unusable, and disabled hunting as a policy prohibition. Its attempt completes, unattributed, when the pursued enemy generation disappears after a pursuit shot from that attempt, because the native death hook names no killer; it is invalid when the target vanished before any pursuit shot, failed when the progress window expired, and partial when it attacked a target that is still present. That evidence is written only while the hunt executes and cleared when an attempt opens. Guarding is unresolved until method admission queries its destination, except against a damageable threat no reachable position can shoot, which is known-unusable with `no-reachable-firing-position` and worth nothing, so it is never nominated or queried. Its release is one attempt-local record written where the commitment ends — a later entry for another threat rewrites the commitment reason but never that record — and it completes, unattributed, on a full clearance window or on its committed threat disappearing while the player lives, and is invalid when the player died.

Hunt requires a currently attackable NPC: an invulnerable hostile is an avoidance problem, not a target worth pursuing. The chooser prices excursions against both regroup time and protection urgency, so a hunt cannot borrow safety from a player the companion would reach too late to help.

## Protection retains the threat that earned its offer

Guard preparation refreshes commitment and clearance evidence, then captures its utility, enemy generation, target position and destination anchor together. Score receives no world context and returns that value without changing commitment or observing the player again. Entry commits the prepared enemy; execution refuses a dead, missing or replaced generation instead of substituting a newly urgent enemy. The next preparation can offer a different threat, which receives a distinct activity identity through the shared owner. This binding establishes which protection task was chosen, not whether a useful firing position or intervention exists.

Guard is scaled past the ordinary utility band by `BehaviourWeights.GuardUrgency`: an incumbent retains its commitment bonus, so an action with a lower ceiling cannot displace it even at maximum raw urgency. SharedSafety can suspend protection independently of this comparison when the companion needs environmental escape or collision avoidance.

Guard scores the observation layer's protection urgency, which compares enemy arrival with the time until the companion can intervene. Entry retains the relevant NPC's spawn identity and pressure; a small retreat does not erase an unfinished protection task. Death, disappearance, slot reuse or sustained loss of relevance releases that commitment. Personal survival can interrupt it. Guard is zero for a dead player; personal defence remains independent.

**Protection is worth the harm an intervention can remove, not the size of the threat.** Guard's value is its pressure times a usefulness share times the ladder scale: the share is one while the arsenal's optimistic removal time for the prepared threat (`Arsenal.EstimateRemovalTicks`, every hit landing, the faster weapon) is within `BehaviourWeights.GuardUsefulRemovalTicks`, and falls in proportion beyond it. Before this, full urgency against a boss made guarding the most valuable thing on screen precisely where standing between the player and the boss helps least; a probe on the earlier code measured guard at 1.045 against the Eye of Cthulhu and 0.518 against a Servant of Cthulhu beside the player. Now the fight's length decides, which is what lets the small eyes on the player keep full protection while the boss itself does not, without any rule naming a boss. A threat no weapon can damage keeps its full urgency, because there is no removal to estimate and its avoidance belongs to shared safety. Hunting is untouched by the share: a fight too long to protect through is still one the companion can choose to fight. Lateness of the current shot remains the observation layer's protection urgency; the share below adds the time to reach a place the shot can be made from.

**An intervention's time includes getting to where it can be made, so the share is taken over access plus removal.** Access is the same answer hunting reads, from the one `ResolveFiringOpportunity` the chooser builds per companion and hands to both activities (`ResolveFiringOpportunity.cs`): zero for a shot from here, the positioner's travel estimate to the nearest reachable sighted tile after moving. Guard prepares first, so hunting reads its cached answer on the same tick rather than scanning terrain twice. Three verdicts need rules of their own. An unsettled reachable region counts no access and the share is removal's alone, because an unfinished flood is not evidence of absence — the same three-valued rule hunting admissibility follows — and the straight-line guess the query returns beside that verdict is hunting's to price, never protection's to charge. A proven absence of any firing position is access that never arrives, so the share is zero and the offer is known-unusable with `no-reachable-firing-position`: guarding yields to shared safety and to company when the companion provably cannot shoot the threat, and getting away from such a threat is shared safety's job, not guarding's. A threat no weapon can damage is not queried and keeps a share of one. `AccessTicks` and `Access` sit beside `RemovalTicks` for recording.

Because the share is one anywhere inside the useful length, access only changes guarding once access plus removal crosses it, and that is what the rule is meant to do: a zombie beside the player that takes a hundred and seventy ticks to remove is worth guarding just as much after a hundred-tick walk around a pillar, while a long fight gets longer by the walk. Measured on 2026-09-13 in `VerifyCombatActorMatrix`: before this change guard read 1.075 in both arms of the pillar pair while intervention went from 169 ticks to infinity, and after it the ordinary zombie still reads 1.075 in both (access 112 + removal 169.7, inside the length), while the same zombie at 600 life reads 0.8101 clear and 0.7738 blocked — 4.48% lower, exactly 112 / (112 + 2389.7). In `VerifyCombatPurpose`'s shaft pair a capped shaft reads guard 0 from a settled flood, the lip 0.2758 and a shot from here 0.2782 with identical danger. The access estimate is the flood's walk pricing and the stand's arc is still proven only by position selection, so a borderline threat can be priced a walk too short or too long.

Guard does not fire a weapon. Independent hands resolve compatible attacks after movement, so a companion walking with the player can shoot as readily as one guarding. Guard asks where to stand within a deliberately wide band: protecting someone means being able to hit what is attacking them. Narrowing that band as danger rose walked a ranged companion into the melee it was trying to address. Before activation, the chooser submits the captured guard request to the positioner. An unestablished intervention destination leaves raw urgency intact but rejects the method and reconsiders the prepared board. The unavailable method may be refined again later; it neither blacklists the threat nor proves that protection is unimportant. Admitted destination evidence still precedes travel, later target motion and native damage.

## Current state — 2026-09-14

Hunting and guarding are both members of the Combat family, nominate independently, and reach both read through the one `ResolveFiringOpportunity` the chooser builds per companion, so whichever prepares second on a tick reads the first one's cached answer instead of scanning the terrain again. Neither reads the Light sense: darkness reaches combat only through whatever the threat sense already makes of it. Reachability comes from the Reach sense's two floods — one-way and two-way — rather than from a route search either activity runs.

A hunt target must have a proven firing position, from the current stand or after moving, and that proof is now taken against the target's forecast at arrival; guarding wins only when the threat arrives within the intervention band. Both meanings of "no" tightened in the 0.25.0 build and in the same direction: a stand sweep that has not been all the way round, and a positioner shortlist that stopped at its budget, are each undecided rather than impossible, so an enemy is refused only on evidence that exists. Independent hands fire when both combat activities and safe movement grant a free hand.
