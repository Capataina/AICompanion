The card is what a click on the HUD notch opens, and it answers three questions for a player: how the companion is, what it may do on its own, and where its things are. It is one panel whose body is either the short overview or one full-height page. The overview is a title bar, the identity strip and three tiles; each tile turns the whole card into its page (Inventory, Mastery, Mining list), and back returns. On the overview the title bar holds the title and close and nothing else: there is no back button, because a back with nothing to go back to did nothing (the mock's commit 0f49104). On a page, back sits at the left and the page's own actions sit right-aligned so they end one gap before close, and the page body has no row of buttons of its own. There is no tab row and no minimise. The Inventory page embeds `../Inventory/CompanionBagUI.cs`; the Mastery and Mining list pages live here.

## Why the card is this sparse

Every region here was cut down by the owner on 15 September 2026, and the reason is the rule to apply to anything proposed for it: **information shown twice, or not needed at a glance, is clutter.** He called the earlier Mastery page "so crowded for no reason", and the rulings that followed apply by kind, not only to the page they were said about:

- The overview has no action line. The strip is the portrait, the name and level, and three large bars with readings beside them.
- A tile is a fill bar, a count and the page's name. There is no sentence under it, because a sentence repeated the count, and no "draft" or "nothing is saved" line.
- The Mastery panel shows a node's name and its effect, then Learn (which names the next level's cost) and a level bar, and nothing else. There is no kind or rank line, no purpose sentence, no "sized against", no needs, no Unlearn, no hint above the graph, no legend and no status line. The panel exists only while a node is picked.
- The Mining list has no count line, no instruction sentence and no caption under the preview.
- The Inventory page has no picture of the companion, no caption on either gear box, no filters, no detail panel and no hover text anywhere, item tooltips included.
- Nothing else on the card shows hover text either: the three work controls have no hint.
- A page's actions are in the title bar, so the page body starts at the top.

The draft mastery content still carries each node's purpose and what it was sized against, for balancing, and they are never drawn. The sentence that described what the companion is doing went with the action line: the card was its only reader, and a harness check that graded its wording went too. What the companion is doing is read from the record and the inspector.

## Layout is one table, and the table is the mock's

Every size comes from `CardRegions`, which records the agreed mock (`../../InterfaceExperiments/companion-card.html`) and the rulings: a 720-wide frame, a 34-tall title bar, one 12px rhythm between every stacked region, a 100-tall identity strip, short tiles, 48px slots at a 52px pitch, and a page content area of fixed height. The frame uses 12px of padding where the mock says 10, because the mock's frame has a 2px border outside its padding and a native `UIPanel` draws its border inside its bounds; 12 puts every region at the mock's outer position, and the same two pixels are why content is two pixels shorter than the frame's inner height. The render fixture holds the drawn card to its own copy of the mock's numbers, not to this table.

The card opens centred, a gap below a docked notch, and a docked notch owns the top of the screen, so the title bar never rises closer to it than a clearance; on a short screen a page's foot runs past the bottom edge rather than its title bar sliding under the notch. The vertical clamp uses the overview's height, not the current page's, so opening a page grows the card downward and never moves its title bar. Only a press on the title bar itself drags; a press on back, close or a page action belongs to that button. The position survives closing and reopening within the session and is not save data.

## The pieces

The identity strip's bars are health, mana and experience in the notch's order and colours, each reading beside its bar in the bar's own colour, so the strip and the notch teach one column; downed, the health bar turns grey and reads the revival percentage. The portrait is the drone, drawn as sixteen art pixels from the mock's own drone art, not a texture; the companion's world body is still the probe placeholder. The name is the NPC's given name.

The three work controls are the only preferences the card offers: mining and chopping at Off, Mimic and Auto, and torches at Off and On, each an item icon beside a joined segment control whose segments write the live preference the brain reads. A joined control is one rounded bar split into segments that share their borders; the first rounds its left end, the last its right, and a lone segment rounds both, by a ruling of the same day, because the mock's lone segment took only the right-hand rounding. The Mastery page's level bar is the same control used as a display that takes no clicks.

**Hunting, pot breaking and following distance are no longer on the card, and the brain still honours their saved values.** The owner ruled that hunting and pot breaking are not toggles in the first version and that distance never changes, so the card draws none of them. The properties, their saves, their readers (`PursueAttackOpportunity`, `CollectNearbyItems`, the distance radii in `../PlayerIntegration/ConfigureCompanionPreferences.cs`) and the recorder's configuration row are untouched, and nothing else in the game lets a player change them, so a character saved with hunting switched off stays that way. Turning them into fixed behaviour is a gameplay change left to the owner.

The Mining list page shows every ore the character has held, from `../PlayerIntegration/KeepMiningList.cs`, and no other ore, so the list never spoils what a world holds. Each ore is its tile's texture, bright where the companion will mine it and dim where it will leave it, whatever the mode, with a mark drawn in the mode's meaning: a red cross where marked ores are skipped, a gold tick where only marked ores are mined, as lines because the game's font has neither glyph. A click toggles the mark in the live list; the preview beside the grid is the hovered ore as a patch of wall, its name, and Will mine or Will leave, in a panel only as tall as those three need (the mock's `#orepanel`) while the grid and its scrollbar keep the page's full height.

The Mastery page draws the tree `DefineMasteryGraph` generates: four ten-node lanes (Combat at the top, then Movement, Survival and Gathering clockwise), each a circle, a circle, a split whose ranks alternate a diamond-left and circle-right with a circle-left and diamond-right, two circles, a meeting diamond and a closing circle, with a shared circle between neighbouring lanes. A diamond opens nothing. The content table is the owner's node list as the agreed mock carries it, ported as data: every node is a flat bonus to a number the companion already reads, a circle takes five levels, a diamond three and a weapon slot (Combat's three diamonds, each an Extra weapon slot) one. The native game still has two weapon slots; the tree's slot diamonds are the proposal, and the Inventory page stays at two. Every node's effect is unbuilt, Plunder's and the two mana nodes' included, so the port changes no gameplay. The default suite's mastery rules row reads the mock itself and fails if any name, level count, effect, need, level line, cost ladder or Core's text here differs from it, so an edit to the list goes into the mock and then here, in that order.

Levels cost points and nothing else, by the owner's ruling of 15 September 2026, which dropped material costs and with them any need to order the tree by the game's boss progression. Each node carries its ladder in its content: a circle's levels cost 1, 2, 2, 3 and 3, a levelling diamond's 1, 2 and 3, a weapon slot 3, and filling the tree costs 415, the figure the unbuilt level curve is to be designed against. Learn names the next level's cost ("Learn · 2 points"), or says "Learned" once the node is full, and the Mastery tile reads the points spent. Nothing is gated on points in this preview: there is no budget, so Learn refuses only by the tree's edges and needs.

The gold centre is a node too, Core, held beside the node table rather than in it because it has no lane, role or edge, at the index one past the last node. A click on the centre diamond picks it. It refuses a level until every other node is full, then takes levels without a cap at a point each, +1% life, mana and damage a level, so points earned after the tree is complete still buy something; its level bar is one segment naming the level reached, because an unbounded node has no last segment to draw. The edges' "from the centre" stays always open whatever Core's level: the centre opens the lanes and Core is what sits on it.

A node whose levels do not step evenly (Piercing, Extra projectile, Bag space) carries one line per level instead of a per-level effect. The panel says the line for the next level to learn, or the last line once the node is full, and hovering a level segment says that level's own line until the pointer leaves it, so the panel still says one sentence at a time. A five-level bar labels its segments with the number alone and a three-level bar with "Level N", because "Level 5" does not fit a fifth of the panel. A lane's label is anchored one node radius plus a gap beyond the lane's last node, with its near edge on that point, because centring the side lanes' labels put them across their last nodes; Reset view fits the tree's real bounds, labels included, and centres it. The page opens with nothing picked and the graph across the whole page. A click on a node picks it and opens the panel, and the graph narrows by the panel's width; the view is a scale and an origin measured from the canvas's top-left, so nothing on screen moves when it narrows, and only if the panel would cover the picked node is the view panned. A press released within the drag threshold on empty background unpicks and closes the panel; a drag pans and never unpicks. Learn adds a level when an edge into the node comes from something learned and everything the node needs is learned, or, for Core, when every other node is full; that rule is `DefineMasteryGraph.CanLearn`, a fact about the graph rather than the page, so the default suite holds it without drawing. Nothing is saved, spent from a budget or applied to gameplay, and the tree gates no behaviour.

## Native coordinate and drawing traps

`Main.UpdateUIStates` runs after `PlayerInput.SetZoom_UI`, and UI-scaled drawing layers use the same conversion. Mouse coordinates there are already UI coordinates: dividing `Main.MouseScreen` again misaligns dragging and hit tests. Layout reads `PlayerInput.OriginalScreenSize / Main.UIScale` because the frame can also be opened from the unscaled health-notch layer. On 2026-09-11 the native 150% fixture reproduced the double conversion as a frame of the wrong width; the fixture asserts frame size and drag displacement.

`mouseInterface` stops world use, but does not stop vanilla inventory slots behind the card from handling the same click. The native inventory draws before this card. `BlockCoveredInventoryInput` wraps that layer: a pointer covered by any card page is temporarily moved offscreen in the engine's raw coordinate cache while the underlying layer draws, then restored in `finally`. Changing only the current mouse coordinates fails because each layer restores them from that cache. Uncovered native inventory remains interactive. Closing the card closes player inventory only when the card opened it.

MagicPixel is an atlas. Lines and fills select a one-pixel source rectangle; stretching the complete texture turns a line into a rectangle.

**The game font at the card's text scale drops the vertical stroke of "+".** At UI scale 1 the Mastery page's zoom buttons were the same dash and only the 150% render showed a plus, so a button labelled "+" or "-" draws its symbol as bars in `DrawCardPrimitives` rather than as text. Any other one-character symbol is worth checking at scale 1 before trusting the font with it.

The rounded shapes are runtime-built textures in `DrawCardPrimitives`, shared with the HUD notch, and **no texture is ever keyed by a shape's width or height.** A rounded rectangle's anti-aliasing lives only in its four radius-sized corners, so `RoundedFill` draws one quadrant mask per radius, flipped into each rounded corner, around solid bands; the notch's fillets are keyed by their side and radius. A cache keyed by pixel size uploaded a new texture mid-draw for every fill width a bar reached and kept it until unload, which the render fixture now fails. The card system releases the cache at unload through `Main.QueueMainThreadAction`, because mod unload runs on a worker thread and FNA3D refuses to dispose a texture there.

`UITextPanel`'s constructor measures its text through the game's font, so constructing any page (every one has buttons) needs fonts loaded; a check that must run without graphics tests the graph's rules, not a page.

## Verification

`../../Tools/EngineReplay/Lifecycle/RenderCompanionCard.cs` renders the production card offscreen at every viewport of `--render-ui` and measures it: every region against the mock's positions, the rhythm between stacked regions, no overlaps, the placement rule against the docked notch, each page's title-bar actions right-aligned before close and clear of the title, the frame's top-left unchanged across pages, the inventory's gear boxes and grid, the mining list's columns, the overview without a back button, the gear row lined up with the bag, the ore preview at the mock's height, the mastery graph filling the page unpicked and narrowed beside the panel picked, Learn and the level bar at the panel's bottom, Piercing at level 2 of 5 saying its next level's line under numbered segments, Core picked by a click on the centre and refused until Learn has filled the tree, then two learns reading Level 2 and the tile 417 points, labels clear of every node, and at scale 1 the drone's core, the three icons, a dim ore dimmer than a bright one, and a lone level segment rounded at both ends. `VerifyNativeCard.cs` drives the controls, the drag guard, the chest buttons against real inventories, the covered game inventory, the mining list's marks and mode, and the mastery page's picking, panning and learning; its `RunMasteryRules` is a default-suite case. Rendering establishes the drawn result under the fixture's seeded state; the feel of interaction in the running game is a playtest surface.
