# Progression — what the companion has earned, priced by the game's own numbers

```
Progression/
├─ CLAUDE.md                  this guide
├─ CompanionExperience.cs     the ledger: level, experience into it, the requirement, both anchors, the pricing, save and load
├─ CreditKillsAndFights.cs    which kills count, whose killing blow it was, when a boss fight ends, and the NPC hooks that feed it
└─ CreditWork.cs              ore, trees and torches: the companion's from its own native calls, the player's from the tile hooks
```

The companion levels so that a level takes a similar amount of play whatever the world is — Journey or Master, vanilla or a modpack of Calamity with Infernum and Fargo's hardest mode — because every number the pricing reads is one the game set for this world: an enemy's maximum life after the difficulty scaled it, and a boss fight's whole life. There are no flat values to tune per difficulty, and a difficulty moves what a kill earns and what a bar costs by the same factor, which the ledger cancels by keeping Normal terms (below). That is the owner's ruling of 15 September 2026, which replaced a placeholder curve and an earlier ruling that only the companion's own completed attempts earned. The ledger is character state, saved with `../PlayerIntegration/`. Nothing in `../Brain/` reads a level: a level changes what the companion can do and survive through the mastery tree, never what it decides.

## What earns, and how much

A kill earns the enemy's maximum life, in whole display experience. The ruling's unit is 0.1% of that life; the ledger keeps its values in that unit and the display divides it back out, because the factor multiplies the earned amount and the bar alike and cancels in every fraction. The companion's killing blow earns in full, the player's in half, anything else's — a trap, lava, a town NPC — nothing. Work earns a fixed share of the bar the level currently needs, the companion's in full and the player's in half: one ore tile broken, one tree felled (once per tree, when its bottom trunk tile goes), one torch placed.

Never counted, and never an anchor: friendly and town NPCs, critters (`NPCID.Sets.CountsAsCritter`), statue spawns, the target dummy and anything immortal. A worm or any body sharing `realLife` is one enemy at its head's life.

## How the bar is priced

**Every value the ledger keeps is in Normal difficulty terms.** A kill's or a fight's life is divided by this world's life multiplier as the credit arrives, and a number is multiplied back only where it is drawn and where the occurrence records it. The multiplier is the game's own: the green slime's maximum life in this world over its maximum life in Normal, both read from `SetDefaults`, so Expert, Master, Journey's strength and a mod scaling enemy life through the game's difficulty all move it. That is the owner's ruling of 15 September 2026, and the reason is that a character carries its ledger into every world it enters: a ledger kept in the terms of the world it was last played in made a Master-levelled character in a Normal world pay three times the kills, for the level in progress through its stored requirement and for every level after through its anchors. A save written before this carries no `units` key; it loads unchanged at the menu, where no world can say which terms it was in, and the first world that reads it converts it once, on the reading that it is the world the character was played in.

Two anchors decide the bar. The enemy anchor is the largest maximum life of any counting non-boss kill so far, by either killer, with the level at which it was set; **the green slime's life in Normal, at level 1, is its floor and not only its default**, so a kill anchors only when it outgrows both. A weaker first kill used to anchor at its own life, price a bar far below the one it replaced, and leave the never-lower rule holding the bar flat for every level until a stronger kill arrived. The boss anchor is the largest whole-fight life of any boss fight ended so far, with its level; there is none before the first boss. The enemy bar is a fixed number of kills of the enemy anchor; the boss bar is the size at which killing the anchor boss again fills a fixed share; each grows by a fixed factor per level since its own anchor was set. The requirement is the larger of the bars that exist, and never below the requirement it replaces — at a level-up as much as at a new anchor. The kill count, the boss share, the growth and the work share are the constants at the top of `CompanionExperience.cs`, and that file is where they are read.

A new, stronger anchor restarts its own growth count. That restart is required rather than a choice of taste: a count from the first level grows without bound, so the strongest boss would soon fill almost nothing.

An anchor-setting credit fills first and anchors second. Every level its experience completes is priced at the anchors as they stood before it; then the anchor moves, and the level in progress is repriced to the larger of its old requirement and the new formula. A fill completes a level within a tiny relative tolerance of the bar, because a thousandth of a bar is not exact in binary and the thousandth ore would otherwise land a rounding error short.

## Who landed the killing blow

The ledger finds a killing blow the way the game finds its own (`NPCKillAttempt`): the target is read just before a strike and again just after, and the strike killed it when the NPC holding the target's life is no longer active. The whole death — `checkDead`, loot, deactivation — happens inside the strike between the two halves. The NPC is held by object reference rather than by slot, because `NPC.NewNPC` builds a new object for every spawn: the object a strike or a fight took still says whether it died after its slot has gone to something else, so this folder keeps no spawn generation of its own (the one generation counter, for everyone else who keys on slots, is `../Brain/Infrastructure/Observation/ObserveHostileAttackSources.cs`). `GlobalNPC.OnKill` is not the kill signal, because NPCLoot returns before it whenever any mod's PreKill refuses, and a ledger fed from it would lose kills silently in exactly the modpacks the ruling names.

The striker is decided at the before half, and there are two doors. The NPC's projectile hook takes a projectile's strike first: a companion projectile is whatever `../Brain/Infrastructure/Interactions/Firing/TrackLandedHits.cs`'s `IsCompanionShot` says is one, and that answer is a lineage: a slot is the companion's from the arsenal's registration of its shot, or from its spawn off a companion parent, until that slot's next spawn, whether or not the shot carried a forecast and whether or not its outcome window is still open — ownership cannot say this, because every companion shot is owned by the local player — and the player's is any other friendly projectile he owns that no trap and no town NPC fired. The player's own `ModPlayer.ModifyHitNPC` and `OnHitNPC` are the second door: tModLoader calls them for every strike he lands, item, projectile and `Player.ApplyDamageToNPC` alike, which makes them the only door a dash, a mount's stomp, contact damage or an aura comes through, and the one for his melee. They take a strike as his only when nothing took it earlier in the same tick. The companion's swing reaches the NPC through that same `Player.ApplyDamageToNPC` on the local player, so `../Brain/Infrastructure/Interactions/Firing/ItemWeapon.cs` brackets its own strike first and the player's door defers to it; the same deferral keeps a companion shot the companion's when the player's projectile hook sees it after the NPC's.

A death with no strike around it — a debuff ticking the last life away, lava, a fall, a despawn — is nobody's killing blow and earns nothing. That is a consequence of the rule, not a guard, and it means a debuff weapon's kill earns only when a hit lands the last point.

## Boss fights

A boss fight is one encounter's NPCs that hold their own life. It opens on a boss body — flagged `boss` or `NPCID.Sets.ShouldBeCountedAsBoss`: the Twins, Moon Lord's core, head and hands, the Destroyer's head, an Eater of Worlds head, the pillars, every modded boss that sets the flag — and while a boss body lives its company joins it too: an NPC in `NPCID.Sets.BossBestiaryPriority`, or one a member's own AI spawned (`EntitySource_Parent` naming it). A tick sweep after NPCs update joins them. The fight ends on the sweep that finds no boss body left. It is dropped as a despawn when every boss body left alive, whatever minions died, and otherwise credited once.

**The fight's life is every member that has to die, and the fight shows which those are rather than a list of ids** (the owner's ruling of 15 September 2026). A member counts at its maximum life unless its type is endless — spawned again after a member of that type left the fight, which is what servants, probes, bees, leeches and sharkrons do and what creepers, worm segments, hands, arms, fists and Golem's head do not — or nothing could ever hurt it, as with Plantera's hooks. So the Eater of Worlds pays every segment whichever end dies first, where counting only boss-flagged bodies paid 3000 head first and 150 tail first, because the game makes the next segment a head by changing its type in place with no spawn. Bodies sharing `realLife` — the Destroyer's segments, the Wall of Flesh's eyes — are one member with the NPC whose life they hold. A member left dead when its own object is inactive with no life, which a PreKill refusal and a slot refilled before the sweep both leave readable.

**The credit goes to whichever of the companion or the player struck any member last**: in full for the companion, in half for the player, and nothing when neither ever struck it. A strike by anything else never overwrites that. It follows the last strike rather than a strike that ended a body, because a boss may pass through a death state first — Moon Lord's core restores its life and turns invulnerable before it dies — and because a boss nobody finished, to a debuff or a trap, belongs to the last of the two who fought it.

Company never earns as an ordinary enemy, even when it dies with no boss body alive, and sets no enemy anchor: counted as ordinary enemies, a Prime arm's thousands of life would pin the enemy anchor for the rest of a playthrough and every hardmode enemy after it would pay almost nothing. Counting company inside the fight was measured against the owner's own examples rather than argued: with only boss bodies the Brain of Cthulhu paid its brain alone and Golem its body without the head, and with every member but no endless rule a boss that launches minions without end would grow its own life without end.

## What reaches in, and what it reaches

`../Brain/Infrastructure/Interactions/Firing/ItemWeapon.cs` brackets the swing; `../Brain/Infrastructure/Interactions/Firing/TrackLandedHits.cs` answers whether a projectile is the companion's. The miner, the chopper and the torch placer in `../Brain/Infrastructure/Interactions/` call `CreditWork` after their native edit, and only when the edit is observed to have happened. The player's tile hooks use the cursor target, the swing and the held tool's power, and skip world generation and the companion's own hits through `TileDamageWatcher.CompanionIsHitting` in `../Brain/Infrastructure/Observation/`. Every credit writes an `experience-credit` occurrence through `../Brain/Infrastructure/Diagnostics/RecordGodsEyeEvents.cs`, naming the source, the earner, the amount, the level before and after, the bar and both anchors with the one it moved. `../HeadsUpDisplay/` and `../ProfileCard/` read `Level`, `IntoLevel`, `NeededNow` and `Fraction`; `NeededNow` saturates at the largest integer, which a late modpack bar can pass, while the ledger keeps the true value.

A character saved under the placeholder curve carries only an integer `total`; it loads as a fresh level 1 and the key is ignored, which the owner accepted because those totals bought nothing.

## What it deliberately does not do

It grants no mastery points and spends none; the card still learns without points. It guards one kind of cheap repeatable work, because the owner ruled it on 15 September 2026: a torch tile is paid once per world, by either earner, and the set of paid tiles is saved with the world, since the companion's torches are free and a place-break-place loop was otherwise experience for nothing. It does not guard the rest — placing and re-mining an ore, felling acorn trees, duplicating ore in Journey — because the owner has not ruled a guard for them, and one invented here would be a product decision made by a bookkeeping file. An ordinary enemy with no strike on the killing tick earns nothing, while a boss fight whose last body died to a debuff credits whichever of the two struck it last, because a fight's blow is its last strike by either of them.

## Traps

- **The fixtures call the static entry points, not the hooks.** The headless harness loads no GlobalNPC, ModPlayer or GlobalTile, so `Tools/EngineReplay/Lifecycle/VerifyCompanionExperience.cs` drives `BeforeStrike`, `BeforePlayerStrike` and `AfterStrike` in the order tModLoader calls them around the game's own `StrikeNPC`, and `PlayerKilledTile` directly. The hooks are one-line delegations for that reason; logic added to a hook body is logic no row runs.
- **A boss's own flags are set by its AI, which nothing headless runs.** The Brain of Cthulhu is `dontTakeDamage` from `SetDefaults` until its creepers are gone, and Golem's parts similarly, so a fight row mirrors that one AI step by hand or the fight rightly reads the brain as something nothing could hurt.
