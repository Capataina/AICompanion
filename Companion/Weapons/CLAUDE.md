# Weapons — the item in the hand, chosen by landed outcome

```

├─ CLAUDE.md                 this guide
├─ CompanionWeapon.cs        the contract the arsenal reads: facts, in-reach, hits, fire

├─ CompanionMana.cs          the pool that mirrors the player's maximum and tires a magic weapon
```

The companion has no weapon of its own. Two weapon slots in the gear (`../Inventory/`) hold real items, from the game or any mod, and the arsenal fires or swings whatever is there. The ruling of 14 September 2026 is that a handed item is read for its numbers and never run: nothing here calls the player's item-use code, and an item whose behaviour lives outside its numbers is refused at the slot and never reaches a forecast. The authored kit that preceded this — a bow and a throwing knife with hand-set flight profiles and balance factors — is gone, and so is its balance argument: an item's damage is the item's, and which of two items wins is what the arsenal works out from what each would land.

## What a weapon is

`CompanionWeapon` is the contract the arsenal reads and `ItemWeapon` its one implementation, built from an `Item`. A weapon states facts and answers two geometric questions in the arsenal's own words, and holds no opinion about when it should be used: the arsenal decides that by working out what each weapon would actually land over its window, and a weapon carrying its own view of "suits a crowd" would be overruling that arithmetic with a guess. That separation is what lets any item arrive as numbers rather than as a rule.

A projectile weapon's facts follow the game's own composition of weapon and ammo, read rather than called. Damage is the item's plus the free ammo's; launch speed the item's plus the ammo's; the projectile is the ammo's where the ammo names one, additively for rocket and solution launchers, as `Player.PickAmmo` does; pierce is the projectile sample's own penetration, with unlimited reported as the width of the arsenal's pierce buffer; the player's class modifier for the item's class is applied with the arrow, bullet and specialist bonuses where the ammo is one, the combination `Player.GetWeaponDamage` builds before its mod hooks. Ammo is free: the ammo is the class's default from the gear's own lookup and is never consumed. The projectile is spawned from the companion's own source and owned by the local player, so kills, drops and on-hit effects stay the player's. Crit is absent because that spawn path carries none, so scoring one would be scoring a fiction.

A magic item reads the mana pool's factor into its damage and spends its cost after the damage is read, so the cast that empties the pool lands at the strength the pool had and an empty pool never refuses a cast. The scorer reads the same damage, so a tired staff ranks below a fresh bow without a rule of its own; give the pool a moment and the staff comes back. `CompanionMana` mirrors the player's maximum the way life and defence are mirrored, regenerates after a delay, and is session state.

A swung item is a one-tick flight of length reach in the aim solver, so the same sweep of angles that finds an arc finds a swing with terrain in the way, and the positioner's flight model still means something for a sword. That flight's box is the orb's own diameter rather than the item's drawn height, because the box only asks whether terrain blocks the sweep: a sword-height box straddled the floor from an orb hovering low over it and found no clear angle at a zombie standing on that floor, which the item-weapon fixture caught on the first merged tree. Reach is the item's drawn width at its scale plus the body's radius, read from the body's one declaration so a resized body resizes every swing. In-reach measures to the nearest point of the target's box, because a boss touching the orb has a centre a hundred pixels away. The swing's crowd count is a sector: every hostile whose box is within reach, whose centre is within the half-angle of the aim, and which the orb can see is struck once per swing, player-owned through `Player.ApplyDamageToNPC`, the game's own path from a player to an NPC's life. The sight test is per body, because the flight model traces only the aim's own ray, and without it a sword struck through a one-tile floor at everything in the sector but the aimed target. A swing takes no aim noise, since it has no launch to be imprecise about, and a swung item that costs mana spends it exactly as a shot does. A swing spawns nothing, so it never passes through the landed-hit hook; its landed count is the strike itself.

A use returns what it put into the world — a projectile slot, or the bodies a swing struck — because the two are never both meaningful and the arsenal registers a shot's slot for landed-hit attribution and arc learning while a swing has none. A swing files the same god's-eye shot record with no slot, so the log reads a swing beside a shot in one vocabulary.

## How the arsenal chooses

The arsenal enumerates its weapons from the gear's two weapon slots whenever the gear's signature changes, re-running each slot's predicate so an item that stopped passing after it was saved is refused here as well as at the slot; a change of gear drops the choice, the held target and every cache, because each names a weapon that may no longer be in hand. With both slots empty the arsenal says so — no target, no engagement, `no-weapon` as the fire outcome, infinite removal and intervention times — so protection cannot read an unarmed companion as about to save anyone. The muzzle is the orb's centre, because an orb has no facing and no hand.

TargetEvidence records considered entity generations, weapon, health-capped damage, projected kills, prevented harm and combined value at TargetEvidenceTick. Held-target ticks retain that stamp rather than presenting an old ranking as a new evaluation. The recorder consumes it alongside position alternatives and links the selected sequence estimate to the shot's projectile generation.

The joint evaluator compares legal first attacks with a bounded greedy continuation using the same candidate attacks. Damage reserves projected health, so overkill and duplicate pellets cannot repeatedly earn kill credit. Time-discounted effective damage, threat removal and a small finishing value share one policy in BehaviourWeights. This is an estimate over current geometry, not a simulation of future enemy decisions or a globally optimal attack schedule. A weapon's prior value is damage on bodies the traced flight or swing already crosses, and whatever it does beyond that — homing past the trace, splitting into children, a debuff that makes the other weapon hit harder — reaches the arsenal only through what the learner below has seen it achieve. Damage over time is still not counted anywhere, and nothing can ignore terrain in the prior, because the trace ends at the first solid it meets.

Threat membership does not mean a target can be damaged. Target retention, engagement, expected-damage bodies and final firing revalidate Terraria's chase predicate; an invulnerable hostile stays in hazard observation but cannot attract a hunt or earn fictional weapon damage. Protection asks the arsenal for an optimistic time to remove the most urgent player threat, including reload, projectile flight and repeat hits. No demonstrated shot means unknown intervention time, represented as infinity, so a wall cannot make the player appear safe. Choice, engagement and intervention caches expire when the muzzle, target pose, life, generation or terrain changes. Their time limits bound unchanged states; they must not hide a newly opened firing window. An urgent player threat can interrupt target retention, and the bounded candidate shortlist orders urgency before proximity.

Pursuit shares this valuation through `EstimateDelayedAttackValue` instead of keeping its own opinion of what an attack is worth. The query reuses the joint evaluator unchanged: the first shot fires no earlier than the reposition wait it is given, and the continuation is the set of trajectory-checked attacks the last `BestTarget` ranking retained from the current muzzle (`FromHereAttacksTick` stamps them; a held target keeps the older set). A target that can already be shot starts from its retained attacks; one that needs a reposition gets an assumed single-hit attack per weapon, with per-hit damage after defence, the weapon's use time and a flight time from the current distance at the weapon's own launch speed. The assumption is optimistic about the arc, which position selection proves at the stand, and pessimistic about the wait, because shots the hands take during the walk are not counted. `EstimateRemovalTicks` is the arc-free companion to `EstimateInterventionTicks`: the hits a target's life needs after defence, spaced by use time, plus one flight, for the faster weapon, assuming every hit lands.

The choice trace and the final use are deliberately separate. A weapon can score only on a solved swept flight, then its accuracy rotation is traced again before anything is spawned or struck. A failed trace is cached only for its unchanged muzzle, target pose and terrain revision and only for a short freshness window; it is not a weapon cooldown, so an opened door or moving target can create a shot immediately. When the chosen weapon's arc is gone by the time the hands fire, the other weapon in hand is tried before giving up.

## A hit pushes, and what it pushes into is charged to the shot

Every hit the companion lands pushes its enemy, and the push is part of what the hit is worth. The owner's playtest of 15 September 2026 showed the companion knocking enemies into the player and into itself again and again, because nothing modelled knockback at all: an arrow from the far side of a zombie was worth exactly what the same arrow from the player's side was worth. Three pieces answer it, and they divide by what each can know.

**What a hit does is learned per weapon item and per enemy type, with the game's own arithmetic as the prior.** `LearnWeaponEffectsOnEnemies.cs` holds, for each pair, a signed push factor on the prior's horizontal velocity change and a damage ratio on the arsenal's own damage prediction, each a median over a small ring of recent samples. The prior is `NPC.GetIncomingStrikeModifiers` and `NPC.StrikeNPC` as decompiled: the knockback is multiplied by the enemy's resistance, a resistance of zero disables it, it is compressed above a series of bands and capped, and then one of two branches runs by whether ten times the damage (fifteen in expert) exceeds the enemy's maximum life. A heavy hit moves the horizontal speed toward the knockback in the hit's direction and clamps there; a light hit sets it to the knockback times the direction times the resistance **again**, so the resistance lands squared on that branch. The native-match row holds the prior to the game's own strike on both branches. It is learned rather than read because knockback resistance differs per enemy, bosses resist it entirely, and a mod can change a hit's knockback or damage inside a hook no reading reaches. The key is the item rather than the projectile, because a push is the item's knockback plus its ammo's, and a swing has no projectile. The factor is signed so a weapon whose pushes go the other way from the direction the prior assumed learns the direction by the same arithmetic as the magnitude. Only the horizontal is learned, because only the horizontal side decides whether a push carries an enemy toward the player or the orb.

The direction the prior assumes is the game's. A projectile's hit is pushed along its own `direction`, which `Projectile.Update` sets from the sign of its horizontal speed, and a swing along the side of its aim — except for the types `Projectile.Damage` overrides to push away from the **owner**, which for every companion shot is the player: types 697, 699, 707, 708 and 759, flails (aiStyle 15) and the 1.4.4 swing projectiles (aiStyles 188 to 191). The flails never reach a slot; the swing projectiles do, because the slot's predicate refuses flails, spears, yoyos and whips by style and these are none of them. That is recorded rather than fixed here: whether the arsenal should fire a projectile that follows its owner is a gear decision, and until it is made the push rule is right about them.

Samples arrive from two places, because the two kinds of use reach the game by two paths. A projectile's strike passes through the NPC hooks: the modify hook runs before `StrikeNPC` writes the push, so `TrackLandedHits` keeps each struck NPC's velocity there, and the on-hit hook reads the velocity the strike left and teaches the table. A swing's `Player.ApplyDamageToNPC` runs no NPC hook, so `ItemWeapon` observes around its own call. A sample is skipped where it cannot be read: a crit, whose extra push the prior does not model; a push the prior predicts as nothing, which is an enemy already at the speed the heavy branch clamps to; and a killing swing, whose life lost is capped at the life that was left. The table is session state, never saved, cleared by process restart and by the engine replay's per-case reset.

**The arsenal charges a hit for the danger its push adds, and never for danger the enemy already carried.** For each body a forecast strikes, `Arsenal.InducedDanger` displaces the enemy horizontally by the push the table expects — the push's added speed held for a settle window, since a knocked walker keeps that speed until its own AI turns it, and its airtime would read every push as a pixel — and re-weighs its urgency to the player, and to the orb standing at the muzzle the forecast is asked about, at the displaced centre with `ThreatUrgency`, the rule the threat sense uses. The orb is weighed at the muzzle rather than at the live body because the same forecast prices candidate stands, and a push toward the stand the orb would fire from is the push that comes back at it. Only an increase is charged, per body, times what one of that enemy's hits takes off that body; speed, sight and reach are held at what the sense decided this tick on both sides of the difference, which is what keeps the baseline out. The charge rides on the hit into the evaluator, which subtracts it from a wound and never from a kill, because a dead enemy is pushed nowhere. Sight is not re-tested at the displaced centre, so a push that brings an enemy into a sight line it lacked is under-charged; the alternative is a line-of-sight test per hit per forecast. The assumed attacks that price a reposition carry no charge, because their stand is not chosen yet and the positioner prices the side at the stand.

**A wound to a dangerous enemy is worth something before it kills.** The evaluator used to credit an enemy's expected harm only on the killing hit, so a wound to a zombie beside the player was worth what the same wound to a harmless slime was. A hit that does not kill now earns a share of that credit in proportion to the fraction of the enemy's remaining life it removes, weighted by the enemy's danger; the kill still earns the full credit on top of whatever its earlier wounds earned.

## Landed hits, and the observer that teaches the aimer

The same slot a shot spawned into is registered in `TrackLandedHits` with the aimed target's identity, and a native NPC hit hook attributes a projectile hit to that registered shot, recording the struck NPC and the aimed one side by side. Where the feet go (pursuit), what the hands chose (aim) and what the game says was struck (landed hit) are three different facts — a piercing arrow aimed at one enemy can land on the one in front — so none of them stands in for another. Every native projectile spawn first forgets its slot, and a companion shot is registered after its own spawn has cleared it, so the player's arrow or a hostile's shot that later reuses the slot can never inherit the companion's identity. The ledger is a brain-side fact rather than a God's Eye record, so a consumer never has to read the diagnostic stream to learn what it hit. Nothing in gameplay reads it yet: hunt progress still counts life lost by the pursued generation.

The same file carries the global projectile hooks that feed the arc learner in `../Brain/Infrastructure/Aiming/`: a spawn forgets the slot's watch as it forgets its shot, every post-AI velocity of a registered companion projectile is observed until the watch is full or the projectile dies, and a death retires the watch so what it showed is fitted. The learner and the aimer own what is done with those samples; this folder only delivers them, under the same slot-reuse discipline as the landed-hit ledger.

## What a weapon's attacks achieve is learned, and weapon, target, stand and aim are one decision over it

The owner ruled on 15 September 2026 that a decision evaluates every option and learns from what its own actions did, and asked that learning how to use a weapon go together with choosing which weapon and which target. Before this, the arsenal's arithmetic was the whole answer: a homing staff was valued as though its bolt flew the straight trace, a splitting shot as though it never split, and a debuff as nothing, so any weapon whose value lived outside the trace was used for the wrong reasons or not at all. No shipped game learns weapon effectiveness at run time; the research behind this weighed contextual bandits, Gaussian-process optimisation, a prior with a residual, and case-based memory, and a contextual bandit won because it learns unmodelled behaviour from outcomes alone, takes aim as an input so each weapon finds out whether aim matters to it, explores by construction, and costs little per decision.

**The arithmetic is the prior and the learner corrects each forecast hit's damage by a factor.** `Arsenal.Forecast` still solves the arc, walks the bodies the flight crosses, reads each hit's damage after armour through the weapon-effects table and prices the push charge; that is the prior mean. `AttackLearning` (`LearnAttackOutcomes.cs`) then multiplies each hit's damage by what this weapon's attacks have achieved against their forecasts in this context. With nothing observed for a weapon the factor is exactly one and no random number is drawn, which is what keeps every forecast, and every combat fixture that never fires, identical to the arithmetic.

**What is learned is the outcome ratio minus one, per weapon item, by Bayesian linear regression sampled with Thompson sampling.** When a use's window closes, its realised yield — damage landed plus a value per body struck, per second of the weapon's use — is divided by the yield the forecast predicted and clamped, and the learner regresses that ratio less one on the context the use was fired in: a bias, distance as a share of the weapon's reach, the aim's offset from the intercept as a share of the widest candidate, the target's speed relative to the orb, the other hostiles along the lane, the orb's own speed, and whether the target carries a debuff the other weapon applied. A plain difference lost because it is in each weapon's damage units, and a log-ratio lost because a miss, the commonest outcome of an aim-sensitive weapon, has no logarithm. Each outcome is a rank-one update of the posterior mean and covariance, the covariance's Cholesky factor is rebuilt only when the posterior changes, and a decision draws one coefficient vector per weapon per tick that every target, stand and aim candidate in that tick shares. **Every draw a decision reads is kept for the decision**: an enemy type the weapon has never struck used to add a fresh number on each call, so the aim candidates inside one forecast were compared against different noise, and a fixture's seed reproduced nothing. Each enemy type carries a bias shrunk toward its weapon's average, updated on what the weapon model leaves unexplained after its own update, so a new enemy type starts at the weapon's average.

**A use's outcome window holds its projectile and every projectile descended from it.** `ShotOutcomes` (`ObserveShotOutcomes.cs`) opens a window when the arsenal fires, against the forecast's own prior — the damage it predicted before any correction, the bodies it expected to strike and the charge it carried — and the context with the aim as it actually left, noise included. The global spawn hook in `TrackLandedHits.cs` forgets a slot's window first and then, when the spawn's source is `EntitySource_Parent` naming a projectile whose slot belongs to an open window, joins the child to that window; the companion's own spawn names the companion NPC, never a projectile, so it never attributes itself. The window closes when its last projectile dies or its bound elapses. **A use whose aimed body was gone before the forecast had it landing, and which never struck that body, teaches the learner nothing**: the player, a trap or another shot killed it, or its slot went to a new enemy, so a zero there is an outcome of the fight rather than of the weapon, and a companion fighting beside the player would otherwise learn that it misses whatever he finishes first. A body that leaves after the landing tick is a miss as usual, and the struck bodies' debuffs are taught either way. The NPC modify hook keeps the struck body's buffs and the on-hit hook compares them, because the game applies a projectile's status between the two (decompiled `Projectile.Damage`), so the diff is exactly what that hit added; each added buff is remembered against the body and the item, which is what "debuffed by the other weapon" reads. A swing's window opens and closes around its own strikes, which `ItemWeapon` reports with the buffs each body carried before.

**A debuff is a rate and a context, and the evaluator carries it through the continuation.** For each weapon and enemy type the learner counts how many struck bodies came away carrying a buff they had not, and for how long. Every forecast hit carries two damages — in the body's current state and against a body the other weapon has debuffed — and that weapon's chance and length of debuff. Once an applied attack lands in `EvaluateAttackOutcomes`' continuation, its body counts as debuffed by that weapon until the debuff runs out, and a later hit from the other weapon is valued between its two damages by that chance. That is how a debuff-then-burst pair opens with the debuff without an eligibility trace or a written combination rule.

**Aim is a decision variable whose prior is the intercept's.** A forecast asks the learner about the intercept and a few offsets off it, all sharing the intercept's geometry, and keeps the aim whose learned factor on the aimed target is largest; a straight arrow learns that aiming off costs its whole value and keeps the intercept, a homing bolt learns that it does not matter. Because the aim input is the offset's size and the model is linear, the answer is either the intercept or the widest candidate, never something in between. An offset shot is fired only if it flies clear of terrain for the intercept's flight (`TrajectoryAimer.TryClear`), since reaching the target's box is what it is finding out; one that meets a wall falls back to the intercept. A swing has no launch to aim.

**Exploration stops under real danger.** When any listed threat's urgency to either body is above the declared ceiling, every forecast uses the posterior mean instead of a draw, so the companion does not try a worse attack while something is about to hurt someone. **A firing stand is priced by every weapon in hand**: the positioner reads the best learned attack any weapon could make from a stand, at arrival, as a share of the best those weapons could do with no geometry in the way (`../Brain/Infrastructure/Position/CLAUDE.md`), which closed AIC-395.

What this deliberately does not do. **Damage over time is not counted**: life a debuff burns after the hit arrives through no hit hook, and crediting a struck body's later life loss would also credit the player's own attacks, so a weapon whose value is its burn is under-credited by exactly its burn. **The push is not in the learned ratio**: the weapon-effects table already learns each weapon's push per enemy type from the same hits and the forecast charges it, so putting the realised push into the ratio as well would count it twice; the closed use's reward subtracts the forecast's charge for the record only. **A companion swing never applies its item's status**, because `Player.ApplyDamageToNPC` applies none; the item-status path lives in the player's melee hit code, which is the item-use code this folder never runs. **An item that fires several projectiles through its own code fires one**: `ItemWeapon.Fire` spawns exactly one projectile, and a spread or a multi-arrow volley lives in the item's `Shoot`, which the companion never calls; only children a projectile's own AI spawns are seen. The intervention and removal estimates protection reads stay on the prior. And no recorder column carries the learner's numbers yet: `AttackLearning.LastSampledFactor`, `LastMeanFactor` and `ShotOutcomes.LastClosed` are there for whoever owns the schema.

## Fixtures

`Tools/EngineReplay/Combat/VerifyWeaponLearning.cs` holds the learning: aim coefficients from synthetic streams, the hands firing the aim the learner chose, a debuff-then-burst pair opening with the debuff against its control, child attribution on constructed parent sources, a swing teaching at once, the danger gate against a planted posterior, AIC-395's stand pricing, and the learner's cost per ranking. `Tools/EngineReplay/Combat/VerifyItemWeapon.cs` puts one real item in a slot and asks the arsenal to fire: a bow fires the wooden arrow's projectile player-owned at the composed speed and damage, a pistol the musket ball's, a wand spends mana and lands at the gradient down to an empty pool, a sword strikes the body in its arc and refuses one out of reach, a yoyo forced into a slot reaches nothing, and an empty gear says `no-weapon`. Enemy AI does not run and spawned projectiles are never advanced there, so it establishes what leaves the muzzle and what a swing does on contact, not a fight. The `channel` plus overridden `ModItem.Shoot` refusal and the modded-ammo-class refusal are unverified: no modded item exists headless.

Two fixture premises moved when the authored kit died, and both are worth knowing before a combat scene is retuned. The combat actor matrix's blocked rows hold the bow alone, because a thrown knife lobs over a three-tile pillar onto a body five tiles away within the arsenal's trace — a correct answer, and not the one the matrix varies. Its guard pair's guarded life is sized so the walk's share of access plus removal is a clear margin under the bow's own damage rather than the authored bow's doubled one. And the fixtures' seeded gear carries the throwing knife as its second weapon because the reposition rows were calibrated against that pair's speeds: a wait priced at the edge of the evaluation window is inside it for the knife and outside it for the bow alone.
