The companion has no weapon of its own. Two weapon slots in the gear (`../Inventory/`) hold real items, from the game or any mod, and the arsenal fires or swings whatever is there. The ruling of 14 September 2026 is that a handed item is read for its numbers and never run: nothing here calls the player's item-use code, and an item whose behaviour lives outside its numbers is refused at the slot and never reaches a forecast. The authored kit that preceded this — a bow and a throwing knife with hand-set flight profiles and balance factors — is gone, and so is its balance argument: an item's damage is the item's, and which of two items wins is what the arsenal works out from what each would land.

## What a weapon is

`CompanionWeapon` is the contract the arsenal reads and `ItemWeapon` its one implementation, built from an `Item`. A weapon states facts and answers two geometric questions in the arsenal's own words, and holds no opinion about when it should be used: the arsenal decides that by working out what each weapon would actually land over its window, and a weapon carrying its own view of "suits a crowd" would be overruling that arithmetic with a guess. That separation is what lets any item arrive as numbers rather than as a rule.

A projectile weapon's facts follow the game's own composition of weapon and ammo, read rather than called. Damage is the item's plus the free ammo's; launch speed the item's plus the ammo's; the projectile is the ammo's where the ammo names one, additively for rocket and solution launchers, as `Player.PickAmmo` does; pierce is the projectile sample's own penetration, with unlimited reported as the width of the arsenal's pierce buffer; the player's class modifier for the item's class is applied with the arrow, bullet and specialist bonuses where the ammo is one, the combination `Player.GetWeaponDamage` builds before its mod hooks. Ammo is free: the ammo is the class's default from the gear's own lookup and is never consumed. The projectile is spawned from the companion's own source and owned by the local player, so kills, drops and on-hit effects stay the player's. Crit is absent because that spawn path carries none, so scoring one would be scoring a fiction.

A magic item reads the mana pool's factor into its damage and spends its cost after the damage is read, so the cast that empties the pool lands at the strength the pool had and an empty pool never refuses a cast. The scorer reads the same damage, so a tired staff ranks below a fresh bow without a rule of its own; give the pool a moment and the staff comes back. `CompanionMana` mirrors the player's maximum the way life and defence are mirrored, regenerates after a delay, and is session state.

A swung item is a one-tick flight of length reach in the aim solver, so the same sweep of angles that finds an arc finds a swing with terrain in the way, and the positioner's flight model still means something for a sword. Reach is the item's drawn width at its scale plus the body's radius, read from the body's one declaration so a resized body resizes every swing. In-reach measures to the nearest point of the target's box, because a boss touching the orb has a centre a hundred pixels away. The swing's crowd count is a sector: every hostile whose box is within reach and within the half-angle of the aim is struck once per swing, player-owned through `Player.ApplyDamageToNPC`, the game's own path from a player to an NPC's life. A swing spawns nothing, so it never passes through the landed-hit hook; its landed count is the strike itself.

A use returns what it put into the world — a projectile slot, or the bodies a swing struck — because the two are never both meaningful and the arsenal registers a shot's slot for landed-hit attribution and arc learning while a swing has none. A swing files the same god's-eye shot record with no slot, so the log reads a swing beside a shot in one vocabulary.

## How the arsenal chooses

The arsenal enumerates its weapons from the gear's two weapon slots whenever the gear's signature changes, re-running each slot's predicate so an item that stopped passing after it was saved is refused here as well as at the slot; a change of gear drops the choice, the held target and every cache, because each names a weapon that may no longer be in hand. With both slots empty the arsenal says so — no target, no engagement, `no-weapon` as the fire outcome, infinite removal and intervention times — so protection cannot read an unarmed companion as about to save anyone. The muzzle is the orb's centre, because an orb has no facing and no hand.

TargetEvidence records considered entity generations, weapon, health-capped damage, projected kills, prevented harm and combined value at TargetEvidenceTick. Held-target ticks retain that stamp rather than presenting an old ranking as a new evaluation. The recorder consumes it alongside position alternatives and links the selected sequence estimate to the shot's projectile generation.

The joint evaluator compares legal first attacks with a bounded greedy continuation using the same candidate attacks. Damage reserves projected health, so overkill and duplicate pellets cannot repeatedly earn kill credit. Time-discounted effective damage, threat removal and a small finishing value share one policy in BehaviourWeights. This is an estimate over current geometry, not a simulation of future enemy decisions or a globally optimal attack schedule. A weapon's value has to be expressible as damage on bodies the traced flight or swing already crosses, or the arsenal cannot see it: there is no term for damage over time, for a deferred effect or for a status, so a weapon whose opening deals nothing scores zero and is never chosen, and nothing can ignore terrain, because the trace ends at the first solid it meets.

Threat membership does not mean a target can be damaged. Target retention, engagement, expected-damage bodies and final firing revalidate Terraria's chase predicate; an invulnerable hostile stays in hazard observation but cannot attract a hunt or earn fictional weapon damage. Protection asks the arsenal for an optimistic time to remove the most urgent player threat, including reload, projectile flight and repeat hits. No demonstrated shot means unknown intervention time, represented as infinity, so a wall cannot make the player appear safe. Choice, engagement and intervention caches expire when the muzzle, target pose, life, generation or terrain changes. Their time limits bound unchanged states; they must not hide a newly opened firing window. An urgent player threat can interrupt target retention, and the bounded candidate shortlist orders urgency before proximity.

Pursuit shares this valuation through `EstimateDelayedAttackValue` instead of keeping its own opinion of what an attack is worth. The query reuses the joint evaluator unchanged: the first shot fires no earlier than the reposition wait it is given, and the continuation is the set of trajectory-checked attacks the last `BestTarget` ranking retained from the current muzzle (`FromHereAttacksTick` stamps them; a held target keeps the older set). A target that can already be shot starts from its retained attacks; one that needs a reposition gets an assumed single-hit attack per weapon, with per-hit damage after defence, the weapon's use time and a flight time from the current distance at the weapon's own launch speed. The assumption is optimistic about the arc, which position selection proves at the stand, and pessimistic about the wait, because shots the hands take during the walk are not counted. `EstimateRemovalTicks` is the arc-free companion to `EstimateInterventionTicks`: the hits a target's life needs after defence, spaced by use time, plus one flight, for the faster weapon, assuming every hit lands.

The choice trace and the final use are deliberately separate. A weapon can score only on a solved swept flight, then its accuracy rotation is traced again before anything is spawned or struck. A failed trace is cached only for its unchanged muzzle, target pose and terrain revision and only for a short freshness window; it is not a weapon cooldown, so an opened door or moving target can create a shot immediately. When the chosen weapon's arc is gone by the time the hands fire, the other weapon in hand is tried before giving up.

## Landed hits, and the observer that teaches the aimer

The same slot a shot spawned into is registered in `TrackLandedHits` with the aimed target's identity, and a native NPC hit hook attributes a projectile hit to that registered shot, recording the struck NPC and the aimed one side by side. Where the feet go (pursuit), what the hands chose (aim) and what the game says was struck (landed hit) are three different facts — a piercing arrow aimed at one enemy can land on the one in front — so none of them stands in for another. Every native projectile spawn first forgets its slot, and a companion shot is registered after its own spawn has cleared it, so the player's arrow or a hostile's shot that later reuses the slot can never inherit the companion's identity. The ledger is a brain-side fact rather than a God's Eye record, so a consumer never has to read the diagnostic stream to learn what it hit. Nothing in gameplay reads it yet: hunt progress still counts life lost by the pursued generation.

The same file carries the global projectile hooks that feed the arc learner in `../Brain/Infrastructure/Aiming/`: a spawn forgets the slot's watch as it forgets its shot, every post-AI velocity of a registered companion projectile is observed until the watch is full or the projectile dies, and a death retires the watch so what it showed is fitted. The learner and the aimer own what is done with those samples; this folder only delivers them, under the same slot-reuse discipline as the landed-hit ledger.

## Fixtures

`Tools/EngineReplay/Combat/VerifyItemWeapon.cs` puts one real item in a slot and asks the arsenal to fire: a bow fires the wooden arrow's projectile player-owned at the composed speed and damage, a pistol the musket ball's, a wand spends mana and lands at the gradient down to an empty pool, a sword strikes the body in its arc and refuses one out of reach, a yoyo forced into a slot reaches nothing, and an empty gear says `no-weapon`. Enemy AI does not run and spawned projectiles are never advanced there, so it establishes what leaves the muzzle and what a swing does on contact, not a fight. The `channel` plus overridden `ModItem.Shoot` refusal and the modded-ammo-class refusal are unverified: no modded item exists headless.

Two fixture premises moved when the authored kit died, and both are worth knowing before a combat scene is retuned. The combat actor matrix's blocked rows hold the bow alone, because a thrown knife lobs over a three-tile pillar onto a body five tiles away within the arsenal's trace — a correct answer, and not the one the matrix varies. Its guard pair's guarded life is sized so the walk's share of access plus removal is a clear margin under the bow's own damage rather than the authored bow's doubled one. And the fixtures' seeded gear carries the throwing knife as its second weapon because the reposition rows were calibrated against that pair's speeds: a wait priced at the edge of the evaluation window is inside it for the knife and outside it for the bow alone.
