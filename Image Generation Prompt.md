# Image Generation Prompt — companion droid concept sheet

This file holds the prompt for generating concept art of the companion's body: one 6×6 sheet of 36 different pixel-art designs, so a design can be chosen by eye and then redrawn by hand in Aseprite with its own animations. The concept sheet is reference only. Nothing generated here ships in the mod, because the final sprite is drawn by hand, and the prompt tells the generator not to imitate any existing Terraria sprite, so the chosen design is our own.

How to use it: paste everything between the two lines below into ChatGPT on the web as one message. If the sheet comes back with fewer than 36 designs, repeated designs, blurred pixels or text in it, reply with the matching line from **Fixes to send back** at the bottom of this file rather than rewriting the prompt.

---

Create one single image: a **concept-art sprite sheet of 36 different designs for a small flying robot companion**, laid out as a **6 × 6 grid** (6 columns, 6 rows, 36 cells). This is for a 2D side-view sandbox game with a pixel-art look similar in spirit to Terraria, and every design must be **pixel art**. Each of the 36 cells is a completely different design idea for the same character, so I can compare them side by side and pick one to redraw by hand.

## What the character is

The character is a **companion droid**: a small flying machine, about the size of a large fist, that travels beside the player and plays the game alongside them. It is not a pet and not a monster. It is a capable little helper with its own opinions: it flies, fights with weapons the player gives it, drills ore and trees with a beam, lights up dark caves, picks up loot, and dodges danger. Think "a clever tool that decided to become a friend".

Things that are true about it and should shape every design:

- **It flies and never touches the ground.** No legs, no wheels, no feet. It hovers and drifts, so the design should look like it floats: a hover glow, small thrusters, fins, a ring, or a glow underneath.
- **Its body is basically round.** In the game its body collides as a circle, so the core silhouette of every design must fit inside a circle. Small parts such as fins, antennae, a ring or a light may stick out a little, but a design that reads as tall, long or humanoid is wrong.
- **It is a machine, and water and lava hurt it.** It should look engineered: plates, rivets, seams, a power core, vents, a lens. It can be cute, but it is made, not born.
- **It has a clear "front" it looks and aims with.** Every design needs one obvious eye, lens, visor or sensor so a viewer can tell which way it is facing and aiming. Draw every design **facing right**.
- **Things come out of it.** It fires the projectiles of the weapon it has been given, shoots a **mining beam** from itself to break ore and cut trees, and can hold up a **light** in dark places. Every design needs a visible spot these come from: an emitter, a nozzle, a lens port, an arm socket or a glowing core.
- **It will be animated later.** Designs with parts that can move on their own (a spinning ring, flapping fins, an opening shutter, a lens that rotates, a core that pulses) are good, because separate animations for idling, mining, shooting, getting hurt and being knocked out will be drawn for it.

## Pixel-art rules (apply to every cell)

- **True pixel art only.** Hard square pixels, no anti-aliasing, no blur, no soft gradients, no painterly brushwork, no photographic texture, no 3D render look, no smooth vector shapes.
- **Scale.** Draw each design as if on a **32 × 32 pixel canvas**, with the round body itself about **20 to 24 pixels across**, then show it enlarged with **nearest-neighbour scaling**, so every art pixel is a crisp, visible square block. Keep the pixel size identical in all 36 cells, so no design uses finer pixels than another.
- **Palette.** At most **12 to 16 colours per design**, with deliberate colour ramps (a dark, a mid, a light and a highlight per material), in the style of hand-made retro game sprites.
- **Outline.** A 1-pixel dark outline around each sprite (a very dark shade of the sprite's own colour, not pure black), so it reads clearly against busy cave and sky backgrounds.
- **Lighting.** One consistent light source from the **upper left** in every cell: highlights top-left, shadows bottom-right.
- **Readability at tiny size.** Each design must still be recognisable if shrunk back to 24 pixels on a noisy background. Favour a strong, simple silhouette and one or two bold accent colours over lots of fine detail.
- **Glow.** Glowing parts (eye, core, thrusters) are shown with bright pixel colours and at most a 1–2 pixel halo of darker glow pixels, never a soft blurred bloom.

## Sheet layout

- One square image, **6 columns × 6 rows**, all cells the same size, with a **thin even gap** between cells.
- Background: one **flat, plain, dark blue-grey colour** behind everything, identical in every cell. No scenery, no floor, no shadows on the ground, no frames, no decorative borders.
- **One design per cell**, centred, same size, same pose (idle hover, facing right, not attacking), with nothing else in the cell.
- **No text anywhere**: no labels, no numbers, no titles, no letters, no signatures, no watermarks. I will refer to designs by row and column.
- **All 36 must be genuinely different designs**, not recolours or small tweaks of each other. Vary the silhouette, the construction, the materials and the personality between cells.

## The six rows (each row is one design direction, six different ideas within it)

1. **Row 1: clean sci-fi orbs.** Smooth sleek spheres and near-spheres with a single visor or lens, panel lines, a hover ring or small thrusters. Minimal, modern and friendly. Vary them: a visor band, a single big lens, a split shell, a floating halo ring, twin side thrusters, a core showing through a gap.
2. **Row 2: forged from ore.** Droids built by a craftsman out of mined metals and gems: copper with green patina, dark iron with rivets, polished gold trim, silver plating, a gem set in as the eye, a glowing crystal core. Chunky, hand-made, workshop-built, a little rugged.
3. **Row 3: arcane machine.** Magic and engineering mixed together: floating runes carved into metal, a glowing magical core, orbiting stones or small plates, a crystal eye, a lantern-like body with an inner flame, a clockwork shell with glowing seams. Mysterious but still clearly a device.
4. **Row 4: nature and biome inspired.** The same small flying machine reimagined with the feel of different underground and surface environments: mossy jungle overgrowth, glowing mushroom caps, desert sandstone and brass, frozen ice-blue plating, dark underworld ember and obsidian, deep-ocean coral. Still a machine, and still round.
5. **Row 5: characterful and cute.** Designs with the most personality: expressive eye shapes, small antennae, a propeller hat, a mouth-like vent grille, a helmet-and-visor look, a cheeky asymmetric design, a tiny scarf-like fin. Charming without becoming a pet animal or a baby face.
6. **Row 6: wildcards with bold silhouettes.** Push the round-body rule as far as it goes while keeping a circular core: an armoured beetle-like shell that splits open, a ring-shaped droid with the eye in the middle, a spiky mine-like shell, a lantern cage, a stacked double-sphere, a shape with long swept-back fins. Surprising, memorable designs.

## Do not imitate existing game art

The designs must be **original**. Do not copy or closely resemble any existing Terraria sprite, especially flying orbs, eyes and robotic things from that game: not the Suspicious-Looking Eye pet or the Eye of Cthulhu, not the Shadow Orb or the Crimson Heart, not the Destroyer's Probe, the Martian Probe, the Deadly Sphere or any Martian machine, not the Companion Cube, not the Wisp or fairy pets, not the Magic Lantern. Also avoid instantly recognisable characters from other franchises (no Portal cores, no Star Wars droids, no Pokémon). A design inspired by a general idea such as "a lantern" or "a gem" is fine. A design that looks like one specific existing sprite is not.

## Quality check before you finish

Before producing the image, make sure that: there are exactly 36 cells in a 6 × 6 grid; each cell holds exactly one droid; every droid faces right, floats, has a visible eye or lens and has a visible emitter or core; the pixel size is the same everywhere and the pixels are crisp squares; there is no text at all; and no two designs are recolours of each other.

## Fixes to send back

Send one of these as a reply if the first image misses, instead of rewriting the prompt. Image models tend to drop cells, blur pixels and add text on large grids, so these target exactly those failures.

- Fewer or more than 36 designs: `Regenerate as exactly 6 columns and 6 rows, 36 equal cells, one droid in each, keeping the same six row themes.`
- Blurry, painterly or smooth: `Redo it as strict pixel art: hard square pixels on a 32×32 canvas per cell, enlarged with nearest-neighbour scaling, no anti-aliasing, no gradients, no bloom.`
- Pixel sizes differ between cells: `Every cell must use exactly the same pixel size; redraw the cells that use finer pixels.`
- Text or numbers appeared: `Remove every piece of text, number and label; nothing but the droids on the flat background.`
- Designs too alike: `Too many designs are variations of each other. Keep rows 1 to 6 as themes but make every cell a clearly different silhouette and construction.`
- Something looks like a Terraria sprite: `Row X column Y looks too much like an existing Terraria sprite; replace it with an original design in the same row theme.`
- A row is weak: `Keep everything else and regenerate only row X with six new designs in its theme.`

## After choosing one (for later, not for the first sheet)

Once a design is picked, a second prompt can ask for that one design only, as a reference sheet to redraw in Aseprite: the design at the same pixel scale shown facing right and facing left, plus single key poses for idle hover, drilling with its beam, firing, holding up a light, taking a hit and knocked out on the ground. Those poses are reference for hand-drawn animation, not frames to import, because generated frames do not keep a consistent pixel grid from pose to pose.
