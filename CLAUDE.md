# AICompanion — “Multi... Player?”

This singleplayer tModLoader mod makes an NPC companion that behaves as a second presence in Terraria: it keeps roughly with the player, fights, collects nearby drops, helps with work already underway, and lights dark places. It is deliberately neither a second player nor a pet. **Since the night of 14 September 2026 its body is a flying orb**, twenty pixels across, that plans over free space and is handed two weapons, a pickaxe and an axe by the player; the player-shaped walking body, its tile-graph route search and its authored weapon kit were retired that night after six negative captures whose every defect was a route the planner proved and the body then did not perform (the Slate Record carries the decision and what lost). The companion's *mechanisms* are a closed set rather than calls into real item use — it fires a handed weapon's projectile itself, swings a handed sword in its own arc, drills with a handed pickaxe's power — so each is reliable and written explicitly; the *items* those mechanisms read are open, any weapon or tool whose use is aiming and releasing.

The lanes that built that body landed on 15 September 2026 (`806a998` the body and its navigation, `c94c797` the gear, the notch and the mastery wheel on main between them), and every folder guide describes the orb. Its description at the level of the product is README's How it works, and at the level of the machinery read from source the section below, "The system in place"; at the depth of a walkthrough it is `Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Movement/CLAUDE.md` for the body and its navigation, `Companion/Inventory/CLAUDE.md` for the four slots and `Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Interactions/Firing/CLAUDE.md` for what the hands do with them.

**What the companion is supposed to do lives in `README.md`, and reading it is the first move on any behaviour work.** The README carries the target: a half-hour of play written as a story with no reference to any system, and the Behaviour By Behaviour table, one named responsibility per row with what is Expected of it. That heading's exact words and each row's opening `| **name**` are read by `Tools/SessionReport/Write/WriteBehaviourParity.cs`, and its self-test in `ChronicleTests.cs` fails `just test` when fewer than 30 rows parse or a mapped name disappears, so a rename of either is a code change too. The observed behaviour lives in `research/Historical Evidence/Observed Behaviour and Assessment Limits.md`, sourced only from named telemetry sessions and carrying each row's observed and system columns; the machinery read from source is "The system in place" below. The rules for maintaining the story and the table are "README's story and behaviour table are kept by these rules" below; the other two carry their own. This guide describes how the code is arranged; the README describes what it is for, and the two disagree only when one of them is stale.

**A play behaviour we want to see, said in a session, is not a note to remember later.** If it is not already in the README's target, it goes into `README.md` in the same sitting: a row in the Behaviour By Behaviour table first, then a scene in the story, in the words a player would use. Nothing in that write-up may name a score, a family, a search or a mode. Prefer thickening a scene that already holds the collision over adding a timestamp. A wish that stays in chat is how the companion we described fails to be the one we build.

## The companion is an opportunistic companion, not an orders system

Since `0bb2c8a` on 21 September 2026 the tick asks a *course* what to do: one frozen observation, discovery across all six domains — companionship, combat, mining, chopping, lighting and collection — then a bounded search over orders priced by the consequences each would actually have, whose next step the tick carries out. What that replaced was a per-tick competition between the six by multiplied score, and that chooser was deleted on 22 September 2026 once the world run's play-measures instrument began grading the course against the capture that motivated it, so there is no second brain standing behind this one. Nothing suspends ordinary activity to keep the body safe: avoiding a hit is a bend in whatever the body is already doing, so the job keeps the body while it dodges, and every liquid is air to the orb, so there is nothing to escape. Only downing and recovery flight take the body. Shooting is independent hands work, so it runs while the feet follow or dodge; the torch fills an otherwise free hand. Missions and player-directed sending were abandoned.

The unbuilt mastery progression is stats, weapon unlocks and movement unlocks, and **nothing on it changes what the brain decides**. That is a ruling rather than a description of what is built: a behaviour sitting behind an upgrade multiplies the numbers to tune and reads to a player as a weird gate, so an upgrade may change what a move costs or what the world does to the companion, never whether an activity is available to choose. Where a mastery node and the brain do interact, the interaction is expected to emerge through the senses rather than be scripted — a torch hat brightens the companion's surroundings, so the light sense reports a lit neighbourhood and the torch decision sees no reason to hold one, with nothing in the torch code aware that a hat exists. A selectable interface preview is not a gameplay ability.

## The architecture has one movement boundary

```
Terraria world ─► Brain/Infrastructure/Observation
                     │   player · terrain · threats · light · reach · intent
                     │
                     ├─► SharedBehaviours/Safety ─► Infrastructure/Movement ─► motor ─► NPC
                     └─► Activities ─► Infrastructure/Selection ─► Infrastructure/Position ─┘
                                  │
                                  └─► Infrastructure/Interactions (incl. Firing) and Infrastructure/WeaponKnowledge
```

**A fact the whole brain needs is a sense, not a private answer.** Light, reach and the player's intent region are the three that were extracted, and they are the pattern for the next one. The region is the newest: "how far from the player" used to be answered in five places with five radii (the reunion pull, the work radius, the meeting place's anchor, the lighting search's centre, the collect radius), and it is now one box carried ahead of the player's feet by his own observed pace that every one of them measures to. Reachability used to be the positioner's own: everyone else either asked the positioner for the one shape it happened to expose or ran a route search of their own, so five callers each paid a fresh bounded search for a question one flood had already answered. Both now sit in Observation beside the threat sense, and the positioner is a consumer like everybody else — it keeps its whole public surface as one-line delegations, deliberately, because renaming it would have been a sweep through files for no behaviour.

Both senses answer in three values rather than two, and the middle one is the reason they are worth having. A tile missing from a flood that has not finished is *not yet known*, not *absent*; a place the engine has not lit is *unread*, not *bright*. An activity that refuses the middle answer is declining to start on an unanswered search rather than asserting the world is empty, which is a distinction each caller used to have to reconstruct and now cannot get wrong. The two senses keep different clocks and this bites: the reach flood grows every brain tick, bounded per slice, but where it is rooted and when a replacement flood takes over is decided on the positioner's rescore, because the region a candidate is scored against has to hold still for a rescore; ageing that decision on the tick multiplies the cadences and refloods the region every few seconds.

`Companion/Brain/CoordinateBrainTick.cs` owns the tick order. `Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Movement/CoordinateMovement.cs` is the single movement request interface; its query surface is how other brain systems ask movement questions. The motor is the only component permitted to write the live NPC, and it is also where the body's contact with terrain runs: the engine's box collision is switched off for the orb, so the mod's circle-against-tiles test is the one body, compiled into the game-free core and run identically by the mod, by NavReplay, by EngineReplay and by the world run over Terraria's own tiles. There is no second body to keep in parity with, which is why the walker's parity recorder and its collision matrix are gone rather than rewritten.

This section describes what is actually built, read from the source. It says what each part is for, how the parts hand work to each other, and where the recordings show a gap between intent and result. It describes the machinery; it does not defend it.

### Maintaining this section

**Update it in the commit that changes the code, never afterwards.** A description of machinery that has moved on is worse than no description, because it is believed. If a commit changes what a part does, changes what owns a decision, or removes something named here, this section changes in the same commit.

**Write the shape of a rule and never the value that fills it in.** "The running behaviour keeps a bonus so a near-tie does not oscillate" survives every tuning session; "the running behaviour is multiplied by 1.15" is falsified the first time anyone touches the constant, and nobody deletes prose when they edit a number. The same goes for budgets, radii, thresholds and timers: name what the number is for and where it lives, not what it currently is. A number stays only when it is a fact about the game itself, a structural count that could only change by redesign, or a dated measurement that says it is one.

**Read the source, not the folder notes.** The per-folder CLAUDE.md files are maintained by hand and can lag. Anything asserted here about how a system behaves is checked against the code that runs, and a claim that came from a comment is either verified or written as a claim the comment makes.

**Say what a part deliberately does not do.** Half the value of this section is stopping the next reader proposing something that was already considered and rejected — so a rejected approach is recorded as the property that failed rather than the patch that expressed it, because a design that comes back wearing new mechanics will otherwise pass unnoticed.

### The whole system on one page

The companion runs one short loop sixty times a second.

1. **It looks.** The game hands the brain what it can observe: where you are and how you have been moving, which enemies and projectiles are near and whom they can reach, what drops are lying around and whether the bag has room, how bright the open air is wherever the game has actually computed light — less every light somebody is carrying, so a passage beside you holding a torch does not read as a lit passage — and the companion's own health, mana and surroundings.
2. **It checks whether it is about to be hit.** Nothing in the world takes the body away from its job: no liquid can hurt it, and being about to be hit takes nothing either — it is noted here, and step 5 bends whatever the body was asked to do out of the way.
3. **Otherwise it decides what is worth doing, as a plan rather than as a pick.** It freezes one account of the world, looks across every kind of work it could do — ore, trees, drops lying about, pots, dark air, and the shots a fight would offer — and searches short ordered sequences of concrete jobs. A pot is seen but never planned for: since 22 September 2026 a job no behaviour performs is dropped before the search can put it in a sequence, and breaking pots is something the companion does in passing rather than a trip it makes. A sequence is priced by what it would actually cause: the work it would get done, the harm it would let reach you and itself along the way, and how far and how long it would be away from you. One sequence is published, and while the piece of work it is about to perform still holds, the companion does not decide again: it finishes what it started and chooses afresh at the end. Staying with you is the empty sequence, which is a legal and sometimes winning answer priced with its own consequences rather than treated as free idling, so keeping you company is still something it chose and not what is left over.
4. **The bound step says what kind of place the body goes to** — next to you, beside an ore it can reach, somewhere it can shoot from — and position selection turns that into a point in free space to hover at. The behaviour that performs the work does not pick the site; the course already did, because letting the behaviour choose again would put a second chooser back after the decision. Where the body can fly to is one flood over the free space around it that the positioner, the tool work and the lighting all read, so a place nobody has worked out yet is unknown to all of them rather than absent from one of them.
5. **Movement gets it there** with the same body rules for every purpose: a route through free space wide enough for the body, steering that carries momentum along it and eases in, a slow hover around the place once it is there, a bend away from any hit the flight is about to take, and one motor that is the only thing allowed to move the NPC and the only thing that keeps it out of walls.
6. **The hands fire only while it is fighting.** Combat is one activity that chooses stand, weapons, targets and aims together; the two weapons you handed it fire that plan, and only while combat is the running job. Mining, lighting, looting and keeping you company leave the weapons quiet. The pickaxe and axe you handed it keep the hand for the whole swing cycle. A torch fills an otherwise empty hand in the dark.
7. **It watches what actually happened** — whether the body moved, whether a block took damage, whether an item reached the bag — and the next loop starts from that, not from what it hoped would happen.

```text
   world ──► observe (frozen for the life of one decision, not one tick)
                 │
                 ▼
     discover opportunities in 6 domains
     (ore · trees · drops · pots · dark air · shots)
     a pot is discovered and then dropped: no behaviour performs one
                 ▼
     search short ordered courses, priced by projected consequences
     (work delivered · harm to you and to it · time apart)
                 ▼
     publish one course ──► keep it while its next step still holds
                 ▼
     the bound step ──► its behaviour + a kind of place ──► choose a spot
                 ▼
     plan and steer movement ──► one control grant (body + hand) ──► motor ──► NPC
                                           │
                      hands: tools, combat's plan, held torch
                                           │
                     observed results feed the next loop ◄───────────┘
```

### One tick, in the order the code runs it

`Companion/Brain/CoordinateBrainTick.cs` owns this order. Every path, including the early ones, ends in the same finaliser.

1. **Observation.** `WorldObservation.Senses` refreshes the player, threats, whether a boss fight or world event is under way, projectiles, loot, light and the companion's own state, in that order. Light is refreshed here and is a field rather than a number: brightness at every open-air tile on a lattice through a window around the companion, and it is rebuilt early when the engine starts, stops or resizes the area it computes, because answering from a reading of a differently-sized frame is answering about a world nobody measured. Home protection and tool cooldowns update. Not every query recomputes every tick: route floods and opportunity searches keep bounded work across ticks and refresh on their own cadences, and the reach flood is refreshed by step 8 rather than here.
2. **Danger timing.** Combat's committed plan estimates how soon the companion could intervene against the most urgent threat to you, and observation turns that into protection urgency: how late help would arrive compared with the threat.
3. **Time apart.** Companionship evidence — how long the two have been out of comfortable range — is recorded before any branch can skip the rest of the tick, so an interruption never resets it.
4. **Distant recovery**, if already under way, keeps flying the companion back through terrain and ends the tick there.
5. **Reflexes.** Reflexes predict imminent collisions with enemy bodies and projectiles and hand the prediction to movement; they take nothing, and nothing else takes the body at this step.
6. **Choosing.** Every behaviour prepares first, because preparing is how a behaviour works out what it would do rather than a part of choosing, and combat has to prepare before the decision rather than after it: its opportunities are a tactical search rather than a world scan, and a shot nobody looked for cannot be weighed against the vein beside it. Then the course owner decides. A published course whose next step still validates against live facts is carried with no search at all; otherwise it freezes an observation, discovers across the six domains, searches bounded orders and publishes one. A decision may span several ticks — a single travel query can cost more than a tick's leftover allowance — and a tick inside one keeps the fight the body is already committed to, and asks the body to keep you company only when there is none, flagged as not yet settled, so that companionship-while-still-deciding cannot be mistaken for companionship-as-the-answer and start recovery flight. That continuation is the committed plan and never an offered one: a fight it is already in survives the thinking, and no fight starts inside it, because breaking off mid-decision is what released the plan, emptied the course and started the next decision in the 22 September play. Discovery serves nothing it can no longer support — every opportunity carries the one fact its admission rested on, and an opportunity whose fact has left the world is retired before anything is priced, so a drop somebody picked up or a body somebody killed stops sitting in the store as usable work for ever — and any opportunity whose kind of work no behaviour performs is dropped before an order can contain it. The named behaviour goes to the activity owner, which keeps its identity and attempt record.
7. **Acting.** The bound step supplies the position request. The behaviour still runs its own tick, for the hand it reserves and the state it keeps, and the request it returns is discarded, because the course has already said where the body goes. A reunion request from far enough away, with no tool in hand and the decision settled, may start distant recovery instead.
8. **Placing and moving.** Position selection resolves the request to a hover point; movement plans a route and steers along it, or hovers where it is. The resulting controls are then run forward against the reflexes' prediction, and bent away only if they would meet one, the activity keeping its attempt and its hands. Resolving is also what advances the reach flood, which is bounded per advance and grows across successive resolves rather than on the tick, so consumers asking between resolves read the flood the last resolve left. When nothing inside a follow objective can be accepted, the request aims at its own anchor until the objective's own arrival test is met, and no candidate is invented to stand in for one.
9. **Finalising.** One control grant applies the movement through the motor and decides what the hand may do; if combat is current and the hand is free, the hands fire the plan's due use (or the best from here at the plan's targets while travelling); progress and outcomes are observed and credited to the executing attempt; and the HUD snapshot is published.

Being downed bypasses steps 2–8: the NPC lifecycle holds the body and revokes the hand through the same finaliser.

### Three families, six behaviours

**The families no longer decide anything, and the table below is the list of what performs the work rather than the shape of the choice.** Since the tick switch the decision looks across six *domains* of opportunity — ore, trees, drops, pots, dark air, and the shots a fight offers — and those are not the six behaviours: pots are a domain of their own rather than a part of collecting, and keeping you company is not a domain at all, because it is what an empty course means. Pots are the domain with no performer: the list of work a course may bind is the list of behaviours that can carry work out — fighting, mining, chopping, collecting and lighting — and since 22 September 2026 a discovered pot is dropped from the search at the point orders are built rather than being ordered and then flown to with nothing to do on arrival, which is what the over-wide light census had let happen. A course names a piece of work in one of those domains, and the behaviour beside it in the table is what carries that work out. The family grouping survives in the health notch's icons and in the list of behaviour instances; it is no longer a stage of the decision.

A **family** is a purpose. A **behaviour** is one concrete way of serving it, with a real target. A family never ran on its own; under the chooser it only ever put forward the best behaviour it had.

#### How the offers are compared

**Everything in this subsection describes the family chooser, which was deleted on 22 September 2026 and is kept here as the account of what the course replaced.** The course compares whole ordered futures rather than single offers, and prices them by consequence rather than by multiplying factors onto a raw value: what each piece of work would deliver measured against its own census, the harm predicted to reach you and the companion along the way, and the gap in companionship the trip would open, integrated over the journey rather than charged at its destination. A threat the course's own kills remove is *truncated* out of that harm forecast rather than paid as a bonus, which is the difference between being worth exactly the harm it prevents and being worth something for killing a thing that was never going to touch anybody. Two of the chooser's rules were carried across rather than reinvented: your protection urgency is still the one route by which your danger reaches optional work, and a boss or event still charges optional non-combat work once, at the greater of urgency and the encounter's own intensity rather than at the two multiplied. The harm forecast prices a threat that keeps hitting, since 21 September 2026: a hit costs its damage and makes its target immune for the game's own window, the scan resumes after that window against the same trajectories, and an actor whose accumulated hits exhaust its life stops, so a zombie walking into you is worth exactly the hits intervening prevents rather than one hit whatever the companion does. What an encounter does to optional work is a rule between fights rather than a discount: an encounter suppresses a whole optional course while leaving every feasible fight worth what it was, and among feasible fights the one the companion survives wins, only while the encounter is on. What follows is kept as history rather than because the chooser is: it was deleted on 22 September 2026, and this account of what the course replaced is the only place the shape of it survives.

Each behaviour **prepares** first: it searches for its opportunity, checks policy and reach, and captures a value, a target and how long the job would take. Comparison only reads what preparation captured, so comparing cannot quietly retarget or advance a job. Preparation is scheduled within a per-family time allowance; a behaviour that did not get to prepare this tick is recorded as deferred, not as having nothing to do.

Every offer also says whether it is **usable**, **unresolved** (worth approaching while its feasibility is still being worked out), **known unusable**, **forbidden by a setting** or simply **absent**. A positive value is never allowed to win beside an absent, forbidden or unusable offer.

The shared evaluator then multiplies each raw value by the same factors, so any zero vetoes an offer:

- **protection** — for optional excursions only, how much your need for help discounts going elsewhere;
- **commitment** — the running behaviour keeps a small bonus so near-ties do not flip back and forth;
- **horizon** — work that would outlast the time before a threat arrives is charged for the overrun;
- **reunion** — for excursions, the cost of the delay the job adds to getting back to you, which grows when you are leaving and when the way back is long.

Keeping company is not an excursion, so none of the excursion charges apply to it; it is modestly discounted instead while useful work is available within the activity allowance, so work can win without the companion wandering off. A fight from here, or whose remaining flight is short, is treated the same way: nearby fighting is ordinary work rather than leaving.

Each family nominates its highest final value. The strongest nomination wins. Before it starts, its captured target is checked again (gone, replaced or re-typed targets are refused), and combat must get its plan's stand admitted by position selection. A refused winner keeps its raw value on the record with a named reason, and the next prepared offer is considered without searching again.

### What each behaviour does

These describe how the work is performed once a course has named it. The site, the target and the order are the course's choice now, so where a paragraph below describes a behaviour searching for its own opportunity, that search is what its domain publishes for the course to weigh rather than a decision the behaviour makes for itself.

**Mining** keeps one vein at a time. It only offers ore the pickaxe in its slot can damage, and it reaches ore wherever free space touches it, ceiling ore included, because a body that hovers has no standing pose to prefer: one query ranks every free cell within reach of the tile and asks the reach sense about each, nearest first. Before every swing it checks, from where the body actually is, that the ore is in the player-derived reach and has an exposed face. Its value includes the approach and the tool time to the next block breaking, from native damage. It never digs ordinary terrain to make a way in. A flight toward ore whose approach is still unknown is given up if the body is not covering ground, and that same tile is not offered again until the terrain changes. A vein cleared by the player is not counted as its work, and a cleared tracked portion of a vein that continues is partial, not complete.

**Chopping** works the same way for trees: nearest admitted trunk it can reach, the player's own tree only when nothing else is left, and a trunk the player takes is released by name. Home protection and the chopping setting are checked again at the swing.

**Combat** is the one fighting job, and the only job whose hands fire. It searches for an attack plan — a short timeline of stands, the weapons and aims to use from each, the bodies those uses are for — and commits the one the sense-driven weights pick from the undominated front. Stand, weapon, target and aim are one choice, because a piercing shot through three things can beat a harder hit on one, a spread that only lands up close is worth flying in for, and a weapon that goes through walls does not need the body to move at all. Protection is a term on that plan (expected hits on you that removed bodies will no longer land), not a second activity; a fight too long to win is worth less of it, so a boss's life bar does not outrank the small enemies on you. A threat it has proven it cannot shoot from anywhere it can reach is worth no combat at all, so it goes about other things and leaves avoiding that threat to the bend every movement gets; while it has not yet worked out whether such a place exists, the offer is unresolved at value zero rather than a refusal. It does not fly toward something no weapon could hit from anywhere it can reach. A stationary fight that produces no planned use and no planned hit is deferred for a while, every body the plan targeted, and changing target is not progress. What the body is flying toward, what the hands are aiming at and what a shot actually struck are recorded as three separate things. It crosses water and lava to reach an enemy as readily as air, because no liquid is a wall to the body. Mining, lighting, looting and keeping you company leave the weapons quiet.

**Lighting** works a dark region rather than visiting a site, which is the difference between lighting a cave and trickling one torch into it per search. The light field nominates the regions of dark air nearest the companion's own body — not the player's, and not a disc around it — and a region whose middle lies outside the work radius around the player is somebody else's darkness and is passed over. Every remaining region contributes its tiles, because offering only the nearest would let one sealed pocket hide every reachable region behind it, and dark air under a floor is nearer than a shelf across the room. Nominating a region and sweeping the world for sites are two stages with two centres, and only the first is about the body: the sweep covers a window of the work radius around the intent region's heading, and what reaches a decision out of it is bounded rather than everything it found. Since 22 September 2026 the census publishes a capped number of sites, usable ones ahead of unusable, then nearest that heading, then darkest, with any site a course is currently working kept whatever its rank so a bound site cannot be cut and then retired as though the world had stopped supporting it. The count it withheld is published beside them, so a site the decision never saw reads as not yet ranked rather than as no dark tile — a descent through a cave had been re-sweeping a window's worth of darkness into every decision, a thousand-odd sites where a couple of hundred is all any search could ever order, and it was the cost of that, not a leak, that made the frozen observation grow through the 22 September play. The executor takes the first site whose access it can prove, scanning outward from the body, and the game's own Smart Cursor still owns attachment and spacing.

Whether a site wants a torch is its whole neighbourhood read from the engine at its own finer stride, never off the field's lattice, whose stride is wider than the lit patches this has to resolve; a neighbourhood nobody has read is refused alongside a lit one, because a torch spent on an unmeasured place is spent on a guess. Light anybody is carrying is not in that reading at all, which is what a permanent torch is for: the place has to stay lit after the carried one walks away, so a pocket you walk into holding a torch, or mine open while holding one, is as much a torch site as a pocket nobody has lit.

The reach sense answers first, and a route search runs only for the tile already chosen. A site the companion must fly to needs a hover beside it that the flood holds; a site in a ceiling, across a chasm or under water is the same query as any other, because nothing about height is special to a body that hovers.

The search asks the reach sense, never a route search of its own: each candidate site is one lookup in the flood the sense already ran, nearest first, and a route is planned only for the site it chooses, through the same movement request every other destination uses. That is what removed the starvation the 14 September 09:28 capture showed, where a fixed count of per-site route searches answered Unknown, remembered nothing, and re-asked the same nearest sites every rescore; the count and its tunable are gone. What bounds the scan now is the tick's planning deadline, and a scan the deadline cuts before it finds a site reports itself unresolved and resumes at the next rescore rather than waiting the full cadence or reporting the deadline as a fact about the world. A refusal proven by a complete flood is remembered so the next scan advances past it; a not-yet-known answer from a flood still growing is neither charged nor remembered. The record carries which sites each scan asked and what the sense answered, so the next capture can say what was considered. The boundary script fails any activity or interaction that calls a route query directly, so the rule holds for jobs not yet written.

Its value is a base plus a term for how much dark the region holds, under a ceiling that keeps the biggest cavern from outbidding protecting you, raised by a factor when you are carrying no light of your own. Enablement is the torch setting plus a torch in supply. The job survives a placement, so a region needing several torches is worked through from where the companion then stands.

**Collecting** compares a known drop against a pot's possible contents. A known drop has to lie somewhere the flood holds, is valued by the amount that will actually fit, is proved again if it moves, and its attempt is concluded from what the bag actually received. A drop still falling is offered where it will come to rest rather than where it is, forecast with the game's own item motion, including the slower fall and the lagged velocity the game applies inside water, honey and shimmer; a drop whose fall outlasts the forecast's horizon is undecided rather than guessed at. Contact pickup still happens during any behaviour. The pot half of that comparison reaches no trip: a pot is discovered and priced like any other opportunity and then refused before an order can hold it, because nothing performs one, so the only pot the companion breaks is one already within reach of where it is.

**Keeping company** chooses, inside one activity, between rejoining you and relaxing nearby. Rejoining is priced against your intent region, the box the player-intent sense carries ahead of your feet: the pull is continuous from the region's own centre outward, never exactly zero while you are travelling, and it joins the far slope at the region's edge without a step, so a moving player is itself a reason to move and the body no longer coasts to whichever edge it entered by. That far pull never sits at 1, so a proven job further across the screen can still win; lost sight and return pressure raise it; the hard leash at fly-home distance is not that cap, and that one still drops everything. Being with you is not geometry alone: arrival needs the body at rest inside the region for a whole rescore, because two bodies passing each other are momentarily a few pixels apart and neither has arrived anywhere. Changing method is a change of request kind, and a change of request kind cancels whatever the body is doing, so inside the region the other method has to be wanted for a rescore and asked for from rest; outside it, or hovering in tiles you are asking for, the change is immediate. Relaxing nearby is hovering: the body drifts slowly around the place it settled, a little up, down and to either side, and never hangs perfectly still. Moving about your region on top of that draws only legs the body can actually sweep to, and none below the top of your head: a leg aimed at the far edge of the region with rock in the way was re-aimed at the clearest turn every tick, which beside a vertical face runs up or down, so the body toured the same overhang for hundreds of ticks in the 22 September play instead of coming back to you; where nothing at the far edge can be reached the walk is drawn from anywhere in the box instead. There used to be a stroll to a random safe cell with a rest between strolls, and it went on 15 September 2026, because a stop between every flight was the stop-start motion the first orb play showed; a hold now also keeps the reach flood growing, which the stroll had only ever done by accident. It gives way when you need the space: aim a block at the tiles it is hovering in, or walk into a one-tile-tall passage it is hovering in, and it moves back to your side, while aiming a weapon or torch at it changes nothing. It never claims a finished job: its attempts are recorded as executed.

### Shared systems

These are not behaviours and never compete in the comparison. Every behaviour runs through them, and each one's mechanism, at the depth a change needs, lives in the CLAUDE.md of the folder that owns it.

- **Observation.** One account of the world per tick, so no two behaviours derive the same fact differently.
- **Light, as a field.** Brightness at open-air tiles across a window around the companion — packed dirt, platforms and furniture are not darkness — taken only where the game's lighting engine has actually computed light, asked of the engine's own bounds rather than approximated by the screen rectangle. A tile the engine has not computed is unknown and never dark, because spending a torch on a zero nobody measured is spending one on a guess. The light it reads is the world's own — placed torches, lamps, lava, glowing tiles and the sky — and nothing anybody is carrying is in it, because the engine computes the two separately and the field is taken between them: the lighting engine scans the tiles into one map, and only its next step merges in every light a body carries before blurring and showing the result. So a held torch, a light pet, a glowstick, a mining helmet and the companion's own torch are all absent from the reading alike, none of them enumerated anywhere in the brain, and the ground beside you while you hold a torch reads as dark as the cave it is in. That is what lets the companion light a mine as it follows you through it. Legacy lighting keeps no such separate map, so under it a carried light reads as room light.
- **Reach, as a sense.** Where the body can fly to, flooded over the free space around it from where it is and read by position selection, tool access and lighting alike rather than owned privately by whichever of them asked first. Free space is the set of tile corners the body's circle fits at, each joined to its neighbours; every liquid is as open as air to the flood. There is one flood and not two, because every edge of that graph runs both ways and a place the body can fly to is a place it can fly back from, so the walker's returnable region and its round-trip proofs have no subject here. At spawn the region is empty rather than stale, and what starts the first flood is the first query that asks the sense. A terrain edit empties it again only where the flood actually looked: every retained search records the box it explored, an announced edit is tested against that box, and an edit the search never read leaves it valid. The answer is reachable, not yet known, or proven absent, and only an absence resting on a flood that ran out of region before its budget is a refusal worth remembering; an unfinished flood is unknown, so an activity that declines it is declining to start on an unanswered search rather than asserting the place does not exist. The flood is a disc rather than a world: bounded at twice a known radius of straight-line distance around the body, so in an open world it finishes instead of running to the search's node limit and finishing unexhausted, which is what the first orb tree did and which left nothing provably unreachable for the life of a session. Inside the known radius a tile the finished flood never claimed is proven absent, meaning every route to it is a detour of about three times its straight line; beyond it the answer is not yet, never absent, because the flood was not asked to go there. Once the body has travelled half that radius from the root a replacement flood grows there while the old one keeps answering, so ordinary travel never empties the region. Underground the ball is mostly rock and closes in a few resolves; in open sky it is the whole disc and takes a few hundred ticks.
- **Player intent.** Your recent movement is kept as a short, bounded history. Steady travel in one direction builds confidence that you are going somewhere; crossing the same small area, stopping, or using tools lowers it; a brief turn weakens the old direction and sustained backtracking replaces it. It estimates a journey, never a destination. That journey becomes a place: the intent region is a box carried ahead of your feet by your own observed pace, low-passed so a halt does not snap it back onto you, grown a little while you travel, and clamped to your own half-screen so the companion is somewhere you can see. It has a gradient, zero at its centre and rising through its edge with no step, measured on both axes rather than as a radius because a tile on the next floor down is not being with you. Every "how far from the player" question in the brain measures to it — the reunion pull, the work radius around you for ore and trees, the lighting search centre, the meeting place's fallback anchor, the reach the chooser allows an excursion — so work near you is work near where you are going rather than behind you.
- **Danger to each actor.** Danger to you and danger to the companion are measured separately (see below).
- **Companionship cost.** Time apart accumulates from observation, and the reunion charge combines how fast you are leaving with how long the companion's route back would take.
- **Hit prediction.** Nothing takes the body away from its job. Avoiding a hit is not a response of its own: movement bends whatever the body is doing away from a predicted hit and leaves the job its attempt and its hands, which replaced a collision reflex and a combat-spacing search that each suspended the job — the second of them is what parked the orb beside a zombie in the first play.
- **Position selection.** Turns a kind of place into a hover point, scoring cheap factors first and paying for trajectory checks only on a shortlist. The candidates are the free corners near the request's anchor, each the same corner the search uses for its nodes, so every candidate is somewhere the body fits; there is no height ceiling on them, and what keeps the companion beside you rather than over your head is the intent region's own vertical half-size for following and, among equally clear corners, the park's preference for the band a few tiles above your head. That preference is measured about you and not about the body — your heading across, the band over your head down — because a park broken by nearness to the body is wherever the body already is, so a low arrival never climbed and the place moved every time the body did, which abandoned almost every journey home in the 22 September play. Reachability is a tier rather than a factor: while any candidate sits inside the flooded region only those are scored, because a spot the body cannot reach is not a worse spot but no spot. When a follow request accepts nothing, it answers nothing, and movement aims at the request's own anchor until the request's own arrival test is met; the walker's substitute, the reachable tile that most closed the gap, was a destination that could be arrived at without achieving anything, and it is gone. A destination already held is kept, without a search, for as long as it still belongs to the region it was admitted against: a follow spot inside the intent region and not in your way, a firing stand whose arc still solves against a target that has not moved past a hold slack, a partial-progress tile the body has not reached that still closes the gap; retention is a rule about membership, not a bonus a rival has to outscore. A firing stand is proved against the target's forecast at the body's estimated arrival and then across a window as long as the trip itself, capped so a flight across the world is not asked for an arc that holds indefinitely, because the shot was being proved against a slime that would not be there, and a stand whose shot closes before the trip ends was never a reason to go. Every stand a real solve refused is remembered against this terrain and this target generation standing about here, and which refusals survive the body moving depends on what each was about: a stand with no arc is a fact about the line to the target and holds from anywhere, while a stand refused because the shot would close before the body could fly there is a fact about that trip and holds only from where the trip started. Whether the sweep has finished is a separate question keyed on the stands and the target alone, never on the body, because keying it on the body was what stopped a moving companion's search from ever finishing. When the memory fills, the target that has sat in it longest loses its entries rather than everyone losing theirs, so one target's refusals arriving cannot restart every other sweep in progress. A shortlist therefore advances past refusals instead of restarting from the top; a search that ran out of solves or time before it found a stand says so rather than reporting that nothing exists.
- **Movement.** One request surface, one game-free core, one motor. The body is a twenty-pixel circle that flies: no gravity, no ground, no jump, no breath. The engine's own tile collision is switched off for it, and the mod's own contact keeps it out of walls — the circle against the tiles, pushed out along the nearest face, its speed into the wall killed and its slide along the wall kept — run in the motor after the tick's velocity is chosen, and run identically by every headless tool from the same source, so there is one body and no second one to keep in agreement with it. Slopes are full tiles to it, platforms are passable, a closed door is solid until the door interaction opens it, and every liquid is air: the body is not hurt or slowed in water, honey, lava or shimmer, and no search treats one as a wall. A destination is planned as a route over the free-space graph, corner to corner, priced by length and by how tight the corridor is so a route prefers the middle of a passage, then smoothed by line of sight without losing the clearance the search paid for; the body follows it with steering that carries momentum, capped in speed, turning under one cap and gathering or shedding speed under a slower one, and easing into its arrival, where it does not stop but hovers around the place it arrived at. Any route with clearance is followable by construction, so there is no proof step between planning and moving and no class of route the planner proved and the body could not perform, which was the walker's whole failure. A search too large for one tick keeps its frontier, and while it is unfinished the body follows what it had, flies straight at the goal where the line is clear, and otherwise hovers where it is; a search that exhausts the free space is a proven absence, reported apart from unfinished. A retained search is thrown away by a terrain edit only where it looked, because the game announces every edit, including a door anybody opens or closes, and a search compares them against the box it explored. A spot the body keeps failing to reach is set aside for a while after two failed tries. Whoever ends a route says so, and the attempt is scored as completed, cancelled, pre-empted or failed rather than folded together. A dodge is neither a route nor a request: after the tick's controls are chosen they are run forward through the same contact against the predicted collision, and only if they would meet one are they bent to the heading that stays clear and keeps doing the job, a stop winning only when it is clearly safer than moving. Nothing plans as if the body stood, and no performer derives a move of its own.
- **Tool access and world permission.** One reach query for mining, chopping, pots and torches, answered from the reach sense and never from a search of its own: the body's own position first, then every free cell within reach of the tile, nearest first. The set of world edits is closed — ore, trees, pots, torches, doors — and bed-anchored homes veto autonomous edits. Tiles are struck through the game's own pickaxe and axe routines with the pickaxe and axe in the companion's own slots, via a temporary player stand-in — an empty slot is no tool, so the work is offered as unusable rather than done with a tool never handed over — and a torch is placed through the game's Smart Cursor choice. A tile is a torch site only when the world's own light there is known to be below the dark level. Under a carried light, which the engine merges in by taking the brighter value, the world light can't be read, so the tile counts as dark only if the light sense saw it dark before that light arrived, and that memory is forgotten wherever a terrain edit lands nearby. A tile the sky lights by the engine's surface rule is never a site. The companion's torches are free and drop nothing when broken. ### A route, a usable position and a productive effect are three different facts

- **A route** says the body can get from here to a tile. Finding one is not arriving, and arriving is not being useful.
- **A usable position** is a pose from which the behaviour's purpose is possible: the ore is inside real tool reach with an exposed face, the firing position has a solved trajectory, you are within comfortable range and in sight. Each behaviour judges this against the body's actual pose, not the waypoint it was sent to.
- **A productive effect** is what the game observed: the block's damage went up or it disappeared, the torch was placed, the item reached the bag, the enemy took damage. Swinging, arriving and being labelled "mining" prove none of these.

The recording keeps all three apart, so a reader can tell a companion that could not find a way from one that arrived and could not reach, and from one that reached and changed nothing.

### Moving with your journey, even by a different route

When you are travelling, keeping company does not chase a point projected from your velocity. It takes places along your apparent journey and prices each one by the companion's own routes: arriving before you costs the wait, arriving after you have passed costs the chase, and far guesses cost more because the evidence for them is weaker. So a route that leads on to your path ahead is taken forward to meet you there, while one that ends at a wall of rock is abandoned for the way round behind. The price is decided only once the companion's route flood has finished, because unfinished route times are upper bounds; until then it aims a short way ahead of you rather than at where you were. A place stays chosen until another is clearly cheaper, and is dropped when you stop, so the companion never keeps flying to a meeting point you have already left. It does not know where you are going, only the journey your recent movement describes. Until a place is priced, reunion aims at the leading edge of your intent region, the point on the region's boundary in your direction of travel, and a still player has no leading edge, so it aims at you. A priced place is a preference among the tiles the region accepts and never a second acceptance box: it used to widen the region by a growth of its own, so a far-off meeting place enlarged the very arrival test it was aiming inside and the body could satisfy following by standing anywhere along the line to a place it had not reached.

### Self-preservation is not player protection

The brain measures two dangers and never lets one stand in for the other.

- **Danger to the companion** comes from threats that can reach it, from fire and bleeding, and from recent damage, weighed against its remaining life. A threat's weight is the hit it would actually land after the game's own defence arithmetic, set against the life the body has left, so armour lowers it, wounds raise it, and the same enemy can weigh differently on you and on the companion. It makes combat back off. A walking enemy counts as able to reach the hovering orb only when the orb is inside the walker's own height plus its highest jump, so a zombie under an orb high above it is no danger to the orb.
- **Danger to you** comes from threats that can reach you, and becomes **protection urgency** once it is compared with how soon the companion could actually intervene. It drives guarding, and it is the one place your danger reaches optional work — once, through the protection factor.
- **A boss fight or a world event** is a third observation, read from the game's own facts: a boss on screen, the Old One's Army, a blood moon, eclipse, pumpkin or frost moon while you are on the surface, an invasion while its invaders are still arriving and you are near the surface within its reach, or a lunar pillar's zone, which are the places the game runs them. An event nothing names is inferred the way the game itself decides the world is full: enemies that can reach either of you are weighed by the spawn slots the game counts them as (a worm is its head, not its eight segments), and only a weight held above the highest spawn cap the game has applied since the oldest of those enemies arrived counts, because ordinary spawning can never push a crowd past that. The highest cap, not today's, is the comparison because your own choices lower the cap under a crowd that has not moved: a calming potion or a peace candle thins future spawns and is not an invasion. While it lasts, optional non-combat work stops wherever the event reaches you, charged beside your danger rather than on top of it, and ordinary work returns the moment it ends.

A threat to you alone never makes the companion retreat; a threat to the companion alone never makes it guard you. Distance is not a stand-in for either: an enemy sealed in a pit may be harmless to both, and one far away may still arrive first.

### The health notch

The notch at the top of the screen is three stacked bars, health, mana and experience, and nothing else: no name, no level, no readings and no icons, by the ruling of 15 September 2026 that information shown twice, or not needed at a glance, is clutter (`Companion/HeadsUpDisplay/CompanionHealthBar.cs`). The family and behaviour icons this paragraph once described were cancelled by that ruling. Docked, it hangs from the top edge like a laptop notch; dragged anywhere else it becomes a free rounded card, and right-clicking docks it again. A click opens the profile card, where the numbers live.

### What the player can change

Per character: mining and chopping each Off, Mimic or Auto, and torch placement, on the card; voluntary hunting and pot breaking are saved per character but no longer on the card. How far it goes is not a choice: new work within 100 tiles of you, and the flight home past 125. Inventory and the four gear slots live in the profile card: two weapons, a pickaxe and an axe, each taking any real item its mechanism can use and saying why it refuses one it cannot; there is no armour, accessory or ammo slot, and ammo is free. The profile card closes on the inventory key, Escape unless you rebound it, and that press does nothing else; the same press closes the brain inspector's menu when no card is open. Its pages shorten to fit a short screen or a large interface scale, so every control stays on screen. With the Inventory page open, shift-click moves an item between you and the companion — into its bag, or from its bag or gear slots to you — and never to the trash; if a chest or piggy bank is also open, shift-click fills that instead, as it normally would. The mastery page is the flat tree of forty-four nodes with their costs, a selectable preview with no stat or ability effects yet. The companion mirrors your maximum life, defence and mana and reads your tool reach; its tool power is the pickaxe and axe in its own slots. A change to any of those reaches mining, chopping, lighting and pot work at once, the next time each looks for work: a longer reach or a stronger tool can open ore, trees and sites that were out of reach a moment ago, and a shorter one withdraws them.

### What this section does not claim

- A green headless check establishes its fixture, not behaviour in every cave or with every mod.
- **The course brain has been played once, and the tree described here has not.** It took the live tick on 21 September 2026 and was played for a minute on the morning of 22 September on packaged 0.38.13, which is the newest capture in Current Behaviour and the only one of this brain. Everything landed after that minute — the three roots the capture showed, the bounded light census, the refused pot, survival-first between fights, the park and the tour, the recorder's own audit — is read from source, from the headless suite and from that capture replayed headlessly, and no second play has happened.
- Opportunistic replacement mid-course is not built. A course worth more than the one already running is never weighed while that course is valid, because comparing two futures needs the incumbent reprojected from the current observation and that reprojection has no caller. The companion finishes what it started and picks again at the end, rather than abandoning a half-flown journey for something better that appeared beside it.
- The family chooser is gone, as of 22 September 2026. It was off the tick's path from 21 September and stayed compiled for one day so that a defect found in play could be attributed to one brain or the other; the world run's play-measures instrument grades the course on the capture that motivated it now, so nothing needs a second brain standing behind it. Three things it held were never decisions and survive it: the list of behaviour instances, the activity lifecycle, and the companionship observation.
- Mastery is a preview: none of its nodes has an effect yet. Liquid immunity is not on the tree, because the body is immune to every liquid from the start.
- A closed door is treated as a wall by route search: the companion opens a door that is the only way, but prefers a detour it can fly over a door it could open.
- The gear refuses a modded item that fires through its own code, and that refusal has never been exercised: no modded item exists headless, and no modded game has been played with this tree.
- Besides the one orb play, which predates the hover, the easing and the evade layer, the recorded route is the only evidence about the orb's following: twenty of twenty places reached on one walker recording, measured to the player's body within the follow comfort. It is a replay of his track through the saved world with no enemies and no play behind it, and nothing else here about following has a capture behind it.
- Boss and event conduct beyond stopping optional work is mostly not built: the wider range with a strengthening pull back to you that Expected Behaviour describes, and survival-first *inside* a fight, where damage and safety are traded against each other. Survival-first *between* fights was built on 22 September 2026 — during an encounter the feasible fight the companion survives wins, and an idle course can no longer take the encounter by having nothing to lose.
- Help that would arrive too late makes protection more urgent rather than making the trip worth less: only a fight too long to matter, or a threat nothing it can reach can shoot, lowers guarding's value.
- Under legacy lighting the engine keeps no separate map of the world's own light, so the field reads the presented light there and a torch in your hand reads as room light.
- One capture in Current Behaviour was taken of this body, 08:30 on 15 September on 0.26.0, before it hovered, eased and bent its flight on top of its job, so it describes the machinery this section replaced rather than this section. Every other capture is of the walking companion retired on 14 September, and the newest of those, 13:27 that day on the packaged 0.24.0, is the player track the orb is measured against headlessly rather than an account of what the orb does; the sessions behind the table's Current column are older still. They do not establish every biome, kit or setting, chopping on the 0.22.41 runs is Mimic, and 11:06 is mostly wet.
- The world run, the corpus mirror and the ledger are instruments over the harness's own fixtures and over recorded play; a green scoreboard says every case reported what it reported last time, not that the companion is good company.

## Rulings that constrain every change

- This is singleplayer only: use `Main.LocalPlayer`; do not add netcode, server branches, `netUpdate`, or player iteration.
- The companion is an NPC. Its temporary stand-in player exists only while hostile AI runs so enemies can target it; life, death and movement belong to the NPC.
- It never teleports. Ordinary following can start continuous recovery flight when far from the live player; combat, work and downing cannot start it. Recovery ignores terrain and is the one exception to ordinary contact.
- The body is an orb and nothing plans as if it stood. A route is a corridor of free space wider than the body; reach is a flood over free cells; the ceiling above the player's feet is a positioning rule, never a wall the body meets. Every liquid — water, honey, lava and shimmer — is air to it: no search treats one as a wall, the body is never hurt, slowed or transformed by one, and nothing takes the body out of one. The owner ruled on 15 September 2026 that this immunity is built in rather than a mastery unlock, because a capability an upgrade switches on would have to be tested with and without it after every later change to the brain. A read of liquid that is about something other than the body — where a torch may go, where a drop will land, how an enemy or a projectile moves — stays. Slopes are full tiles to it and platforms are passable. There is one body: the orb's contact with terrain is the mod's own circle-against-tiles test, run identically in the mod and in every headless tool, with the engine's box collision switched off for it.
- A decision is a comparison of whole options on what they are worth, never a priority branch and never a scenario-specific rule. **Which comparison is a question the owner opened and has not closed.** What is implemented since `0bb2c8a` on 21 September 2026 is the retained course: bounded orders of concrete opportunities, each priced by its forecast consequences (`Selection/Courses/`), replacing the multiplication of considerations per activity per tick, which was deleted on 22 September 2026 with `AIC-419`. Whether that is the right brain is what the acceptance play decides; what attributes a defect to it is the world run's play-measures instrument rather than a rival brain kept compiled beside it. `Selection/EvaluateConsiderations.cs` survives and is not that chooser — it is the `Consideration` response curves, which `KeepCompany`, `CollectNearbyItems`, `SearchAttackPlans` and `ChooseUsefulPosition` all read. The route-search question closed on 2026-09-14 with the orb, whose search is A* over free cells with a clearance cost.
- **Keeping the player company is what the companion does only when there is nothing else to do, and it never competes with work.** The owner ruled this on 25 September 2026, after a play where three zombies below the player were declined because every fight was charged for the time the orb would spend away from him and the empty course, charged nothing, won. It is the one deliberate exception to the rule above: `CompareCourseOutcomes.NominalOrder` ranks an order that does any useful work above one that does none before it compares anything else, and nothing prices the companionship gap — not the course objective, not combat's stands, not a committed fight's release. Nearness still orders jobs among themselves, through the census windows around the player's heading and through how soon each job lands, so a torch beside him beats a fight fifteen seconds away; what nearness can never do is make doing nothing win. The only safety left over the choice of job is the evade layer, downing and recovery flight at fly-home distance.
- Reuse a decompiled Terraria path when it is not gated on the local player. The mod reads game item numbers but never runs companion abilities through `Player.ItemCheck`; a handed weapon or tool is read for its facts and used by the companion's own mechanisms, and an item those mechanisms cannot express is refused by its slot.
- A decision evaluates every option it has and learns from what its own actions did, never a hand-written proxy for the question. The owner ruled this on 15 September 2026 about weapon and target choice and said it holds for every system from here on: a firing spot is priced by the best attack every handed weapon could make from there, not by the weapon chosen last; how a weapon flies, shoves and hurts each enemy type is learned from its own hits; and interactions between weapons, such as one applying a debuff the other exploits, are to be learned the same way rather than written as rules. A factor that stands in for a quantity the brain could compute is a defect waiting for the case where the two disagree.
- Four slots and no more: two weapons, a pickaxe and an axe. No armour, no accessories, no ammo slot; ammo is free, mana mirrors the player's and tires magic rather than stopping it.

## README's story and behaviour table are kept by these rules

The README carries the target: the half-hour story and the Behaviour By Behaviour table's two columns, Behaviour and Expected. The rules below governed those sections when they lived in the README and govern every edit to them now; they moved here on 27 October 2026 because they are instructions to whoever edits the README, not text a player reads. The table's *Current* and *System* columns moved with the observed account to `research/Historical Evidence/Observed Behaviour and Assessment Limits.md`, so the three-column rules below apply across the two files: Expected in the README, Current and System in the evidence file, each row found in both by its bold title.

### The story

**This section is the story; the table further down is the specification.** A behaviour is settled as a row in Behaviour By Behaviour first, and a scene here narrates it afterwards in a situation that could actually happen. So the question when editing is never "should this go in the story or the table" — it goes in both, in that order, and the scene is where a reader finds out what the row feels like at sixty frames a second.

**Nothing here may name a system, a score, a search, a mode or a behaviour-as-a-thing.** Write what the companion does and what it is weighing in the words a player would use. The failure is subtle because it reads as precision: "it checks every route before committing" sounds like a stronger sentence than "it makes certain it can get there, and where it cannot be certain it does not go", and it is a worse one, because it has decided that routes are checked and that there is a thing called a route.

**Nothing here compares against what the mod currently does.** No "this does not exist yet", no "this is the biggest gap", no "unlike today". The moment a scene knows what is built, it starts arguing with it, and this section has to stay readable as a description of a finished thing by someone who has never seen the code.

**A scene earns its place by containing a decision that could have gone the other way.** The pattern every scene follows: name the alternatives the companion actually weighed and rejected, not just the one it took; shift one thing mid-situation and show it adapting inside the same decision; add a branch that ends in a *different* action, with the reason; and close by naming the principle without naming a rule. A scene with one outcome teaches a rule and a scene with three teaches the thing that generates them, which is what this section is for.

**Prefer changing an existing scene to adding one.** The half-hour is a spine and it is more useful dense than long. A new behaviour usually belongs as a complication inside the scene it naturally collides with, and only earns its own timestamp when there is nowhere it fits.

**The body is an orb and no scene may need it to stand.** It hovers, drifts, dips and climbs; it never jumps, lands, walks up a stair or drops through a platform. A scene that finds itself wanting a floor under the companion is describing the old body and gets rewritten.

### The behaviour table

This table is the source of truth for what the companion is for, and it is maintained rather than written once: a new responsibility we discover mid-development becomes a row here first, stripped of any language about how it might be built, and the Expected Behaviour section above narrates it afterwards.

There is deliberately no column scoring how close each row is or where the blame sits. Both were tried and removed. A percentage conflates how complete a system is with how far off the behaviour looks, and those come apart badly: decision commitment moved from 55.5 to 22.3 ticks per choice on a single constant with no structural change at all, so any number claiming a behaviour was a fifth of the way there was describing a value rather than a system and would have swung to nearly all of the way there on a one-line edit. Blame is worse, because these systems are not separable enough for it — the clearest case in this table is a companion that looks bad at avoiding damage while the evidence points at the collision reflex interrupting its escape, which belongs to a different row entirely. Where a judgement of that kind is actually supported, it belongs in the System cell as a named hypothesis with the check that would settle it, which can be argued with; a confidence label cannot be.


**The three columns answer three different questions and the commonest way to damage this table is to answer one of them in another one's cell.** Expected says what should happen, Current says what was observed, System says what is built and why we think the two differ. Mixing them is not untidiness — it makes the table lie.

**Expected never mentions a mechanism.** The test is whether the sentence would still be true after the system underneath it is thrown away and rewritten differently. "It searches more widely when a route fails" names a design and goes stale the moment that design changes; "it reaches the player through terrain where the first leg goes the wrong way — dropping into water, crossing under a wall, opening the gap on purpose in order to close it" names a behaviour and survives any implementation. The second form is also the one that leaves the design open, which is the point: a row that names a mechanism has quietly made an architecture decision in a document that exists to hold the requirements.

**Current is observation only, with the session named.** No reasoning, no cause, no "which suggests". If the sentence contains the word because, it is in the wrong cell. Numbers come from a recording and carry which one, so a reader can go and check; a sentence sourced from watching the game rather than reading the log says so, because a narrated impression and a measured column are not the same kind of claim and this table should not disguise one as the other.

**System carries both what is built and every hypothesis about why the gap exists, and each hypothesis is marked as one.** Write the word unverified, then the reading, then the specific check that would confirm or kill it — and prefer a check the existing telemetry could answer, because those are cheap. Where a plausible cause has been ruled out, say so and give the number that ruled it out; that is often more useful than the surviving hypothesis, because it is what stops the same idea being tried again in a month.

**Current without System misleads by omission, and the enemy selection row is the worked example of how.** It once read that nothing was selected on two thirds of ticks and that shots fired accounted for under 1% — both true, and together they read as "it picks targets and does not fire", which is the opposite of what is happening. Firing is one tick and the reload is 28% of the session, so it shoots perfectly well when it has a target; and a target chosen with no shot available accounts for 175 ticks in a session, so it is not picking blocked targets either. The actual finding is that it selects nothing at all while something is in range, and none of that is visible from the observation alone. Any row whose Current column invites an obvious explanation needs that explanation stated and tested in System, or the row will be read as whatever the reader assumes.

**When a new behaviour comes up mid-development, it becomes a row here first.** Strip the system language out of however it was said, write Expected, and leave Current and System as honest blanks if nothing has been observed or built. A row with two empty cells is more useful than no row, because it records that we want the thing.

**When something contradicts an existing row, the contradiction is resolved here before any code is written.** Two rows wanting opposite things, or a new observation that makes an Expected cell wrong, is a decision about what the companion is — so the losing version is deleted rather than left standing beside its replacement, and the reasoning goes in the commit that removes it. A table holding two answers to one question stops being a specification.

**Rows split when one of them turns out to be two responsibilities.** Finding a route was carved out of Traversing terrain for exactly that reason: one was about choosing a sequence of moves and the other about performing a single move, they fail independently, and a single row could not say which was wrong. If a row's Current column needs the word "sometimes" in two unrelated senses, it is probably two rows.

**Row order carries no meaning.** It was once ascending by a score that no longer exists. Put a new row anywhere.

## Operating manual

Compile without packaging while the game may be open:

```
cd "$HOME/Library/Application Support/Terraria/tModLoader/ModSources/AICompanion"
sh Tools/build.sh                           the mod, without packaging, errors only
sh Tools/build.sh engine-replay             one tool project; `all` does the mod and every one
sh Tools/build.sh --full mod                the compiler's whole output rather than only its errors
```

That wraps `dotnet build -nologo -v q -p:BuildMod=false` for the mod and `dotnet build Tools/<project> -nologo -v q` for a tool, prints the error lines deduplicated and one word per target that compiled, and exits 1 if any target did not. The flags are not optional and not guessable — `BuildMod=false` is what makes a build safe with the game open, and `UseAppHost=false` is the SDK fault `Directory.Build.rsp` describes — which is why they live in a script rather than in anyone's memory. The target names are discovered from `Tools/*/*.csproj`, so `sh Tools/build.sh --help` lists what this tree actually has.

The pass is zero `error CS` lines and a fresh `bin/Debug/net8.0/AICompanion.dll`. Build with packaging only when the game is closed. Run the navigation-boundary check after movement-core changes:

```
sh Tools/check-navigation-boundary.sh
```

The whole check, and the one nearly every commit body in this repository cites as its evidence, is:

```
sh Tools/verify.sh
```

It builds the mod (without packaging) and every tool once, proves the DLL is newer than every source file that feeds it rather than trusting "Build succeeded", runs `check-navigation-boundary.sh`'s four boundaries, and then runs every instrument from its built assembly in three phases. First, in parallel: the ledger, NavReplay, SessionReport and CombatAudit self-tests, and the native engine suite split into one shard per performance core. Second, the timed lane: every case tagged timed or perf-tier, alone on the machine. Last, the world runs, one at a time. Finally the ledger's scoreboard runs, and its exit code is the script's. Every instrument runs to the end whatever the one before it did. The perf tier, the cases heavy enough that an ordinary verify skips them, runs when anything the mod is compiled from changed since the last perf run; `--perf` and `--no-perf` override that. Measured on 24 September 2026 at `f3b45fa`, a whole run with the perf tier took 260 s, 126 s of it the crowd planning case alone. The same work took 324 s at `987319d` without that case being isolated. `Tools/CLAUDE.md` owns the phases and why their order keeps a timing honest.

**No timing is a pass line, by the owner's ruling of 24 September 2026, and every run says how fast the machine was.** A wall-clock figure is a ledger measure compared against its own history under the same run conditions, never a threshold that goes red. The scoreboard prints a machine benchmark taken at both ends of the run, each instrument's case durations as a ratio to the baseline's, and each process's heap and gen-2 collections. That is the evidence which once took an hour of building the parent commit to establish that a red was the machine and not the code. `Tools/Ledger/CLAUDE.md` owns the rules.

**Exit 0 means no row went red, and that is less than "everything passed".** A case that stopped reporting — skipped, or filing no row at all — is not a red row, so the scoreboard names it in its own block and carries the count on the closing line, and the closing line is therefore the thing to read rather than the exit code. That is deliberate rather than a gap: a fresh clone has no capture for the play measures, because `Telemetry/` is gitignored, and failing the run for that would punish the clone for the store's shape. Exit 2 still means a check could not be asked at all, which is the absent-ripgrep case and is neither a pass nor a violation.

```
sh Tools/verify.sh --case "ore work"        only cases whose name contains the fragment
```

`run-case.sh` is the standalone half of the comparison the flake paragraph below rests on, and it is a different question from `verify.sh --case`: that one drives the whole harness with a filter and files rows, this one builds the instrument, runs the matching cases in a bare process with the `DYLD_LIBRARY_PATH` the native fixtures need, and files its rows to a temp path outside `Tools/Ledger/runs/` — the only directory the scoreboard scans for baselines — so the store gains no file a later run could resolve as one. It counts from those rows rather than from what the instrument printed, and that distinction is the whole of it: `EmitLedgerRows.Row` appends and returns without printing, so ledger, nav-replay and session-report have no stdout line a *pass* can produce, and the first version of this script, which opened no run file and counted the terminal, reported cases that ran and passed as never having been asked. A fragment matching nothing is exit 2 with a sentence saying so and a count of what the instrument declined by name, because an unmatched filter otherwise prints no rows and exits 0, which on a terminal is indistinguishable from a case that passed. A clean run prints its rows; a failing one prints everything, because the reason is why you ran it. A second argument picks the instrument (`engine-replay` by default, then `ledger`, `nav-replay`, `session-report`, `combat-audit`) — and `combat-audit` ignores the fragment and runs its whole self-test, measured on 2026-09-21 with a fragment matching no case of its eight, which is a property of that instrument and reaches `verify.sh --case` the same way.

A red is a stop whatever the reruns show: a case that fails once and then passes four times prints `FLAKY` with its interval and the run still exits 1, because the fail row is a row.

Read a playtest with `sh Tools/build.sh session-report` then `dotnet Tools/SessionReport/bin/Debug/net8.0/SessionReport.dll Telemetry` — never `dotnet run --project`, which fails on this machine with "an error occurred trying to start process" because `Directory.Build.rsp` turns apphost generation off for `dotnet build` only and `dotnet run`'s launch honours command-line properties alone; replay and world-window procedures live in their respective tool folders. `Telemetry/` is gitignored, so a fresh clone has nothing to read until a playtest writes one.

## Traps

- **The shell build says "Build succeeded" in under two seconds.** That is real; check the DLL timestamp before trusting it. `Tools/verify.sh` already checks this for you — it refuses a DLL older than any source file feeding it — so the trap bites hardest when building by hand.
- **`sh Tools/verify.sh` is not reliably green under load, and a single green run is not evidence that it is.** Measured 2026-09-14 on this machine at effd5e9, five consecutive runs with a droid fleet building in the same checkout: three exited 0, two exited 1, both on `VerifyOreWork.RaisedLipsAtBothGravitiesProduceWork` at `Tools/EngineReplay/Gathering/VerifyOreWork.cs`, "raised lip must produce a native ore break", the `mirrored=True, mode=HeldActivity` case at floor 90 on one run and floor 60 on the other. Five runs at 1554e0b with the machine idle were all green. The practical consequence when reading or writing a commit body: a citation of "verify.sh exits 0" after one run under load confirms much less than it implies, and a red on this fixture alone is probably not yours. Anything depending on this suite needs a batch of runs sized to how rarely the flake fires, taken idle. **The row that flaked then no longer exists**: `RaisedLipsAtBothGravitiesProduceWork` was one of the walker's gravity rows and went with the body on 2026-09-15, so that exact signature cannot recur on it; the per-case reset that rebuilds the tile map (`Tools/EngineReplay/ResetProcessState.cs`) removed the one order dependence the rebuild did find, which was terrain rather than time.

  **The suite is about 2.3x slower per operation than a standalone run, and two rows flake on exactly that difference.** Measured 21 September 2026 across the twelve whole-suite runs of that evening (`0d1f12d-…-185702` to `2cbd503-…-214318`): `ore work breaks ore without excavating ordinary terrain` failed on 5 of 12 in-suite and passed 3 of 3 alone; `combat is admitted only where it can be executed` failed on 2 of 4 in-suite and passes alone. The second one prints its own number, which is what turns a flake into a measurement — **2.692 ms per score standalone against 6.143 and 6.339 on the two failing suite runs.**

  **That direction is the opposite of the one `Tools/EngineReplay/CLAUDE.md` documents**, which warns that a fixture run alone pays JIT for the whole planning path while the same fixture inside the suite does not — so the suite ought to be the faster of the two and is the slower, with JIT working against the result. Whatever it is, it is not cold code. **`6c14e4c` then split the cost and the harness is innocent**: driving the whole harness — build, boundary check, ledger, every instrument — with one case selected and 141 skipped printed 2.947 ms per score, against 2.692 ms standalone and 6.143 / 6.339 ms with every case running, so the harness costs about a quarter of a millisecond and running the other two hundred cases first in the same process costs another three and a quarter. That run was taken under the same conditions as the failing ones and landed on the standalone figure, which removes machine load as the explanation rather than merely narrowing it. What is left is accumulated process state, and the next step is total managed memory and per-generation collection counts printed at each case boundary — a monotone climb names heap pressure without a profiler, and the per-case reset is where to look first, because it restores statics and rebuilds the tile map and reclaims no memory. - **`Main.DrawTileCracks` adds `offScreenRange`** unless `drawToScreen`; `TileCracksRenderer` cancels it.
- **`CheckActive` returns false**, so the companion is never culled for distance.
- **The health bar draws in raw screen pixels** because `Main.mouseX/Y` are screen pixels.
- **The engine-only `moved` column cannot see our AI-phase position writes.** `oldPosition = position` is assigned inside the engine immediately before `position += velocity`, so the telemetry's `moved` column spans the engine's own move and nothing our brain or motor wrote to `npc.position` beforehand — the walker's step-up helper once lifted the body back onto a platform it was descending for 265 ticks while reading as a body with a large velocity, no collision and no movement. ### What the walker established that still binds

The walker's folder guides were replaced rather than kept, so the properties they learned live here as properties rather than as the patches that expressed them; a design that comes back wearing new mechanics is refused by the property, not by a diff.

- **A fallback whose trigger is the absence of the ordinary path's precondition fires hardest while the planner is still thinking**, which is the moment it can do the most damage. Anything reviving a reactive layer answers which fact it fires on: that the planner failed, or merely that it has not finished yet.
- **A bound that ran out is a third value, not a refusal.** Optional work does not start on an unanswered search; only a completed sweep with nothing found is a proven absence. This is why every sense answers in three values, and why the reach flood's clock is the resolve rather than the tick.
- **Invalidation is spatial.** A retained search records what it read and is discarded only by an edit inside that; a caller that restarts on any edit anywhere starves under a digging player.
- **A destination is kept by membership, not by a bonus.** A held spot stays while it still belongs to the region it was admitted against, and no rival is asked to outscore it.
- **A live interference footprint must not suppress the intent region's lead.** A region pulled back onto a walking player's feet asks the companion to stand exactly where he is going; courtesy is answered where the body is chosen, never by blinding the sense that says where he is heading.
- **A speed discretised into a search node cannot carry the real speed accurately enough to perform what it proved**; soundness needs a finer quantisation than performance affords. The orb's search carries no speed because momentum steering follows any clear route, and the property returns the day a move depends on arrival speed.
- **Two timings are comparable only when taken the same way**: a fixture run alone pays JIT for the whole search path and the same fixture inside the suite does not, and a twelvefold difference was once credited to a change that had moved the worst tick by nothing.
- **A number quoted about a capture is a claim until an instrument reproduces it.** Twice a filter written over a file once was reported as a measurement and was wrong; anything asserted about a recording is reproducible through the session reader's measures or it is not yet evidence.

Not built, and named as such: the mastery wheel's effects (speed, acceleration and the dash; there is no liquid immunity to earn, because every liquid is air to the body from the start), chaining several jobs into one trip, boss and event conduct beyond stopping optional work, a fought retreat home, and the gear's refusal of a modded item with its own firing code, which no headless run can exercise. Also not built: any cue that leads a player to the four gear slots (the first orb play's gear was empty all session), what an unarmed companion treats as its job in a fight, and a world run with a hostile in it, which is why the freeze was found in play and not headlessly — **that last one has since been built**: `f330aec` on 17 September added `verify.sh`'s combat world-run variant, six hundred ticks of the recorded route with `--combat`. Route search still treats a closed door as a wall. These checks establish the recorded fixtures, not reliable flight through every live cave or compatibility with every mod.
