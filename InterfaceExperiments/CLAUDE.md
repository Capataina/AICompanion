# Interface Experiments — archived visual references for the native companion menus

```

├─ CLAUDE.md              this guide
└─ companion-card.html    the next revision of the HUD notch and the native profile card, at the pixel sizes the C# will be built to: the page opens on the notch, the card's pages and controls are live, and the mastery wheel is a clickable draft whose nodes say what they do; its header lists every departure from the 2026-09-13 render
```

## The 2026-09-15 revision is a proposal on top of the agreed card

The owner asked on 15 September 2026 for the mock to catch up with the orb and to start the mastery tree's content. Four things in it go beyond what the game or its records hold, and none is a ruling yet: **four weapon slots** where the game has two (root `CLAUDE.md`, "Four slots and no more"), three of them opened by Combat's diamonds; **Combat** as the lane Guarding was; the name **Lumen** in place of Aria, which the owner asked to replace (checked against vanilla item, NPC and projectile names only, not against other mods); and **every node's name and number**, drafted against the nearest item in Terraria, Calamity or Thorium and marked where no comparison exists. The owner said he will go through the nodes and change them, and that the tree's decisions are made after the build lanes of that day are merged; until then the native `DefineMasteryGraph` and this mock disagree on purpose.

This folder holds no game code. Nothing here is compiled or loaded by the mod, and `build.txt` excludes it from packaging. These early HTML experiments preserve layout and progression ideas as visual references. New interface work belongs directly in the native Terraria menus, by the user's instruction; extending a web preview does not deliver an in-game feature. The native render harness checks the real panels offscreen without repeatedly launching the game.

## What the card is and what it replaces

`../Companion/HeadsUpDisplay/` draws a draggable health notch which opens `../Companion/ProfileCard/`. Its Inventory page embeds `../Companion/Inventory/`'s native bag slots. The notch therefore reaches behaviour preferences as well as storage; the bag's gameplay data remains independent of whichever interface presents it.

The native card is one panel whose body swaps between the profile overview, Inventory and Mastery; the two bottom status tiles are the way in, and there is no tab row. The overview presents live preferences; Inventory uses the game's item slots; Mastery offers a selectable four-lane preview without gameplay effects, material payments or saved progression. Whitelists remain mock-only. The HTML layouts supply composition references, while the game owns its typography, panel rendering and interaction conventions.

## Mimic policies and voluntary activity toggles are different controls

Mimic means the player's corresponding action triggers work. Mining and chopping therefore have Off/Mimic/Auto choices. Voluntary hunting, pot breaking and supplied torch placement instead have Off/On choices. Disabling hunting does not disable guarding or self-preservation. Carrying a torch remains independent of placing one. Fishing is not implemented.

The native preferences in `../Companion/PlayerIntegration/ConfigureCompanionPreferences.cs` own these settings. The original HTML card predates the voluntary toggles and uses longer policy labels; its control inventory is not the current native capability list. A scored behaviour can be disabled by an eligibility policy without replacing utility scoring.

The native card explicitly identifies guarding and survival as automatic. The inspector and telemetry switches belong to tModLoader Mod Configuration rather than this card.

## The other decisions the mockup encodes

The original mock uses named distance stops. Native controls use Close/Standard/Free profiles, which scale following comfort, activity acquisition and recovery distance together. There is no remote mission mode.

**The overview has no action line.** It used to carry the companion's current action with its reason ("Mining · copper vein 12 tiles below · opportunistic"), as the answer to a player unable to tell a working companion from a stuck one. On 15 September 2026 the owner removed it with everything else the card said that it did not need at a glance, and the three bars grew to fill the strip. What the companion is doing is not on the card now; the in-game overlay is where it can be read, and if the working-or-stuck question comes back in play, the answer should arrive as a shape or a state rather than as that sentence.

**The gear the companion uses is item slots on the Inventory page, not a loadout on the overview, and the drone stands beside them.** Since the orb pivot of 14 September 2026 the companion fights and works with real items handed to it, so the page carries game item slots above the bag grid, each accepting only what the companion can use, and a handed item is chosen by putting it in the slot the way every other equipment choice in the game is made. That replaced the carried pair's chips and the twenty-weapon kit's Weapons page, which died with the kit. The native build carries two weapons, a pickaxe and an axe; the mock draws the 2026-09-15 proposal of four weapon slots, a pickaxe and an axe, with the first weapon slot open and each other one locked behind the Combat diamond it names, so learning that diamond in the mastery preview unlocks the slot and clicking a locked slot goes to the diamond. The overview's portrait is the drone, drawn as the rough symmetrical disc `../Assets/CLAUDE.md` describes; the Inventory page does not picture the companion at all. Its top row is two bordered sections that together span the page, Weapons and Tools, with the slots evenly spaced at their fixed size and no hover text beside them, by the owner's ruling of 15 September 2026 that the page had no use for a portrait or for words about a slot. The identity strip stacks health, mana and experience as large bars in the notch's order and colours, each reading beside its bar in the bar's colour; the notch has the bars without the readings.

**The page opens on the HUD notch, because that is how a player meets the card.** In play the card is never open until the notch is pressed, so a mock that opened on the card agreed to a first impression the game never gives. The notch hangs from the top edge with square top corners, rounded bottom corners and two concave fillets, carries health, mana and experience as three stacked bars and nothing else: no name, no level, no numbers and no family or behaviour icon, because the owner wants the HUD to be something glanced at, and the numbers live on the card. Clicking it opens and closes the card, dragging frees it from the edge and right-click docks it again, which is what `../Companion/HeadsUpDisplay/CompanionHealthBar.cs` does; that file still draws mana and experience side by side as two half-width pills, and the stacked column is the owner's 2026-09-15 request for the native build. A docked notch owns the top of the screen, so the card is placed below it and never slides under it on a short screen.

**The three bottom regions are summary tiles, not buttons, and each takes over the whole card.** A button that says "Inventory" spends a sixth of the card to say a word the player already knows; the tile carries a fill bar and a count beside its name, and opening is what happens when it is clicked. The sentence each tile used to carry under its name ("18 of 120 slots occupied", "21 ores found · will mine 19") went on 15 September 2026 because it said the count a second time, and the tiles and the overview shortened with it. The overview itself is short — title bar, identity strip with the three surviving controls on its right, three tiles — and Inventory, Mastery and the Mining list each replace it with a full-height page, so a page never shares the card with the strip it does not need. A page's own actions sit in its title bar, right-aligned before the close button at the round buttons' height — the chest buttons on Inventory, Skip marked and Only marked on the Mining list, −, + and Reset view on Mastery — by the owner's ruling of 15 September 2026, because a row of buttons above a page's content spent a band of the page on controls the title bar had room for. Hunting and pot breaking are not toggles in the first version and following distance never changes, so the overview draws none of them.

**The ore list hides what has never been picked up, and ores are the only thing with a list.** An ore appears once the player has held one in their inventory at least once, recorded as a saved set on the character so that selling the last of it does not remove it, and never before, so the list does not spoil what a world or a mod holds. A "mine N first" threshold was the earlier design and is dropped because it can be gamed. One mode says what a mark on a tile means — skip the marked ores, or mine only the marked ores — and whatever the mode, an ore the companion will mine is drawn bright and one it will leave is drawn dim, so the picture answers "what will it dig" without reading the mode. The preview under the grid shows the hovered ore *as it appears in the wall*, because that is the form the player has to recognise while digging and the item icon never teaches it. No other behaviour has a list: the rest are Off/Mimic/Auto or Off/On.

**The inventory page is the grid and Terraria's own chest buttons, nothing else.** Loot All, Deposit All, Quick Stack and Restock are the game's names and the game's semantics, so a player already knows them and the native build can reuse the chest routines; the bag sorts itself after every transfer, so there is no Sort button, no filters and no detail panel. An earlier version had type filters that dimmed what they excluded and a selected-slot panel on the right, and both went because they spent the space the grid should have.

**The card's mastery wheel has four lanes, and it is symmetric on purpose.** They are the lanes the orb can grow: Combat (named Guarding until 2026-09-15) holds damage, fire rate, pierce, staggered extra projectiles, critical strikes, a share of armour ignored and the extra weapon slots; Movement holds top speed, acceleration, speed while flying to a job, knockback taken and the speed of flying home; Survival holds life, defence, damage reduction, regeneration, self-revival, an emergency repair, the link that takes part of the player's damage and the lifeline that heals the player from the companion's own damage; Gathering holds all tool use (mining and chopping speed as one number, extra wood and ore, reach, the loot pull, the flashlight, the ore glow for the player and the bag) and ends on Plunder, more loot from every enemy. The three weapon-slot diamonds are all called Extra weapon slot, because the tree's edges and needs already order them and the numbers only made the names harder to read.

**Plunder can reach every drop from every mod, and a failed roll getting a second try is the shape that keeps guaranteed drops single.** Every enemy drop, vanilla or added by a mod to any enemy's loot, resolves through the game's `ItemDropResolver.ResolveRule`, and a common drop succeeds when `info.player.RollLuck(denominator) < numerator` (read from the decompiled `CommonDrop` on 2026-09-15). Re-rolling a failed roll with the node's chance turns a 1% drop into about 1.25% at level 5 and leaves a 100% drop untouched. Rerunning the whole loot table was the other way to express it, and it loses because it doubles guaranteed drops, boss bags included, a quarter of the time. Game luck already works as a second roll, so this follows the game's own shape. Unverified until built: whether the private `ResolveRule` takes a tModLoader hook, or every rule in the drop database has to be wrapped once after all mods have loaded; and how nested rules (one-from-options, chains) report a failed roll. The four weapon-class lanes and their per-weapon trees went with the authored weapon kit on 14 September 2026, and Tools and Support went earlier. Every lane is one ten-node template rotated by a quarter turn: a circle, a circle, then a split whose ranks alternate — a diamond on the left beside a circle on the right, a circle on the left beside a diamond on the right, then two circles — meeting at a third diamond and ending on a circle. That is the owner's shape from 2026-09-15; it replaced a nineteen-node lane of five diamonds, and an eight-node version whose two side diamonds shared a rank was sent back because the diamonds should alternate and ten nodes are what Combat's list needs. A shared circle sits between neighbouring lanes, opened from either lane's first-rank node, and the page computes the nearest pair of nodes so a layout change that breaks it is caught by a number rather than by eye. Three findings shape the template: an earlier irregular-branching version varied each spoke and was rejected because the spokes read as unequal; an eight-spoke fork kept at four spokes rendered as a plus sign with empty quadrants; and the first eight-node lane, at the old lane length with the old node size, drew its nodes about six pixels wide once the detail panel took the graph's right side, so the lane is shorter and the nodes larger. The native `DefineMasteryGraph` still generates the nineteen-node wheel and changes once the tree is decided.

**Every node is a flat bonus to a number the companion already reads, and never a new thing it can do or choose.** The owner ruled this on 15 September 2026: a node that switched on a capability, such as flying through water or a dash, would have to be tested with and without it after every later change to the companion, while fire rate, tool reach or a loot pull's radius is applied by the system that already reads it and changes nothing else. So the dash, its cooldown, Slipstream, Afterburner, Strafe (attack speed near top speed), Evasive (speed after a miss) and Cutting beam (a new damage source) left the tree, and water and lava immunity became part of the companion itself (Slate AIC-397). Some bonuses need care in how they are built rather than a place off the tree: each extra projectile leaves a fifth of the weapon's own reload after the one before, so a bow's arrows never overlap; and piercing adds its enemies after the weapon's own last hit, which keeps the weapon's damage, while a weapon that already pierces without limit, or a swing, gains nothing from it. Armour piercing is a share of defence rather than a flat amount, because a flat few points mean nothing against an enemy with thousands of defence. A circle that can level has five levels, a diamond that can level has three, and a weapon slot has one; the level bar shows "Level N" for three segments and the number alone for five, because "Level 5" does not fit a fifth of the panel. A node whose levels do not step evenly (Piercing, Extra projectile, Bag space) carries a line per level: the panel shows the line for the next level to learn, and hovering a segment shows that level's line, so the page still says one sentence at a time.

**The tree is flat: a diamond opens nothing.** A Combat diamond opens a weapon slot; the other lanes' diamonds are bigger bonuses of three levels, such as the link that takes part of the player's damage or the loot pull. Earlier the same day every diamond opened its own eight-node tree of upgrades, and before that a fifteen-point tree and a four-line tree of chains; the owner removed trees inside diamonds altogether because two levels of upgrades are more than the companion needs and are harder to balance and to maintain, and the best of their ideas — an extra pierce, an extra projectile, the link's share — moved onto the lanes. With no tree per slot the first weapon slot is no longer the one without upgrades: Combat's damage and attack-speed nodes apply to every weapon it holds.

**The Mastery page says the minimum, by the owner's ruling of 15 September 2026.** With nothing picked the tree has the whole page and Reset view centres it there. A click on a node opens a panel on the right with its name and what it does, and nothing else; at the panel's foot sit a Learn button and a level bar drawn in the same joined-segment shape as the overview's Off/Mimic/Auto controls, one segment per level, learned levels selected, a single level rounded at both ends. The graph narrows by the panel's width keeping its left edge and scale, so nothing on screen jumps when the panel opens, and a picked node the panel would cover is panned into view. A click on empty background closes the panel, so browsing the tree needs no close button, while a drag to pan leaves it open; the two are told apart by the notch's six-pixel drag threshold. The side lanes' names are anchored at their near end past the lane's last node, because a long horizontal label centred on the lane lay on that node, and the fit reads the drawn tree's own bounds so the labels' real widths are what it leaves room for. Learn adds a level when an edge into the node comes from something learned and everything it needs is learned, and there is no Unlearn, because a learned level stays learned. An earlier revision the same day filled the panel with the node's kind and rank, a purpose sentence, what it was sized against and what it needed, and put a hint line above the graph and a legend and status line below it; the owner sent it back as crowded for no reason. The sizing research still lives in each node's data, where it serves balancing, and the nearest-node check still runs as a console assertion rather than as text. The same rule trimmed every page: the Mining list lost its instruction sentence, its caption and its "found · will mine" count line, which the tile already carries; the gear row lost its default note and its "Weapons" and "Tools" captions, because the two boxes say which is which and the caption row pushed the slots off centre; and the Mastery tile lost its "draft" line. Nothing is saved.

**Magnitudes are modest and follow Terraria's own shapes, from research done on 2026-09-15.** The owner judged the first draft too strong once imagined fully learned, so each lane now tops out at about one or two good accessories' worth. Slots are always whole (+1 per item, never a percentage); unconditional damage reduction stays small (a Worm Scarf's 17%) while the larger numbers are conditional (a Frozen Turtle Shell's 25% below half life); a Paladin's Shield is the precedent for taking a share of an ally's damage (25% within 50 tiles, at most once every 0.67 s), though the owner dropped the companion's distance condition; nothing in the game shortens respawn, so self-revival has no number to copy, and its five levels take the owner's 20 seconds down to his 5; and no primary source was found for added pierce, homing or a light radius, which is why those nodes say so.

**Edges stop at a node's boundary, and a node is hit anywhere inside its shape.** A line used to run centre to centre and showed through every circle and diamond; it is now shortened at both ends by the circle's radius, or by the diamond's own radius along the line's direction, so it joins the sides. The shapes had no fill, and SVG hit-tests painted area only, so a node was clickable on its stroke alone and a press inside it fell through to the graph and started a pan; the fill is transparent now, edges take no pointer events, and the pan handler ignores a press that began on a node.

## Making it read as Terraria

A standalone mastery map (`mastery-map.html`, with Fieldwork, Scavenging and Thrown among its lane names and a point-and-stage purchase preview) was deleted on 2026-09-14 because the card's own mastery page is the design now and two maps invited confusion; its lane ideas survive as the card wheel's four lanes above. Circles carry five levels and diamonds three or one, and no diamond opens a tree of its own. Nothing in the mockup persists to game saves or changes stats, equipment, inventory or XP.

**The mastery tree never gates a behaviour.** The draft's learnings are stats, speed and acceleration, extra weapon slots and bonuses to what the companion's own mechanisms already do; none of them makes an activity available or unavailable to choose, and none adds a capability, which is the stronger rule above. A companion behaviour is not gated by whether the player has reached a node; the behaviour exists or it does not.

The intended progression separates three owners: companion levels award points, graph connections govern which node can be opened, and material/progression requirements govern purchases and upgrades. The balancing target is roughly half the full tree opened by Moon Lord, not a claim supported by this preview's point supply. Only actions the companion completes earn experience — its kills, ore, torches and wood — and the player's own actions earn it nothing, by the owner's ruling of 2026-09-14, which superseded an earlier note giving player actions a third; the ledger consumes the attempt-outcome record the brain already emits with attribution, so a new activity earns experience without new hooks, and `../Companion/Progression/` holds the total and the curve. Repetition limits, recipes and values remain design work. Shared tool upgrades cover mining, chopping and placement together. A speed or dash rank changes what the body can do and never what the brain decides. Interface grouping may collect the profile, bag panel and mastery view, while bag storage and item transfer remain gameplay services.

Four things carry nearly all of the resemblance, and a mockup that misses them reads as "a game menu" rather than as Terraria:

1. **Every string has a hard black drop shadow**, offset down-right with no blur. It is set once on `body` so everything inherits it, which is also how the game does it, and it is the single most recognisable trait.
2. **Panels are a saturated blue-violet at about 86% over the world**, so the world shows through and the panel never reads as a page.
3. **Item slots are a lighter blue with a bright thin border**, square, at a fixed 48px.
4. **Names are coloured by rarity rather than by role**, on Terraria's own ladder — white, blue, lime, yellow, cyan, red — so an item's name reads as progression to anyone who has played it.

The page draws a plain sky-and-ground backdrop behind the notch and the card for the second of these to mean anything: without something to be translucent against, an 86% panel is just a solid colour.

## Traps

- **No CSS transitions or keyframes anywhere, deliberately.** Terraria's interface does not animate, so an animated mockup would agree to a look the real surface cannot deliver, and every hover and selection state here changes instantly on purpose.
- **A slot sized as a fraction of its panel is wrong at every panel width.** Ten columns of `1fr` across the card produced 82px slots, roughly four times Terraria's own, and stopped reading as an inventory. Slots are a fixed pixel size and the column count follows from the width.
- **A selector scoped to a container will silently miss the copy of that element outside it.** `.row .lbl em` did not match the distance stepper's hint, which rendered at full size and full brightness — and a too-loud label still looks like a label in a screenshot, so nothing about it reads as broken.
- **Chrome floated over a pannable surface will land on its content.** The mastery legend sat on a node label as soon as the graph used its corners; it is a footer bar outside the surface now.
- **Mock state is mock.** Any value here that reads as real — a bag count, a level, a branch percentage, an ore threshold — is invented for the picture and must not be quoted back as a fact about the mod. The one exception is the `WorkPolicy` names, which are copied from the enum on purpose.
