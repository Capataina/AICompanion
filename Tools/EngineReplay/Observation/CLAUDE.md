# Observation fixtures — what the brain recorded, and what it cost

The recorder, the sparse event writer, the diagnostic transport, the course that now owns the tick, the shared comparison it replaced, and the two instruments that measure rather than assert. `--observation`, `--travel-episodes`, `--evidence-scenes` and `--brain-cost` select subsets, and `--retained-course-recording` reaches the transport rows; every one of those rows is also named individually in `../Movement/VerifyEngineMotion.cs`'s default table, so the ledger sees a failing transport contract during whole-project verification rather than only when somebody remembers the flag.

**Two fixtures here test a brain that is no longer on the tick, and they are kept deliberately.** `VerifyPreparedActivities` and `VerifyFamilyOffers` exercise the family chooser's shared comparison and its nomination contract. Since `0bb2c8a` the live tick asks a course instead; the chooser is still compiled and still correct, and it survives until the course brain has been played, so that a defect found in play can be attributed to one brain or the other. Both pass. Read them as a contract about a component rather than as a description of what the companion does.

## One AI update writes exactly one row

`CompanionNPC.AI` writes its own telemetry row; calling `Record` again duplicates that tick and hides the cardinality of actual decisions. Fixture assertions address columns **by name** and require one complete row per invoked AI update — the schema appends columns at the end, so a reader that names its columns survives a bump, but only if it names ones that exist.

Each lifecycle update also requires one motor application and one advancing control grant, including ticks that skip the chooser, and compares the grant against the motor's actual owner and controls rather than trusting the grant's own count. Recorder rows must retain fresh grants during recovery and downing, with an available hand during flight and an unavailable one while downed.

`VerifyObservationLifecycle` walks that whole lifetime: reserved retry names, zero-tick metadata, the recording switch, inspector geometry, the notch's opening press, one complete sample, an ended ore job, recovery not refreshing the choice, and liquid under a body with no ordinary offer on the board.

**It was red from the tick switch until `b595fbd` and nine assertions stood between it and green, each hidden behind the one in front of it — which is the normal shape of progress in an abort-on-first-failure fixture, not nine new defects.** Three of them are worth carrying because the reasoning generalises. *The empty board.* The scene emptied it by killing the player, which worked under the family chooser because a dead player zeroed every score; the course keeps working through a death on purpose, so that lever left mining on the board. It disables the work now, which is the thing that actually empties it, and the player still dies there because that is the other fact the tick is about. Emptying the *registration* instead is not available: the row writer emits one `<activity>_offer` column per registered activity and the header is written once at world load, so clearing the list mid-session writes a row narrower than its own header. The assertion was inverted rather than deleted — an empty board is companionship, by `ExecuteCourseBinding`'s contract that "the course found nothing worth doing" must not look identical to "the course told the body to freeze" — so what is asserted is the half that did not change. *`phase=None` is gone from the sequence because the state is gone*, not because the recorder stopped writing it: keeping company is registered in every production session, so under the course there is always an activity. What the row still proves is that execution is distinguished from each suspension, each names its cause, and a resumption is its own record. *Attempt outcomes are counted by interruption, not by total* — exactly two interrupted, the recovery and the downing — because a spurious interruption and a genuine extra activity both move a total and only one is a defect. One real brain defect came out from behind them: `ExecuteCourseBinding` built a tile-work request from `binding.Pose`, which is where the body goes, so the recorded work tile was whatever air the body hovered in.

The liquid scene removes every ordinary candidate and pours water under the body: the liquid must be read and recorded, the body must lose no life, nothing may take it or suspend anything, the ordinary tick must grant once and publish a comparison only as a new identity, the recorder must carry no escape or hurting column, and downing must still take the body and the hand. Until 15 September 2026 it required the second tick to hand the body to an environmental escape with its own identity and no fresh comparison; every liquid became air to the orb that day, so it asks the opposite of the same scene.

**Two walker columns are gone from its list and their absence is deliberate.** `head_submerged` was Terraria's drowning rectangle over a forty-two-pixel body; this body has no head, and what the recorder carries instead is `liquid`, which liquid the circle touches; the orb's `hurting` column that stood beside it went in schema 0.36.0, when nothing hurt it any more. `observed_left`, `observed_bottom` and `npc_width` were the walker's body box, reconstructed by readers into a feet point; there is one body and one contact, a circle about a centre, so the recorder writes that centre as `npc_px` and SessionReport's own reader asserts the old names are gone.

**The gravity trio was deleted rather than loosened.** It set `NPC.gravity` by reflection and a different model gravity beside it, then required the recorded event to carry both values and the tick they were read on — which proved the recorder read the engine's side and the model's side of the *same* tick instead of writing one of them twice. That worked because the two numbers could differ. This body has no gravity on either side: the engine only adds its velocity, and the recorder writes `gravity-observation-tick=-1;engine-gravity=0;model-gravity=0;gravity-enabled=False` as fixed text to keep the columns a reader names. Asserting a constant against itself is a row that cannot fail, which is worse than no row. What replaces it is the one-row-per-tick property the surrounding fixture exists for — exactly one `navigation-state` event per AI update — and it is worth saying plainly that **the two-sided read itself is no longer covered anywhere**, so the day this body gains a per-tick engine quantity worth capturing, this is the row that should have caught a duplicated read.

## The producer half of SessionReport's rules

`VerifyAttemptEvidenceProducers` proves the real brain, recorder and event writer write what SessionReport's self-test captures assume. Its mining scene starts the body outside reach of one copper tile, drives the whole brain until the tile breaks natively, runs thirty more ticks so the next comparison concludes the attempt, then reads the session back by column name: every strike must name a non-zero attempt that its tick's row holds open or closed on that tick, and the outcome for that attempt must contain the strike and name its activity. Planting the activity id where the attempt id belongs makes the scene fail on the first strike, so it reads the identity rather than a coincidentally equal counter.

Its collection scene lays ore along the floor, drives the brain with pot breaking off until the drop leaves the world through the real contact pickup, and requires the pickups naming a collection attempt to be of that ore and name one attempt, whose outcome is a companion completion claiming exactly the quantity those pickups delivered. It then moves the player and drives reunion so a follow destination's region is written.

Both captures are read for their success regions: every tool-reach row names the scene's ore and a stand inside its own reach box, every recorded arrival verdict equals the geometry recomputed from its own row, and a changed destination tile always carries a new destination revision. **The body point that geometry is judged against is the orb's centre, read from `npc_px`** — the one column that carries it, and the point the navigator steers and the intent region measures.

Neither scene produces a claimed arrival inside a declared region, and for the same reason each time: a tool activity holds as soon as it can swing, and reunion hands over to keeping company's accompanying motion as the body enters the player's region, both before the navigator reports arrival. So the arrival verdicts are proven only by SessionReport's own fixtures; in the captures the idle company walk that does end in `Arrived` is an exact request declaring no region, and in the tree today a body inside the region is accompanying him, which closes its journey as reached without the navigator reporting arrival at all.

**Its collection scene drives one tick before it turns pot breaking off, and the order is the assertion rather than a detail.** Since schema 0.45.0 the `# config=` preamble is written from the first recorded row rather than from world entry, because the character's preferences load between the two; so a preference changed *before* that first row is part of the session's baseline and writes no `configuration` occurrence, while one changed after it is a mid-session change and writes one. The scene is about the second, so it takes a tick first. `VerifyCaptureHonesty` is about the first, and holds the header against a preference set in exactly that window — with the mutation being the producer's own previous shape, which reproduces the 22 September capture's signature verbatim: `# config=…;chopping=Opportunistic;…` on a session that ran Mimic.

**`TaskOrderReadsThePublishedCourse` is called from here with `requireBoundStep: true`, and only from here.** Every other scene in this folder can satisfy a consistency rule over the order columns with every row a dash, which is precisely what the retired chooser's columns wrote for a day; the mining scene binds a course, so it is the one entitled to require the column to hold something.

Both captures must also name a forty-digit source revision and a clean or dirty tree, a configuration snapshot carrying the preferences and switches, and ordered closure records: the producer's closing counts, the writer's diagnostic totals, and the terminal end marker. Offered occurrences must equal the sidecar's rows and the writer's actual completed count, with none dropped, no terrain chunk evicted and no incomplete or failed diagnostic closure. The collection scene turns pot breaking off after the world loads and must record exactly one configuration occurrence carrying the new value; the mining scene none.

## The event writer, the comparison and the nominations

`VerifyGodsEyeEvents` compiles the actual sparse event writer and its native NPC, projectile and terrain hooks with only test-local stubs, writes and parses a temporary JSONL session, then deletes it. It proves sequence, schema and timestamp validity, normal session closure, snapshot-at-occurrence behaviour, reused NPC and projectile slot generations, shot-to-terrain correlation through the first projectile generation, and tile dirtiness becoming a changed local terrain snapshot only after post-update sees the engine edit. Its terrain fixture includes a slope and water and requires the recorded chunk to retain glyph, liquid and material fields.

`VerifyPreparedActivities` exercises the production shared comparison on repeated and reordered immutable inputs, invalid numeric values, overflow, an unlimited threat horizon and a seeded reference of the preceding chooser arithmetic. It proves numeric preservation for those boards, not correct opportunity discovery. Its reunion rows read the one separation cost a job pays — one minus keeping company's rejoin slope, before its cap, at the job's stand — on an excursion and on a job done from where the orb hovers alike, and the surface-zombie scene builds that stand's region through `PlayerIntentRegion.Around` and `Chooser.SeparationAt` rather than typing a factor, so a change to the region's shape or the slope reaches the row.

`VerifyFamilyOffers` is the named selection contract: a matched flat reference, an empty family, a deferred child and absent offers. The family-allowance rows register two gathering probes, a winning combat probe and a non-excursion probe, set the chooser's share to zero and compare three times, requiring exactly one gathering child prepared per comparison with the other reported `Deferred` at zero value, the next comparison to start from the deferred sibling, the incumbent and non-excursion probes to prepare every time, and a deferred sibling's retained higher value to lose.

Offer and attempt contracts are exercised at three depths: a pure fixture requiring a positive value beside a no-opportunity, policy-forbidden or known-unusable offer to be rejected, a zero-valued one to stay a truthful absence and an unresolved one to keep bounded value; a probe activity driving the real activity owner through selection without execution, reselection, suspension and repeated suspension, resumption, replacement and an empty selection, requiring distinct attempt identities and interruption that never consults the activity; and the native suites' own attempt conclusions.

## The course owns the tick, and the row that proves it is not the row that would have caught the cost

`VerifyTheCourseOwnsTheTick` is the fixture the tick switch landed with. `TheLegacyChooserIsOffThePath` proves the family chooser no longer decides, and **it could never have caught what the switch actually broke**, because it only ever looked at the chooser's return value. `Chooser.Choose` did two jobs and only one of them was choosing: its first thirty lines observed companionship — the estimated return time, the delay cost, the departure reading and the regroup urgency — so taking `Choose` off the tick left all four stranded at their defaults for a day while the apart-tick count, reached directly, kept climbing beside them. Nothing went red; a frozen float is a legal float. The row added for it walks the player away for forty ticks while the body is held and requires the return estimate, the regroup urgency and the delay cost to respond. It is proved by mutation rather than asserted, restoring the pre-fix wiring reddens it with the same message, and its first draft was wrong in an instructive way: it asserted a delay cost that a standing player and a body that flies home legitimately leave at zero, so the separation has to be *sustained* and the player has to be the one opening it, which is also the case the number exists to price. **The general form, for any fixture written against a replaced decision: ask what the old procedure wrote, not only what it returned.**

## The tripwires are pairs, and the quiet half is the half that matters

`VerifyDecisionTripwires` holds the six contracts `AuditDecisionContracts` audits every published decision against, and **every row is a pair**: a scene that breaks the contract and the neighbouring scene that does not. A tripwire that fires on its own scene has proved only that it can fire; what a reader of a capture needs is that it stays quiet on the legal case next door, because a violation kind that appears on every decision is one nobody reads twice. So the empty-course rule meets a decision whose refusals include a third, legitimate reason; the overrun rule is run at exactly its ceiling; the size rule at exactly its bound; the release rule four ticks after its publication rather than one; and the activity-exit rule on a *settled* decision that changes activity, which is the brain choosing.

The scenes carry the 22 September 2026 capture's own numbers wherever one exists — combat admitted three usable against twelve `target-capture-missing`, collection four against sixteen `assistance-target-unresolved` — so a red is read against a recording somebody has already looked at rather than against invented arithmetic. Two rows are about the instrument rather than a contract: one drives fifty ticks of one frozen observation and requires the contradiction to be named once rather than fifty times, because the census and the refusal tally persist while a course is carried; and one drives three hundred contradicting decisions through the real writer, reads the sidecar back, and requires the records to be a handful rather than three hundred *and* the last one to carry the full count. That second row is what found the closing-flush gap — coalesced totals stop at the last written record, so a session of three hundred closed at a recorded 241 until the recorder's own close learned to flush.

**The row that will catch a silent break is the producer pin**, and it exists because the contracts read two refusal strings out of two files this project does not compile: `target-capture-missing` from `Activities/Combat/CombatCourseOpportunity.cs` and `assistance-target-unresolved` from `Selection/Opportunities/BindAssistanceOpportunity.cs`. A rename there makes every contract above unable to fire while every other row here still passes, which is this folder's own trap about a diagnostic reading a producer that moved, so the literals are read back out of those two files by path.

**`VerifyFrameLedger` is the producer half of the frame columns, and every row in it asserts a relationship rather than a duration.** A fixture that slept twenty milliseconds and required an interval near twenty would be a wall-clock threshold judged in a regime no row declares, which is the shape two of this suite's flaking rows already have. What it asserts instead cannot depend on the machine: an interval with no predecessor reads as unmeasured rather than as the fastest frame in the file; the draws counted are the draws that happened and reset with the interval; an update the engine caught up on carries no draw while still carrying a measured interval, which is the distinction the whole column set exists for; and the remainder is exactly the interval less the four parts subtracted from it, with an unrecorded brain or record cost left out rather than subtracted as a NaN.

## The player is inside his own region

`VerifyRetainedCourseObservation` is the game-free contract for retained-course observation facts. It proves continuation membership is the union of admission and the player-centred resting rectangle, so a bounding rectangle cannot silently add diagonal places. **Its residual-coverage and player-motion-episode rows went on 22 September 2026 with the two classes they were the only caller of** — neither `ObserveForecastErrors` nor `ObservePlayerMotionEvidence` had a production caller anywhere, so both rows were proving that a component the game never runs behaves as written, which is coverage that cannot go red for a reason anybody plays. The properties are in `Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Observation/CLAUDE.md`'s planned work, to be re-asserted against a consumer when the plan's step for them is reached. Its capability rows require slot replacement, slot swapping, in-place tool/weapon mutation, cargo occupancy and public-policy changes to produce immutable current facts and only the appropriate revision; a repeated identical observation must not churn, and reset must make the next epoch distinct. Run it with `dotnet run --project Tools/EngineReplay -p:UseAppHost=false -- --retained-course-observation`.

`VerifyIntentRegionHoldsThePlayer` holds the owner's ruling on the region's shape in two halves that report together, because a clamp mutation reddens the geometry first and an abort on the first failure would hide whether the replay still holds. The geometry half runs on all three distance modes — the Close box is too short for the player to sit at its bottom third by the slack, and a Standard-only row would pass a placement that fails there — and writes the expected base as the follow comfort from the comfort constants rather than reading the base-scale constant, so a change to that constant is a red row. The replay half drives the committed player track of the 14:16 capture of 15 September 2026 through the real activity inference and the real lead filter, with no game world, since that player stands thousands of tiles from anything a headless tile map holds; the pass line is zero rows outside, where the recorded region had 5,226. **The replay cannot see where inside the box the player sits**, because containment holds wherever he is placed; that is what the geometry rows are for, and the mutation that put him at the centre left the replay green while all three placement rows went red. Beside those, a held direction with no displacement still slides the box to the third-line or the floor, a one-tile drop with nothing held is a nudge rather than a slam, and a long fall with nothing held still fills the clamp.

## Gate G10 has one mechanical half here and the rest of it is named as absent

`VerifySharedSpatialFacts` asserts that no file under `Companion/Brain/Activities/` or `Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Interactions/` builds a player-intent region, a clearance field, a reach flood or a light field of its own — they read `ctx.Senses`. It sweeps the source by path rather than driving a scene, for the reason `check-navigation-boundary.sh` already sweeps source for route searches: "nobody recomputes this privately" is a statement about every call site, and a behavioural fixture exercises only the call sites it happens to reach, so the seventh domain added next month is exactly the one a scene would miss. `VerifyDecisionTripwires` already reads two refusal literals back out of their producers by path, so this is the suite's own existing device rather than a new one.

**Its own first version got the thing it is about wrong, and the premise that now stops it is the finding.** A file in this tree is named for what it does and its types are named for what they are, so the senses are declared `ReachSense`, `LightSense` and `PlayerIntentRegionSense` in files called `ObserveReach.cs`, `ObserveLight.cs` and `ObservePlayerIntentRegion.cs`. Three of the six refused patterns were spelled from the *filename*, matched nothing anywhere in the tree, and made half the row a check that could not fail — the same shape as the audit finding the commit one before it landed, where `CaptureDecisionSnapshot` the file was grepped for and `CourseDecisionSnapshot` the type was missed. The row now asserts, before it sweeps, that every refused type name is declared somewhere under `Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/`; a rename therefore reddens it instead of quietly switching that clause off. Restoring `new ObserveReach(` reddens the premise with `'ObserveReach' is refused below and is declared nowhere under Companion/Brain/Infrastructure`.

Three further properties of how it is written are each there because the opposite has failed somewhere in this tree. It is a **refusal** rather than a presence test, because requiring a domain to *name* the sense is satisfied by a file that names it once and computes its own answer beside it. It **counts the files it swept and refuses a sweep that read too few**, because a wrong root or a moved folder leaves it reporting exactly what a held boundary reports — the failure the navigation script met on a machine without ripgrep. And it **finds its own repository root by walking up for `Companion/Brain`**, because the working directory differs between `run-case.sh`, `verify.sh` and a hand run, and a fixed number of parents is a silent skip on two of the three.

**The gate is not covered, this row does not claim to be it, and both of the plan's G10 mutations survive it.** A sentinel planted each and the row stayed green: *remove a destination consumer* — replacing `FindToolAccess.Approach(tile, …, context.Senses.Reach, out pose)` in the ore census with a hard `Reach.Yes` and a computed pose — and *apply the clearance cost twice*, squaring the factor in `CornerGraph.EdgeCost`. The navigation boundary stayed green under both too. That is the honest measure of the distance between this row and the gate: the row refuses a domain from *building* a shared fact and says nothing about a domain that stops *reading* one, which is what a destination consumer being removed looks like.

G10 asks that all six domains inspect the same clearance, intent and capability facts at discovery, binding, destination and use — a four-by-six enumeration — plus surface and cave hover and two-by-two horizontal, vertical and diagonal passage controls. What exists beside this row: clearance is enforced negatively by `check-navigation-boundary.sh`'s reach boundary; hover is `../../NavReplay/VerifyAccompanyStaysAboveHisHead` and `../Assistance/VerifyTheParkIsAboutThePlayer`; passage widths are `--free-space`'s corridor rows. What does not exist is the enumeration itself, and the honest reason is that it is a piece of work rather than a row — it has to name, per domain and per stage, which fact is read and where, and nothing in the tree records that today. A row that caught "remove a destination consumer" would have to read that enumeration; nothing shorter can.

**What the fixture buys over the shell script is a ledger row, and it is worth being exact about that** because the script was the cheaper home for the sweep itself. `check-navigation-boundary.sh`'s reach boundary already sweeps the same three trees, already refuses a missing directory and a missing search tool, and already names `ClearanceField\.`; six more alternations in its `reach_pattern` would have carried the constructions. What it cannot do is file a row: a boundary-script failure exits non-zero and the scoreboard never sees it, so it cannot be selected by `--case`, rerun by `--rerun-red`, or noticed when it stops running. The cost of the fixture is a third home for "which trees are consumers", and `ConsumerTrees` carries the warning at its declaration.

## Journeys

`VerifyTravelEpisodes` drives the whole brain into a follow journey, kills the body inside it, waits out the self-revival and reads the recorded journey back. **It counts the downed ticks itself while driving them**, so the producer's figure is checked against an independent count of the same quantity rather than against its own arithmetic, and it asserts that the reported duration and mean speed exclude them.

The death has to land inside the journey, and the fixture says so as a premise failure rather than passing: a journey is found by the downing it holds, not by comparing tick stamps against the engine counter, because the recorder rebases its own ticks on the world load. Exactly one downing is driven, so exactly one journey may report one — and on a producer that charges the death to travel, none reports one at all. The unload is what closes the open episode, so a fixture reading the sidecar without unloading finds the journey it cares about still open.

This fixture is unusually sensitive to reunion valuation, and that is a feature: a change that makes the body reach the player sooner moves the tick the journey closes on, and a death after that tick measures nothing. It caught exactly that during the body change, and a candidate change to the reunion charge was withdrawn because of it — `Gathering/CLAUDE.md` carries that episode.

## The instruments

`--brain-cost` runs one seeded full-brain scenario — a flat copper vein, then a player walking away and back — for 600 ticks with native collision advancing the body. A discarded warm-up with recording on pays JIT compilation for both paths. Two measured runs under the production allowances report every brain phase, and the AI time the brain did not account for, as p50/p95/max/mean with the three costliest ticks named. With the allowances lifted it runs twice with recording off and once on, and **exits 1 unless all three agree tick for tick** on activity, request, applied controls, grant owner, hand grant and body position. The first repeat is the determinism control; only then does the recording comparison prove recording changes no decision. It also samples the recorder's own `record_ms` as a separate phase, so the outside-brain figure splits into recording and everything else.

Why the invariance runs lift the allowances: under production allowances the warm-up and the first measured run once diverged at tick 40 with identical prior traces, one run still travelling and the other already swinging from the same position, because the planning deadline decided how far a search got.

`--evidence-scenes[=dir]` is an instrument rather than a suite: it asserts nothing about behaviour and **keeps** the captures it writes. It drives four stalls, each already built by an existing fixture, through the whole brain, the real recorder and the real event writer for twenty seconds apiece, one per family's path — a drowned hop take-off (gathering), a drop sealed inside a dirt box (collection), a zombie sealed in rock (combat), the player sealed in a box (following). For each it prints the capture path, the row and occurrence counts, the actions recorded and the attempt outcomes, so `dotnet run --project Tools/SessionReport -- <capture>` can be run on each and asked which contract it names. The save path is redirected before anything touches `Terraria.Main`, whose static constructor combines paths from it. A scene that throws still closes its session and makes the run exit non-zero. **The scenes are fixed rather than tuned: a report naming nothing for one of them is a finding about the report or the brain, not about the scene.**

## Traps

- **The recorder's seeded travel scenario must advance the native tool-contact world clock.** Advancing `GameUpdateCount` alone does not age `TileDamageWatcher`, whose owner is `TileDamageClock.PostUpdateEverything`. Without those callbacks a previous fixture's ore contact persists through every travel sample and correctly lowers intent confidence — so the combined suite fails while the isolated recorder test passes. Preserve the world lifecycle in the fixture rather than weakening the production interpretation of work.
- **A fact the motor publishes is published at the end of a tick.** The liquid scene pours its liquid *before* the first recorded tick, because `ReadLiquid` runs inside `Commit` and decides the liquid from the circle's own contact, then writes `npc.wet` itself — so a hand-set `wet` is both ignored and overwritten, and asserting on the very next tick asks the brain about a fact no `Commit` has published yet. The first recorded tick discovers the liquid, the second is the first that could act on it, and the third is the downing.
- **Fixtures that close the recorder by reflection pass `fixture-close` as its closure reason**, so a capture's trailer says which it was.
- **`VerifyCompanionLifecycle.Create` starts with a dead player** to exercise autonomous lifecycle handling; a fixture needing ordinary companionship must make that player alive before its first brain update.
