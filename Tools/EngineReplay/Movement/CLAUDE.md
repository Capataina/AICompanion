# Movement fixtures

Native NPC collision, portable route persistence, follow, recovery, escape, round-trip evidence and projectile motion. `VerifyEngineMotion.cs` is the default entry that then calls the other groups.

## Door passage

`VerifyDoorPassage` (`--doors`, in the default run) is A25 on native tiles. It holds `WorldGen.OpenDoor`'s swing matrix (open both sides swings east, east blocked swings west, both blocked and locked Lihzahrd style refuse). It asks the live walker across a closed door (no), opens the door through the real `DoorOpener` for a body walking into it, and asks again with the edge cache clock unmoved (yes), then closes it behind the body (no) — this check fails when the door interaction stops announcing its terrain change. Through the whole brain, a door that is the only passage must be opened and walked through; a locked door must never open and must be pressed against no longer than a stone tile in the same doorway; a locked door beside a passage under the wall must be routed around. A fifth scene with an openable door beside that passage is printed as MEASURE and not asserted: the planner treats a closed door as a wall, so the companion takes the passage. Every type the live brain reads is imported through the `live` alias, and native `WorldGen.CloseDoor` walks every player slot in `Collision.EmptyTile`.

## Movement failures

`VerifyMovementFailures` (`--movement-failures`, in the default run) is P07's held-goal contract on native collision: a goal the body can reach is delivered, and one it cannot is refused with the class `Navigator.LastFailure` should name. It drives `CoordinateMovement` directly rather than the brain, lifts the wall-clock allowances, and rebuilds terrain and route memory per case. One corridor is searched with the allowance lifted and with a deadline that permits one unit of work per tick: a reachable goal must be delivered either way and read unfinished, while a sealed twin must end absent only once its search is exhausted. The same starved corridor with a distant tile changed and announced every twenty ticks must still strike twice when sealed, but still deliver when open. Sixty-three ledge and lip entries at four columns up, three sub-tile offsets and three horizontal velocities must all arrive. A body reaching the shelf faster than it walks, whose drop is refused and rescued by a prefix that sheds speed, must take no strike and record no rejected entry and still be delivered; that case starves the preparation by work rather than by the clock, holding `PlanLocalMovement.PrepareWorkTicks` at 1 so the refusal cannot depend on how busy the frame was, and restoring it to whatever it read on entry rather than to a literal. A body pinned mid-walk by a position write must read native mismatch and retire the remembered connection, while attribution to an external hit must read pre-emption, fail nothing and keep the connection. A full-height wall placed with no terrain callback must be refused at entry by actual-state proof; the same wall announced must make the goal absent.

## Round-trip evidence

`VerifyRoundTripEvidence` (`--round-trip`, in the default run) holds the shared round-trip query's verdicts against what the native body does on the same tiles. A ten-row drop into a walled pit must read outward yes and return no, and the native body driven into it must end absent without arriving; a two-row drop must read return yes and the body must walk back. The deep pit asked with a one-expansion budget must answer unknown both ways. A flooded basin with a submerged staircase out must cost no breath for a dry trip at any reserve, fit a full bar for its far end, and be refused with a reserve that covers outward submersion but not return's. The movement-failure corridor flooded to its floor must read yes both ways with every step charged as submerged, and the native body walked out and back must spend no more head-underwater ticks than charged.

## Projectile motion

The same executable verifies every projectile in the current companion kit against native `Projectile.VanillaAI` for a bounded free-flight run. It compares phase, gravity, drag, terminal velocity and default hitbox, then checks that a swept trace rejects a thin blocking tile, reopens when that tile is removed, and rejects an accuracy-rotated launch that no longer reaches its target. This establishes the solver's supported projectile profiles and collision sampling; it does not prove every possible modded projectile or a live combat playtest.

## Observed motion

`VerifyObservedMotion` runs the shared target forecast against the same initialised Terraria tile map. It checks a stationary grounded hostile remains supported; a tile-colliding flyer stops at a wall while a phaser crosses it; observed acceleration changes the short forecast; a jump is not extrapolated as repeated impulse; `Forget` removes a reused NPC slot's old track; and a position correction during the same engine tick replaces an already-built forecast. It compares dry custom gravity/fall cap and wet custom movement slowdown against native `UpdateCollision` exactly, then bounds the remaining slope/step-policy difference while requiring non-zero current gravity to travel through `SlopeCollision`. It also keeps the pure regroup pressure monotonic as separation, return time, player movement away and stalled travel grow, while a nearby stationary companion remains calm.

## Keeping company and local motion

`VerifyCompanyLocalMotion` (in `--follow` and the default run) runs the whole brain on native collision with the planning allowances lifted. The hazard case removes every activity but keeping company and puts three hazards inside the calm band of a standing player: a lava pool two rows deep, a water pit three rows deep whose floor puts standing head underwater, and a pit twelve rows deep with a way down and none back. Over six thousand seeded ticks no held goal, no resolved destination and no body pose may lie over or beside a hazard; no resolved destination may be outside the returnable region; nothing may be recorded as productive work; at least three goals must be strolled to. The idle case keeps every activity registered in an empty world with light unmeasured, requires keeping company to be the only activity chosen, and over three game minutes allows no jump start, a rest share between a quarter and three quarters and more than one goal. The same envelope is asserted on a floor of full blocks rising one row per column; the scene asserts that every column near the player has a standable, supported tile and a neighbour with no floor on that tile's row. The rim case removes every activity but keeping company and stands both actors on a slab with open air twelve rows down on both sides and no liquid. Over six thousand ticks no held goal or resolved destination may be a slab end, over the edge or below the slab; the body must stay on the slab; more than one goal must be strolled to. The empty-world reunion case keeps every activity registered, walks the player forty tiles along the floor and stops them, and requires keeping company to be the only activity chosen, a reunion request, and the body inside the follow objective at the stopped player. These establish local motion and reunion on those scenes, not comfortable spacing in real caves or behaviour under enemy pressure.

## Lifecycle updates

Each lifecycle update requires one motor application and one advancing control grant, including ticks that skip the chooser. The same assertion wraps full-brain captured escape, recovery and raised-lip mining updates. It compares the grant against the motor's actual owner and controls rather than only trusting the grant's own count. Recorder rows must retain fresh grants during recovery/downing, with an available hand during flight and an unavailable hand while downed.

Recovery admission is exercised with one alternate executor issuing reunion, exact-work, guard and hold requests at the same distant player position. Only reunion can start flight. This catches a concrete follow-class dependency and a coordinate-only shortcut independently of the native recovery flight/clearance checks.

## Raised-lip mining

The mining-baseline fixture (`--mining-baseline`) runs the same two-block lip at two altitudes in both orientations with normal decisions, mining as the only offered activity, and an initially native-clear working pose. Every case measures the actual ore tile disappearing and preserves the dirt obstruction. With `LimitPlanningWork.Unbounded` set. It also prints initial useful-pose routes and jump proposals, then invalidates the diagnostic caches before running the brain. Native altitude is part of a movement fixture: in the miniature world with worldSurface=50, a floor at tile row 60 receives reduced sky gravity; identical floor at row 90 receives ordinary gravity. Fixed ordinary-gravity hop impulses left the reduced-gravity approach cases without crossing edges, while supplied useful poses could mine. Environment-sized hop proposals repair that distinction; the default suite retains both altitudes.

## Traps

- **A fixture that starves a static field by writing it must restore what it found, not a literal.** `VerifyMovementFailures`'s spent-preparation case sets `PlanLocalMovement.PrepareWorkTicks` to 1 and used to put a hard-coded number back in its `finally`. Restoring a literal is the current default written twice: the day the real default moves, every fixture ordered after this one in the same process runs under the stale one, and a suite that already carries one unattributed flake gains a second failure nobody can attribute. Read the field's value before starving it and restore that.
