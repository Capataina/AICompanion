# Combat fixtures

Hunting, guarding, arsenal valuation, combined safety, encounter context, actor-specific danger, offer validity with destination retention, the handed gear, the item-backed weapon and the learned arcs. `VerifyEngineMotion` runs these in the default suite; `--combat-purpose`, `--combat-cost`, `--safety-layer` and `--offer-validity` select subsets. A subset flag reaches its fixture before the default suite's own setup has run, so a fixture that touches `Main` must set Terraria's save path itself or the static constructor throws on a null path.

The companion every fixture creates through `VerifyCompanionLifecycle.Create` holds a wooden bow and a throwing knife in its weapon slots and a copper pickaxe and axe in its tool slots. The pair is the two items the authored kit read, because the combat scenes were calibrated against that pair's speeds: a reposition priced at the edge of the arsenal's evaluation window is inside it for the knife and outside it for the bow alone. Two scenes deliberately hold less. The actor matrix's rows hold the bow alone, because a thrown knife lobs over a three-tile pillar onto a body five tiles away within the arsenal's trace — a correct answer, and not the one the matrix varies — and its guard pair's guarded life is sized so the walk's share of access plus removal is a clear margin under the bow's own damage rather than the authored bow's doubled one. A scene that needs a particular item in hand sets the gear itself; a scene that leaves the gear empty when it returns has changed what the next scene tests, because a fixture that reuses the player without creating a fresh companion inherits it.

## Handed gear, the item weapon, and learned arcs

`VerifyHandedGear` asks every one of five real items — a wooden bow, a copper broadsword, a wand of sparking, a copper pickaxe and a wood yoyo — of every gear slot against a written table, plus a flintlock pistol for the ammo default: the bow, sword, wand and pistol fit a weapon slot, the pickaxe only its own, the yoyo nowhere, each refusal carrying its reason. It round-trips the gear through save and load, loads a full 100-slot bag save into the 120-slot bag (every item in its own slot at its stack and the twenty new slots empty, with the loading bag's last twenty slots pre-filled so a load that clears only the old hundred shows; the items are built with `SetDefaults` because the headless content samples hold only the items the other rows read), and holds the pick power the slot reports against `Player.GetPickaxeDamage` through the same delegate the live miner binds: copper's power produces no damage on obsidian and gold's does, both damage stone, and no pickaxe is no power.

`VerifyItemWeapon` puts one item in a slot, stands a zombie where it can reach, and asks the arsenal to fire. The bow fires the wooden arrow's projectile, player-owned, at the bow's plus the arrow's speed and damage; the pistol the musket ball's projectile at the composed speed, flat at a level target; the wand spends its mana and lands its own damage at a full pool, and after the pool is drained and the reload waited out fires again at the empty factor; the sword strikes a zombie one tile away through the game's strike path with no projectile, and refuses one ten tiles away; a yoyo forced into a slot beside a bow leaves one enumerated weapon; and an empty gear says `no-weapon` with infinite removal time. The scene lays its own floor, because the per-case reset does not rebuild the tile map and a flat bullet once met a tile an earlier case had left in the air. The player's strike path reads the banner-buff table off `Main.SceneMetrics`, which the game constructs at start-up and this host does not, so the scene constructs an empty one. Enemy AI does not run and spawned projectiles are never advanced, so it establishes what leaves the muzzle and what a swing does on contact, not a fight. The `channel` plus overridden `ModItem.Shoot` refusal and the modded-ammo-class refusal are unverified: no modded item exists headless.

`VerifyArcLearning` holds the learner's two priors — the arrow-flagged style-1 projectile and the thrown-object style 2 — against native `Projectile.VanillaAI` for a bounded free-flight run, comparing phase, gravity, drag, terminal velocity and default hitbox; shows a bullet's prior straight; checks that a swept trace rejects a thin blocking tile, reopens when that tile is removed, and rejects an accuracy-rotated launch that no longer reaches its target; and takes the measure the learner is judged by, hits per shot at a zombie standing still in open air, solving with whatever the learner believes and flying the native projectile with the game's own AI. A vanilla arrow with its prior lands from the first shot at thirty tiles. For a type the learner is told flies straight — what any modded projectile starts as — hits per shot are taken before learning, then calibration shots are fired with the learner watching until it has a fit, then hits per shot are taken again with the learner frozen; the pass line, declared before the first run, is after above before and at least nine in ten, and the learned onset, gravity, drag and cap are held against the game's. The arrow is measured at thirty tiles rather than twenty because over twenty its drop is inside a zombie's half-height and the straight guess would hit; the knife at twenty. All five figures are ledger measures. Everything in it goes through the `live` alias, because this project compiles its own copy of the Aiming folder and a fixture that watched samples into one copy and solved from the other would prove nothing. It does not exercise the global projectile hooks, which no loader runs headless, nor a homing or steered modded projectile.

## Knockback

`VerifyKnockbackAwareness` (`--knockback`, in the default run) holds what the owner's playtest of 15 September 2026 asked for, when the companion kept knocking enemies into the player. Its prior row strikes a zombie through `Player.ApplyDamageToNPC` and requires the weapon-effects table's prior to predict the velocity the strike left exactly, in three arms chosen so each branch of the game's arithmetic is the only one that can pass it: a heavy hit, a light hit on a zombie with a great deal of life already moving the other way — where the resistance lands a second time — and a zombie that resists all knockback. A swing row requires one landed swing to file one push sample and one damage sample against the sword and the zombie, each a factor of one. A learning row feeds the table half-pushes and requires half the settled push for that pair and the prior for every other pair, a reversed push to turn the settled push round, and a crit and a heavy hit on an enemy already at the push's speed to file no push sample. An owner-side row holds the direction rule to the game's list.

The acceptance scene stands the player left of a zombie and asks about two mirrored muzzles, one on each side. Its zombie's resistance is set to full, because a wooden bow at the zombie's own resistance pushes about half as far and every number would sit closer to rounding; the prior row uses the zombie's own. The orb's half of the charge is weighed at the muzzle the forecast is asked about, and each muzzle's own shot pushes the zombie away from that muzzle, so the orb's half is zero at both and any difference between them is the player's. The shot from the far side must carry a charge and the shot from the player's side none, and the far shot must be worth less than itself with a learned push of zero. A stand row then holds the orb's half apart from the player's: the same leftward push asked about from the left muzzle, toward which it carries the zombie, must charge strictly more than from the right muzzle, away from which it carries it, because the player's half is the same push both times. Comparing the far shot with its own push-free self rather than with the near shot is deliberate: the aim sweep can land the mirrored arcs a tick apart, and a tick of timing moves the value more than an equality's tolerance. The position half requires `Positioner.FiringStandShare` — the stand's best learned attack over every weapon in hand, as a share of the best attack with no geometry in the way — to keep more on the player's side than on the far side, where the shot pays the push charge, and `ScoreSpot`, called by reflection with a line-of-fire request anchored on the zombie and each stand's share as its fire factor, to score the player's side strictly higher. Its control compares the far stand with itself under a learned push of zero, whose share must rise, for the same reason the shot row does: mirrored stands are never compared for equality. The row was restated when position selection stopped reading the last-chosen weapon's push; the side-share function it first held is gone. A last row holds the evaluator: a wound to a dangerous enemy outvalues the same wound to a harmless one by exactly the partial credit, a killing hit carrying a large push charge is worth the same as the clean kill, and a wounding hit's charge is its danger at the charge weight, discounted like the harm it would have prevented.

Every pass line was declared before the first run, and each row was proven by planting its mechanism's absence and watching that row go red: the push charge removed from the value, the firing-stand share held at one for every stand, the light branch's second resistance dropped, the swing's observation removed, the learned factor ignored in the settled push, the partial credit removed, a kill charged for its push, and the orb's half priced at the live body instead of the muzzle, which charged both stands identically. Enemy AI does not run and no projectile is advanced, so this proves the arithmetic and the decisions it feeds rather than a fight, and it does not exercise the projectile hook path, which no loader runs headless; that path's arithmetic is the method the swing row drives.

## Weapon learning

`VerifyWeaponLearning` (`--weapon-learning`, in the default run) holds the learner that values weapon, target, stand and aim by what the companion's shots achieved (`Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/WeaponKnowledge/CLAUDE.md`). The learner rows feed synthetic outcome streams from seeded generators, because the global projectile hooks that deliver real outcomes do not run headless; the arsenal rows put real items in the gear and ask the real arsenal with the learner's state taught or planted.

The aim row feeds an aim-blind weapon and an aim-sensitive one the same forty contexts and requires the first's aim coefficient near zero and the second's clearly negative. The hands row plants a posterior and fires: a positive aim coefficient must leave exactly the widest offset and a negative one exactly the intercept. The bow's own aim noise is set to nothing there, because the first version of the row compared against thresholds that overlapped across the noise and passed both arms at the same angle; the zombie floats far enough away that the widest offset misses its box, so that shot is fired only through the terrain-only clearance walk. The debuff row requires the burst weapon's learned factor against a debuffed zombie to exceed its factor undebuffed, then asks the arsenal to open with two bows in hand — the burst bow in the first slot, a weaker bow whose hits debuff in the second — and requires the burst bow with the debuff unlearned, which is the control, and the weaker bow with it learned. The attribution row constructs parent sources and holds the whole window: a child and a grandchild of the shot's projectile join its window, a projectile from an NPC or an unregistered projectile joins nothing, a reused slot is forgotten first, the window closes only on its last projectile, and a buff the hit added is read off the body. The swing row fires a sword and requires its window closed on its strike. The danger row plants a wide posterior that ranks the platinum bow below the wooden one, finds a seed whose draw explores to the platinum bow at no danger — the premise that exploration is live — and requires the wooden bow at the same seed above the danger ceiling. The stand row is AIC-395: the weak bow has learned it pushes nothing, the arsenal's last choice is the weak bow (made for a slime the strong bow barely hurts), and the stand where the strong bow's push goes away from the player must outscore the far stand; on the side-share proxy the two scored identically. The cost row times full rankings of eight zombies with both bows, untrained, trained and untrained again back to back, and requires the learner to add under a millisecond.

Six more rows in the same file are ledger cases of their own, run together by `--weapon-outcome-credit`, and each holds a defect the lane D review found. The enemy-type row teaches the bow three demon-eye outcomes in a flier's context and asks about a zombie it never shot, in two arms from one seed — the eye shots all missed, or all landed their forecast — so the arms hold the same evidence and draw the same numbers, and it requires the zombie's factor, its no-target count over two hundred rankings and its mean attack value to be no worse after misses. The swing-kill row swings at a slime with its ordinary life and at one with two life and requires both to record the strike's damage. It guards a property the swing already had rather than a fix: the review read the swing's own comment, which said a killing strike was capped at the life left, while the game's strike takes the whole damage off life with no floor, so a capped recording is what the row turns red. A kill cannot run through the player's strike headless, because that bookkeeping sends a client strike and reads loot tables and achievements, so the row substitutes `ItemWeapon.DeliverStrike` with the game's hit modifiers and `NPC.StrikeNPC` under the paused-client allowances `VerifyCompanionExperience` uses, and the swing's own reading of what the strike took runs unchanged. The charge row prices a far-side stand for an untrained and a half-landing bow against a zombie that ignores pushes and one that takes them, and requires the charge to scale by the learned factor; the danger gate is held shut there so the factor is the posterior mean. The shared-kill row drives the outcome windows directly with a forecast landing twenty ticks out and five ways the aimed body leaves. The hold row reads the evidence tick: ordinary drift must keep it, and a hostile appearing or leaving, a learner revision and a hundred-pixel jump must each renew it. The attribution row calls the real global spawn hook class directly and reads the experience system's striker test, so it holds the contract that system relies on rather than a copy of it.

## Combat purpose

`VerifyCombatPurpose` (`--combat-purpose`) holds P08's matched combat scenes. Every scene asserts who it threatens before reading an outcome. The consequence matrix holds one zombie between player and companion and varies one input per scene — player defence, difficulty, endurance, companion defence, or either actor's remaining life — requiring each to move only its own actor's urgency in the direction the game's damage arithmetic moves it. The baseline row recomputes the replaced raw-damage share from public facts and requires equality at zero defence and full health.

## Offer validity and destination retention

`VerifyOfferValidity` (`--offer-validity`, in the default run) holds the three-valued offer, the arrival-time shot and the partial-progress fixed point. Its cut row buries an enemy in rock so far more standable candidates surround it than a rescore may solve, and reads the reason after *every* resolve rather than every twelfth: with nothing held the rescore gate never fires, so each tick runs a whole scored pass and sampling one in twelve reads a pass by which the refusal memory has already absorbed the shortlist and the cut has happened unwatched. Measured on 2026-09-14, between three and seven undecided passes precede the proven absence over 29 scored candidates, and the row asserts the shape rather than that count: a warm suite floods further per resolve than a cold standalone run, so how many candidates each pass reaches — and therefore how many passes exhaust the shortlist — is a property of what ran before it. One undecided pass is presented to the chooser to confirm it carries a null destination with an undecided reason.

Its arrival row needs a scene where "where the target is" and "where it will be" differ across a wall, and that costs two flags. An ordinary walker's forecast applies Terraria's own tile collision, so it stops dead against the pillar and never reaches the far side; a phasing walker then falls through the floor it no longer collides with, because the forecast still applies its gravity, and ends up inside rock where nothing can shoot it. The enemy therefore both phases and flies. The scene asserts it is discriminating before it reads any result, and the row asserts the recorded evidence says `clear-arc` rather than `clear-arc-unforecast`, because a solve that fell back to the current position would otherwise pass the row while proving nothing about the forecast. The body starts a real walk from the stands that matter: standing on top of them makes every trip zero ticks, at which arrival and now are the same instant.

Two costs are worth knowing before adding a row here. Resolving a firing request repeatedly is bounded by a millisecond budget, so how deep one pass gets differs between a warm suite and a cold standalone run — a row that resolves a fixed number of times is machine-dependent, and the fix is to resolve until the search settles. And the motion track only accumulates error samples across consecutive observations, so a setup loop must end on an observation rather than on a position advance, or the forecast carries no measured confidence and every solve honestly falls back to the current position.

## Combat cost

`--combat-cost` runs one full-brain scenario with four stationary hostiles (full-health zombie, nearly dead zombie, slime and boss-flagged Eye) while the player stands, walks away and returns, under production millisecond allowances. It prices threat consequence, hunt target choice, protection, the arsenal's hand steps, the hit prediction and the evade layer but not work selection. Reports per-phase p50/p95/max/mean and the three costliest ticks with activity, request, evade reason, aim target and encounter source.

## Safety aftermath

`VerifySafetyIsALayerOnTheJob` (`--safety-layer`, in the default run; it was `VerifySafetyAftermath` while an escape still released the body after danger, and was renamed once nothing did) holds the scenes that make safety a layer on the job rather than a takeover, which is what the owner ruled on 15 September 2026. Its first scene is the reproduction of the first orb play's freeze: a damaging zombie beside the orb while the player walks forty tiles away, where the activity must never be suspended, the body must never sit still for the session reader's still run while he walks, and it must arrive after he stops. At the commit before the change that scene was red with spacing owning the feet and the activity suspended; it carries the takeover's signature and not the thirteen-second duration, because enemy AI does not run headless. The firing rows require a companion beside a zombie to keep firing its granted hand while nothing is suspended. The shot row puts a projectile on a guarding orb and requires a handful of ticks owned `evade` with the hand still available, the same guard identity throughout, and no hit. The intervening-hostile row requires guarding to pass a hostile between the body and the player without touching it. A pool scene and a surfacing row required an escape from water not to be vetoed into drowning by a shot above it; every liquid became air to the orb on 15 September 2026, there is no escape left to veto, and both went with it.

## Encounter context

`VerifyEncounterContext` saves and restores every world-event global it sets and runs fresh real `ThreatSense` and `EncounterSense` per row with the player's depth set by moving the surface line. Blood moon must be recognised on the surface and absent underground; Old One's Army and an observed Eye of Cthulhu at any depth. Invasion rows follow the spawner's gate; the pressure rows write the spawn cap the game would compute because the spawner cannot run headless. Every observation in the suite is its own engine tick because the pressure baseline is the admission ceiling.
