# EngineReplay — native NPC collision is the independent oracle

This console tool loads the installed tModLoader assembly without opening a game window. It compares SimulateTerrariaBody with Terraria’s own NPC collision wrapper; it does not compare two copies of the portable text-world simulator.

The occurrence fixture also instantiates the real player observer through ModPlayer.NewInstance and invokes OnHurt with surviving and fatal damage. It asserts the callback's pre-subtraction life and explicitly expected successor health. Player is a read-only view of the attached entity; assigning that property through reflection is not the native attachment lifecycle.

The real recorder fixture follows ordinary selection, an already active recovery, an empty post-recovery board, a fresh companionship activity and downing. It requires distinct brain/chooser freshness and stable comparison identity during skipped selection. An empty comparison ends ordinary ownership rather than inventing execution of a zero-value fallback. CompanionNPC.AI writes its own telemetry row; calling Record again duplicates that tick and can hide the cardinality of actual decisions. Fixture assertions address columns by name and require one complete row per invoked AI update.

Each of those lifecycle updates also requires one motor application and one advancing control grant, including ticks that skip the chooser. The same assertion wraps full-brain captured escape, recovery and raised-lip mining updates. It compares the grant against the motor's actual owner and controls rather than only trusting the grant's own count. Recorder rows must retain fresh grants during recovery/downing, with an available hand during flight and an unavailable hand while downed.

The native mining interruption fixture requires a productive pick strike, retained tool ownership during its next cooldown tick, then a projectile-only collision forecast. That forecast must suspend the same mining activity, release its tool phase and produce one avoidance grant without another mining effect. Enemy absence is asserted so ordinary combat selection cannot accidentally satisfy the test.

Recovery admission is exercised with one alternate executor issuing reunion, exact-work, guard and hold requests at the same distant player position. Only reunion can start flight. This catches a concrete follow-class dependency and a coordinate-only shortcut independently of the native recovery flight/clearance checks.

Guard preparation tests replace the observation's most urgent enemy after scoring. The prepared identity, score and requested anchor must remain bound to the original enemy, including at entry. Removing that enemy must produce Hold at execution. These checks expose a score-to-execution substitution; they do not establish arrival at a useful firing pose or a native protective hit.

VerifyCompanionLifecycle.Create starts with a dead player to exercise autonomous lifecycle handling. A fixture requiring ordinary companionship must explicitly make that player alive before its first brain update. A last-registered zero-score fallback used to hide this precondition; an empty activity owner cannot be suspended merely because the fixture labels the next step recovery.

Native companion attachment is bidirectional: ModNPC.Entity points at the body and NPC.ModNPC points back at the behaviour. Assigning only Entity lets direct AI calls pass while native lethal damage bypasses the companion's CheckDead and enters vanilla NPC loot/death handling. A drowning-to-downing test must preserve both links rather than suppressing lethal strikes.

The empty-offer safety fixture removes every ordinary candidate, then exercises environmental exposure through the real brain and recorder. It requires an independent safety identity and fresh control grant without a fabricated ordinary comparison; downing must cancel that response and revoke the hand. The observation suite restores the mining policy it changed, so a later fixture does not inherit disabled work. Captured pool checks repeat with and without ordinary offers. Mirrored awning checks require sustained dry clearance and release of safety ownership, and reject a landing response reverting to an air-seeking phase under the same response identity.

The personal-danger fixture runs the real threat observer against sealed native-tile chambers. It checks both player/companion arrangements for a walker, a tile-colliding flyer, a wall-crossing phaser, and entry into an enemy's chamber before a cached reachability refresh. These isolate destination coupling; they do not measure live combat judgement or a modded hostile's own pathfinding skill.

Responsive-following checks drive PlayerSense around a repeated local path and require it not to report travel. The production intent estimator also receives matched movement with and without local-work evidence, brief reversal, sustained backtracking, pause and vertical movement. Duplicate timestamps cannot manufacture samples; observation gaps and position corrections clear the history. These checks establish those observation sequences, not knowledge of the player's future destination. Existing native full-brain following fixtures remain the separate integration check.

Keeping-company checks require one registered candidate instead of separate following and wandering candidates. A seeded local sequence must offer both rest and movement, a departing player must change that same activity to a reunion request without changing its identity, and sealed-pocket context must retain roaming. This exercises method selection without integrating local strolls through terrain; native following and recovery fixtures remain the physical checks.

The recorder's seeded travel scenario resets and advances the native tool-contact world clock. Advancing GameUpdateCount alone does not age TileDamageWatcher, whose owner is TileDamageClock.PostUpdateEverything. Without those callbacks, a previous fixture's ore contact persists through every travel sample and correctly lowers intent confidence; the combined suite then fails while the isolated recorder test passes. Preserve the world lifecycle in the fixture rather than weakening the production interpretation of work.

`dotnet run --project Tools/EngineReplay -- --brain-cost` runs one seeded full-brain scenario — a flat four-tile copper vein, then a player walking away along the floor and back — for 600 ticks with native collision advancing the body. A discarded warm-up with recording on pays JIT compilation for both paths. Two measured runs under the production millisecond allowances then report every brain phase, and the AI time the brain did not account for (which is where recording lands), as p50/p95/max/mean with the three costliest ticks named. With `LimitPlanningWork.Unbounded` set, it runs twice with recording off and once on, and exits 1 unless all three agree tick for tick on activity, request, applied controls, grant owner, hand grant and body position. The first repeat is the determinism control; only then does the recording comparison prove recording changes no decision. It is not part of the default run.

Why the invariance runs lift the allowances, measured on 2026-09-13: under production allowances the warm-up and the first measured run diverged at tick 40 with identical prior traces, one run still travelling and the other already swinging from the same position, because the planning deadline decided how far a search got. Lifted, 600 ticks matched across repeats and with recording on. The same run showed where cost concentrates on this machine and scene: brain p50 0.025 ms, p95 0.57 ms, max 12.2 ms. The maxima were mining approach recomputation in the decide phase at ticks 0, 115–116 and 160–161 (10–12 ms each, the whole 12 ms total allowance), and at tick 0 the cut-off search held the body for a tick where an unbounded search walked. Recording added about 0.03 ms per tick at the median, with flush spikes of 2–7 ms. These are one scene on one machine, not a live-world distribution. After tool approach queries began asking nearest poses first, the same scene measured decide max about 4.2 ms (from 11.9–12.5) and gathering preparation max about 4.1 ms; the tick 115–116 and 160–161 spikes are gone, and the first allowance-changed decision moved from tick 0 to tick 39. Tick 0 remains the costliest tick at 10–12 ms of whole AI, now spread across decide, position and navigate doing their first work in one tick, rather than one approach query spending the allowance.

`dotnet run --project Tools/EngineReplay -- --combat-cost` exists because the brain-cost scene has no hostile in it, so nothing threat consequence, hunt target choice, protection, the arsenal's solves in the hands step or shared safety spend ever reaches its numbers. It runs the same full brain on the same copper floor with four stationary hostiles — a full-health zombie in weapon reach, a nearly dead zombie behind the companion, a slime and a boss-flagged Eye above the floor — while the player stands, walks away and returns, discards one run for JIT and reports per-phase p50/p95/max/mean, the three costliest ticks with activity, request, safety kind, aim target and encounter source, and ticks per activity and per encounter source. Because the Eye carries the game's boss flag, the whole scene is an observed boss encounter, so optional mining is suppressed by design and the scene prices guarding and hunting rather than work; a cost comparison across the commit that introduced encounter context compares different activity mixes. Enemy AI does not run and spawned projectiles are never advanced, so it prices queries against one arrangement rather than a fight, and it asserts nothing. It is not part of the default run.

It requires both reachable and unreachable approaches among them, so an equivalence that only ever compared failures cannot pass.

The family-allowance fixture registers two gathering probes, a winning combat probe and a non-excursion probe, sets the chooser's share to zero and compares three times. It requires exactly one gathering child prepared per comparison with the other reported Deferred at zero value, the next comparison to start from the deferred sibling, the incumbent and non-excursion probes to prepare every time, and a deferred sibling's retained higher value to lose. Offer and attempt contracts are exercised at three depths. The pure fixture requires a positive value beside a no-opportunity, policy-forbidden or known-unusable offer to be rejected, a zero-valued one to stay a truthful absence, and an unresolved one to keep bounded value. A probe activity then drives the real activity owner through selection without execution, reselection of the executing purpose, suspension and repeated suspension, resumption, replacement and an empty selection, requiring distinct attempt identities, interruption that never consults the activity, and conclusions that come from the replaced activity. The probe also counts `BeginAttempt` calls (one per opened attempt, none on reselection or suspension) and requires a second owner's first attempt identity to exceed every identity the first owner issued. The native ore suite asserts disabled mining is a policy prohibition and the same ore enabled is usable, that a transformed deposit concludes invalid, that a single-tile vein cleared by the companion's own strikes is a companion completion while the same vein cleared externally is invalid, and that full-brain mining interrupted by a projectile closes as Interrupted carrying its credited strike. Two further native checks run on one engine tick throughout: a first job cleared externally concludes its attempt invalid, and the attempt opened afterwards discovers a second job and must conclude attempted rather than inherit the first clear; and a two-tile vein whose one tile the companion removes and the player takes the other must conclude complete and shared. The guard fixture opens an attempt after an earlier release and requires it not to conclude from that release, then removes the committed threat while the player lives and requires an unattributed completion. The sealed hunt fixture requires a known-unusable classification with the no-firing-position reason, and the real recorder fixture requires exactly two attempt-outcome occurrences — recovery and downing interruptions — with no invented attempt for the empty board between them. Every test-local activity that scores positive must classify its offer, as production activities must.

`VerifyPreparedActivities.cs` exercises the production shared comparison on repeated and reordered immutable inputs, invalid numeric values, overflow, an unlimited threat horizon and a seeded valid-domain reference of the preceding chooser arithmetic. It proves numeric preservation for those prepared boards, not correct opportunity discovery or improved behaviour.

The actual chooser rejects an all-zero board without entering its final registered adapter. The recorder fixture checks that all three family nominations are serialised.

The native activity fixture also prepares an actual loot candidate, changes the live pickup list and item position, and verifies that repeated scoring retains the captured value, trip duration and target. A disappeared item must then be refused at execution. Same-type world-slot replacement leaves the old object's active flag intact deliberately: preparation and execution must reject the detached object, and activation must name the replacement while reconsidering the prepared board without rediscovery. A newly observed replacement remains selectable under its own identity. Item fixtures therefore register their objects in Main.item; an unattached object is not evidence of an available world drop. Collection fixtures prepare a native pot footprint as an uncertain contents opportunity with a nonzero forecast, introduce a valuable known drop at the feet and require the same activity to select it on the next preparation, then remove the drop and reconsider the pot. `dotnet run --project Tools/EngineReplay -- --mining-baseline` runs the same two-block lip at two altitudes, in both orientations with normal decisions, mining as the only offered activity, and an initially native-clear working pose. Every case measures the actual ore tile disappearing and preserves the dirt obstruction. Exit 1 means at least one case failed. The held-activity case changes only available activities, not the mining code; the fixed-pose case isolates native tool execution and does not prove a route to that pose. The fixture is constructed from the reported geometry, not an exact replay of the original cave, and runtime planning deadlines remain timing-sensitive.

**Native altitude is part of a movement fixture.** In the miniature world with worldSurface=50, a floor at tile row 60 receives reduced sky gravity; an identical floor at row 90 receives ordinary gravity. Fixed ordinary-gravity hop impulses left the reduced-gravity approach cases without crossing edges, while supplied useful poses could mine. Environment-sized hop proposals repair that distinction; the default suite retains both altitudes. The row-60 failure therefore cannot establish the cause of a reported cave-mining failure. Native simulation fidelity includes world dimensions and altitude, not just the drawn tile window.

Native outcome checks distinguish partial pick damage, actual tile removal, a weak pick producing no change, cooldown producing no new attempt, axe-owned damage and a vanished trunk rejected before a swing. The sparse-writer fixture serialises damage and no-change snapshots through the actual writer and checks actor generation, attempt identity and the explicit limit on yield attribution. Remaining-work checks compare a prepared mining forecast before and after native damage at the same pose, requiring comparison to stay unchanged until preparation refreshes it. These checks establish the tools' next-completion model under those native inputs, not a correct choice between finishing work and rejoining the player.

The separate departure pair seeds sustained player travel, holds ore geometry and separation fixed, and changes only whether native mining has left one hit or an untouched block. `--replay-water=Telemetry/<stamp>.tsv --tick=<tick>` reconstructs local terrain from event snapshots preceding that sample's wall-clock time, restores the observed body and breath, and runs the full brain with native collision. The wall clock joins the files because their game-tick origins can differ across world entry. Exact tile flags, frames and liquid amounts are restored when present; older glyph snapshots normalise material and disclose that loss. Uncaptured terrain is closed, unknown mod content is rejected with context, and moving liquids and other entities are not reconstructed. This is a bounded static replay, not a saved-world loader or a complete reproduction of enemy interactions.

Full-brain water tests require sustained native head clearance while alive. A one-cell head check can report air while the native breathing rectangle remains submerged. The captured older pool starts with its recorded remaining breath; full liquid cells are an explicit reconstruction assumption. Cosmetic combat-text slots are occupied in headless fixtures so native drowning strikes apply damage without requiring graphics fonts; the damage path itself remains active.

The setup assigns Terraria.Program.SavePath before Main’s static constructor, constructs a small Tilemap through its non-public constructor and marks the process dedicated-server for headless operation. Each collision comparison creates an ordinary NPC, runs its private gravity setup, applies shared controls and the step helpers, adds the engine’s gravity, then invokes private UpdateCollision. It fixes wetCount to suppress liquid entry/exit audiovisual effects. That collision matrix invokes no game AI, enemy spawning, save loading or full NPC update. The separate lifecycle fixture invokes the actual companion's AI entry point; the captured escape fixture invokes its production survival action and senses over native collision.

The matrix covers floor and ceiling slope orientations, flat/sloped platforms, dry movement, water, honey, shimmer, liquid transitions, offsets, jump and fall-through controls, and both space-scaled and ordinary gravity. Position, velocity, wet state, liquid priority and stair state must agree; prediction must restore all seven Collision scratch fields. Native route cases cover flat travel, a two-tile ledge, staircase ascent and staircase descent. Their output reports recovery faults even when the destination is reached.

The same executable also verifies every projectile in the current companion kit against native `Projectile.VanillaAI` for a bounded free-flight run. It compares phase, gravity, drag, terminal velocity and default hitbox, then checks that a swept trace rejects a thin blocking tile, reopens when that tile is removed, and rejects an accuracy-rotated launch that no longer reaches its target. This establishes the solver's supported projectile profiles and collision sampling; it does not prove every possible modded projectile or a live combat playtest.

`VerifyObservedMotion` runs the shared target forecast against the same initialized Terraria tile map. It checks a stationary grounded hostile remains supported; a tile-colliding flyer stops at a wall while a phaser crosses it; observed acceleration changes the short forecast; a jump is not extrapolated as a repeated impulse; `Forget` removes a reused NPC slot's old track; and a position correction during the same engine tick replaces an already-built forecast. It compares dry custom gravity/fall cap and wet custom movement slowdown against native `UpdateCollision` exactly, then bounds the remaining slope/step-policy difference while requiring non-zero current gravity to travel through `SlopeCollision`. It also keeps the pure regroup pressure monotonic as separation, return time, player movement away and stalled travel grow, while a nearby stationary companion remains calm. The test snapshots Terraria's collision scratch flags around each forecast, because a target forecast that changes shared collision state can corrupt the movement prediction it is supposed to inform.

`VerifyGodsEyeEvents` compiles the actual sparse event writer and its native NPC, projectile and terrain hooks with only test-local telemetry and mod stubs. It writes and parses a temporary JSONL session, then deletes it. The fixture proves sequence/schema/timestamp validity, normal session closure, snapshot-at-occurrence behaviour, reused NPC/projectile slot generations, shot-to-terrain correlation through the first projectile generation, and tile dirtiness becoming a changed local terrain snapshot only after post-update sees the engine edit. Its terrain fixture includes a slope and water, checks the rolling initial capture, and requires the recorded local chunk to retain glyph, liquid and material fields.

These are repeatable collision and route fixtures. They do not establish general world navigation, threat prediction quality or comfortable companionship. The historical text-world corpus and a recorded playtest answer those different questions. Private engine method names are intentional verification dependencies: if a game update removes them, the test must fail visibly rather than silently substitute another simulator.

`EngineReplay.csproj` compiles a handful of mod sources a second time, by an explicit per-file list, beside the `live` alias of the whole mod. A new file that one of those listed sources calls must be added to that list too: the mod's own build compiles everything and passes, while this project fails with the new name missing, and every run afterwards silently uses the previous binary.

Inside an interpolated string, an alias-qualified expression must be parenthesised: `{live::…Count}` compiles as the value `live` followed by a format specifier, and fails with "'<global namespace>' is a namespace but is used like a variable", while `{(live::…Count)}` is the expression. Search the file for `{live::` before building rather than finding each hole through its own compile error.

A recorded telemetry row with any hostile present writes `NPC.TypeName` into its target, top-threat and engage columns, and `TypeName` reads `Lang._npcNameCache`, which is all null until localisation loads. The recorder fixtures written before the combat identity columns never recorded a row with an enemy in it, so the writer's enemy-name path had no headless coverage and throws a null reference the first time one does. A fixture that records combat supplies `LocalizedText.Empty` for exactly the null cache entries and restores them to null afterwards.

Loader templates must be registered once per content type. Registering a fresh template for every fixture makes `ContentInstance<T>.Instance` null when more than one instance exists, so isolated tests pass while the combined process fails. Attach each fresh ModPlayer through its inherited `Entity` property and preserve the one template registration. Native tile placement also expects every player slot to contain an object, including inactive slots; a null slot is a fixture defect before it is evidence about placement.

The firing-position fixture resolves the positioner repeatedly rather than once, because its reachable region is flooded incrementally across rescores and a single call sees a region a few tiles wide — a one-call check measures flood budget rather than candidate scoring. Its shaft geometry is asserted to be discriminating before the result is read: the near floor must be blind and the lip sighted, or a green result would mean nothing. Terraria's `CanHitLine` tests three tile rows at each step, so an opening exactly as wide as the shaft leaves every solid-floored lip blocked and the fixture would be asserting an impossible shot.

The same fixture seals the shaft and requires both hunting and guarding to return no destination while retaining evidence that blocked candidates were evaluated. Reopening the shaft must restore attack access, and removing the weapon profile must invalidate the held attack destination immediately. These checks establish destination admission against native terrain and the production trajectory solver; they do not integrate travel to the spot or establish a subsequent native hit.

The sealed-shaft fixture separately gives the threat player-protection urgency and a wall-crossing capability, without simulating enemy AI. The real chooser must preserve positive guard desirability but reject its unestablished method, retain tested no-arc evidence and preserve the previous ordinary destination. Reopening must let guarding win with clear-arc method evidence. Closing the shaft must invalidate a previously solved method immediately, before its normal rescore interval. This checks the connection between nomination and position admission; the source-of-danger fields are supplied observations and do not establish an enemy's future attack script or a native protective hit.

The same fixture opens the live assembly's occurrence writer and parses its temporary JSONL capture. Every queried guard method must produce one record, including consecutive rejections with unchanged activity, followed by admission when the shot opens. Assertions join comparison and target identity, require the pre-activation phase and preserve explicit unobserved native effects. The fixture closes the writer and deletes its capture even on failure.

The shared-spacing fixture runs the real brain and native body with an invulnerable but harmful nearby enemy, first with an ordinary activity and then with an empty offer set. It requires a spacing response, one motor grant per tick, an available hand, increased separation and release at a stable low-exposure landing. A failed-search release cannot pass. The opposite arrangement requires player danger without companion danger to leave personal spacing inactive. The fixture does not run enemy AI or prove actual shots, effective-damage calibration or combined projectile/water escape.

Arrival assertions measure the actual body against the follow objective and local sight. The positioner's follow-status flag belongs to its current request and is cleared when selection yields to idle; using that flag alone can label a completed route failed precisely because following ended correctly.

Activity tests exercise the native Smart Cursor torch selector and `WorldGen.PlaceTile`, including nearby-light rejection, unsupported/occupied sites, companion-first resource consumption and shared cursor scratch preservation. Room fixtures include enclosed bedrooms and door boundaries beyond the conservative open-room fallback. Observation checks cover independent configuration switches, prediction state surviving recording opt-out, inspector bounds and the HUD's opening-click consumption before drawing. These contract tests complement rather than replace in-game UI inspection.

The project references the actual mod under the `live` assembly alias for lifecycle and behaviour checks. Those tests attach a real CompanionNPC through ModNPC.Entity, disable rendering/first-tick logging that require unavailable graphics/loader services, and keep the production brain, senses and movement components. `--lifecycle` checks owner death; `--escape` checks captured water escapes; `--route-persistence` round-trips a full-capacity archive through compressed native TagIO; `--follow` checks vertical intent and a live C-turn; `--protection-recovery` checks guard retention and continuous recovery; `--ore-work` checks work selection and resumption; `--observation` checks the actual recorder's retry and lifecycle evidence. The default run includes all of them. This does not launch a game, run enemy AI or test save-file loading through the full world loader.

The observation fixture assigns deliberately different engine and model gravity before the real AI-entry capture and checks both values and their source tick in the actual navigation event. NPC.gravity has a non-public setter; test setup uses reflection as the observed-motion fixtures do, while production only reads it. This checks that recording does not duplicate one side or read a later body pose; native collision comparisons establish the gravity rule separately.

`VerifyCombatPurpose` (`--combat-purpose`) holds P08's matched combat scenes, and every scene asserts who it threatens before it reads an outcome. Its consequence matrix holds one zombie between the player and the companion and varies one input per scene — player defence, the defence effectiveness the game would assign by difficulty, endurance, companion defence, and either actor's remaining life — requiring each to move only its own actor's urgency, in the direction the game's damage arithmetic moves it. Its baseline row recomputes the replaced raw-damage share from the record's public facts and requires equality at zero defence and full health, so a change that quietly rescaled every danger threshold fails. A headless `Player` has never run `ResetEffects` and carries zero defence effectiveness, so the scene sets it; without that, defence reads as ignored for the wrong reason. The J13 pair runs the full brain against an undamageable three-damage slime with no ordinary offers and differs only in the companion's life: full health must not start combat spacing and twelve life must. On the code before effective consequence, the matrix failed at its first varied row with both urgencies identical to eight digits, and a probe of the J13 pair read companion danger 0.151 at both lives.

The same file's pursuit rows are Proposal 1's hidden-5%-versus-visible-100% pairs on the firing-position fixture's shaft geometry: a nearly dead zombie on the shaft floor, visible only from the lip, and a full-health zombie eight tiles along the open floor. Rows change one input from the first — the companion's start (a three-tile or a twenty-six-tile reposition), the hidden enemy's damage (sixty against one), or an opening that lets the hidden enemy be shot from where the companion stands — and read the hunt's chosen target beside the arsenal's aim. Both hidden variants pass through rock so both are observed and examined; a sealed harmless walker would be filtered out by hunting's on-screen rule, which is empty headless, and its row would pass without valuing anything. Each row first requires the verdict it depends on (after moving, or from here), a much longer wait in the costly row and more danger in the dangerous row. The expected choices are the arsenal's own valuation: pursue the dangerous enemy after a short step while aiming at the visible one, not after a long walk, never the harmless one, and both feet and hands on the dangerous one once it is in sight.

The guard rows put one hostile beside the player with the companion across an open floor and read guarding's and hunting's prepared values. The matched pair is one zombie at its ordinary life and at 4,500: urgency must be identical, the removal estimates must sit either side of `GuardUsefulRemovalTicks`, and the tank's guard value must equal the ordinary one times its usefulness share, so the fight's length is the only thing that moved it. The boss pair places the Eye of Cthulhu or a Servant of Cthulhu and requires the servant to be worth more protection than the Eye, reading no boss flag. Both long fights must still be offered by hunting.

The identity rows fire one real shot through `Arsenal.BestTarget` and `TryFire` at one of two zombies, then call the native hit hook directly: first with the zombie the shot was not aimed at, then with the aimed one. `TrackLandedHits` must record the struck and aimed identities separately both times, ignore a projectile in another slot that the companion never fired, and ignore the shot's slot after a new spawn has cleared it. The loader registers no global hooks headless, so the fixture calls the spawn and hit hooks itself; production relies on tModLoader invoking them. A recorder row then checks the appended identity columns by name against the same hunt, aim and hit facts.

`VerifyEncounterContext` runs inside `--combat-purpose` and saves and restores every world-event global it sets. Its observation rows feed `EncounterSense` hand-built threat lists and a player above or below a stated surface line: a blood moon must be recognised on the surface and absent underground, the Old One's Army and an observed boss at any depth, and a reachable crowd at the threshold must read half strength at half its window, full strength when sustained, nothing one hostile short or with one walled off, and nothing on the first thinned tick. Its evaluation rows put mining, hunting and keeping company on one board and require a full encounter to zero mining while leaving the other two exactly as they were, urgency and an equal encounter to charge 0.4 once rather than 0.16, and a stranded companion to pay nothing. Its live rows run the full brain on the ore-work floor three times — quiet, blood moon with the player on the surface, blood moon with the player underground — and require the quiet and underground scenes to mine at an identical value and the surface scene to choose something else with mining at zero. With the evaluator's encounter term replaced by urgency alone, the run failed on the full-encounter evaluation row; with the evaluation rows also skipped, it failed on the live surface row, whose mining value stayed identical to the quiet scene's while the observation read the blood moon, so the live pair discriminates on its own.

`VerifySafetyAftermath` (`--safety-aftermath`, and in the default run) holds P08's combined-safety scenes. The retreat row puts a zombie beside a companion at twelve life and requires one tick where combat spacing owns the feet while the hands are granted, aimed at that zombie and report a real shot; separate counts of spacing ticks and fired ticks would pass on code that merely alternates. With the spacing request's hand grant replaced by `Unavailable`, the run still spaced for 61 ticks and failed with no qualifying tick. The J12 row first requires guarding to be executing, places a hostile arrow on a collision course, and requires that tick's single grant to be owned by `combat-reflex` with the hands available and guarding suspended under the same executor and activity id, then the same id executing again once the arrow is removed; it never asserts that the dodge worked. The intervening-hostile row requires zero raw-hitbox overlap with the zombie between the companion and the player and requires the companion to get past it, because standing still also avoids contact. The surfacing row runs the captured pool twice, the second time with a stationary hostile shot where the first run broke the surface, and requires sustained air with life left while printing the delay and life lost. `--dodge-repro` is outside the default run: an arrow advanced by hand at body height on dry floor, exiting 1 while it hits.

Hostile shots are written into `Main.projectile[Main.maxProjectiles - 1]`. `Main.ActiveProjectiles` iterates only the first `maxProjectiles` entries and the array's last element is the engine's dummy entry, so a shot placed there exists, reads active and is never observed; the first run of these rows placed the shot there and read a reflex that never fired, and the dodge reproduction reported a hit with no safety response at all.

`VerifyCombatActorMatrix` runs inside `--combat-purpose` and repeats the four-actor matrix — nobody, the player, the companion or both threatened — once on an open floor and once with a three-tile pillar beside the companion that blocks every line from it without sealing the floor. A damageable zombie a few tiles from one actor and roughly thirty from the other threatens only that actor. Every row must threaten exactly the actors it names; the pillar alone must decide whether each zombie can be shot from here, whether the hunt reads `FromHere` or `AfterMoving`, and whether the hands have a target; and pursuing the same zombie must be worth more clear than blocked. Only the player row is a clean pair: in the companion row the pillar also slows the zombie's path and halves companion danger, so part of its value drop is prevented harm rather than the reposition. With the arsenal valuation's reposition wait replaced by zero, and the earlier pursuit rows skipped so the matrix actually ran, the player row failed with both arms valued identically while the companion row alone would still have passed. Guard value, removal ticks, intervention ticks and protection urgency are printed per row and not asserted; they are the measurement recorded in the Combat guide that guarding does not read shot feasibility.
