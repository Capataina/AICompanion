# EngineReplay — native NPC collision is the independent oracle

This console tool loads the installed tModLoader assembly without opening a game window. It compares SimulateTerrariaBody with Terraria’s own NPC collision wrapper; it does not compare two copies of the portable text-world simulator.

The occurrence fixture also instantiates the real player observer through ModPlayer.NewInstance and invokes OnHurt with surviving and fatal damage. It asserts the callback's pre-subtraction life and explicitly expected successor health. Player is a read-only view of the attached entity; assigning that property through reflection is not the native attachment lifecycle.

The real recorder fixture follows ordinary selection, an already active recovery, an empty post-recovery board, a fresh companionship activity and downing. It requires distinct brain/chooser freshness and stable comparison identity during skipped selection. An empty comparison ends ordinary ownership rather than inventing execution of a zero-value fallback. CompanionNPC.AI writes its own telemetry row; calling Record again duplicates that tick and can hide the cardinality of actual decisions. Fixture assertions address columns by name and require one complete row per invoked AI update.

Each of those lifecycle updates also requires one motor application and one advancing control grant, including ticks that skip the chooser. The same assertion wraps full-brain captured escape, recovery and raised-lip mining updates. It compares the grant against the motor's actual owner and controls rather than only trusting the grant's own count. Recorder rows must retain fresh grants during recovery/downing, with an available hand during flight and an unavailable hand while downed.

The native mining interruption fixture requires a productive pick strike, retained tool ownership during its next cooldown tick, then a projectile-only collision forecast. That forecast must suspend the same mining activity, release its tool phase and produce one avoidance grant without another mining effect. Enemy absence is asserted so ordinary combat selection cannot accidentally satisfy the test.

Recovery admission is exercised with one alternate executor issuing reunion, exact-work, guard and hold requests at the same distant player position. Only reunion can start flight. This catches a concrete follow-class dependency and a coordinate-only shortcut independently of the native recovery flight/clearance checks.

Guard preparation tests replace the observation's most urgent enemy after scoring. The prepared identity, score and requested anchor must remain bound to the original enemy, including at entry. Removing that enemy must produce Hold at execution. These checks expose a score-to-execution substitution; they do not establish arrival at a useful firing pose or a native protective hit.

VerifyCompanionLifecycle.Create starts with a dead player to exercise autonomous lifecycle handling. A fixture requiring ordinary companionship must explicitly make that player alive before its first brain update. A last-registered zero-score fallback used to hide this precondition; an empty activity owner cannot be suspended merely because the fixture labels the next step recovery.

Native companion attachment is bidirectional: ModNPC.Entity points at the body and NPC.ModNPC points back at the behaviour. Assigning only Entity lets direct AI calls pass while native lethal damage bypasses the companion's CheckDead and enters vanilla NPC loot/death handling. A drowning-to-downing test must preserve both links rather than suppressing lethal strikes.

The empty-offer safety fixture removes every ordinary candidate, then exercises environmental exposure through the real brain and recorder. It requires an independent safety identity and fresh control grant without a fabricated ordinary comparison; downing must cancel that response and revoke the hand. The observation suite restores the mining policy it changed, so a later fixture does not inherit disabled work. Captured pool checks repeat with and without ordinary offers. Mirrored awning checks require sustained dry clearance and release of safety ownership, and reject a landing response reverting to an air-seeking phase under the same response identity.

The personal-danger fixture runs the real threat observer against sealed native-tile chambers. It checks both player/companion arrangements for a walker, a tile-colliding flyer, a wall-crossing phaser, and entry into an enemy's chamber before a cached reachability refresh. These isolate destination coupling; they do not measure live combat judgement or a modded hostile's own pathfinding skill.

Responsive-following checks drive PlayerSense around a repeated local path and require it not to report travel. The production intent estimator also receives matched movement with and without local-work evidence, brief reversal, sustained backtracking, pause and vertical movement. Duplicate timestamps cannot manufacture samples; observation gaps and position corrections clear the history. These checks establish those observation sequences, not knowledge of the player's future destination. Existing native full-brain following fixtures remain the separate integration check.

Keeping-company checks require one registered candidate instead of separate following and wandering candidates. A seeded local sequence, resolved through the positioner after every execution as the brain does (a stroll goal must lie in the positioner's returnable region, which grows only on the resolver's cadence, so executing without resolving left it at a two-tile first slice and no stroll could be chosen), must offer both rest and movement, a departing player must change that same activity to a reunion request without changing its identity, and sealed-pocket context must retain roaming. This exercises method selection without integrating local strolls through terrain; native following and recovery fixtures remain the physical checks, and `VerifyCompanyLocalMotion` integrates the strolls.

`VerifyCompanyLocalMotion` (in `--follow` and the default run) runs the whole brain on native collision with the planning allowances lifted. The hazard case removes every activity but keeping company and puts three hazards inside the calm band of a standing player: a lava pool two rows deep, a water pit three rows deep whose floor puts a standing body's head under water, and a pit twelve rows deep with a way down and none back. The scene asserts each is what it claims before running, including the drop through `MovementQueries.RoundTrip`, and the drop sits where the walker can actually reach it (a drop beyond the water pit is unreachable and would prove nothing). Over six thousand seeded ticks no held stroll goal, no destination the positioner resolves for it and no body pose may lie over or beside a hazard by the scene's own geometry, which is deliberately not the production predicate; no resolved destination may be outside the returnable region; nothing may be recorded as productive work; and at least three goals must be strolled to. The idle case keeps every activity registered in an empty world with light unmeasured, requires keeping company to be the only activity chosen, and over three game minutes allows no jump start, a rest share between a quarter and three quarters, and more than one goal. The window is that long because the earlier random hop was rare enough that one minute drew none on this seed. The empty-world reunion case keeps every activity registered in the same empty world, walks the player forty tiles along the floor and stops them, and requires keeping company to be the only activity chosen, a reunion request, and the body inside the follow objective at the stopped player; the C-turn case keeps the full chooser too but its player never moves. These establish local motion and reunion on those scenes, not comfortable spacing in real caves or behaviour under enemy pressure.

The recorder's seeded travel scenario resets and advances the native tool-contact world clock. Advancing GameUpdateCount alone does not age TileDamageWatcher, whose owner is TileDamageClock.PostUpdateEverything. Without those callbacks, a previous fixture's ore contact persists through every travel sample and correctly lowers intent confidence; the combined suite then fails while the isolated recorder test passes. Preserve the world lifecycle in the fixture rather than weakening the production interpretation of work.

`dotnet run --project Tools/EngineReplay -- --brain-cost` runs one seeded full-brain scenario — a flat four-tile copper vein, then a player walking away along the floor and back — for 600 ticks with native collision advancing the body. A discarded warm-up with recording on pays JIT compilation for both paths. Two measured runs under the production millisecond allowances then report every brain phase, and the AI time the brain did not account for (which is where recording lands), as p50/p95/max/mean with the three costliest ticks named. With `LimitPlanningWork.Unbounded` set, it runs twice with recording off and once on, and exits 1 unless all three agree tick for tick on activity, request, applied controls, grant owner, hand grant and body position. The first repeat is the determinism control; only then does the recording comparison prove recording changes no decision. It is not part of the default run.

Why the invariance runs lift the allowances, measured on 2026-09-13: under production allowances the warm-up and the first measured run diverged at tick 40 with identical prior traces, one run still travelling and the other already swinging from the same position, because the planning deadline decided how far a search got. Lifted, 600 ticks matched across repeats and with recording on. The same run showed where cost concentrates on this machine and scene: brain p50 0.025 ms, p95 0.57 ms, max 12.2 ms. The maxima were mining approach recomputation in the decide phase at ticks 0, 115–116 and 160–161 (10–12 ms each, the whole 12 ms total allowance), and at tick 0 the cut-off search held the body for a tick where an unbounded search walked. Recording added about 0.03 ms per tick at the median, with flush spikes of 2–7 ms. These are one scene on one machine, not a live-world distribution. After tool approach queries began asking nearest poses first, the same scene measured decide max about 4.2 ms (from 11.9–12.5) and gathering preparation max about 4.1 ms; the tick 115–116 and 160–161 spikes are gone, and the first allowance-changed decision moved from tick 0 to tick 39. Tick 0 remains the costliest tick at 10–12 ms of whole AI, now spread across decide, position and navigate doing their first work in one tick, rather than one approach query spending the allowance.

`dotnet run --project Tools/EngineReplay -- --combat-cost` exists because the brain-cost scene has no hostile in it, so nothing threat consequence, hunt target choice, protection, the arsenal's solves in the hands step or shared safety spend ever reaches its numbers. It runs the same full brain on the same copper floor with four stationary hostiles — a full-health zombie in weapon reach, a nearly dead zombie behind the companion, a slime and a boss-flagged Eye above the floor — while the player stands, walks away and returns, discards one run for JIT and reports per-phase p50/p95/max/mean, the three costliest ticks with activity, request, safety kind, aim target and encounter source, and ticks per activity and per encounter source. Because the Eye carries the game's boss flag, the whole scene is an observed boss encounter, so optional mining is suppressed by design and the scene prices guarding and hunting rather than work; a cost comparison across the commit that introduced encounter context compares different activity mixes. Enemy AI does not run and spawned projectiles are never advanced, so it prices queries against one arrangement rather than a fight, and it asserts nothing. It is not part of the default run.

`TheNearestFirstApproachMatchesTheExhaustiveScan` in the ore suite keeps a verbatim copy of the previous exhaustive approach and compares verdict and stand with the production query across 25 combinations of ore height, body side and distance, and a sealing wall, with all terrain final and the edge cache invalidated before the first query. It requires both reachable and unreachable approaches among them, so an equivalence that only ever compared failures cannot pass.

The family-allowance fixture registers two gathering probes, a winning combat probe and a non-excursion probe, sets the chooser's share to zero and compares three times. It requires exactly one gathering child prepared per comparison with the other reported Deferred at zero value, the next comparison to start from the deferred sibling, the incumbent and non-excursion probes to prepare every time, and a deferred sibling's retained higher value to lose. Two fresh non-incumbent siblings then give the counter-case (one deferred under a zero share) and the lifted case (both prepared with `LimitPlanningWork.Unbounded`). The cost harness also reports each family's preparation milliseconds and deferred count.

Offer and attempt contracts are exercised at three depths. The pure fixture requires a positive value beside a no-opportunity, policy-forbidden or known-unusable offer to be rejected, a zero-valued one to stay a truthful absence, and an unresolved one to keep bounded value. A probe activity then drives the real activity owner through selection without execution, reselection of the executing purpose, suspension and repeated suspension, resumption, replacement and an empty selection, requiring distinct attempt identities, interruption that never consults the activity, and conclusions that come from the replaced activity. The probe also counts `BeginAttempt` calls (one per opened attempt, none on reselection or suspension) and requires a second owner's first attempt identity to exceed every identity the first owner issued. The native ore suite asserts disabled mining is a policy prohibition and the same ore enabled is usable, that a transformed deposit concludes invalid, that a single-tile vein cleared by the companion's own strikes is a companion completion while the same vein cleared externally is invalid, and that full-brain mining interrupted by a projectile closes as Interrupted carrying its credited strike. Two further native checks run on one engine tick throughout: a first job cleared externally concludes its attempt invalid, and the attempt opened afterwards discovers a second job and must conclude attempted rather than inherit the first clear; and a two-tile vein whose one tile the companion removes and the player takes the other must conclude complete and shared. The guard fixture opens an attempt after an earlier release and requires it not to conclude from that release, then removes the committed threat while the player lives and requires an unattributed completion. The sealed hunt fixture requires a known-unusable classification with the no-firing-position reason, and the real recorder fixture requires exactly two attempt-outcome occurrences — recovery and downing interruptions — with no invented attempt for the empty board between them. Every test-local activity that scores positive must classify its offer, as production activities must.

`VerifyPreparedActivities.cs` exercises the production shared comparison on repeated and reordered immutable inputs, invalid numeric values, overflow, an unlimited threat horizon and a seeded valid-domain reference of the preceding chooser arithmetic. It proves numeric preservation for those prepared boards, not correct opportunity discovery or improved behaviour.

The same fixture compares production family nominations with an independent flat maximum using identical candidate values and tie keys. It reverses and duplicates candidates, exercises tied values across families, and requires empty/zero/invalid families to produce no winner. The actual chooser rejects an all-zero board without entering its final registered adapter. The recorder fixture checks that all three family nominations are serialised.

Activation tests invalidate the strongest prepared candidate while a later adapter prepares: they change its purpose identity, despawn or respawn its NPC, empty its item or replace its item type. The actual chooser must activate an already-prepared candidate within the same family or another family, enter the invalidated candidate zero times, prepare every adapter exactly once and retain the rejected raw value with a named cause. An entirely invalidated board must activate nothing. This exercises reconsideration through production family selection rather than only the pure nomination function.

The native activity fixture also prepares an actual loot candidate, changes the live pickup list and item position, and verifies that repeated scoring retains the captured value, trip duration and target. A disappeared item must then be refused at execution. Same-type world-slot replacement leaves the old object's active flag intact deliberately: preparation and execution must reject the detached object, and activation must name the replacement while reconsidering the prepared board without rediscovery. A newly observed replacement remains selectable under its own identity. Item fixtures therefore register their objects in Main.item; an unattached object is not evidence of an available world drop. A fresh preparation after the completed-work allowance expires must remove that opportunity. These checks exercise the real adapter rather than only the shared scalar evaluator.

Collection fixtures prepare a native pot footprint as an uncertain contents opportunity with a nonzero forecast, introduce a valuable known drop at the feet and require the same activity to select it on the next preparation, then remove the drop and reconsider the pot. Disabling pot permission after preparation must preserve the tile; restoring it before the next preparation must immediately restore that opportunity without waiting for the old discovery deadline. A full cargo bag suppresses unknown contents. These tests establish selection and refusal, not native successful pot destruction or predicted drop landing geometry.

Hunt admissibility fixtures prepare before comparing, then clear the live threat list and move the selected NPC to check that comparison retains its captured target, value and duration. A despawn must be refused at execution. The sealed-tree fixture repeatedly scores without preparation and requires the approach-cache timer to remain unchanged, then prepares successive observations to exercise its normal retry cadence. Preparation cost remains included in the hunt admissibility measurement.

The pursuit-progress fixture supplies three hands-boundary outcomes to a stationary real hunt: firing at another enemy, remaining in cooldown, and firing at the pursued enemy. Only the last may renew attack progress. The fixture sets those boundary properties directly and does not create projectiles, so it proves attribution rather than native shooting or eventual damage. Existing movement and alternating-target cases remain separate checks of the same deferral mechanism.

The suspension fixture selects and executes an actual hunt, records its failure counter, suspends it through the production activity owner and delivers a full window of outcome observations. Neither the counter nor target eligibility may change. After reselection and execution, stationary failure must expire normally. This establishes callback ownership across interruption and resumption; full-brain escape tests separately exercise the safety controller and native movement.

The two-tile ore fixture removes a real tile and moves the body after preparing a candidate. Context-free scoring and forecasting must retain the old candidate and leave the vein untouched; the next preparation must incorporate the removal and relocate to the remaining tile. Direct scenario calls use PrepareAndScore when they intend a new observation; purity assertions deliberately omit preparation.

`VerifyMiningHops` attacks the hop method where the ore suite's minimal ceiling case cannot. A pit beside the ceiling ore's take-off must hold a body arriving at walking speed from either side under native NPC collision, and the whole brain must mine without its feet ever dropping below the floor; before the arrival-slide admission rule the chosen take-off overhung the pit by two pixels and a walking arrival fell in. A body dropped into that pit, or set down in it, must stop being offered its old take-off within a bounded time and close a failed attempt with no strike. Water poured onto a proven take-off (liquid motion announces no terrain revision) must end the job as `interaction-jump-lost-take-off` and keep that take-off unoffered for the whole of the fixture's watch, which is shorter than the retry wait in `BehaviourWeights`, and a terrain change must end the wait and let the drained take-off mine. A scan whose planning deadline has already passed must answer Unknown both where the unbounded scan says Yes and where it says No. A tall ceiling needs a pose more than six rows below standing reach, and a ledge take-off must be climbed to, mined from and landed back on. Every case except the deadline one lifts the planning allowances, because the whole-brain cases catch a phase by condition and a deadline-cut search would decide which phase they catch; the deadline case cannot, since a lifted limit sets no deadline to expire. G1 is a known limitation asserted as it stands: above a pillar top two tiles wide the scan must answer No although a jump from rest on that top reaches the ore and the walker reaches the top, while the same top three tiles wide admits a take-off, so a change to arrival-slide admission turns it red on purpose. The suite also prints, under the production allowances, decide and whole-tick timings on a scene where every ore has a proven standing No, and landings, hop-target releases and decide and Gathering timings on a walk to a take-off that needs traversal jumps (a two-tile step, since a one-tile rise is climbed by native step-up with no landing); those numbers describe this machine and are never asserted.

`VerifyGatheringCooperation` runs P09's cooperation and permission contracts through the whole brain on native tiles, and every whole-brain case diffs the full tile map so an edit to anything but the work target fails it. The player hitting the companion's trunk mid-chop must move it to the separate trunk with no further strike on the shared one, end the attempt it left as partial with `player-took-trunk`, and after the player fells the first trunk leave no shared completion behind. A trunk felled externally before any strike is an invalid attempt, and after a companion strike a shared completion. The chop departure pair repeats the mining one with the trunk's native remaining hits. A bed placed after the approach, for both tools, while walking and after the first strike, must stop every further strike, release the tool hand and end the attempt partial or invalid, and removing it must let the work finish. The work policy is switched off, or to Mimic with no player contact, while walking, in the cooldown between strikes and during a ceiling hop in flight, with the same requirements. A player axe hit observed under Mimic, followed by chopping disabled for longer than both the watcher's contact memory and the Mimic job window (the fixture advances the watcher's clock itself), must leave Mimic, switched back on, waiting for the player rather than reading the old contact as recent. An ore the pick cannot damage must never be struck while the whole brain fells the usable tree beside it. A retained mining job must leave a new tree prepared and valued on the same board, and must lose to it on the next comparison once the vein is worth nothing; whether commitment should lose to a shorter remaining trip is left to the reunion valuation. Mining and chopping forecasts, less their native remaining work, must equal the walk in pixels over walking speed. Trees within ten tiles of the world edge are never searched, so tree fixtures sit inside that margin.

`VerifyWorkAccounting` checks what a mining job reports against what the world shows. A two-tile vein the companion clears with its own strikes while the player places a third copper tile touching it must conclude partial as `tracked-portion-clear-vein-continues`, never complete, and the added tile must become the next job. Equal remaining work is compared across two scenes that differ only in who removed one tile of a two-tile vein, the companion's native strikes (with its cooldown finished) or the player, with the companion on the same feet: value, forecast, remaining tiles, target and native remaining hits must be identical, and the remaining count must be one. A third scene adds the player's own partial pick damage to the remaining tile and requires the companion's offer unchanged, because the damage lives in the player's hit table. Work cancelled by range runs under the close distance mode, since an active job's allowance under the standard mode is wider than this world can separate player from ore; after one removal the player leaves, and no strike may follow, the job must end with two tiles present and one companion removal, and the attempt must be partial.

Two copper drops a tile apart merged by the game's own `TryCombiningIntoNearbyItems` (both reserved for the local player, as the native merge requires) must leave the walked-to drop's attempt as `drop-merged-into-world-drop` with the absorber offered next, partial when the bag took some first, while a drop taken with its same-type neighbour beyond merge distance stays invalid. `VerifyAssistanceTrips` (in `--ore-work` and the default run) drives the real lighting and collection activities through the shared nearby-interaction executor on an island scene: the companion and player stand on a short floor whose own torch sites sit inside a measured lit disc, and the only dark sites and the pot are on the floor of a pit twelve rows below the island edge. A second scene puts a two-tile shelf eleven columns from the companion, with a pot on it or its top as the only dark torch sites (a band of measured light over the floor vetoes every floor site while the area stays dark). The scene asserts both premises before preparing. Each case lifts the planning allowances. Hostile shots are written into `Main.projectile[Main.maxProjectiles - 1]`. `Main.ActiveProjectiles` iterates only the first `maxProjectiles` entries and the array's last element is the engine's dummy entry, so a shot placed there exists, reads active and is never observed; the first run of these rows placed the shot there and read a reflex that never fired, and the dodge reproduction reported a hit with no safety response at all.

`VerifyCombatActorMatrix` runs inside `--combat-purpose` and repeats the four-actor matrix — nobody, the player, the companion or both threatened — once on an open floor and once with a three-tile pillar beside the companion that blocks every line from it without sealing the floor. A damageable zombie a few tiles from one actor and roughly thirty from the other threatens only that actor. Every row must threaten exactly the actors it names; the pillar alone must decide whether each zombie can be shot from here, whether the hunt reads `FromHere` or `AfterMoving`, and whether the hands have a target; and pursuing the same zombie must be worth more clear than blocked. Only the player row is a clean pair: in the companion row the pillar also slows the zombie's path and halves companion danger, so part of its value drop is prevented harm rather than the reposition. With the arsenal valuation's reposition wait replaced by zero, and the earlier pursuit rows skipped so the matrix actually ran, the player row failed with both arms valued identically while the companion row alone would still have passed.
