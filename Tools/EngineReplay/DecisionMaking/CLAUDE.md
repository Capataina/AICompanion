# Decision fixtures distinguish core contracts from native behaviour

The retained-course plan's gates require production paths. Pure policy and lifecycle fixtures here call the same classes compiled into the mod, through the `live` alias. They establish the stated arithmetic and state transitions, not real tile effects, combat accuracy or the full integrated brain.

```text
DecisionMaking/
├─ CLAUDE.md               evidence scope and execution
├─ VerifyCourseCore.cs     fixed objective, identity, bounds, resources, repair and budget cuts
├─ VerifyProjectionContracts.cs causal effect reads, resource phases, fair discovery and receipt retirement
├─ VerifyCourseOrderProjection.cs one-operation cuts, completed-query extensions, empty-order cost handoff, frozen-input guards, shared region-curve equivalence, and which end of the course the consequence forecast is priced from
├─ VerifyCompanionshipForecast.cs region integration, subdivision invariance, whole-course suspension and captured arrival evidence
├─ VerifyCourseTravelScheduling.cs native query fairness, model-catalogue completion, deferred terrain edits, capacity and reset
├─ VerifyNativeConsequencePricing.cs the native provider's honesty: travel and enemy motion suspended and forwarded, a hostile on the flight path priced as real harm and the same hostile off it costing nothing, the tail unresolved even with every enemy modelled, each order priced from its own start
├─ VerifyWholeCoursePricing.cs one non-empty course through the real binder and the real forecast: a contact priced during the prefix, the origin handoff, and the live pricing branch asserted rather than assumed
├─ VerifyCourseBindingExecution.cs a bound step's activity and position request, and every source having a binder at all
├─ VerifyAdmittedOpportunitiesBind.cs the whole tick over a scene that loses bodies: a census admission and its binder read one evidence
├─ VerifyADecisionInFlightKeepsTheFight.cs the whole tick with the decision held open: an unsettled tick keeps the fight rather than exiting it
├─ VerifyWhatEachDecisionCosts.cs what a decision costs to assemble and to carry, in bytes and counts rather than milliseconds
├─ VerifyTheSearchRetainsItsRunnerUp.cs the best order the winner beat, kept through the search rather than discarded
├─ VerifyEncounterConduct.cs gate G13: an encounter suppresses a whole optional course without repricing a fight,
│                            among two feasible fights the survivable one wins while it is on, and the player's
│                            death leaves it standing
└─ FuzzTheDecisionContracts.cs seeded event sequences nobody wrote, driven through the live recorder against the six contracts the audit keeps
```

**`FuzzTheDecisionContracts` is the only file here that generates its own world, and the only one that drives the mod's own recorder rather than the audit's seam.** Every scene in `Tools/EngineReplay/Observation/VerifyDecisionTripwires.cs` was built by hand from the capture of 22 September 2026, so each of the six contracts has been shown to fire on a case somebody already knew about and to stay quiet on the neighbouring case somebody already thought of; what none of them establishes is that the contracts stay quiet on a sequence nobody composed. This one draws spawns, deaths, teleports, drops appearing and taken, mined tiles, the player walking and stopping, work-policy flips and operation cuts from a seeded generator, six sequences of two hundred whole-brain ticks, and reads the *mod assembly's* own `AuditDecisionContracts.Counts` back after every tick. It composes no payload: `DecideCourseEachTick.Trace` builds the decision record's fields privately and exposes no accessor, so a local copy of them would be a drift waiting to happen — the fixture attaches a real `BrainTelemetry`, lets `Load` install `ReadLiveCourseForAudit`, and ticks through `CompanionNPC.AI`, which is the path a play takes.

Three things about it are load-bearing and the first two are findings rather than construction details.

- **A fixture's companion is invisible to `CompanionNPC.Instance`, so the audit's live source returns null on every call.** `ReadLiveCourseForAudit.Read` reaches the course by scanning `Main.ActiveNPCs` for the registered type, and `VerifyCompanionLifecycle.Create` builds a body with a reverse attachment and no slot. Measured while building this: **1,200 decisions audited and 0 frozen observations read** — which is exactly the broken-installer signature the audit's own comments describe, and it leaves four of the six contracts unable to fire while every contract row reports green. The premise row is what caught it and is why that row exists. It was this fixture's own private repair for a day; the class is closed in `../OpenTheRecorderOnACompanion.cs` now, which every recorded fixture in the suite goes through, and this one opens its recorder the same way as the rest.
- **Two of the six fire legitimately when the world removes what a course was holding.** `accepted-use-absent-next-tick` and `activity-exited-during-decision` cannot tell the loop they are named for from a target this generator killed or teleported, so every firing is dated to its tick and classified against the events injected in the three ticks up to it, and the assertion is on the unattributed ones. Both counts are printed, because suppressing the attributed half would hide a real coarseness in those two contracts rather than record it.
- **`decide-overran-allowance` is reported and never asserted**, for this suite's standing regime rule: the allowances are lifted and the suite runs about 2.3× slower per operation than a standalone process, so a count of overruns measures where in the run the case sits. It fired 183 to 223 times across runs of this fixture, which is that regime and not a finding about the brain.

Its mutation is the defect lane C fixed: commenting out `DiscoverOpportunities.RetireAdmissionsThisObservationCannotSupport` makes the census serve admissions the frozen observation no longer supports, and the fuzzer finds it on its first fixed seed — **895 firings of `census-admitted-binder-refused` over five seeds with 55 unattributed, first at seed 1 tick 12, and 82 of `empty-course-beside-usable-work` with 3 unattributed**. `AIC_FUZZ_SEEDS=1,7,13,29,101` replays exactly that set; with the variable unset the five fixed seeds run plus one taken from the clock, which is what stops the fixed list from becoming the only five worlds these contracts have ever met, and the clock seed is printed in the row so a red on it is replayable too.
**`VerifyEncounterConduct` is gate G13's three missing clauses and deliberately not its fourth**, which already has rows under other names: `an unarmed companion offers no combat` and `combat is admitted only where it can be executed` are the safe-conduct half. Referencing them rather than rebuilding them is the point — a second row asserting one property is two things to keep in step, and one of them drifts.

**The whole-brain suppression control is cited by its ledger case name, `combat keeps its purpose across a substituted enemy`, and getting that wrong is a trap this file fell into.** `VerifyEncounterContext` is registered in `DefaultCases` nowhere; it runs inside `VerifyCombatPurpose.Run()`, so its own method sentence — *"the live brain stops mining only where the event reaches it"* — is not a case name and matches nothing under `--case`. The first draft of the fixture and of this guide cited that sentence as though it were one. **The general rule for this suite: a fixture's method name and its ledger case name are different strings, and only the outer `DefaultCases` entry is the second.**

**The player-death clause was the one clause of the four that had no detector anywhere**, proven rather than suspected: a sentinel planted `if (player.dead) { Intensity = 0f; Source = "none"; Recognised = false; return; }` at the top of `EncounterSense.Update` and both of the other two rows stayed green, and so did `combat keeps its purpose across a substituted enemy`. It has one now, and it is a live sense over live NPC slots rather than arithmetic, because the clause is about what the sense reads. Two properties of its scene matter: one boss and nothing else, because `boss |= threat.IsBoss` is taken before the reach filter so recognition does not need a reachable body, and because a crowd past the headless spawn cap of five reads as an inferred encounter and would prove the wrong thing; and the pair is alive-then-dead-then-boss-removed over one scene, so a sense answering `boss` unconditionally fails the last arm.

Both of its rows are whole-course rather than arithmetic, which is what separates them from `the course charges an encounter once and only to optional non-combat work` one layer down. The suppression row asserts *both* directions, and the second is what makes it a check: an optional course must lose its whole value under a boss, and a fight must be worth **exactly** what it was in the calm world, because an implementation that answered "combat wins" by scaling combat up satisfies a one-sided row and needs a magnitude nobody can derive.

The survival row's scene is built so the safer fight is the *less valuable* one — it kills less and costs the companion almost nothing — and that shape is a finding rather than a flourish. Built the obvious way round, with the safer course also the higher-total one, the row passed with the encounter key deleted from `NominalOrder`, because the total decides it either way. It was written that way first and the mutation found it green.

**What `NominalOrder` does with a course that is not a fight is an open hole this row does not close.** During an encounter it compares companion harm before anything else, with no feasibility or meaningfulness qualifier, so an empty course — zero effects, zero harm — outranks any feasible fight that takes a single hit. The plan's own G13 column names that as a mutation which must fail (`idle artificially wins by zero damage`), and on this tree it is the behaviour: for an idle course `I` and a fight `F`, `NominalOrder(I, F, encounter: true)` reduces to `F.CompanionHarm.CompareTo(0)`, which is positive whenever the fight costs anything at all. Whether it is reachable in play depends on what the search hands the comparison, and an empty course is legal and sometimes winning by this tree's own account. It is recorded rather than asserted because a red row is a stop for every lane, and the ordering rule is `Selection/`'s to change; the row to add once it is ruled on is a third arm on this same fixture, asserting `NominalOrder(idle, feasibleFight, encounter: true) < 0`.

**`VerifyTheSearchRetainsItsRunnerUp` guards the column the recorder writes as `task_order_runner_up`**, and its two rows are the two answers that column must be able to give. Two drops on the floor price several orders across distinct first steps, and the runner-up must start somewhere the winner does not — the first step is the discriminator because it is the only part of an order the tick performs, so two orders that begin the same way are one answer to "what would it have done instead". An empty floor prices exactly one order and the runner-up must be *null*, because "there was no alternative" and "the alternative is unreadable" are different facts and a column that is always non-empty stops being read.

**`VerifyWhatEachDecisionCosts` measures bytes and counts rather than milliseconds, and that is this folder's rule rather than its preference**: the root guide records the suite running about 2.3 times slower per operation than a standalone run, so a wall-clock threshold here would test how far through the suite the row sits as much as the code under it. Its per-fact row builds two thousand facts with their keys and values prepared *outside* the measurement — a capture composes facts out of values it already holds, so the strings are the capture's cost rather than the fact's — and throws one warm-up pass away, because the JSON serialiser and the hash both warm reflection on first use. Measured 22 September 2026: 112 bytes a fact lazily against 1,387 eagerly. **What that row does not measure is how many facts are asked for a digest anyway**, and a review the same day found 26.7% of a 500-decision scene's facts still hashed because every tracked read took one — so the per-fact saving is a per-fact figure and the whole-session saving it implies was an extrapolation until `FactRead` stopped recording a hash at all. A row measuring a rate wants a scene, not a loop over constructors.

Its bound row is a guard rather than a repair and says so. The play's observation grew from 150 facts to 1,603 and never fell; no scene reachable from this harness reproduces that, and the two candidates were eliminated here rather than argued about — the ore seam peaks at 46 and falls to 29 as the census window leaves the ground behind, and eight hostiles peak at 54 with at most 36 combat-use facts. A green on that row therefore means the property still holds, not that the play's growth was fixed.

**`VerifyAdmittedOpportunitiesBind` is the only file here that drives `Brain.Tick` rather than a piece of the course**, and it is the reproduction of the play of 0.38.13: a standing player, five hostiles, three drops, and then deaths, a pickup and two spawns while the run continues. The scene is dynamic on purpose — a static one passes on the parent tree, because an admission only goes stale when the world it was decided against moves on. Its first row asserts, on every one of the five hundred decisions, that a candidate served `KnownUsable` still has its own evidence `Observed` in the snapshot the binder will read; the failure message prints the candidate key, the binder's key and what the snapshot actually holds, because a refusal tally says a search threw orders away and nothing about which target or which key.

**`VerifyADecisionInFlightKeepsTheFight` is the second whole-tick file here, and the way it forces its state is the part to read before writing another.** A decision spans ticks when the allowance runs out, and the suite lifts every millisecond allowance on purpose, so the only lever left is an operation count — `Brain.PlanningOperationAllowance`, which is `long.MaxValue` in play. The usable window is narrow and structurally so: every activity's `Prepare` spends the same allowance *before* the decision does, so a cap low enough to guarantee an unfinished search also starves combat's own tactical search and there is nothing left to keep. Measured 22 September 2026: four thousand operations finishes every search, a hundred leaves combat with no plan to offer, and 150 to 400 is the window where the decision stays open and the fight stays alive. The fixture therefore measures the cap on a ladder instead of hard-coding one, and calibrates on two premises — the decision stays unsettled, and combat still offers a plan — that both hold on the parent tree, so the calibration cannot smuggle the property in.

Its assertion is narrower than "the plan survived", and the narrowing is the finding: combat legitimately releases and re-commits in that scene for its own reasons (`company-gap-doubled` fires because the player is 750 px away), so a plan id that changes proves nothing. What a deciding tick must never be is the *cause*, and `activity-exited` is the one release reason that can only come from the tick selecting something else — so the row counts a *new* activity exit paired with an unsettled tick, and neither the sticky invalidation string alone nor a count of ticks carrying it would separate the defect from ordinary play.

Its second row is deliberately weaker than it could be, and the weakness is the finding. It asks that a collect step is bound and that combat orders reach *pricing* with nothing else on the board — not that a fight wins. Whether five zombies round a standing player are worth flying at is the objective's answer and `AIC-439`'s open question; what the discovery seam owes is that the comparison happens at all. Measured at `013aa03` plus the fix: seven orders priced with only combat admitted, and the empty course still winning them.

**Two of these files were each other's blind spot, and `VerifyWholeCoursePricing` is the row that crosses them.** `VerifyNativeConsequencePricing` drives the real forecast and prices an empty order in every row, so the state a course starts from and the state it ends at are the same value; `VerifyCourseOrderProjection` drives real multi-step orders through a stand-in forecast that ignores its successor. A binder handing the post-course state to a forecast whose contract wants the pre-course one was therefore invisible from both sides at once, and for an order of duration T the whole prefix went unpriced. The new row binds one real step, prices it through `ForecastCourseConsequences` with `RetainCourseModelQueries` answering travel and enemy motion, and measures a hostile contact at tick 31 against an arrival at tick 67 — harm during the journey, which a forecast handed the end state cannot produce. Restoring `state.Fork()` at the binder's handoff reddens it.

Three things that fixture had to learn, each of which is what a stand-in binder lets you skip and a real one cannot:

- **A binder binds against the captured travel, it does not assert numbers at it.** Hard-coding a travel duration is refused as `bound-travel-duration-changed` and letting the arrival pose default to the requested point is refused as `bound-arrival-pose-changed`, because native arrival has slack and must not teleport the hypothetical body onto the point that was asked for. `ReadCourseTravel.Read` is what every real binder uses and what this one uses.
- **A suspended binder must name what it waits on.** Returning `Unresolved` without a `RequiredTravel` request parks the candidate for ever; the fixture stalled at "pending=1, asking for nothing" until the request was carried on the `BindingResult`.
- **Only a snapshot the owner actually appended to is a model extension.** Re-handing the same snapshot is refused by contract, and rightly: a search told its inputs changed when they did not restarts a frozen comparison for nothing.

**The projection clock is relative to the decision and always starts at zero**, which is worth knowing before writing another scene here. `DecideCourseEachTick` builds its initial state with no start tick, and `SampleContactTrajectory` refuses any contact trajectory whose first pose is not at tick zero. A scene starting elsewhere is not more realistic — it is a different clock, and it throws at that guard.

**These files are reached two different ways, and the flag does not cover them all.** Five run together under `--retained-course-core` — `VerifyCourseCore`, `VerifyProjectionContracts`, `VerifyCourseOrderProjection`, `VerifyCompanionshipForecast` and `VerifyCourseTravelScheduling`. The other four are named directly in `../Movement/VerifyEngineMotion.cs`'s default-case table, each as its own case with no flag of its own, and those names are what the scoreboard and `--case` match:

```
a course is priced for the harm it flies into and still cannot certify that it harms nobody   VerifyNativeConsequencePricing
a non-empty course is priced end to end from the tick it starts at                            VerifyWholeCoursePricing
a bound course step names the activity that performs it and the place it happens              VerifyCourseBindingExecution
seeded event sequences nobody wrote break no decision contract                                FuzzTheDecisionContracts
```

The fuzzer is registered **last** in that table, after `VerifyTheCensusFrontIsCurrent`, and the position is the same decision that put that one there: it attaches the live recorder, drives six scenes of two hundred whole-brain ticks with hostiles, drops, mined tiles and policy flips, and is the widest process footprint the table has. It puts its own world back — the npc and item slots, the companion's own slot, the work policies, the operation allowance, the screen, Lang's NPC name cache and the diagnostics config — and running last means nothing inherits whatever that misses.

So a change to one of those three is not exercised by the flag, and a reader looking for them under `--retained-course-core` will conclude they do not run. Run the five from the repository root with `dotnet run --project Tools/EngineReplay -p:UseAppHost=false -- --retained-course-core`, and the three through the default suite or `sh ../../verify.sh --case "<fragment>"`. Each row states the relevant plan gate, but a row covering a core seam does not complete that gate's native scene. Generated native event sequences, matched reactive/exact comparison, model fidelity and exact snapshot replay remain distinct obligations of the full plan.
