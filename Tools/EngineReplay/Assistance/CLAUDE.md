# Assistance fixtures — lighting, collecting, keeping company, and the senses they read

The nearby-assistance family, plus the two senses every activity in it consumes. These are the fixtures most changed by the body, because almost every one of them used to ask where a body could *stand*.

```
Assistance/
├─ CLAUDE.md
├─ VerifyLightAndReachSenses.cs   the acceptance set for light and reach as two senses
├─ VerifyTorchPlacementRule.cs    torches wherever his smart cursor could place one in the dark, and every search exit named
├─ VerifyCandidateFunnel.cs       each refusal named with its stage, his cursor's tile as the reference, the recorded factors
├─ VerifyCompanionActivities.cs   the seven activities offered and chosen, and danger charged once
├─ VerifyUsefulAssistance.cs      assistance that is useful rather than merely nearby
├─ VerifyAssistanceTrips.cs       a trip goes and returns; hover sites; incidental pots
├─ VerifyCollectionContracts.cs   a drop is claimed only for what arrived
├─ VerifyCapabilityRevision.cs    a capability change reaches the next preparation
├─ VerifyCompanyLocalMotion.cs    strolling without walking into hazards
└─ VerifyCourtesy.cs              getting out of the player's way, and staying out
```

## The two senses, and why every row is written to fail on the code before them

`VerifyLightAndReachSenses` is the acceptance set for light and reachability as *senses* rather than as private answers. Each row asserts the old answer beside the new one in the same scene, so it fails on the pre-sense code without that code being checked out.

A lit chamber wide enough to fill the torch's hold radius, inside a world dark everywhere else, first requires that the retired ambient scalar — recomputed in the fixture as one mean brightness over every open-air tile of the window, read from the engine rather than from our own code — calls the scene dark. Then it requires the field to read the body's own neighbourhood lit and the real torch to go out saying `lit-room`. The same scene with the heading forty columns out in the dark must raise the torch saying `dark-ahead`, which no single number for the window can express.

A body outside the presented area beside a measured bright heading must hold its torch, which is the case a rule letting an unread query merely abstain puts out. A world lit by nothing but the companion's own torch — presented as the engine would present it, the torch's colour decayed by Manhattan distance and floored at the ambient — must not read as a lit room and must never nominate a dark region on the companion's own hand, which is what subtracting the torch instead of inverting the engine's own maximum produces.

**Carried light is covered by the same discriminator, as one scene with and without a carrier.** A held torch, then a light pet of a different colour and strength, are announced through `Lighting.AddLight`; every tile the field still measures must read exactly what it read before, while some tiles must become unknown. A carrier that occluded nothing would let the row pass against a sense that discounts nothing at all. The passage must still read dark around the body afterwards, which is the behaviour the lane exists for. A row with two hundred dust-bright lights prices the worst input.

A dark wing away from a lit body must be offered with a destination the reach sense itself calls `Reachable` rather than `NotYet`. A floor dark everywhere, with only lighting and keeping company registered, must place two torches with keeping company owning no tick between them. And a player on a shelf hanging in the air, where the comfort box holds no tile that is both free and flooded, must still be answered with a reachable tile that closes the gap, declaring `Undeclared` rather than the follow region — a destination outside the objective that declared it would be recorded as a contract violation on every arrival.

Its `e:` rows are the spatial-invalidation set: a retained search survives an edit it never read and dies on one it did, the invalidation margin is the flood's own explored bounds either side of it, a revision older than the record's window is treated as changed, the reach flood refloods for an edit it read and not for one it did not, and a door toggled by anything but the companion announces its rows. The far-outside probe for those rows sits **below the floor**, not above it: a flyer's flood fills the sky, so the unread free space in this world is underneath.

## Trips, hovers and the sites no floor pose can reach

`VerifyAssistanceTrips` drives the real lighting and collection activities through the shared nearby-interaction executor on an island scene: the companion and player stand on a short floor whose own torch sites sit inside a measured lit disc, and the only dark sites and the pot are on the floor of a pit twelve rows below the island edge. Without a way back the pit is a one-way drop, and neither a lighting site nor the pot may be offered under either one-way setting; with drops allowed the lighting refusal must name the missing return. With steps from the pit floor back up, the same pit must offer a site and the pot. The scene asserts its own premise through `MovementQueries.RoundTrip` before any activity is prepared, so a staircase that failed to make a round trip reads as a broken fixture rather than a behaviour result.

The island is built as solid rock with the chamber carved out of it, not as a one-tile shell, so the chamber's air is the only open air near its sites; a shell once let a neighbourhood darkness test read the lit open air outside the chamber, and the row passed while proving nothing.

## The torch rule and the funnel, on light the engine itself presents

`VerifyTorchPlacementRule` and `VerifyCandidateFunnel` present light the way the colour engine's blur state does rather than painting brightness per tile: the world's colour on every tile, solid tiles masked so light decays through them at the engine's solid rate, placed lights merged in the way the tile scan merges a standing torch, carried lights merged by maximum and also announced through `Lighting.AddLight`, then the engine's own `LightMap.Blur`, all at the game's own global brightness. That is not realism for its own sake. Every older lighting row wrote brightness straight into the map at a global brightness of one, the one setting at which no light can exceed one, so a defect living in that difference — a carried torch modelled below the engine's own reading of it — was invisible to all of them while it read a whole cave lit in play.

The rule's rows are split by what they read, and the split is how their reds were taken. `VerifyTorchPlacementRule` reads only surfaces that existed before it did, so each row was run red against the production code of its parent with that code stashed and the fixture left in place: the carried torch in a dark cave, the dark room his cursor could light, the search the deadline cut, the stand beyond the known radius and the refusals past the store's old size. Its lit-room and daylight rows were green there and are held by mutations instead — lighting with its darkness stage removed, and the placement reading with only the model unclamped. `VerifyCandidateFunnel` reads what the lane added, so every row there is held by a mutation named in its commit: a refusal recorded under the wrong stage, the attachment filter without its wall term, the player's cursor centred on his corner, the time factor recorded as one.

**The executor's exits are driven through a fixture method, not through lighting.** `Sites` is the shared executor with a fixed list of sites asked in list order and nothing of its own, and it can expire the planning deadline the moment gathering ends. That is the one deterministic way to reach the cut inside the stand loop rather than inside a gathering scan, and the one way to order a beyond-radius stand ahead of a refused one without depending on a subclass's costs.

What these two families do not establish: the engine's tile scan lighting a placed torch rather than the fixture merging its colour in, legacy lighting, an engine batch the blur consumed while the sense held the previous one, and anything about the reference or the funnel in a played capture.

**The shelf rows are the orb's version of the walker's hop, and they are the clearest example of what the body changed.** A two-tile shelf eleven columns away, whose sites no floor pose reaches, must be offered with a hover beside it and worked from there — the torch placed, the pot broken — with the body's centre recorded at the height it worked from. The walker needed a proven take-off and could lose it; the orb needs a free cell and a clearance.

The `J08` pair runs the same trip with a pot on the floor between the companion and its destination, out of reach where the walk starts, with only lighting and keeping company registered so breaking the pot can be no activity's work. With pot breaking on, a tile of the pot must disappear during the lighting activity **after the body has moved**, with that tick's request and applied movement owner equal to the tick before — which is what makes it incidental rather than a detour — and the lighting attempt must close complete with exactly one productive effect. With pot breaking off, the torch must still be placed and every pot tile must remain. A separate row asks the scan with the live planning allowance already spent: a permitted pot within reach must be left alone and nothing recorded, and with the allowance ended on the same game tick the pot must break, which shows a refused tick keeps the scan's turn.

## Collection, capability and company

`VerifyCollectionContracts` drives the real collection activity and the real bag transfer path. A drop at the bottom of a sealed pit is known-unusable both with one-way drops refused, where it is unreachable, and allowed, where it would strand the companion from a player it can reach, while a drop on the open floor stays usable. A drop that rolls after preparation makes execution hold, and the next preparation walks to where it lies. A stack of fifty valuable bars is worth less with cargo room for two than for fifty; taking the two ends the attempt partial and the full cargo refuses the remainder for capacity. A drop the bag took whole is the companion's own completion, one the player took with nothing transferred is invalid, one split is shared, and a drop already taken by contact pickup leaves no offer. Four drops at the bottom of a sealed pit, every one nearer than a reachable floor drop, must not hide it: the floor drop is offered on the **first** preparation. That row used to assert a bound on how many preparations the delay could take, which made the delay part of the contract; collection asks the reach sense rather than searching, so nothing rations the question and the delay is gone rather than shortened.

`VerifyCapabilityRevision` changes one player-derived capability between two preparations of an activity already holding evidence derived from the old value. Mining inside reach swings from where it is; at a smaller reach the next preparation must send it to a stand that reaches, never the stale pose, and back again when reach grows. Ore out of range at reach 1 must be offered on the very next preparation at reach 11. A trunk refused at reach 0 must be offered at reach 5 on the next preparation rather than after its deferral. Lighting must never walk to a stand the current reach cannot swing from, and must search again on the preparation after reach grows. A copper axe swapped for a gold one must price the same trunk with the gold axe's damage. A known drop's offer, forecast and target must not change with tool reach, because pickup is by contact.

**One row was deleted here rather than loosened**, and the reason is a property of the body: *"a torch site refused under a small reach is offered as soon as the reach grows"* cannot be stated about a companion that hovers. Reach cannot gate whether work is offered at all for a body that can sit beside any free cell — only reach 0 refuses, and only for a solid target it cannot occupy. An air target can never be refused by reach, so the row had no orb meaning to translate into.

`VerifyCompanyLocalMotion` runs the whole brain with the allowances lifted. Its hazard case puts a lava pool, a water pit deep enough to submerge, and a twelve-row pit with a way down and none back inside the calm band of a standing player, and asserts each is what it claims — including the drop, through `RoundTrip` — before running. Over six thousand seeded ticks no held stroll goal, no resolved destination and no body pose may lie over or beside a hazard by the scene's own geometry, which is deliberately not the production predicate; nothing may be recorded as productive work; at least three goals must be strolled to. The idle case, in an empty world with light unmeasured, requires keeping company to be the only activity chosen and allows a rest share between a quarter and three quarters with more than one goal over three game minutes — that long because the random hop is rare enough that one minute drew none on this seed. The same envelope is asserted on a floor rising one row per column, on a scene that first proves a same-row neighbour rule would refuse it. The rim case stands both actors on a slab with open air twelve rows down on both sides, where the rim rule is the only rule that can refuse the slab ends.

`VerifyCourtesy` is the whole brain with only keeping company offered, its resting method held by reflection after the first tick because activating the activity resets local movement and a random stroll would move the reference scene. The player aims a dirt block at the companion's tile without swinging: an empty hand leaves it there (the premise), the block moves it off and keeps it off while staying near the player, and a bow or a torch aimed at the same tile reproduces the empty hand's trajectory tick for tick. The player then walks a floor with a roof directly over a standing body: after a thirty-tick grace the companion may never stand still inside his body or the two columns ahead. The footprint assertion adds a radius before calling the bottom-anchored `BodyTiles`, matching what the positioner does. **The fixture moves the player by position**, so it shows the companion leaving the player's way, not that its body would have blocked a real player.

## What these fixtures deliberately do not establish

A hop near reduced sky gravity, a take-off displaced mid-walk, an incidental torch, an arm that has just fired, a pot inside a protected home, comfortable spacing in real caves, or behaviour under enemy pressure.

## Current state — 15 September 2026

Green. Two premises in this folder were corrected during the body change and are worth knowing about, because both failed in a way that reads as a brain defect:
