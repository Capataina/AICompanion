The nearby-assistance family, plus the two senses every activity in it consumes. These are the fixtures most changed by the body, because almost every one of them used to ask where a body could *stand*.

**`VerifyCandidateFunnel.cs` is gone, not renamed, and the file this guide used to describe under "the torch rule and the funnel" no longer exists.** It read `Chooser.LastScores` against `VerifyTheCourseOwnsTheTick` asserting that same ledger empty, so whichever was satisfied the other had to fail; it had been failing on its own premise since `0bb2c8a` and was retired outright with the chooser in `0bc481a` (`AIC-419`), because its subject — a factor list multiplying to a final — has no course equivalent. Its own rows, and what became of them, are described where the torch-rule section used to attribute them to it, below.

**Which brain a row drives is the first thing to establish before reading its verdict, and since 22 September 2026 there is only one.** `0bb2c8a` put the course on the tick the day before; `AIC-419` then deleted the family chooser, and the migration this folder was in the middle of is finished. Two vocabulary traps come with the port, and both read backwards without warning. **The shared course reader's `Companionship` field is a cost**, carried beside `Final` rather than folded into it, where the family chooser's reunion multiplier was a fraction *kept*: more is worse, and a row ported from one to the other asserts the wrong direction while looking right. And **course nominals are signed net values, not the chooser's 0..1 utilities** — chopping and lighting both lead at negative worth in a perfectly healthy calm scene — so any ported row asserting `raw > 0` as "this work has a real opportunity" carries a premise the course does not share.

`VerifyLightingOpportunityCapture` runs through `--retained-course-lighting` and the default table. **It tests `CaptureLightingOpportunities`, which is a second lighting vocabulary nothing consumes**: that capture emits `lighting-site`, `lighting-deficit` and `lighting-coverage`, and no production code and no discovery source reads any of it — the domain the live brain speaks is `light-target`, from `CaptureAssistanceOpportunities`. So a green run here establishes the capture's own contract and says nothing about the census the companion acts on. Which of the two survives is an open design call. It checks sliced native-site capture, shared darkness cells, unread coverage, private projection against an actual placed ordinary torch followed by a controlled native-blur scan, and snapshot isolation under later scans and global-brightness changes. The before and after maps must use identical masks and decay: `WriteMeasuredLight` deliberately disables propagation and cannot be the before-map for a normal-decay comparison. Unsupported styles, outside-map placement and zero operation allowance remain unresolved. This is a controlled model comparison, not a complete native tile-scanner or live-game acceptance test.

## Keeping company is the fallback, measured on the native brain

`VerifyAssistanceOpportunityDiscovery` runs in the default suite as of 22 September 2026, under the case name `every assistance census reaches its own discovery, keeps its identities and is walked in the rank it published`, and through `--retained-course-opportunities` beside the gathering census checks. **Before that registration nothing ever ran it**: the flag is not in `verify.sh`, `run-case.sh` cannot select a flag, and no ledger run carries a row from it — so `G03`, the seam guard this guide calls its most productive row, had never once been asked by the harness. Its newest row, `G02 discovery walks the census's rank, so a cut slice is the sites the census ranked highest`, is the one that found the rank never reaching the search: twelve light sites published nearest-first, a store that can hold four of them, and the four examined must be the four the census ranked highest rather than the four with the lowest tile keys. It reds on either of the two sorts that used to destroy the rank — the source's own `OrderBy(f => f.Key)` and `DecisionFactSnapshot`'s, which is the one that made removing the first inert. Its pure rows retain separate drop/light/pot opportunities, resume through a new snapshot ID, and distinguish replacement generations. Its native row uses Terraria item slots and the real drop capture: mutate the second stack between one-operation slices, require the frozen quantities and shared denominator, recapture unchanged items, then replace one slot. Unknown geometry must round-trip through JSON as unknown. These assertions cover observation and discovery; they do not prove native placement, complete item lifecycle attribution or course execution.

**`G03 every assistance domain's real capture feeds its own discovery` is the standing seam guard, and it is the most productive row in this folder.** It drives the real captures into the real sources for all five capture-backed domains and asserts each can call its census *exhausted*, rather than asserting a site count — because an empty floor legitimately has no drops, no pots and no dark tiles, so the counts are honestly zero and only the coverage answer separates "looked and found nothing" from "never finished looking". It was written for lighting and pots, whose coverage facts were never published at all: under this tree's rule that optional work does not start on an unanswered search, that meant the companion would **never** have placed a torch or broken a pot, not rarely, with every layer answering honestly and nothing in the record naming a cause. Extended from those three domains to all five, it went red on `chop-coverage` on its first run — a complete, sliced, budgeted trunk census that production never called. The durable property, which is why the extension was worth more than the fix: **two halves of one seam that are each only ever tested against a hand-written stand-in for the other will agree with their fixtures and not with each other**, so a seam earns one row that runs both real halves together. Keeping company is the sixth domain and is deliberately not a source at all.

`VerifyCompanyIsTheFallback` holds the ruling that keeping company is what the companion does when nothing is worth doing, never a job that out-competes one. The scene hands the companion a wooden bow and declares a play-sized screen that follows the player, so the new-job radius and the intent region are the ones play builds. Four rows, each printing its numbers before its verdict:

- a slime above an idle player is hunted within a bound of ticks, rather than hovered beside;
- the same slime, starting past the new-job radius and moving away, is never hunted;
- keeping company's raw value, sampled out to just short of the hard leash, stays at or under the far cap and strictly below what the leash takes past it. The second comparison exists because the first reads the constant it guards: a cap raised back to one passed the first unchanged, and only the second went red;
- inside the region with a column between the orb and the player, keeping company is worth only the floor, because losing sight of the player is not distance.

It does not establish the hunt's own stand refusals seen in play, or how the companion moves inside the region.

**It was one of the last two fixtures that drove `Chooser.Choose` whose subject survived the switch, and neither was about the family chooser.** Its rows are about keeping company's own rejoin arithmetic and where it caps, so they prepare the activity and read what it publishes — `Score()` after a preparation is the same number the row used to take off the chooser's ledger, at its source. Keeping company is the one activity with no course domain, which is the design rather than a gap, so `ReadCourseWorthPerActivity` finds nothing for it and there is no course offer to read instead. `Board`, which prints the numbers into every failure message here, was still reading `LastScores` and therefore printed a row of dashes in exactly the messages a reader needs them for; it reads the shared course reader now and prints both offers.

## The two senses, and why every row is written to fail on the code before them

`VerifyLightAndReachSenses` is the acceptance set for light and reachability as *senses* rather than as private answers. Each row asserts the old answer beside the new one in the same scene, so it fails on the pre-sense code without that code being checked out.

A lit chamber wide enough to fill the torch's hold radius, inside a world dark everywhere else, first requires that the retired ambient scalar — recomputed in the fixture as one mean brightness over every open-air tile of the window, read from the engine rather than from our own code — calls the scene dark. Then it requires the field to read the body's own neighbourhood lit and the real torch to go out saying `lit-room`. The same scene with the heading forty columns out in the dark must raise the torch saying `dark-ahead`, which no single number for the window can express.

A body outside the presented area beside a measured bright heading must hold its torch, which is the case a rule letting an unread query merely abstain puts out. A world lit by nothing but the companion's own torch — presented as the engine would present it, the torch's colour decayed by Manhattan distance over a dark cave — must not read as a lit room, and every tile that torch lights must still be measured at the cave's own dark rather than going unknown, which is the state that hid every pocket beside a torch from placement.

**Carried light is covered by the same discriminator, as one scene with and without a carrier.** A held torch, then a light pet of a different colour and strength, are presented the way the engine presents one — its colour decayed from the carrier's tile, merged into the presented map over the same world light — and every tile the field measured before must read exactly what it read before, with none of them becoming unknown. A scene where the carrier lit nothing as presented would let the row pass against a sense that read the presented map, so the row asserts that too. The passage must still read dark around the body afterwards, which is the behaviour the lane exists for. Two more rows hold the mechanism the whole thing rests on: the world's own light is taken once per scan whether the game draws once or twice per brain tick, which is why the draw observes it at all; and the engine's own `ProcessArea` is driven through its blur state to show that the scan taken before it holds none of the torch that blur merges. A cost row prices the copy and the blur on the lighting area of a 3840x2160 screen.

A dark wing away from a lit body must be offered with a destination the reach sense itself calls `Reachable` rather than `NotYet`. A floor dark everywhere, with only lighting and keeping company registered, must place two torches with keeping company owning no tick between them. And a player on a shelf hanging in the air, where the comfort box holds no tile that is both free and flooded, must still be answered with a reachable tile that closes the gap, declaring `Undeclared` rather than the follow region — a destination outside the objective that declared it would be recorded as a contract violation on every arrival.

Its `e:` rows are the spatial-invalidation set: a retained search survives an edit it never read and dies on one it did, the invalidation margin is the flood's own explored bounds either side of it, a revision older than the record's window is treated as changed, the reach flood refloods for an edit it read and not for one it did not, and a door toggled by anything but the companion announces its rows. The far-outside probe for those rows sits **below the floor**, not above it: a flyer's flood fills the sky, so the unread free space in this world is underneath.

## Trips, hovers and the sites no floor pose can reach

`VerifyAssistanceTrips` drives the real lighting and collection activities through the shared nearby-interaction executor on an island scene: the companion and player stand on a short floor whose own torch sites sit inside a measured lit disc, and the only dark sites and the pot are on the floor of a pit twelve rows below the island edge. Without a way back the pit is a one-way drop, and neither a lighting site nor the pot may be offered under either one-way setting; with drops allowed the lighting refusal must name the missing return. With steps from the pit floor back up, the same pit must offer a site and the pot. The scene asserts its own premise through `MovementQueries.RoundTrip` before any activity is prepared, so a staircase that failed to make a round trip reads as a broken fixture rather than a behaviour result.

The island is built as solid rock with the chamber carved out of it, not as a one-tile shell, so the chamber's air is the only open air near its sites; a shell once let a neighbourhood darkness test read the lit open air outside the chamber, and the row passed while proving nothing.

## The torch rule, on light the engine itself presents

`VerifyTorchPlacementRule` presents light the way the colour engine holds it between a scan and its blur rather than painting brightness per tile. The scan is the world's colour on every tile with solid tiles masked so light decays through them at the engine's solid rate, and placed lights merged in the way the tile scan merges a standing torch; the presented map is the same with carried lights merged by maximum and the engine's own `LightMap.Blur` run over it, all at the game's own global brightness. That is not realism for its own sake. Every older lighting row wrote brightness straight into the presented map at a global brightness of one, the one setting at which no light can exceed one, so a defect living in that difference — a carried torch modelled below the engine's own reading of it — was invisible to all of them while it read a whole cave lit in play. The painted presenter in `VerifyUsefulAssistance` writes its values into the scan and leaves the presented map's decay at zero, so the blur of the scan leaves a painted field exactly as written; a scene that wants the engine's real propagation uses the torch rule's presenter instead.

Its rows were each run red against the production code of its parent with that code stashed and the fixture left in place: the carried torch in a dark cave, the dark room his cursor could light, the search the deadline cut, the stand beyond the known radius and the refusals past the store's old size. Its lit-room and daylight rows were green there and are held by mutations instead — lighting with its darkness stage removed, and the placement reading with only the model unclamped. The two pocket rows — a dark room he walked into already holding a torch, and one he mined wider while holding one — were red against the remembered-darkness rule they replaced, and are held by a mutation that reads the presented map in place of the scan, which is the one edit that puts a carried light back into the world's own light.

**`VerifyCandidateFunnel`'s own rows — a refusal recorded under the wrong stage, the attachment filter without its wall term, the player's cursor centred on his corner, the time factor recorded as one, and the nine-factor course-arithmetic row described above — are not ported anywhere and no longer exist in this tree.** They were a different file's rows, not `VerifyTorchPlacementRule`'s, and `0bc481a` deleted them with the file rather than moving them; anyone looking for that coverage now finds none of it here.

**The executor's exits are driven through a fixture method, not through lighting.** `Sites` is the shared executor with a fixed list of sites asked in list order and nothing of its own, and it can expire the planning deadline the moment gathering ends. That is the one deterministic way to reach the cut inside the stand loop rather than inside a gathering scan, and the one way to order a beyond-radius stand ahead of a refused one without depending on a subclass's costs.

What this family does not establish: the engine's tile scan lighting a placed torch rather than the fixture merging its colour in, legacy lighting, a scan taken while the game is really drawing, and anything about the reader recovering a course's own terms from a played capture (`Tools/SessionReport/Checks/CheckCourseDecisions.cs`'s half, `AIC-420`).

## The census bound: what survives a cut is the nearest sites, not the darkest, and the park is chosen about the player rather than the body

**`VerifyTheCensusPublishesItsBestSites` is what replaced an unbounded light census on 22 September 2026, and the fixture exists because a bound built the obvious way was wrong.** The census used to sweep the player's whole work window and publish a `light-target` fact for every placeable dark tile in it — 1,540 of them on one decision of the 22 September capture, most of a 125x125 square underground — and discovery spent one allowance unit, two tracked reads and a deserialisation per site regardless of whether the search would ever reach it, so the census set the decision's cost by coordinate rather than by anything about the world. `RankCensusSitesByWorth.PublishTheBest` cuts the sweep to what the window could ever use: two torches must be more than `CompanionTorches.SpacingTiles` apart in both axes, so a window can never usefully hold more than `MostSitesAWindowCanHold` computes from its size and the spacing — 196 at the current radius, moving with either if either changes.

It was tried as a tighter reading first — bucket the window on the spacing grid and publish the best site of each cell — and that version reddened `J08 a lighting trip breaks a permitted pot in passing`: two sites nine tiles apart shared one cell, the rule kept one and withheld the other, and the decision left lighting for a dedicated pot trip nobody owned rather than merely moving the torch two tiles. The property underneath: two sites inside one exclusion are substitutes in *value* and not in what the course prices, which is a route through a contact pose the census cannot compute, so a census that picks between them is deciding something it has no evidence about. The fixture asserts a global count instead — a global cut never fires below the bound — and requires everything a crowded-but-under-bound sweep found to publish in full, which is the row that caught the cell version.

The surviving order is **tier, then squared tile distance from the intent region's heading, then darkness, then tile**, and darkness is deliberately last: a light site's `Amount` is the binary `IsDark`, so every usable site scores exactly one and a top-K by it is the arbitrary tile order already given, wearing a ranking's name, and a darkness-first cut would keep the blackest tiles of a cave the companion is nowhere near while dropping the dim ones under its feet. Cost is measured from the heading rather than from the body so the published set does not depend on where the flying body happens to be this tick. **A site the cut drops is a third value** — swept, real, `not-yet-ranked` — carried on `light-coverage`'s new `withheld=N` field rather than folded into `assistance-target-not-observed`, because a bound with no count would make every withheld site read as a proven absence, and this tree's own rule is that a bound that ran out is a third value rather than a refusal. A site the course is already working is pinned and published beside the bound rather than out of it, so the fact count can exceed 196 by exactly the number of sites in flight, which is **one** — the pin matches a single bound target — and the audit's per-kind margin is that one rather than the sixteen it carried until 22 September 2026.

**Two of the nine rows here are about the wiring rather than the helper, and they exist because the other seven were not.** Every row that hands `PublishTheBest` a keep predicate of its own proves the helper keeps what it is told to keep and says nothing about whether the census tells it anything: a sentinel neutralised the production call site's predicate to `_ => false` and all seven stayed green. `the census's own call keeps the bound site` drives `CaptureAssistanceOpportunities.PublishLightSites`, which is the call `CaptureLighting` makes, with a sweep past the real bound at the real work radius and the pinned site made the worst thing in it by every term the rank uses, so its survival is attributable to the pin alone; that mutation now reds it. `the census derives the pinned site from the published course` is the other half and is a source pin, named as one: the live light window is intersected with the engine's own processed area, which is empty in a fixture, so no headless sweep publishes a light site at all and no scene can watch the census keep one across a rank change. The end-to-end grading is the play-measures replay's window row.

**`VerifyTheParkIsAboutThePlayer` holds the company park's tiebreak, and it replaces a tiebreak that was silently anchored to the body.** `ClearestInsideCorner` maximises `MovementQueries.CombinedClearance`, capped at eight tiles, so any open room has a whole ring of corners sitting on the cap and the tiebreak decides among them; that tiebreak used to be nearest to the body, which meant a park that arrived low never climbed (nothing in the comparison knows which way is up) and a park that re-picked itself every rescore as the body drifted, cancelling the journey to the last one — which is what the 22 September capture's `WithPlayer: asked 27, reached 1, abandoned 26` against `FireFrom: asked 212, reached 188` was reporting, and what the owner had already named as "it never stayed up." The tiebreak is nearness to the player now: horizontally where he is heading, vertically a few tiles above the top of his head, derived from the sense's own centre and feet rather than from a fixed height so a player of a different height carries the band with him. The row asks the positioner the same question twice with the body at two different heights over the same column, and requires the same park both times, above his head, within a tile of his heading, and at the lowest corner as clear as the best — measured off `ClearanceField` rather than recomputed from the rule, so it cannot pass by agreeing with the implementation's own arithmetic. Its second row holds the band against the other way of reading "above his head": in a column clear from floor to sky the park must sit a few tiles up rather than at the column's very top or hovering at the player's own level, because the band is a preference among equally clear corners and not a pull to the ceiling.

**There is no hover ceiling, and the park's height is bounded by the region's own top edge rather than by any ceiling constant.** `HoverCeilingTiles` is not merely unread — `7902729` removed it from the tunables and from README, because nothing read it and two sentences taught a rule the body never had — so a search for it returns only `Position/CLAUDE.md:151`, which records the removal correctly, and this paragraph. Anyone reading an older account of a ceiling is reading a rule that never existed.

**The shelf rows are the orb's version of the walker's hop, and they are the clearest example of what the body changed.** A two-tile shelf eleven columns away, whose sites no floor pose reaches, must be offered with a hover beside it and worked from there — the torch placed, the pot broken — with the body's centre recorded at the height it worked from. The walker needed a proven take-off and could lose it; the orb needs a free cell and a clearance.

**The shelf's pot row asserts a collection trip, since the owner ruled on 23 September 2026 that a pot is a collection task.** `hover pot: a pot no floor pose reaches is a collection trip, broken from a hover beside it and credited to collection` requires three positive things, because each alone is satisfied by a wrong route: the course bound the pot (read off the published course while the run goes), collection owned the body on the tick the pot broke, and collection's attempt completed with the break as its one productive effect. From 22 to 23 September the same row asserted the opposite — that no attempt of the method ever ran and the pot broke in passing under keeping company — because no activity then performed a bound pot; `3826d32` carries that episode. The lighting arm is unchanged in kind and its done-test is a torch on any shelf site rather than on the site the activity's own search offered, because the hand works the site the course bound and the two need not be the same tile.

**The `A2` and `A3` rows are what hold the hand to the course.** `A2 the hand walks to the drop the course bound` and `A2 the hand breaks the pot the course bound` put a near and a far target in front of collection, bind the far one through the real census and binder (`BindCensusSite`, zero-travel because which target the hand works does not depend on the priced journey), confirm as a premise that the activity's own search prefers the near one, hand the step through `OwnCurrentActivity.Select`, and require the hand to work the far one; planting the old behaviour back — the drop hand reading the search's candidate, the tile hand reading the search's target — reds both. `A3 a pot in reach during a recognised encounter is not broken in passing; the same pot with no encounter is` drives the in-passing scan directly after freezing a course observation (`ObserveForTheCourse`): under a blood moon with the player above the surface line the course refuses the pot as `incidental-optional-work-suppressed-by-danger` and it stands, and with no encounter it breaks with the interaction carrying the accepted binding's id; removing the relevance check from `AcceptIncidental` reds it. Headless, a broken pot spawns no items (`world drops now 0` on the trip row), so no row here can assert the pot's contents becoming collectable drops; that half is play's to show.

The `J08` group is four rows now rather than a pair. The first two run the same trip with a pot on the floor between the companion and its destination, out of reach where the walk starts, with only lighting and keeping company registered so breaking the pot can be no activity's work. With pot breaking on, a tile of the pot must disappear during the lighting activity **after the body has moved**, with that tick's request and applied movement owner equal to the tick before — which is what makes it incidental rather than a detour — and the lighting attempt must close complete with exactly one productive effect. With pot breaking off, the torch must still be placed and every pot tile must remain. A third row asks the scan with the live planning allowance already spent: a permitted pot within reach must be left alone and nothing recorded, and with the allowance ended on the same game tick the pot must break, which shows a refused tick keeps the scan's turn; it freezes a course observation first, because the scan proposes the census's own sites and the course accepts against that observation. **The fourth, `J08 a pot the course binds is a trip collection performs: an attempt, the break and its credit`, is the trip half of the ruling**: torch placement off, both work policies disabled, nothing else in the world, so the pot is the only usable opportunity — its premise requires the census to have admitted the pot as usable, and its verdict is that the course binds it, collection holds the body while it is bound, the pot breaks, and collection's attempt completes with the break as its one productive effect. Until 23 September 2026 this row asserted the reverse, that no `break-pot` step was ever bound.

## Collection, capability and company

`VerifyCollectionContracts` drives the real collection activity and the real bag transfer path. A drop at the bottom of a sealed pit is known-unusable both with one-way drops refused, where it is unreachable, and allowed, where it would strand the companion from a player it can reach, while a drop on the open floor stays usable. A drop that rolls after the course bound it is retired by the binder's `ValidateNextUse` as `assistance-application-changed`, and bound again it is walked to where it lies; the falling-drop rows count a retirement of the bound step, on every tick of the fall, where they once counted the activity's own holds. Every row here that walks a drop hands collection its step the way the tick does (`WalkBound`: the census site, the real binder, `OwnCurrentActivity.Select`), because the hand works only the step it is handed. A stack of fifty valuable bars is worth less with cargo room for two than for fifty; taking the two ends the attempt partial and the full cargo refuses the remainder for capacity. A drop the bag took whole is the companion's own completion, one the player took with nothing transferred is invalid, one split is shared, and a drop already taken by contact pickup leaves no offer. Four drops at the bottom of a sealed pit, every one nearer than a reachable floor drop, must not hide it: the floor drop is offered on the **first** preparation. That row used to assert a bound on how many preparations the delay could take, which made the delay part of the contract; collection asks the reach sense rather than searching, so nothing rations the question and the delay is gone rather than shortened.

`VerifyCapabilityRevision` changes one player-derived capability between two preparations of an activity already holding evidence derived from the old value. Mining inside reach swings from where it is; at a smaller reach the next preparation must send it to a stand that reaches, never the stale pose, and back again when reach grows. Ore out of range at reach 1 must be offered on the very next preparation at reach 11. A trunk refused at reach 0 must be offered at reach 5 on the next preparation rather than after its deferral. Lighting must never walk to a stand the current reach cannot swing from, and must search again on the preparation after reach grows. A copper axe swapped for a gold one must price the same trunk with the gold axe's damage. A known drop's offer, forecast and target must not change with tool reach, because pickup is by contact.

**One row was deleted here rather than loosened**, and the reason is a property of the body: *"a torch site refused under a small reach is offered as soon as the reach grows"* cannot be stated about a companion that hovers. Reach cannot gate whether work is offered at all for a body that can sit beside any free cell — only reach 0 refuses, and only for a solid target it cannot occupy. An air target can never be refused by reach, so the row had no orb meaning to translate into.

`VerifyCompanyLocalMotion` runs the whole brain with the allowances lifted. Its pool case puts a lava pool and a water pit deep enough to submerge inside the calm band of a standing player and asserts each is what it claims before running. Every liquid has been air to the orb since 15 September 2026, so the pools are space the body may use rather than hazards: over the seeded run every resolved destination must be a place the body fits and inside the region it can come back from, and nothing may be recorded as productive work. Until then the case also required no destination or body pose over or beside either pool. The idle case, in an empty world with light unmeasured, requires keeping company to be the only activity chosen and allows a rest share between a quarter and three quarters with more than one goal over three game minutes — that long because it was written against the random hop, which was rare enough that one minute drew none on this seed. The accompanying motion is a tour now and the body moves on essentially every tick, so the envelope's lower bound is slack rather than discriminating; what actually holds coverage is `VerifyAccompanyingThePlayer`'s reach-both-outer-thirds row in `../Movement/`.
