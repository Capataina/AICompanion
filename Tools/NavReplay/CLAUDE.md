# NavReplay — the game-free movement core, proved without Terraria

`Program.cs` compiles the movement core under `Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Movement/` — everything except `TerrariaIntegration/` — with no game assembly at all, and runs it against text worlds. It is the first check for a navigation claim, because the search, the contact, the smoothing and the steering it runs are the same source the mod runs rather than a copy: the core is one compile unit here and one in the mod, and a property proved here is a property of the orb the game moves. It launches nothing and infers nothing from a picture.

```
NavReplay/
├─ CLAUDE.md                     this guide
├─ NavReplay.csproj              lists its own sources by name, then globs the movement core and the tunables it reads
├─ Program.cs                    the self-test's ledger rows, and the scenario extractor's entry
├─ ReadScenarioBlocks.cs         a scenario file as blocks: header keys, the glyph grid, the actors and the trail
├─ VerifyCorridorMiddle.cs       the clearance cost, the smoother and the steering law, each measured against the same corridor
├─ VerifyArrivalHolds.cs         a navigator driven with the motor's own law to its goal, then held there and watched
├─ VerifyAnchorsAndEvade.cs      a hover anchor carried across a wall or outliving its goal, and a dodge between two shots free to use the lava below
├─ VerifyEvadeKeepsTheJob.cs     the captured slope pin flown through the movement boundary, a route running above a predicted hit, and a cornered body in a pool
├─ VerifyEvadeBendsAccompanying.cs a body keeping the player company, kept with no hit and bent by a hit on its own walk's path
├─ VerifyAccompanyingTargetIsContinuous.cs the accompanying target resuming at the body after a job, a journey and a downing, and moving at the walk's pace while the player turns
├─ VerifyAccompanyPrefersClearance.cs      the accompanying walk climbing off a floor toward clearer air
├─ VerifyAccompanyGetsPastAnObstruction.cs the 10:05 capture's own pocket, replayed: the walk must not live on rock standing inside its box
├─ VerifyAccompanyStaysAboveHisHead.cs     an empty room: no leg may be drawn below the floor the caller names
├─ VerifyTheWideLegUnderATravellingPlayer.cs a box travelling along a tunnel narrower than it: what a leg drawn from the whole box does to a walking player's companion
├─ VerifyTheEscapeCostsTheSameEveryTick.cs a footprint the player never lifts: the redraw's tile reads a tick, flat rather than climbing
├─ VerifyEvadeUnderSustainedFire.cs a sentinel's seven hazard scenes flown with the evade and by the job alone, a shot corridor among them
├─ VerifyNavigatorKeepsMoving.cs the body flying while its search runs, and a finished search not run again for an unchanged goal
├─ VerifyNavigatorConclusions.cs what a kept conclusion about a goal may outlive: drift, an edit, an interrupt, a jittering goal
├─ VerifyMirrorExactness.cs      the reflection transform proved an involution over the committed corpus and a flood proved symmetric
├─ MirrorScenarioWorlds.cs       a block reflected left to right: tiles, glyph pairs, the actors, the trail and the orb's centre
├─ VerifyTheCutReasonRidesInTheHeader.cs a reason written ahead of every parsed header key, requiring the mirror to read it unmoved
├─ VerifySweptClearSkipsOnlyFarTiles.cs  the swept test's unread tiles checked against the exact distance over seeded segments
├─ ExtractScenarioFromCapture.cs a recorded tick's terrain cut out of a capture into a committed-format scenario, with an optional `--reason` appended after every key
└─ ReconstructTerrainWindow.cs   the terrain around a recorded moment rebuilt from the capture's snapshots; compiled into ../SessionReport too
```

**`NavReplay.csproj` lists its own sources**, because `EnableDefaultCompileItems` is off: a file added to this folder and not added there does not fail to build, it is simply absent, and whatever row it carried reports nothing. The movement core arrives through a glob, so a new core file is compiled here without anyone editing the project, and a core file that names a Terraria type breaks this build before it breaks the boundary script.

## Two commands

```
dotnet run -p:UseAppHost=false --project Tools/NavReplay -- --self-test
dotnet run -p:UseAppHost=false --project Tools/NavReplay -- --extract-scenario <capture.tsv> <tick> [--size WxH] [--reason "<why this window>"]
```

The self-test is a handful of ledger rows, each a property of the core with no scene of the walker's kind behind it, and `sh Tools/verify.sh` runs it after the ledger's own self-test. It runs with the millisecond allowances lifted, because a corpus verdict is an oracle only when it cannot depend on how busy the machine was; nothing here is about a deadline.

## The rows, and what kills each

**The route through a corridor sits nearer its middle than its walls, and the steered body stays there.** One corridor with a bend, flooded twice: once with the clearance term switched off, once with it on. The unpriced route must hug the wall it starts beside — a premise assertion, because without it the comparison measures nothing — and the priced route must sit nearer the middle by a clear margin and within a tile of it on average. Smoothing is then applied and must not pull the route back toward the walls, and every smoothed segment must be clear of them by the contact's own swept test. Finally the navigator steers a body along the result under the motor's law, and the body must arrive, fly within a tile of the middle on average, and never overlap a wall. Two measures travel with the row: the priced route's mean offset from the middle and the steered body's, both in pixels and both better when lower. The row is what refused the first smoother, which skipped from one wall-hugging end of the corridor to the other and undid the search, and what raised the turn authority until the steered body kept clearance through the bend.

**A body the navigator has called arrived stays arrived, on one search, reads as at rest, and is never still.** A navigator is driven along a straight corridor tick by tick with `OrbPace.Step`, the motor's own law; once it reports arrived the row keeps driving for a hundred and twenty ticks and requires the status to hold, the search identity not to change, the body never to drift past the settle radius, its speed at the end to read under the settled threshold in `BehaviourWeights`, and — the owner's ruling that the orb is never strictly still — no run of ticks under the session reader's still speed as long as its still run. It also requires the ramp from rest to nine tenths of the cap to fall inside a band declared before the easing change: well over the twelve ticks the single shared acceleration took, which read as a snap, and inside a second. Two measures travel with it, the ticks held and the ticks to cap. It fails on the walker's own mislanding shape — a body coasting out of the radius and replanning — and it was made to fail before it passed: a navigator that pushed at the pace after arrival lost the arrival after six ticks and moved to a second search. When the still run trips, the failure prints the last two dozen ticks of centre, velocity, desired velocity and hover target; that trace is what found the hover's two defects, a seek that slowed to nothing whenever its target doubled back and a wall mirror that bounced its target back into the ceiling, and `Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Movement/Steering/CLAUDE.md` carries both.

**A hover anchor never pulls the body back behind a wall, and a dodge between two shots is free to use the lava below them.** A body carried across a wall mid-wait, and a wait interrupted and followed by a new unreachable goal, must each drift a bounded distance from where they now are rather than back toward the old anchor; and a body squeezed between two closing shots over lava must bend, reach into the lava, and never be hit. Until 15 September 2026 the property was that the dodge never touched the lava; every liquid became air to the orb that day, and the same scene now requires the lava to be used. It fails on an anchor that outlives its goal or survives the swept line to it being blocked, and on a dodge that still treats liquid as something to stay out of.

**The body flies toward a goal while its search runs, and a finished search is not run again for an unchanged goal.** Four scenes from the second play of 0.27.0: the recorded tick-10418 plan dump, whose pocket the search proves unreachable once and hands back; a goal ten tiles into a floor, approached; a sealed pocket past the node limit in a large open world, where each search must begin nearer than the last; and a long search through a wall's far gap, flown while it runs. It fails on a navigator that hovers until a search returns or asks a finished question again every tick.

**A kept conclusion about a goal is forgotten once its goal has moved or its terrain changed, and only a finished search settles the body short.** Seven scenes, three of them a sentinel's findings against 59b184a that were red at 530b16d: a settled-short answer and a proven absence each drifted past where they were true, and a tile edited elsewhere after a node limit. The rest hold the mechanisms the fix keeps, each placed to kill one mutant: an edit opening a proven pocket is searched again, a goal behind a surface is searched once and hovered at, an attempt interrupted on its first node limit and resumed from the same place searches again, and a goal jittering inside the replan distance does not re-aim a partial route into rock. **The interrupt scene holds the body still until the interrupt, and that is its premise:** left to fly the pending search's partial route, the body is past the progress distance by the node limit, the resumed ask is earned on distance alone, and a kept node-limit answer becomes invisible — which is how the first version of the scene let the mutant through. One mutant is not killed, a route found after a partial flight joined at the search's start rather than beside the body: its cost is bounded by the join radius, and a scene that shows it needs a search starved enough to lend a partial route longer than that.
**A dodge bends the job's flight only where the job's own flight would meet harm, and never presses the body into a wall.** Five scenes, each driven through `CoordinateMovement` the way the brain tick drives it — the job's controls, then `Evade`, then the motor's law and the contact. The first is the third orb play's slope pin, the capture's plan dump at the stuck tick cropped verbatim into the row, with a hazard placed just below the lowest point the unbent flight reaches, so it lies beside the route rather than on it: a premise requires the straight-line reading (the layer asked with no forecast) to bend on some tick of that flight, and the row then requires the body to arrive, never be still for the session reader's still run and never be bent. Without the premise the predicate once fired nowhere along the route, and the row passed with the forecast switched off. A fifth scene puts a body against a wall with the job asking into it and requires the layer to refuse a heading for going nowhere and choose one that moves the body at least a radius; before it existed, switching the going-nowhere rule off left every row green. The second is the same scene with a hit waiting a few pixels along the route, so the headings have to choose, and the one pressing into the slope must lose to one that moves. The third is a route descending past a pillar and running above a floor a hit is predicted on, which must not be bent at all: holding the descent's first velocity drives the body into that floor, so only a keep test that flies the job's own curving steering leaves it alone. The fourth is a body inside a pool with a shot closing and the job asking into it, which must turn away. Each was shown red before its mechanism existed, in the versions that stood before every liquid became air to the orb on 15 September 2026: at the commit before the change the first two sat still asking `-9,0` into the slope for their whole run, the third, then a route along a pool's surface, bent 101 ticks of a dry flight, and the fourth flew into the shot because every heading read as hit on its first wet tick. The third and fourth were restated that day, the pool giving way to a predicted hit in the third and staying as scenery in the fourth. Every failure prints the ticks' job, ask, bend and position.

**An accompanying body is bent only where its own walk across the region would be hit, and kept everywhere else.** Keeping the player company is a walk across a moving box, and the keep test must fly that walk rather than an earlier request's steering. Two scenes in an open room with the box moving right at a walking pace: with the predicate active and predicting nothing, every tick's applied controls are the walk's own; with a hit placed on the first scene's own trajectory well past the lookahead, the runs match until the first tick the verdict reports a hit, that verdict's predicted tick lands within a few ticks of where the unbent body really entered the hit, that tick is bent, and the bent body never enters it. The prediction is the part that proves the forecast: a keep test flying a drift toward a target held still reports the hit late or not at all, and a body the walk carries at the region's pace is already on top of it.

**Under sustained fire a dodge takes no more hits than the job alone and never keeps the body from arriving.** A sentinel's seven hazard scenes — a floating enemy parked on the line with and without a routed obstacle, a stream of shots across the route, a goal inside a standing enemy, a two-tile corridor with a shot down it every sixty ticks, and two bodies holding a spot against a wall — are each flown twice through `CoordinateMovement`, once with `Evade` and once by the job alone. Every scene requires no more hit ticks with the evade and no still run of ten ticks, and the four whose goal is not itself a hazard require the evade to arrive wherever the job alone does. The hazard predicate answers nothing past the dodge lookahead, as the production predicate does, so the row cannot pass on foresight the game does not have. The corridor is the scene the rule exists for: without the retreat budget the body flees ahead of each shot and never arrives, and with it the body arrives late, which the row deliberately does not bound.

**The accompanying target starts where the body is and never moves faster than the walk.** Six scenes in an open room with the box standing still, so every motion of the target is the walk's own. Three walk the target to the right of the box, then hover the body at a job, fly it to a place, or hold it for a downing, each for three hundred ticks on the box's left, and require the first accompanying target to be within one walk step of the body and the next ninety ticks never to move it faster than the step with the open part's edge easing across it. One enters the walk from behind a travelling player, beyond the box's inset, and requires the same first step. Two walk the target into the part a left lead closes and turn the player round, the lead sweeping through zero or flipping in one tick, and require the same per-tick bound and that the target ends inside the part left open. Each interruption scene is premised on the walk having really been on the right, and each turning scene on the target sitting in the part that closes, or the row passes on nothing. At the commit before the fix the three interruptions put the first target 399, 413 and 409 px from the body, the entry 264 px, and the turn moved the target 201 px in one tick at lead −9.

**The accompanying walk tours what it can reach rather than living on a wall inside its box, and this row's scene is the capture's own terrain.** The 10:05 capture of 22 September 2026 has the body's `npc_px` x frozen at 55733 for more than two hundred ticks while its y slides, at a recorded clearance of 0.1 px, under `control_source=accompany` with the navigator idle. The row replays it: `--extract-scenario` at tick 1,950 cut the terrain into `Tools/Scenarios/extracted-2026-09-22_10-05-56-125-tick-1950.txt`, and the row drives `Accompany` over it with the recorded box centre, half-size, derived lead and body position for six hundred ticks, requiring no run longer than sixty ticks within two pixels of rock — a second of play, against the capture's two hundred. A premise asserts first that the body really does start against the rock, because a scene that does not reproduce the contact is measuring something else and would be green for nothing.

**Its first version drew what the reading described and the defect was not there**, which is the finding worth more than the row: one wall from ceiling to floor with the box's far third behind it, and the walk handled it at 101 % coverage of the reachable width with a longest hug of seven ticks. The real geometry is a pocket rather than a wall — a five-wide pillar, rock closing overhead into a westward-opening wedge, a floor two rows under the body rather than directly beneath it, and the only way out west along two rows and then down and round — and on it the unfixed walk sat on the rock for 600 of 600 ticks. **A scene drawn from a description of a defect tests the description.** With the two-stage draw the same scene reads 3 of 600 and the body ends east of the pillar on the player's side; `Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Movement/Steering/CLAUDE.md` carries the rule.

**The accompanying walk draws no leg below the floor its caller names.** An empty room, deliberately — the moment terrain is inside the box the escape branch may legitimately take the walk below the floor, and that exemption belongs to the row above — with a box whose lower part reaches well past the floor line, the body starting above it, and every tick's target required to stay at or above it over six hundred ticks. The *target* is what is asserted rather than the body, and the distinction is the point: the body lags its target and drifts around it, so a body a few pixels under the line is the hover doing its job while a target under the line is the tour having aimed there. The body's own worst depth is printed beside it so a reader can see the difference rather than infer it, and a premise asserts first that the floor really does cut into the part the walk would otherwise use, because otherwise an unclamped tour satisfies the row by geometry. Removing the clamp reddens it at 149 of 600 ticks aimed below the floor, worst 25.0 px.

**The corpus mirror is exact.** Every committed block is reflected twice and must come back as itself line for line, beside a check that at least one block actually changed once, because a transform that returned its input would satisfy an identity perfectly. Floor slopes must flip to their pairs, a symmetric glyph must survive, a short row is padded with solid on the right before it is reversed or the reflection moves that wall across the window, and the header's recorded tiles and the orb's centre reflect through the window's own span. A left-right symmetric room is then flooded both ways and must reach the same corners and the same goal, which is what makes the mirror mean something about the search rather than only about the file.

The mirror is here as a transform and a proof of the transform, not as a replay of the corpus: the walker's corpus verdict — plan every block, follow every route, compare the two directions — was a verdict about a body and a graph that no longer exist. What is kept is the property that nothing about a tile world prefers a direction, so a route proven one way must be proven the other, and it is checked on one symmetric room rather than across a corpus of walker fixtures.

## Cutting a scenario out of a recording

`--extract-scenario` cuts the terrain around the companion at a recorded tick out of a capture's own `terrain-snapshot` events, with the orb's centre from `npc_px`, the destination the brain asked for on that tick, the player's feet and their trail, and writes it in the committed scenario format `Tools/Scenarios/CLAUDE.md` describes. It is how a failure the player can feel becomes a window the world run replays, and it is what produced both committed checkpoints. **A tile no snapshot covered is written solid and counted in the reported coverage, never left as air**: the glyph alphabet reads anything outside it as air, so missing terrain would become open sky and a window with holes in it would read as a window with an easy route. Snapshots are joined to the row on the recorder's own stopwatch rather than on the tick, because the two streams have different producers and only the elapsed millisecond means the same thing in both. **Coverage is coverage as *last written*, not as it stood at the tick**, because the recorder writes a chunk only when it has changed since it last wrote one, so the header carries how far behind the tick the oldest contributing snapshot was, and that number prices a full-coverage window rather than the percentage. Which recorded columns are pixels and which are tiles is read off the column and never inferred from the text.

**The reconstruction itself is `ReconstructTerrainWindow.cs`, and it has two consumers that want opposite things from an unknown tile.** The extractor closes it as solid for the reason above; the session reader's tick picture (`../SessionReport`, `--picture` and `--pictures`) paints it its own colour, because a picture that drew missing terrain as rock would be read as rock. So the file returns what is known apart from what is drawn and each caller chooses its unknown glyph, and it lives here and is compiled into `SessionReport.csproj` by path, the way the ledger's row writer is shared, because the two tools are separate executables and a rule about which snapshot counts must not be able to differ between a cut fixture and a drawn picture. It names no game type and no FNA type. Moving the extractor onto it was proven inert on 24 September 2026 by cutting ticks 900, 1,820 and 2,300 of `2026-09-22_10-05-56-125` before and after and comparing the three files with `cmp`, byte for byte. The one behaviour it changed is on a damaged sidecar: a snapshot that cannot be read is counted in `Malformed` rather than thrown mid-read, and the extractor still refuses to cut a window when any is. **Every unreadable snapshot reaches that count**: a line that names `terrain-snapshot` and does not parse is handed on marked `Unreadable` rather than skipped, and a snapshot with no tile payload counts as one with the wrong dimensions does. The first version of the shared file skipped both silently where the extractor it replaced had thrown, so a sidecar cut mid-line produced a fixture with a hole nobody was told about; measured 25 September 2026 by truncating one snapshot line of `2026-09-22_10-05-56-125`'s sidecar, the old build cut tick 900 at exit 0 and this one refuses at exit 70 naming the count, while the clean sidecar still cuts tick 829 to the same grid as the committed fixture.

**`--reason` writes why the window was cut onto the header, and it is the file rather than a guide that has to carry it.** A committed scenario outlives the session that cut it by months and the file is the whole of what a later reader has, so a reason recorded in `Tools/Scenarios/CLAUDE.md` instead is one fact in two homes and the guide is the one that drifts — three windows cut on 22 September 2026 were exactly that until the flag existed. It is appended after every recorded key for a reason worth a case of its own: the header is parsed by *first occurrence* of each key, `MirrorScenarioWorlds.MirrorHeader` taking `goal ` and reading to the next space, so free text written ahead of the provenance is read as the data. `VerifyTheCutReasonRidesInTheHeader` puts every one of those words inside a reason deliberately and requires the parse to be unmoved; writing the reason first instead reddens it by rewriting the reason's own coordinates as the header's. The flag is optional and an absent reason writes nothing, because a tick somebody was curious about has no reason to invent.

The extractor's own liquid fidelity is the text world's: a wet tile is water or lava with a full cell, and the walker's captures carry `~` in their snapshots. Both committed checkpoint windows hold no wet tile in the saved world they are replayed in; the snapshots' `~` tiles lie outside those windows.

**`NavReplay.csproj` compiles `Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Diagnostics/ProfileBrainSections.cs` beside the movement core**, because the navigator opens profiler sections. It names no game type, and nothing here calls `BeginTick`, so every scope in this tool is inert and costs one branch.

## Traps

- Marker glyphs are air. Move actors through the header keys — `orb x,y` is the centre in pixels — never by writing a marker over a slope or a platform.
- `MovementQueries.World`, `FreeSpaceSearch.WorldOverride`, `ClearanceField.Shared` and `MovementQueries.Hazards` are process statics the core reads; a case that plants a world or a set of hazard boxes and does not clear it hands the next case its terrain. `Program.cs`'s inline reset (`EmitLedgerRows.ResetBeforeCase`) clears all four before every case, including the enemy boxes one component publishes for another to read within a tick, since 0366809 — before it, only five fixtures cleared `Hazards` themselves in their own setup (`VerifyAccompanyGetsPastAnObstruction.cs`, `VerifyAccompanyStaysAboveHisHead.cs`, `VerifyAccompanyPrefersClearance.cs`, `VerifyTheEscapeCostsTheSameEveryTick.cs`, `VerifyTheWideLegUnderATravellingPlayer.cs`), which fixed the one reader somebody remembered and left the next one order-dependent. Those five local clears stay: they are harmless and they document what each scene assumes about the hazard set it starts from. Any new row relying on process state does the same.
- `OrbPace` is written by the motor in the game and by nothing here unless a row writes it; a row that does not is steering the body a plain player would produce, from the fallback in `BehaviourWeights`.
- A green self-test is evidence about the core. It never substitutes for the native suite, where the same contact runs against Terraria's own tiles, or for a world run, where the whole brain runs behind a recorded player.
