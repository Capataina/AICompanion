# Debug — the overlay and the record

Two instruments for the same question, "why did it do that": the overlay answers it live above the companion's head, and the telemetry answers it afterwards from a file, which is the one that matters when the behaviour was watched by someone dictating and not by an agent.

```
Debug/
├─ CLAUDE.md
├─ BrainOverlay.cs    the overlay: scores, danger, request, spot, both weapons' expected damage, path dots, threat labels
├─ BrainTelemetry.cs  one tab-separated line per companion tick to Telemetry/<timestamp>.tsv under the repository, and a tile-window dump per failed plan (throttled) or per detected scenario (not) to Telemetry/<timestamp>-plans.txt beside it, each carrying the player's trail
└─ ScenarioCapture.cs the seven detectors that turn a playtest failure into a dump under its own reason: a follow failure, a traversal fault, a stuck run, a hit through an approved dodge, a missed mode, a one-way place entered, and the tier holding the body out of a player it can only reach one-way; thresholds and cooldowns live here because they tune the instrument and not the brain
```

## Every failure the player can feel becomes a block the tool replays

The design's pass line is the player's own: if the player got through, the companion must. `ScenarioCapture.Watch` runs on every recorded tick and keeps four counters. Walking with the player, more than a leash of tiles behind for seconds, and no nearer than when the count began is a follow failure (a companion closing the gap at the player's pace is doing its job, and the Codex review of the first version showed the bare distance test firing every cooldown on an ordinary chase); a body that has not moved for seconds while the follower had a path is stuck, counted here and not on the navigator's own counter, which every replan resets; life lost within a second of a reflex approving a dodge, lava and fire excluded, is a hit through a dodge, which means the simulation the dodge was judged against was wrong; the player mining or chopping for seconds of activity, pauses of up to a second allowed because the ore-hit marker lives less than a second, while the matching action scored zero throughout, is a missed mode, which is the pickaxe icon never showing. Every duration is in game ticks, and every counter resets when a session opens, so a previous world's dodge cannot label the next companion's first scratch. Each fires at most once per cooldown and writes the same tile window the failed-plan dump writes, with the reason in the header, the start at the companion's feet and the goal at the navigator's goal or the player's feet. Every window, whichever path wrote it, carries a `trail` line: the player's feet tiles over the last while, oldest first, one per change of tile, kept on the player sense. The replay tool reads that line back and reports how many trail tiles fall inside the window and the first one the grid refuses, either not standable or outside the region the companion can reach from its start, so a failed follow reads as "the grid refuses tile X, which the player stood in" rather than as a red verdict.

The plan dump is the instrument for "why could it not get there": the navigator calls `DumpPlan` on every plan that returned nothing or only a partial path, and at most once every five seconds the writer appends the tile window spanning the start, the goal and the player's feet (padded, cut to a readable size on the start's side, because the first missing link is near the start; the player is inside it because "could it have reached the player" is the replay tool's second verdict, and a goal picked above a pit said nothing about a player six rows below the window) as one character per tile, written through `TextTileWorld.Glyph` in `../DecisionMatrix/Navigation/World/` so the replay tool reads back exactly what was written: `#` solid, `=` platform, `_` half block, `\` and `/` floor slopes (solid below the line drawn), `<` and `>` ceiling slopes (solid on the side the bracket points away from), `~` water or honey, `L` lava, `o` air the body can stand in, `.` other air, with `S` start, `G` goal, `E` where a partial path ends, `N` the companion's feet and `P` the player's feet drawn over air tiles only, and a `markers` line under the header naming every one of those positions outright, because on a half block or a floor slope the feet tile is the supporting tile and a marker written there erased the support from the replay (2026-09-08 Codex review); the parser takes the line over the grid and still reads old dumps off the grid. The header line carries the tick, the reason, the expansions used and the window's bounds. It exists because the third run of 2026-09-08 showed 1,136 expansions on every plan for its last 3,468 ticks (about a minute at sixty a second) with the player over 200 tiles below, and nothing in the per-tick line says which tile the grid refused.

`BrainTelemetry.cs` opens a file on world load under the mod's source folder (`Main.SavePath/ModSources/AICompanion/Telemetry`, which is this repository; the folder is in `.gitignore` and `buildIgnore`), writes a header naming every column, then one line per tick from the NPC's `AI` after the body has synced: the state (up, downed, player dead), the action and reflex, every action's raw and final score in the chooser's order, danger, horizon, threat and reachable counts, the most urgent threat as name:class:urgency:ticks:reachable, the request's target, the loot count, the request kind, anchor and chosen spot with its score, the path's length, index, next step kind, plan failure and expansions, the body's tile, pixel position, velocity, ground, wet, both collision flags, direction and life, breath (with `u` while the head is under) and self danger (with `L` in lava, `f` on fire), what the hand holds, the chosen weapon, whether a shot solved and what *both* weapons' expected damage scored, the torch state and ambient light, and the player's tile, intent, dead, attacking, chopping and mining flags; then the cost: the last plan's and the last reach flood's wall-clock (sticky, repeated until the next search), this tick's milliseconds for each phase of the brain (senses, reflex, decide, position, navigate) and the whole, how many tiles hold cached edges, how many ticks the body has been stranded (zero unless sealed off from the player; the count runs through the retry windows too, so a roam is the row whose request column reads Roam, never a wander row with a positive count alone), and the reachability tier: how many tiles the flood reached, how many of those the body could come home from, whether the spot it picked is one of them, and whether the refusing flood was thrown away because the player stood outside it. Those four are the only view of the decision about entering somewhere unrecoverable, because the replay tool runs the planner and the follower and the positioner needs the game; `returnable_n` well under `reach_n` with `spot_home` at 1 is the tier doing its job, `player_one_way` at 1 is the companion deliberately following him somewhere it cannot come back from, and `spot_home` at 0 for a sustained stretch is the state it can get stuck from, which is also a dump. The phase columns are the profiler Caner asked for on run 6 (2026-09-08, "so laggy, unplayable"): a sum of `brain_ms` over a stretch of rows against the rows' 16.67 ms each is the brain's share of the wall, and the phase with the number is the suspect; the overlay shows the same line live. Flushed once a second, closed on world unload and mod unload. The two expected-damage columns are the first place this file records an option that was *rejected* rather than only the one taken, which is the gap that made "why is it using that weapon" unanswerable from the record: the log said which weapon won and never what the alternative was worth, so the comparison had to be re-derived by hand from the situation. A row where the loser's number is the higher of the two is a defect with no other tell, and it is the shape every other decision in here still wants — the runner-up spot beside the chosen spot, the second-best action beside the winner (which the score columns already give, and which is why they were worth their width).

Reading it: a jump is a tick with upward velocity after a grounded one; stuck is a run of ticks with the same tile, a path, and an index that does not move; a wrong choice is the row where the winner's final score beat the one you expected, with the raw scores beside it saying which consideration did it. There is no "mark" key by Caner's ruling on 2026-09-08: he dictates over the gameplay, so the clock in the file name and the tick column line up with what he said.

**Reading it by hand is no longer the way, and that is the point of the columns added on 2026-09-09.** `../../Tools/SessionReport/` takes a session and prints what is definitely wrong, what is probably wrong and what merely looks odd, addressing every column by name because the indices move whenever this file grows a fact. Five columns were added specifically so that reader could separate causes it otherwise had to guess at: `self_threat` is the companion's own danger beside the player's, which is what makes "it was hit in a tick it scored as perfectly safe" a definitive finding instead of an ambiguity; `press` is whether the motor was asking to fall through, which is the whole tell for the platform freeze; `engage` is what the hands are shooting at, independent of what the feet were told, so "it was following me and not attacking" is a row where that reads `-` beside a threat count; `exp_target` is what the chosen weapon expected against the target it actually fired at; and `fire` is *why* no projectile left the hands — `fired`, `cooldown`, `no-arc`, `no-target`, `hands-busy` — because a reload and a body standing in a place with no shot both read as `shot 0` and want opposite fixes.

The general rule those five follow: **record the rejected option and the reason for the negative, never only the outcome.** A record that says what happened supports one question, "what happened"; a record that says what else was on the table and why it lost supports the question anyone actually asks, which is "why that". The two expected-damage columns were the first of this shape and the fire reason is the second; the runner-up spot beside the chosen spot is the obvious third and is not written yet.

## Traps

- **Every number on a telemetry line is read inside `AI()`, before the engine moves the body that tick.** `CompanionNPC.AI` calls `Record` at its end, and the engine's collision and its `position += velocity` run afterwards, so `npc_px` is the position the previous tick's movement left, `collide_x` and `collide_y` are the previous tick's collision result, and `press` is this tick's request with nothing having consumed it yet. Three attempts at the platform freeze were argued from position and collision cells nobody had established were a tick apart from each other. The `moved` and `vel_cut` columns exist to end that: `moved` is the engine's own displacement and `vel_cut` is what its collision took off the velocity it was handed, so "blocked" and "never integrated" are two different pairs of cells instead of an inference across twenty rows.
- **A column's meaning is established from the writer before any arithmetic is argued from it.** `npc_px` writes `npc.Bottom`, whose X is the body's *centre*; read as the box's left edge it moves every clearance calculation ten pixels and produced a confident, wrong diagnosis of the platform freeze on 2026-09-09 — that the body was too wide for a shaft it fits with three pixels to spare.
- **A detector's "how long ago" is a subtraction, and a sentinel of `long.MinValue` makes it overflow.** The dodge detector compared `tick - lastDodgeTick` against its memory window with that sentinel, the subtraction wrapped a signed long to a large negative number, and the negative passed the `<=` test: every hit in a session where no reflex had ever fired was dumped as a hit through a dodge, with an age of −9,223,372,036,854,773,868 ticks printed in the window header. An age test needs the event itself to have happened, checked as its own clause, never encoded in a sentinel value. Found on 2026-09-09 by `../../Tools/SessionReport/` printing the window reasons; the detector had been fabricating scenarios since it was written.
- **An instrument that reports a defect it invented is worse than one that reports none**, because the invented one gets diagnosed. Every detector here is a claim about the game, so it is held to the bar the brain is held to, and a detector whose output looks impossible is a bug in this folder until proved otherwise.
- **A column inserted in the middle shifts every index after it**, and this file inserts columns often. Nothing reads it by position: `../../Tools/SessionReport/Session.cs` addresses by header name for that reason, and a hand-rolled `awk -F'\t' '{print $57}'` is wrong within a week of being written — which it was, on 2026-09-09, reporting a mean distance of three and a half thousand tiles.

`BrainOverlay.cs` registers the toggle keybind (default the left square bracket, which needs no Fn where the F-row does; the key left of 1 was tried first and is dropped by FNA before it becomes a key on a Mac ISO keyboard, see the root file's traps — and note that the registered default is not what makes the overlay reachable, since a saved binding outranks it for ever and the raw key list in `../../Players/CompanionPlayer.cs` is the thing that answers) and, when on, draws above the companion: the running action and reflex, danger, horizon and intent, every action's raw and final score, threat and loot counts, the request kind, the chosen spot and its score, the path length and last plan cost, and the chosen weapon beside what both weapons' expected damage scored. It also draws the path as dots (green walk, orange jump, blue drop, violet fall-through), the chosen spot in magenta, and a label over every threat (class, urgency, ticks to player, shooter flag, `x` when unreachable).
