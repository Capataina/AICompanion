# Debug — the overlay and the record

Two instruments for the same question, "why did it do that": the overlay answers it live above the companion's head, and the telemetry answers it afterwards from a file, which is the one that matters when the behaviour was watched by someone dictating and not by an agent.

```
Debug/
├─ CLAUDE.md
├─ BrainOverlay.cs    the F6 overlay: scores, danger, request, spot, path dots, threat labels
├─ BrainTelemetry.cs  one tab-separated line per companion tick to Telemetry/<timestamp>.tsv under the repository, and a tile-window dump per failed plan (throttled) or per detected scenario (not) to Telemetry/<timestamp>-plans.txt beside it, each carrying the player's trail
└─ ScenarioCapture.cs the four detectors that turn a playtest failure into a dump under its own reason: a follow failure, a stuck run, a hit through an approved dodge, a missed mode; thresholds and cooldowns live here because they tune the instrument and not the brain
```

## Every failure the player can feel becomes a block the tool replays

The design's pass line is the player's own: if the player got through, the companion must. `ScenarioCapture.Watch` runs on every recorded tick and keeps four counters. Walking with the player and more than a leash of tiles behind for seconds is a follow failure; a body that has not moved for seconds while the follower had a path is stuck; life lost within a second of a reflex approving a dodge is a hit through a dodge, which means the simulation the dodge was judged against was wrong; the player mining or chopping for seconds while the matching action scored zero on every one of those ticks is a missed mode, which is the pickaxe icon never showing. Each fires at most once per cooldown and writes the same tile window the failed-plan dump writes, with the reason in the header, the start at the companion's feet and the goal at the navigator's goal or the player's feet. Every window, whichever path wrote it, carries a `trail` line: the player's feet tiles over the last while, oldest first, one per change of tile, kept on the player sense. The replay tool reads that line back and reports how many trail tiles fall inside the window and the first one the grid refuses, either not standable or outside the region the companion can reach from its start, so a failed follow reads as "the grid refuses tile X, which the player stood in" rather than as a red verdict.

The plan dump is the instrument for "why could it not get there": the navigator calls `DumpPlan` on every plan that returned nothing or only a partial path, and at most once every five seconds the writer appends the tile window spanning the start, the goal and the player's feet (padded, cut to a readable size on the start's side, because the first missing link is near the start; the player is inside it because "could it have reached the player" is the replay tool's second verdict, and a goal picked above a pit said nothing about a player six rows below the window) as one character per tile, written through `TextTileWorld.Glyph` in `../DecisionMatrix/Navigation/` so the replay tool reads back exactly what was written: `#` solid, `=` platform, `_` half block, `\` and `/` floor slopes (solid below the line drawn), `<` and `>` ceiling slopes (solid on the side the bracket points away from), `~` water or honey, `L` lava, `o` air the body can stand in, `.` other air, with `S` start, `G` goal, `E` where a partial path ends, `N` the companion's feet and `P` the player's feet drawn on top. The header line carries the tick, the reason, the expansions used and the window's bounds. It exists because the third run of 2026-09-08 showed 1,136 expansions on every plan for its last 3,468 ticks (about a minute at sixty a second) with the player over 200 tiles below, and nothing in the per-tick line says which tile the grid refused.

`BrainTelemetry.cs` opens a file on world load under the mod's source folder (`Main.SavePath/ModSources/AICompanion/Telemetry`, which is this repository; the folder is in `.gitignore` and `buildIgnore`), writes a header naming every column, then one line per tick from the NPC's `AI` after the body has synced: the state (up, downed, player dead), the action and reflex, every action's raw and final score in the chooser's order, danger, horizon, threat and reachable counts, the most urgent threat as name:class:urgency:ticks:reachable, the request's target, the loot count, the request kind, anchor and chosen spot with its score, the path's length, index, next step kind, plan failure and expansions, the body's tile, pixel position, velocity, ground, wet, both collision flags, direction and life, breath (with `u` while the head is under) and self danger (with `L` in lava, `f` on fire), what the hand holds, the chosen weapon and whether a shot solved, the torch state and ambient light, and the player's tile, intent, dead, attacking, chopping and mining flags. Flushed once a second, closed on world unload and mod unload. Reading it: a jump is a tick with upward velocity after a grounded one; stuck is a run of ticks with the same tile, a path, and an index that does not move; a wrong choice is the row where the winner's final score beat the one you expected, with the raw scores beside it saying which consideration did it. There is no "mark" key by Caner's ruling on 2026-09-08: he dictates over the gameplay, so the clock in the file name and the tick column line up with what he said.

`BrainOverlay.cs` registers the toggle keybind (default F6; the key left of 1 was tried and is dropped by FNA before it becomes a key on a Mac ISO keyboard, see the root file's traps, so the F-row is back and needs Fn on a MacBook unless the F-keys are set to standard) and, when on, draws above the companion: the running action and reflex, danger, horizon and intent, every action's raw and final score, threat and loot counts, the request kind, the chosen spot and its score, the path length and last plan cost, and the chosen weapon. It also draws the path as dots (green walk, orange jump, blue drop, violet fall-through), the chosen spot in magenta, and a label over every threat (class, urgency, ticks to player, shooter flag, `x` when unreachable).
