# Body — one arithmetic for the simulated body and the real one

The body's numbers and shape tests live here and nowhere else: the motor in `../../../../Companion/` forwards to them, the planner simulates with them, the reflexes roll dodges out with them. A constant that existed in two places was the first navigation bug class (a jump the planner found and the body could not fly), and one home is the fix.

```
Body/
├─ CLAUDE.md
├─ BodyPhysics.cs    the body's numbers (20 by 42, the walk speed, the jump velocity, acceleration, slowdown, NPC gravity), StepVelocity (the player's shape: a small gain per tick up to the walk speed, a larger loss when stopping or reversing, ground and air alike), SteerToward (the in-air steering rule the follower and the jump simulation share), SimulateJump (a jump as a loop over BodyMotion.Step with the jump's own steering, returning the landing pose and the flight time), and the shape tests: Fits (the rectangle overlaps no solid, half blocks and slope triangles included), RestBottom (the highest surface under a span, a platform top counted), PlatformTopCrossed (the platform top a downward move passes), Stand (a pose with the feet in a tile, trying a few sideways offsets, resting only on a surface below the row's top edge so a platform through the shins does not refuse the tile)
├─ BodyState.cs      the body at one tick (left edge, bottom, velocity, on the ground, the last sideways move met a shape, stuck inside a shape) and MobilityState, the counters of the moves it has left (air jumps, a latch, a dash cooldown), all zero today and carried on the planner's node so a stateful move plans without a rewrite
├─ Controls.cs       what is asked of the body for one tick: a horizontal speed to accelerate toward, a jump at a share of the full velocity, whether to pass the platform underfoot, and whether the move in hand is going down; the only thing that ever moves the body, simulated or real
└─ BodyMotion.cs     Step: one tick of the body under the game's order (controls, the kerb rules, gravity, sideways clipped by shapes, downward in short steps landing on a platform unless asked through it, a standing body kept on its surface), the one rule every traversal is simulated with and the replay drives; the kerb rules are the game's StepUp as the player holding up runs it (a block, a half block or a slope ahead lifts the body a tile at most, and so does a platform at its knee) and the game's StepDown (a fall ahead of more than half a tile and less than a tile is stepped down onto; less is left to gravity as a hop)
```

A pose is a left edge and a bottom, nothing else; the body is a rectangle of fixed size and a tile is a shape, so "does it fit" is rectangle against triangles and "where does it rest" is the highest surface under the span, which for a floor slope is the diagonal under the body's near edge, the game's own rule. `BodyMotion.Step` is the tick: it takes a state and a set of controls and returns the next state, in the game's own order (the AI sets the velocity, the kerb rules run, gravity is added, the collision clips the move), so a move simulated as a loop over it is the move the motor performs, and the jump simulation is exactly that loop with the jump's steering. The step-up and step-down inside it are the geometry of the game's `Collision.StepUp` and `StepDown`, read from the decompile: StepUp lifts a body a tile at most over a block, a half block or a floor slope ahead of it, and onto a platform at its knee only while the player holds up, which the motor asks for on every tick that is not part of a descent (the fighter AI never asks for it at all, and a companion that walked through platform treads could not make a walk the plan had proven); StepDown snaps the feet down a fall ahead of more than half a tile and less than a tile, only when nothing at the current level still holds the body (a platform under any pixel of its width does), and leaves a smaller fall to gravity, so a body walking down a slope hops a few air ticks at a time as it does in the game, and the descents' notion of having left the ground is falling faster than such a hop ever falls.

**The platform lift is refused while `Controls.Descend` is set, and the two sides of that gate cost this project three failed fixes.** This rule gated its lift on the body being grounded, which a falling body is not, so a descent simulated clean; the motor gated its `Collision.StepUp` call on `velocity.Y >= 0f`, which a falling body *is*, so in the game the body climbed the platform it was passing on every tick while gravity ran to the fall cap. Same intent, two conditions, and only one of them safe — which is why the 2026-09-09 shaft replayed in 264 ticks with no fault and froze in play. Both sides now read the one bit the traversal sets, and the bit is owned by the step in hand rather than derived from where the goal is, because a fighter's "the target is below me" would fall through a platform a planned route means to walk across. The `diverge` column in `../../../Debug/` measures the remaining gap between this rule and the engine live, every tick, because the two being different rules is the failure that produces a body standing still holding a valid path. Both run only for a standing body, so an airborne body moves as it always did; the corpus under `Tools/Scenarios/` read the same 65 passed, 32 sealed and 6 failed before and after the extraction (2026-09-08), which was the identity check for the extraction. The lift looks in the row below the feet as well as the rows above, because a body riding a slope sits a fraction of a pixel into the slope's row and the diagonal rises ahead of it by less than that fraction; a lift that never looked there wedged the body against the slope's last half pixel. A ceiling slope pushes the body down instead of stopping it, as the game's slope collision does: a sideways move refused only by the ceiling slopes over the body's head is made with the body lowered by exactly what their diagonals require, when it fits there, so a body hopping down a staircase of floor slopes under a matching staircase of ceiling slopes passes with its head a fraction of a pixel under each diagonal; a move refused for that fraction sealed the fourth run's pocket a second time, after the slopes were shapes. The trace the replay's `--trace-jump` prints reports each tick after the body has moved.
