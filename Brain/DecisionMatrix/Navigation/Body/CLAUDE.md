# Body — one arithmetic for the simulated body and the real one

The body's numbers and shape tests live here and nowhere else: the motor in `../../../../Companion/` forwards to them, the planner simulates with them, the reflexes roll dodges out with them. A constant that existed in two places was the first navigation bug class (a jump the planner found and the body could not fly), and one home is the fix.

```
Body/
├─ CLAUDE.md
├─ BodyPhysics.cs    the body's numbers (20 by 42, the walk speed, the jump velocity, acceleration, slowdown, NPC gravity), StepVelocity (the player's shape: a small gain per tick up to the walk speed, a larger loss when stopping or reversing, ground and air alike), SteerToward (the in-air steering rule the follower and the jump simulation share), SimulateJump (a jump as a loop over BodyMotion.Step with the jump's own steering, returning the landing pose and the flight time), and the shape tests: Fits (the rectangle overlaps no solid, half blocks and slope triangles included), RestBottom (the highest surface under a span, a platform top counted), PlatformTopCrossed (the platform top a downward move passes), Stand (a pose with the feet in a tile, trying a few sideways offsets), CanSlide (the body fits all along the line between two poses, riding the ground where it is higher than the line)
├─ BodyState.cs      the body at one tick (left edge, bottom, velocity, on the ground, the last sideways move met a shape, stuck inside a shape) and MobilityState, the counters of the moves it has left (air jumps, a latch, a dash cooldown), all zero today and carried on the planner's node so a stateful move plans without a rewrite
├─ Controls.cs       what is asked of the body for one tick: a horizontal speed to accelerate toward, a jump at a share of the full velocity, and whether to pass the platform underfoot; the only thing that ever moves the body, simulated or real
└─ BodyMotion.cs     Step: one tick of the body under the game's order (controls, kerb step-up, gravity, sideways clipped by shapes, downward in short steps landing on a platform unless asked through it, a standing body kept on its surface or stepped down a one-tile fall), the one rule every traversal is simulated with and the replay drives
```

A pose is a left edge and a bottom, nothing else; the body is a rectangle of fixed size and a tile is a shape, so "does it fit" is rectangle against triangles and "where does it rest" is the highest surface under the span, which for a floor slope is the diagonal under the body's near edge, the game's own rule. `BodyMotion.Step` is the tick: it takes a state and a set of controls and returns the next state, in the game's own order (the AI sets the velocity, the kerb rules run, gravity is added, the collision clips the move), so a move simulated as a loop over it is the move the motor performs, and the jump simulation is exactly that loop with the jump's steering. The step-up and step-down inside it are the geometry of the game's `Collision.StepUp` and `StepDown` (a one-tile kerb is walked, a one-tile fall is stepped down onto) and run only for a standing body, so an airborne body moves as it always did; the corpus under `Tools/Scenarios/` read the same 65 passed, 32 sealed and 6 failed before and after the extraction (2026-09-08), which is the identity check. The trace the replay's `--trace-jump` prints reports each tick after the body has moved.
