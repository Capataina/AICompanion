# Navigation — getting there

```
Navigation/

├─ World/       every tile question: the interface, the live game world, the text world the replay and the dumps share
├─ Body/        the body's numbers and shape tests: how wide, how it accelerates, where it rests, whether it fits
├─ Planning/    what a node is, the search over generated edges, the path a search returns, reachability for threats
└─ Following/   the navigator: plans on a cadence, walks the path through the motor, counts strikes when the body stands
```

The four folders are the four owners the industry's own split names: a world you ask, a body you simulate, a planner that proves edges and a follower that performs them. The split exists because seven runs (2026-09-08) kept producing one defect, a body standing still with a valid path in hand, and every time the cause was the planner and the follower being two implementations of one move. Each folder's own file carries its files and its seams; this file carries the mechanism they share.

## A node is a place the real body fits

The grid used to be a column test: feet on a support, three clear tiles above, and (after the third run) at least one clear neighbouring column. That model cannot see two things the game's collision does. The body is 20 px wide against a 16 px tile, so it always overlaps a second column, and which column it overlaps is its own choice: shifted a few pixels it fits between a wall at head height on one side and a wall at foot height on the other, where a centred body hits both. And a tile is not a box: worldgen smooths cave corners into slopes and half blocks, the game's `Collision.TileCollision` skips a slope from its open side and `SlopeCollision` rests the body on the diagonal, so a staircase of slopes is walked while every tile in it "has a solid tile". So a node is now a pose: `BodyPhysics.Stand` tries the body centred in the column and then a few pixels off each way, rests each on the highest surface under it (a block or platform top, the middle of a half block, the point of a floor slope's diagonal under the body's near edge, which is the game's own rule), asks that the feet land in the tile asked for, and asks `Fits`, the rectangle-against-shapes test, for the rest of the body. On a slope the feet rest partway down the slope's own tile, so the slope tile is the node; `FeetWorld` returns the pose, and the follower steers to it.

Edges are poses too, and every one of them is the body driven. A walk exists to the neighbouring column wherever the body, driven from the node's pose at the walk speed through its own tick rule, ends standing in that column on a node at most one row from where it started: the kerb rules inside the tick lift it over a block or onto a platform at its knee (the game's StepUp as the player holding up runs it, which is what the motor asks for) and step it down a fall of half a tile to a tile (the game's StepDown window; a smaller fall is a hop gravity closes), and a slope lowers or raises it along its diagonal, so a walk down a staircase of slopes under a ceiling slope is found because the body was driven under it. A body that meets a shape sideways has no walk that way, which is what refuses the end of a platform tread where the tread's last pixels still hold the body and its head meets rock one column on; a body that falls for real has none either, because that is the drop's edge. A one-row step down is a walk, not a drop, because a slope or a kerb lowers the feet a row without an edge to fall off. The slide test that proved walks until the traversal work of 2026-09-08 (the body fits at every few pixels along the line between the two poses, riding the ground where it is higher than the line) accepted the tread's end and the body parked at it (run 5 block 12); proving the walk by driving the body is what every other move already did.

A drop is the body lowered, not the grid asked. The open span beside a lip is the run of columns the body is clear in with nothing beneath (or a platform beneath, for a fall-through), and where in that span the body falls decides what it lands on: a shaft wider than the body has lips on alternate sides that only a body hugging that side's wall rests on, and a lip one row down that supports the body is a walk step-down and no drop at all. So the descent is proven by driving the body's own tick rule from the lip's pose toward a line in the span (a few pixels off its left wall, its middle, a few pixels off its right wall) with the descent's steering until it leaves the ground and rests again, and each distinct landing is an edge carrying the line, the fall and the ticks; the follower performs it with the same steering from wherever the body is, braking first if it arrives faster than rest, because the edge was proven from rest. Until the traversal work of 2026-09-08 the scan lowered the body a row at a time with a fixed drift toward the hugged wall and carried the X the body had drifted to at the landing as the line to steer to at the top, which parked the run-7 body four pixels onto the block beside its platform, pressing down at a solid, and mislanded every diagonal drop in the follow harness's first baseline. Two failures taught this on run 5 (2026-09-08): a platform lip one row down was scanned past because the old scan skipped the first row to protect the overhang case and read a platform as not a block, so a drop was offered that the body at the lip never made and it stood there for the rest of the session; and a zigzag shaft three wide was descended in the plan and fallen straight through by the body, because the plan asked whether a lip was standable and the follower steered to the middle. A third came on run 6 (2026-09-08, tick 2400 to the end): the column beside the lip was open to the body and had a platform under it, the lowered-body scan of the time passed through the platform because a platform is not a block, and a drop was offered off a lip onto a floor; in the game the body walked onto the platform, stood, and pushed at the drop line for thirteen hundred ticks with the player forty rows below. So a drop needs the column beside the lip open underneath as well as clear, and the way through a platform is only ever the fall-through edge from the tile standing on it, which the same plan now takes (walk onto the platform, fall through, in the replay of that dump).

Every tile's edges are remembered between searches, because simulating them is where the time goes: about a sixth of a millisecond a tile in the game's build, so the positioner's reach flood of a few hundred tiles every dozen ticks cost sixty milliseconds of one tick in twelve on run 6 and a full-budget plan two hundred, which was the lag Caner called unplayable. The cache holds the geometry alone (the steps and their move cost as the tiles decide it, the fall depth so the one-way rule still applies on read) and prices are put on when an edge is read, since enemies move every tick; it is keyed by the tile and the lava switch, and a new world starts it empty. A killed or placed tile (the senses' tile watcher calls `TileChanged`) drops only the entries whose scans could have read it: a tile's edges look down as far as a drop falls and up as far as a jump rises plus the body, and sideways as far as the drop scan reads (the neighbouring column, the open span's reach past it, the walk a steered body covers in the ticks the deepest drop takes to fall, and the body's own width), summed into `EdgeReachX` from those constants rather than set by hand, because the hand-set width fell three columns short of a support the scan read and kept an edge that support had changed (Codex review of 87e8d20); so the box is that tall around the change and that wide, and the rest of the cache survives, which is what keeps the flood cheap while the player or the companion is mining and a tile dies every few ticks (the first version dropped the whole cache on any change, which while digging was no cache). Every entry also carries the tick it was made and is remade after `EdgeCacheLifeTicks`, because the game announces a tile change only from the player's own placing and from `KillTile`: liquids settling, sand landing, a door opening and a boulder rolling change tiles through no hook, and a stale entry is a price rather than a wall since liquid and lava are read live when an edge is priced. The box is proved by the replay's `--churn`: every tile a found path stands on or steps through is broken in turn, the planner told the way the game tells it, and the plan from the warm cache must equal the plan from an empty one, step for step including the jump profile and the steer line each step carries, because two plans over the same tiles asking for different moves are two plans; the corpus reads 6,499 tiles broken and 0 stale plans, and a box one row tall reads 172, so the check can fail (2026-09-08). The replay proves it changes no verdict: `--no-cache` runs every search afresh and the corpus reads the same 65 passed, 32 sealed, 6 failed either way (2026-09-08), at three quarters of the time even with every block a fresh world. One trap it set on its first run: the replay's sealed verdicts read whether a flood touched the window's edge, and a flood served from the cache touches nothing, so the tool empties the cache before each flood it reads the flag from. A drop deeper than the jump box is a door that closes behind the body, and it exists only while the brain says the companion is following or guarding the player or saving itself: a hunt took one fifteen rows into a sealed pocket on run 5 (tick 7155) and stood at its rim for the rest of the session. The reach flood the positioner uses shares the rule, so a firing spot at the bottom of a pit is not offered either.

The replay tool under `../../../Tools/NavReplay/` runs exactly this code on a text window; `../../../Tools/WorldWindow/reshape.py` rewrites an old dump's walls into shapes from the saved world and, with `--pad N`, grows the window from the world; `../../../Tools/Scenarios/` holds the committed cases. The window's edge is a wall only to the tool, so a block whose plan fails with a complete region and the goal outside is one of three things, and the text world's `AskedOutside` flag (set whenever a search reads a tile beyond the window) tells them apart: a start region that never asked is a pocket the world seals, which is a rescue's job and not the planner's (Caner's ruling, 2026-09-08: a pit too deep to jump out of is not a pathfinding failure); a goal region that never asked is a spot the positioner must not offer; both clipped is undecidable as cut and wants padding. A sealed block counts as its own verdict and does not fail the run. As of 2026-09-08 the fourth run's 32 dumps, padded 48 tiles from the saved world, pass 28 to their recorded goals and the other 4 are sealed goals (the old positioner's picks inside closed cavities, AIC-135's finding); padded 6 as the run wrote them they passed 10, and 22 "failed" for the edge. The six-thousand-tick pocket was therefore the grid's (slopes read as walls) and never the world's: no tile was placed or dug there, the run ended with the companion still inside, and today's planner walks out of it. The fifth run is the other case, and the first the tool has seen: its 62 dumps, padded 96 (48 left nine blocks undecidable), read 28 passed, 28 sealed and 6 failed, and the sealed ones include two SEALED START pockets, 34 tiles at 3498,569 (ticks 7182 to 8157) and 30 tiles at 3557,562 (tick 10116, the player inside it too). The saved world diffed against those dumps shows three platforms and a dug stair in the first and four platforms in the second, placed between dumps, and the telemetry shows the companion out of each afterwards: both were pits with no way out that Caner built out of by hand, exactly his ruling, and the tool now says so instead of FAIL. The reshape script counted the dump's `markers` line as a row of tiles until then, which shifted every row down one and made the replay start from the legend's own letters; a dump from before the markers line (run 4) never showed it. The four that fail are two where the flood's budget runs out before it can answer (ticks 4268 and 4953, a goal 27 rows straight up and one 66 tiles away) and two the same at pad 96 because the start's region grows past the budget (16212, 23192); none has been read as a planner defect yet. Two more (14086, 15583) read undecidable even at 96 while the slide test proved walks, and read SEALED GOAL since the walk is proven by driving the body (goal regions of 177 and 154 tiles that close without touching the window); which edge the slide test accepted out of those regions is not identified. The fifth run's other lesson is the follower's: after the platforms went in, blocks 12 to 16 and their like plan a path and PASS, and the companion still stood at 3558,556 for five thousand ticks with that path in hand, which is the run-up and jump execution AIC-129 judges in play and the replay cannot see.

A jump edge is the follower's own jump, simulated, and the step carries the profile that landed it. For the rise (the pixels between the two poses' bottoms rounded up to tiles, which differs from the row difference by a whole scale step on a slope or a half block) every velocity scale of the fighter AI's table and the full jump whose apex clears it is tried, lowest first, each at the walk speed, half of it and from a stand, and the first that lands is the edge, because the shortest flight is the cheapest edge and the arc least likely to meet a ceiling: the table's own scale over-jumped a four-tile rise onto a platform above it, and the walk-speed start hit a three-tile overhang that the half-speed arc clears (run 5, 2026-09-08, the two ledge scenarios under `Tools/Scenarios/`). A jump straight up is offered from a stand only. The box the targets are taken from reaches the full jump's seven tiles, because the box only says where to look and the arc decides. Each profile flies from the node's pose, steering toward the landing column with `BodyPhysics.SteerToward` (full speed toward it, coasting once inside the stopping distance, the same rule the follower runs in the air), under NPC gravity, half speed while the feet are in liquid, stopped by any shape it meets sideways, stopped under a ceiling, landed on the first surface crossed on the way down, a platform's top included because a jump never asks to fall through one, and abandoned once it has fallen past the row it was aimed at; the edge exists only where the simulated body's feet end in exactly the tile asked for, and it costs its flight time in walked tiles plus one. A landing level with the start or lower is only flown from an edge (the next tile that way not standable), because on a floor the walk reaches the same tiles for less and the edge node past the walk offers the gap jump itself; that is what keeps a flat floor from flying sixteen jumps per node for nothing, and with it a full-budget failed plan on a bare floor costs about 40 to 60 ms and a reach flood 10 to 15 ms on this machine (2026-09-08, the Codex review's fixture), which is still above a frame and is AIC-143's reason to spread a search across ticks. The speed the body carries into the jump is not part of a node, so the follower makes it part of the move: a jump step arriving at its take-off slower than its profile needs backs away along the take-off row, as far as the runway that speed needs and the standable tiles behind allow, runs in at the profile's own speed (not the walk speed, because a faster body flies a longer arc into the overhang the profile was chosen to clear) and jumps as it crosses the take-off; a standing jump settles onto the take-off first; past the take-off it jumps at once, because the next tile is the edge; and a runway too short to reach the speed is run once and then jumped from whatever speed it gave, so a body is never backing away for ever. That is Caner's "take a couple of steps back and then jump" (2026-09-08), and it is what makes a full-height running jump and a hop two different moves rather than one jump at whatever speed the walk left. Each profile costs a simulation, so a node's jump candidates cost a few times what they did with two starts, which on the run-5 platform scenario is about ten milliseconds against seven for a ninety-expansion plan (bench, Release, 2026-09-08), and AIC-143's spread of a plan across ticks is still the answer to a full-budget failure. `dotnet run --project Tools/NavReplay -- --trace-jump <scenario>` prints every profile's arc from S to G tick by tick, which is how the gap of run 4 case 9 was read: hunting the column at full speed landed one tile past it, and the coasting rule is what fixed it. The grid never plans through tiles: no digging, no building, by ruling. Water and lava are priced rather than banned, by Caner's ruling on 2026-09-08 (a player crosses a pool, and jumps a lava stream to escape two zombies): the price shapes are in `AStar.Price`, and the one thing not priced yet is breath along a path, so a submerged route longer than the breath is planned like any other and the survive action in `../../Actions/Survival/` is the backstop.

## Traps

- **A one-tile rise is a step, never a jump.** The motor runs the game's `Collision.StepUp`/`StepDown` after the brain each tick, as the fighter AI does. The follower jumps only for rises of two tiles or more (at the fighter AI's heights: -6, -7, -8, then the full jump) or for a wall, and a wall is read from the tiles, solid at the feet row and the row above in the walking direction, never from `collideX` alone: the game sets that flag for a kerb on the tick it is met, before StepUp lifts the body, so a follower that trusted the flag still hopped every kerb after the StepUp fix (second run, 2026-09-08, "frantic jumping"). Straight-line walking uses the same wall test.
- **Water is not air to the body.** The game halves a wet NPC's movement, so a jump edge planned from inside a pool reaches about half its envelope and the follower jumps in place under the ledge for as long as the plan says jump (second run, 2026-09-08, the companion stuck at the pool's edge). Edges from liquid cost double and the jump envelope from liquid is halved, so the search walks out along the floor where it can.
- **A platform is a ceiling to a grid with no fall-through edge.** The companion waited above a shaft capped with platforms (second run, 2026-09-08). The fall-through edge and the NPC's `CanFallThroughPlatforms` hook, set for one tick by the follower, are what let it press "down".
- **The body is 20 px wide, the grid is one tile wide.** The motor drifts across tile columns; the follower advances a step when the feet are within 10 px, so a path is a suggestion the motor approximates. Two consequences bit on the third run (2026-09-08): a column walled on both sides is a slot the body cannot enter however clear it is, so a node needs one open neighbouring column; and a body centred on one column of a two-wide shaft overhangs the lip by a couple of pixels, which the game counts as standing, so it sat on the edge of the player's shaft for 300 ticks with velocity flipping between +0.09 and −0.09 while the drop step aimed at a tile centre it was already on. A drop or fall-through now steers to the middle of the open span at the lip.
- **A search that runs out used to return nothing, and the companion stood where the goal went out of view.** From 150 tiles above the player every plan exhausted a closed region of 1,136 nodes (third run, 2026-09-08). The search now returns the path to the closed node nearest the goal, marked partial; the follower walks it and the record still counts it as a failed plan. Why that region had no link into the shaft is the open question the plan dump in `../../Debug/` exists to answer.
- **Enemies are not tiles, and the search walked through them.** All 45 jump starts in the third run came from jump edges, six of them on a hunt path to a firing spot on the far side of a zombie. Reachable enemies' hitboxes, inflated, are priced on the route like lava, and the positioner discounts a firing spot whose straight line from the companion passes through one.
- **Planning from the air fails, and a failed plan is not retried for a while; together they were a jump loop.** The fourth run of 2026-09-08 had 222 jump starts in three minutes and a failed plan on 88% of ticks, nearly all "no standable tile at the start" with the body five tiles up: a plan attempted mid-jump failed, the retry wait made the follower walk straight and hop kerbs for ninety ticks, and the hop put the body in the air for the next attempt. The navigator plans only while the motor is on the ground and keeps following its path in the air. The other half was a price tie: a same-row gap jump was offered over standable tiles at exactly the walk's cost, so flat ground was hopped along; the gap jump now exists only where a tile between is not standable, and every jump costs more than the walk of the same width.
- **A lip is a place to stand, not a bridge, and a shaped tile is a place to land before it is a block.** With poses, both columns at the mouth of a two-wide shaft are standable by a two-pixel overhang, and a walk between those two poses was accepted because `CanSlide` only asked whether the body fits along the line, never whether anything holds it up, so the walk existed and the drop (offered only where no walk was) did not: a two-wide shaft could not be descended while three-wide could (2026-09-08 Codex review of 807df2e). The slide test was taught to refuse a sample with no surface under the span, and has since been replaced by driving the body, which falls off the overhang on its own; the descents run beside the walks rather than instead of them. The same review found the drop and fall-through scans calling a half block a block before asking whether the feet could rest inside it, which they can (the rest is the block's middle, so the feet row is the half block's own row); both scans ask for a landing first. Three fixtures under `Tools/Scenarios/` hold each case.
- **A jump is landed by coasting, never by hunting the column.** The follower steered at full speed toward the landing column and re-read the sign every tick, so a running jump reached the column at tick 20, kept going, reversed over eight ticks and came down a tile beyond, on the next slope's top (run 4 case 9, read with `--trace-jump`). Anything that steers the body in the air goes through `BodyPhysics.SteerToward`, which stops asking for speed once the remaining distance is inside v² over twice the slowdown; the planner simulates with the same function, so a different rule in the follower silently breaks every jump edge.
- **A slope is not a wall, and `tileSolid` cannot tell you.** The fourth run of 2026-09-08 parked the companion for six thousand ticks above a staircase of five worldgen slopes the grid drew as `#`; the player's tile trail and the companion's own pixels sat inside those tiles, and the saved world's shape bits confirmed it. Every tile read goes through `ITileWorld.Shape`; a test that asks "solid?" wants `IsBlock` (anything a body cannot pass) or `IsSolid` (a full block only, for a wall test), and the difference is the whole staircase.
- **A recorded goal can be unreachable by construction.** Nothing between a position request and the navigator asks whether the spot can be reached: the positioner scores every standable tile in its box and the navigator snaps an exact request to the nearest standable tile, neither with a reachability test. On the fourth run of 2026-09-08 (tick 3431) a walk-with anchor beside the player at (3522,492) scored best at (3526,481), a standable tile inside a sealed cavity eleven rows up, and the grid that now walks the whole window still cannot get there. The positioner now floods the walker's edges from the companion's feet on its rescore cadence and scores only candidates inside that region while any is (AIC-135), and the exact-request snap prefers a reachable tile; the replay tool prints the recorded goal's result, the player's, and whether each lies inside that flood, and on the 32 shaped scenarios every failing goal is outside a complete region and every passing goal inside it.
- **A replan alone never unsticks a body; the step it stood at has to change price.** Run 5 (2026-09-08, ticks 11600 to 11736) replanned three times from the same platform lip at 3.6, 3.3 and 3.5 ms and got the same bogus drop each time. A stuck replan now adds the body rectangle of the step it stood at to the search's avoid list for a while (priced like an enemy, not banned) and counts a strike; the brain reads two strikes against one goal as "this spot is not reachable however the grid plans it" and asks the positioner to refuse the spot for a while, so the answer is another place. The grid defect underneath is still fixed on its own; the escalation is what keeps the companion moving while the next one is found.
- **A goal you chose for a scenario is not a place the player stood.** The run-5 pit was cut into rungs with a ledge under an overhang as the exit, and two hours went into why no arc landed there before the trail showed the player never stood on it: the companion had dropped fifteen rows into a sealed pocket for a hunt target, and the real exit did not exist. Cut a scenario's goal from the trail or the recorded goal, and read the replay's trail line before the map.
- **Budget exhaustion in Reachability means reachable.** A cave full of enemies far from the player costs the walker budget per enemy per second.
- **A static planner flag set by the brain is read by the senses on the next tick.** `AllowOneWayDrops` is set from the request at the end of the brain's tick and the threat sense's walker search runs at the start of the next, so during a hunt an enemy that reaches the player by dropping into a cave read as unreachable until `Reachability` forced the flag on around its own search. Any new per-request switch on `AStar` needs the same treatment there, or the enemies inherit the companion's rules.
- **A scenario window is a wall to the tool and nothing else.** Twenty-two of the fourth run's blocks failed for two sessions with a complete region of 54 tiles, and every one of them was the route leaving a six-tile window; padded from the world they pass. Read the flood line's sealed or clipped verdict before reading the map, and pad before touching the planner.
