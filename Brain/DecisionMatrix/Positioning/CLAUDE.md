# Positioning — where to stand

`Positioner.cs` turns a `PositionRequest` into a feet position. `Exact` and `Hold` pass through; `Roam` is answered from the reach flood itself, the farthest of a handful of reachable tiles drawn at random, kept for a while (`Weights.RoamHoldTicks`) so a walk is a walk, and since the flood refuses every edge with no way back a pocket is walked and never deepened; the rest sample standable tiles in a box around the anchor (radius 14 tiles, stride 2) and score each on: distance band to the player (tight under threat, loose when calm, from `Weights`), sight line to the player, line of fire to the target through the aimer with the chosen weapon's profile, danger from predicted threat paths over the next 40 ticks, openness (solid tiles around eye height), travel bias along the player's intent, and for line-of-fire and retreat requests a standoff band from the target and a clear way: a spot whose straight line from where the companion stands passes within a body's width of a reachable enemy is discounted hard, because a firing spot on the far side of a zombie is reached by walking into the zombie (six such jumps in the third run, 2026-09-08). The weights per request kind are the `switch` in `ScoreSpot`. Re-scored every 12 ticks or when the request kind or target changes. A spot the navigator was stuck twice on the way to is refused for a while (`Ban`, duration in `Weights`), by every kind including Exact, and the next resolve scores again without it, so a companion that cannot reach the place the grid says it can goes somewhere else rather than standing.

The flood the tier is drawn from refuses an edge with no way back whatever the request, which is what makes this file the place the companion decides whether to enter somewhere it cannot leave. The region therefore means "everywhere the body can go and come home from", and the tier does the rest: while any candidate is in it only those are scored, because a spot the body cannot reach is not a worse spot but no spot; when none is, the tier opens and the unrecoverable candidates are scored instead. Read forward, that produces a companion that fires into a pit from the rim rather than jumping in, with nothing in `ScoreSpot` naming a pit or an enemy, by Caner's ruling of 2026-09-08 that those cases are outcomes a generic system should produce rather than branches to write. What the tier does *not* do is open because a rim spot cannot shoot: the tier is settled before the aimer solves anything, and a failed solve contributes a fraction rather than a zero, so a fight with no solvable rim shot ends in not engaging rather than in dropping into the pit — which is the request-kind gate's answer and the one `record.decisions` rules, since a hunt is never offered a one-way drop at all.

`RefreshReach` floods a second time without the refusal when the first region does not hold the player, and adopts that second region only where it does, which is the line that makes the companion follow him into a pocket. It is not a courtesy: the tier is closed by *any* returnable candidate anywhere in the sample box, and a shaft's rim is returnable however far its floor is below, so an unconditional refusal parks the body on the rim of every hole the player climbs down. The condition is his own definition of stuck — no valid path to him, rather than no path back — so a place he is standing in is by construction not somewhere the companion strands itself.

Both halves of that condition carry weight, because a player outside the returnable region is either down a one-way drop or walled off from the body altogether, and only the first is an argument for opening the tier. Granting the exception to the second would hand the roam a region full of drops with no way back and deepen the very pocket the refusal exists to keep the body out of, so the raw region is read and then discarded unless it reaches him; the flag the telemetry and the capture read stays false in that case, and a companion walled off behind a sand fall walks its own pocket as before. The second flood is paid only when he is outside the first region. `../Navigation/CLAUDE.md` has how an edge with no way back is recognised.

The jump-shot from the design (a jump apex as a candidate spot) is not sampled yet; candidates are ground tiles only. Add it as a second candidate set with the apex height from `NavGrid.JumpHeightTiles`.
