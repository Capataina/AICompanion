# Positioning — where to stand

`Positioner.cs` turns a `PositionRequest` into a feet position. `Exact` and `Hold` pass through; `Roam` is answered from the reach flood itself, the farthest of a handful of reachable tiles drawn at random, kept for a while (`Weights.RoamHoldTicks`) so a walk is a walk, and since the flood ran under the brain's one-way rule (off for a roam) a pocket is walked and never deepened; the rest sample standable tiles in a box around the anchor (radius 14 tiles, stride 2) and score each on: distance band to the player (tight under threat, loose when calm, from `Weights`), sight line to the player, line of fire to the target through the aimer with the chosen weapon's profile, danger from predicted threat paths over the next 40 ticks, openness (solid tiles around eye height), travel bias along the player's intent, and for line-of-fire and retreat requests a standoff band from the target and a clear way: a spot whose straight line from where the companion stands passes within a body's width of a reachable enemy is discounted hard, because a firing spot on the far side of a zombie is reached by walking into the zombie (six such jumps in the third run, 2026-09-08). The weights per request kind are the `switch` in `ScoreSpot`. Re-scored every 12 ticks or when the request kind or target changes. A spot the navigator was stuck twice on the way to is refused for a while (`Ban`, duration in `Weights`), by every kind including Exact, and the next resolve scores again without it, so a companion that cannot reach the place the grid says it can goes somewhere else rather than standing.

The jump-shot from the design (a jump apex as a candidate spot) is not sampled yet; candidates are ground tiles only. Add it as a second candidate set with the apex height from `NavGrid.JumpHeightTiles`.
