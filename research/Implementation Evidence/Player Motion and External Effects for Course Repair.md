# Proposal 05 planning map: live player motion, committed work, and observed interference

This is a read-only map against `d315bf2` (0.30.7). It identifies the smallest cross-cutting contracts Proposal 05 must add or reshape to retain a course through ordinary weaving and repair it after actual changes. It does not prescribe a gameplay implementation or treat random motion as predictable.

## The present path and its clocks

```text
native player state / callbacks
  -> PlayerSense.Update (every brain tick)
  -> InferPlayerActivity bounded history + held controls
  -> PlayerIntentRegionSense filtered, clamped region
  -> Chooser captures prepared activities and one current activity
  -> activity or combat plan chooses destination/use
  -> position/navigation/firing
  -> native effects and telemetry, mostly consumed next tick
```

`Brain.Tick` establishes the relevant ordering: `Senses.Update` occurs before chooser preparation, the chooser executes a selected activity in the same tick, position/navigation follow, and firing happens in finalisation after navigation ([`CoordinateBrainTick.cs:101-221`](<../../Companion/Brain/CoordinateBrainTick.cs:101>)). Therefore a course may consume the just-observed player position, held direction, threat snapshot, loot/light state and new native callback state, but it cannot know a later input, impact, player action or movement from the same rendered frame. A plan must call those facts *unknown future*, rather than predict them as commitments.

The total shared planning allowance is currently 12 ms, not 4 ms; player-side reach consumes its own 1 ms/1,500-expansion slice before position/navigation, family preparation has a 3 ms allocation, and meeting search has 1 ms ([`BehaviourWeights.cs:90-93`](<../../Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Selection/BehaviourWeights.cs:90>), [`BehaviourWeights.cs:138-146`](<../../Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Selection/BehaviourWeights.cs:138>), [`BehaviourWeights.cs:179-180`](<../../Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Selection/BehaviourWeights.cs:179>)). Proposal work must retain a validated current step whenever its future repair/search is unfinished; it cannot assume an uncapped all-course solve.

## Intent-region inputs: what is known and what fails under weaving

`PlayerSense.Update` samples `player.Bottom`, velocity, current held controls, recent local work and a finite trail every brain tick. It derives `HeldMove` directly from current input and `Intent` from `InferPlayerActivity`; it also records physically observed player mining/chopping and a distinct live interference footprint ([`ObservePlayer.cs:70-124`](<../../Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Observation/ObservePlayer.cs:70>)). A held direction is an observation of current desire, not a destination. It is the fastest horizontal cue during an about-face, while `Intent` is deliberately late, history-weighted evidence.

`InferPlayerActivity` keeps a 120-tick circular displacement/work history. On a non-consecutive tick, death, or displacement exceeding velocity plus 32 px slack, it clears the entire history; otherwise it computes `Travel = (net displacement / samples) * coherence * evidence support * (1 - local-work discount)`. A left/right weave lowers net-over-path coherence toward zero, hence lowers both confidence and travel instead of asserting an onward destination ([`InferPlayerActivity.cs:25-68`](<../../Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Observation/InferPlayerActivity.cs:25>), [`BehaviourWeights.cs:150-156`](<../../Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Selection/BehaviourWeights.cs:150>)). This is appropriate evidence for “no reliable journey”; it does not distinguish purposeful weaving from indecision, and it gives no discontinuity identity for a retained course to repair against.

`PlayerIntentRegionSense.Update` drives horizontally from current held X, vertically from held Y or history intent Y, filters toward a **clamped pose** over 96 ticks, adds vertical observed travel at 0.2 gain, then clamps again. Its clamp is specifically designed so a reverse at the lead limit starts on the first opposite input rather than unwinding an accumulated lead ([`ObservePlayerIntentRegion.cs:299-357`](<../../Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Observation/ObservePlayerIntentRegion.cs:299>), [`BehaviourWeights.cs:588-631`](<../../Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Selection/BehaviourWeights.cs:588>)). That is a strength for repeated left/right weaving: the region follows the latest physical input without claiming that the next weave is predictable. The present region has no revision/episode identity; consumers see only geometry and `IsTravelling`.

The side-to-player proof is intentionally tri-state. Its flood remains rooted until finished or invalid, grows one bounded slice per tick, and reports `NotYet` unless an exhausted bounded flood contains both live player and companion corners ([`ObservePlayerIntentRegion.cs:245-293`](<../../Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Observation/ObservePlayerIntentRegion.cs:245>)). A player falling into a cave can therefore make the course’s return route **unknown**, not false; ordinary optional work must not turn that unanswered state into a forced abandonment or a fake continuation.

## Existing following and work costs

Keeping company switches only on the region’s latched inside/outside fact. Both methods issue `WithPlayer`, so crossing the boundary does not issue a Hold that cuts movement; rejoin value is zero inside the region and rises only beyond it ([`KeepCompany.cs:54-152`](<../../Companion/Brain/Activities/NearbyAssistance/KeepCompany.cs:54>)). This already satisfies the correct principle for a weave: retain the body’s continuous movement, update its reference frame, and do not call each alternating side of a boundary a new job.

For a travelling player, `ChooseMeetingPlace` searches a small ladder along the observed travel vector, but makes a decision only on a finished flood; while undecided it keeps a still-ahead incumbent or heads to the nearest valid point inside the current region ([`ChooseMeetingPlace.cs:107-214`](<../../Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Position/ChooseMeetingPlace.cs:107>)). Its current `StillAhead` invalidates an old place once the player has passed it, turned away, or it lies beyond the ladder ([`ChooseMeetingPlace.cs:248-261`](<../../Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Position/ChooseMeetingPlace.cs:248>)). That is a usable precedent for repair-by-membership, but it only covers following anchors rather than a mixed work course.

The chooser already projects a site against `Region.Heading + Intent * min(jobTicks, 300)`. `PlayerFit` fades from the new-work radius to active-work radius, and `Separation` projects the region for the job duration and charges only the gap beyond it plus an established route detour ([`ChooseBehaviour.cs:230-275`](<../../Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Selection/ChooseBehaviour.cs:230>)). This is a present formula that can price “will this course still be near the player?” but it has two limits: it is only one projected endpoint, and it does not name the evidence epoch on which the projection was made. Repeated weaving should reduce its confidence to a short, local bound; a cave drop/correction should invalidate the extrapolation immediately and retain only a safe current action plus return/reach repair.

## Combat: stronger concrete binding, but incomplete course dependencies

Combat already owns a concrete `AttackPlan`: up to three timed segments, concrete stands, weapon/aim/use/target slots, target generations, and an immutable admission record containing terrain, knowledge, threat, region and company-gap facts ([`AttackPlan.cs:64-97`](<../../Companion/Brain/Activities/Combat/Planning/AttackPlan.cs:64>)). `FightEnemies.Prepare` validates an existing committed plan, reevaluates its remaining uses against this tick’s forecast, and only searches again when that path fails ([`FightEnemies.cs:177-226`](<../../Companion/Brain/Activities/Combat/FightEnemies.cs:177>)). This is the closest current model for Proposal 05’s stable planned work.

`CommitAttackPlan.Validate` retains by membership rather than a percentage incumbent bonus. It invalidates on segment completion, unreachable/unallowed stand, knowledge revision, unplanned target disappearance, a material new/urgent hostile, company-gap growth beyond the admitted gap plus geometric slack, or lack of a planned use/hit for 120 ticks while combat runs ([`CommitAttackPlan.cs:150-318`](<../../Companion/Brain/Activities/Combat/Planning/CommitAttackPlan.cs:150>), [`BehaviourWeights.cs:359-390`](<../../Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Selection/BehaviourWeights.cs:359>)). The three mechanisms that should survive into a general course are concrete identities/generations, declared admission facts, and a reasoned release event. The time-based combat stall and urgency slack are combat-specific controls, not a general “hold N%/N ticks” answer for work courses.

The combat forecast is refreshed once per tick from the threat sense and each actual use is re-solved from the live muzzle. A due use may substitute another weapon; travel-time firing selects among the committed plan’s targets, but never opens a new target front ([`CompanionCombat.cs:153-168`](<../../Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Interactions/Firing/CompanionCombat.cs:153>), [`FireDueUse.cs:124-255`](<../../Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Interactions/Firing/FireDueUse.cs:124>)). This provides a bounded first action under enemy movement, but its plan is still a short combat timeline. It must not become an opaque whole-course commitment: random enemy movement, knockback and player interference require reforecast/rebinding at each executable step.

An enemy hit is attributable to a registered companion projectile and preserves aimed target, struck target, spawn generations, damage and tick ([`TrackLandedHits.cs:26-129`](<../../Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Interactions/Firing/TrackLandedHits.cs:26>)). Shot outcome windows also distinguish an aimed enemy that disappeared before impact, in which case the weapon learner is deliberately not taught a miss ([`ObserveShotOutcomes.cs:198-237`](<../../Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Interactions/Firing/ObserveShotOutcomes.cs:198>)). This is a sound consequence receipt for weapon learning, but it does not yet propagate a realised knockback/new geometry event into a newly bound next combat step.

## Player interference, observed effects, and capability changes

The player has one authoritative hit callback: `CompanionPlayer.OnHurt` receives the engine’s final `HurtInfo` and records it to telemetry/God’s-eye events ([`ObservePlayerEvents.cs:8-19`](<../../Companion/PlayerIntegration/ObservePlayerEvents.cs:8>)). It guarantees that the player was hurt, final damage/cause/knockback as supplied by Terraria, and callback timing. It does **not** by itself prove which course choice failed to prevent it, which planned enemy caused it, or that another available action would have prevented it. A course should consume this as an observed consequence that can trigger a threat/return re-evaluation, never as a retroactive causal credit assignment without matching source identity.

Player interference is currently narrow and observed: a real block/wall placement footprint overlapping the orb, or a one-body-high passage the player is walking into. It has an `InterferenceRevision` only when the footprint changes and holds evidence for 60 ticks after it was last seen ([`ObservePlayer.cs:47-56`](<../../Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Observation/ObservePlayer.cs:47>), [`ObservePlayer.cs:99-184`](<../../Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Observation/ObservePlayer.cs:99>)). It explicitly does not predict a future click and the intent-region lead must not be suppressed because of it ([`ObservePlayerIntentRegion.cs:302-311`](<../../Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Observation/ObservePlayerIntentRegion.cs:302>)). Proposal 05 should preserve that boundary: repair/move a current step when its live footprint conflicts; do not model a possible next placement as a hard invalidator.

Activity ownership has attempt IDs and an aggregate productive-effect count. Identity changes end an open attempt, and `RecordWork` increments the count only from an observed native productive effect ([`OwnCurrentActivity.cs:42-127`](<../../Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Selection/OwnCurrentActivity.cs:42>), [`ChooseBehaviour.cs:60-73`](<../../Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Selection/ChooseBehaviour.cs:60>)). Mining further distinguishes companion removal, shared clear, player/world removal and invalidated work in its conclusion ([`MineOre.cs:92-121`](<../../Companion/Brain/Activities/Gathering/MineOre.cs:92>)). These are valuable receipts, but no shared typed effect stream currently says “the bound drop was picked up”, “this torch site became lit”, “this site was changed by the player”, or “this capability revision invalidated this one course step.”

Combat gear changes release a plan immediately, because plan weapon slots cannot survive a changed loadout ([`CompanionCombat.cs:114-151`](<../../Companion/Brain/Infrastructure/Interactions/Firing/CompanionCombat.cs:114>)). Mining separately observes tool power, mining-list version, terrain revision and approach origin to recompute only the approach while retaining the vein identity ([`MineOre.cs:190-227`](<../../Companion/Brain/Activities/Gathering/MineOre.cs:190>)). This demonstrates the desired split: preserve a useful concrete target when its capability still supports it; invalidate only the dependent binding when gear/policy/world facts change. It is not yet a common capability-revision contract.

## Minimum plan-facing extensions and dependency order

The minimum addition is a small **course evidence snapshot**, not a new full-world predictor. Each committed executable step should record: (1) concrete opportunity identity plus generation/revision, (2) the resources/capabilities it requires, (3) its admitted player-motion evidence epoch and region/return facts, (4) terrain/reach/knowledge revisions it read, (5) the typed observed effect(s) that count as progress, and (6) an explicit safe current action. A course validates the *next* step against these facts; if one dependency changes, it repairs from the first invalid step and keeps a still-valid current action. Unknown reach retains the current safe step or returns `Unknown`; it does not manufacture a rejection.

`PlayerMotionEvidence` is the smallest new observation surface needed for weaving and discontinuities. It should expose a monotonically increasing episode/revision, the latest observed position and input, a bounded displacement/history summary, and an explicit classification such as `continuous-supported`, `reversal-observed`, `discontinuity-observed`, or `insufficient-evidence`. The classification is evidence, not a path forecast. A left/right weave raises revisions on observed reversals but can leave a local movement reference valid; a large correction/death/teleport-like displacement starts a new episode and invalidates only forward projections and player-relative future bindings. It should not treat a player falling into a cave as a known future destination; the live location is known next tick, while side/reach/return can remain `NotYet` until the bounded flood answers.

`ObservedEffectReceipt` should unify existing specialised facts without replacing their native owners. It needs source tick, concrete target identity/generation, effect kind, actor attribution (`companion`, `player`, `world`, or `unknown`), and the revision(s) altered. Existing projectile-hit/outcome and mining conclusions can supply early producers; collection, lighting, pot and capability/policy changes need parallel producers. It must record “unknown attribution” when Terraria only proves a changed object, so the course repairs its binding without falsely charging the companion or player.

`CourseStep.Validate` should be ordered cheaply: identity/activity existence → required capability/policy → live interference footprint → direct observed receipt → terrain/knowledge/motion epoch → reach/forecast refinement. This preserves the present validated first-action rule: execute a current step that remains valid while later reach/ordering computation continues; a new urgent enemy can insert an immediate combat step only when its concrete forecast/urgency proves it dominates, not merely because the category is combat. Boss/event conduct stays the separate explicit Expected-Behaviour contract.

Implementation dependencies should therefore be staged as follows:

1. Add observation revisions and typed receipts, with recorder fields that distinguish raw observed event, actor attribution, and subsequent course-repair reason. This is prerequisite evidence; without it a repair may be correct but unexplainable.
2. Extract the common capability/policy revision surface from combat gear and gathering tool-policy checks. Verify that one changed slot/policy invalidates only dependent planned steps.
3. Build a course-step validity object and repair cursor above existing activity ownership. Reuse concrete activity identities, `PreparedTargetRejection`, tri-state reach and current executor grants; do not replace the motor, native interactions or current combat planner.
4. Attach player-motion evidence only to future player-relative estimates. Keep current intent-region following continuous, and make discontinuity/reversal invalidate projections rather than stopping the body or applying a percentage hold.
5. Integrate combat as a local course step: its existing plan remains a bounded tactical solver, while a realised hit/target departure/urgent new enemy repairs the next tactical binding. Add the missing realised-knockback-to-next-geometry discriminator before claiming chained combat sequences.

## Discriminating checks before implementation decisions

| Probe | Separates | Required observation |
|---|---|---|
| Repeated left/right weave while a short drop/torch step remains valid | Stable local course with updated frame vs reversal-triggered abandonment | motion revision, current-step identity, requested movement continuity, effect receipt |
| Player drops into a cave while a surface fight is committed | Known player discontinuity plus `NotYet` return proof vs stale projected surface commitment | motion episode, region/side reach verdict, stand separation, plan repair reason |
| Player removes a target drop/ore or places into the current footprint | Receipt-local repair vs unrelated full-course reset | target generation, actor attribution/unknown, invalidated step only, successor current step |
| Random enemy enters while a noncombat step is executable | Forecast-proven immediate combat insertion vs category-first interruption | threat generation/urgency, executable incumbent, bounded combat forecast, decision cost |
| Companion hit knocks an enemy into new piercing geometry | Rebind next combat action from realised state vs follow stale planned sequence | landed-hit receipt, pre/post enemy motion, next-use forecast and selected target |

No current source establishes that player movement is teleporting or random, that a player-hurt event identifies a preventable source, or that any existing course can repair these cases. The plan should preserve those as explicit unknowns and instrument them before attributing a gameplay failure to the new course logic.
