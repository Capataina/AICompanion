# Attack D — GOAP with utility-selected desired outcomes and shared retained execution

**Scope.** This is an attack on Approach D as the global chooser: utility picks a desired outcome; a GOAP planner regresses through action preconditions/effects to one course; shared retained execution carries out and repairs that course. It is not an argument that a GOAP library is unavailable, nor a verdict against an unimplemented shared foundation. The comparator is the finished-player contract in `README.md`, especially the Behaviour By Behaviour rows on enemy selection, chaining, and several steps ahead (`README.md:676-678`), and Proposal 04's requirement to choose *which concrete torch, enemy, drop, vein, and combat move*, not family labels (`research/proposal/04 Plan a Course of Action and Follow It.md:8-10, 30-34`).

**Finding.** GOAP is a credible *causal subplanner* when the companion has several genuinely different, observed ways to obtain one bounded result. As the global brain it is conditional at best: it must add a numerical, geometrically grounded state/effect model, multi-goal partial-order/resource scheduling, and retained-plan repair before it can meet the stated product. Those additions remove the simple symbolic/one-goal attraction and converge toward the scheduled course comparison or tactical simulator contenders. The decisive risk is not merely search time: a compact literal such as `reachable`, `enemy-safe`, `drop-collected`, or `area-lit` either lies about continuous, changing world facts or explodes into target/site/time/geometry groundings.

## What GOAP actually promises, and does not

Jeff Orkin's original GOAP account represents actions with preconditions, effects and costs, then searches action space for a plan satisfying a goal; it deliberately pairs that planner with a small execution state machine and replans after observed failure. In the F.E.A.R. door example, a failed open may lead to kick, then window, because the failure becomes shared working-memory evidence. That is a real strength for alternate workflows, not a promise that selecting a goal, modelling physics, concurrency, or repair policy happens automatically. [Orkin, *Applying Goal-Oriented Action Planning to Games* (2003)](https://static.hlt.bme.hu/semantics/external/pages/GOAP/alumni.media.mit.edu/_jorkin/GOAP_draft_AIWisdom2_2003.pdf); [Orkin, *Three States and a Plan* (GDC 2006)](https://www.gamedevs.org/uploads/three-states-plan-ai-of-fear.pdf).

The implementer evidence agrees with that boundary. CrashKonijn's maintained Unity GOAP package says its agent brain decides which goal to request from current state; goals/actions/sensors are shared stateless definitions, while action-local data must carry per-agent target and timer state. Its setup example makes a wait action manually retain and decrement a timer. That supports, rather than removes, the need for a companion-owned execution record, target identity, duration and interruption semantics. [CrashKonijn setup documentation](https://github.com/crashkonijn/GOAP/blob/master/Package/Documentation/Introduction/Setup.md). A current small implementation states the usual algorithm directly: choose the highest-priority valid goal, search backward through action effects/preconditions, then execute the returned array one action at a time; if the current action's preconditions cease to hold, it replaces the plan. [pixelrogueart/goap-godot-4 README](https://github.com/pixelrogueart/goap-godot-4). These are design/implementation sources, not Terraria measurements.

The project already recognised the same division before this attack: its earlier comparison says GOAP earns its place through alternate workflows, but that crisp `reachable`, `safe`, or `enemyRemoved` facts are unsafe without procedural checks, monitoring and replanning (`research/Utility AI and Its Alternatives.md`, “GOAP earns its place through alternative workflows”). Proposal 04 is explicitly not a licence to implement a whole-brain GOAP rewrite (`proposal/04…:1-15`).

## Required state is richer than ordinary symbolic world state

| Product requirement | Minimum truthful representation | Why bare GOAP literals are insufficient | Consequence for D |
|---|---|---|---|
| Choose seven torch sites, three drops, enemies, veins and combat moves as one course | Stable concrete identity, site/target pose, value, completion receipt, travel estimate and route evidence per candidate | `lit=true` or `loot=true` loses which object/site, its route and the ordering consequence; one goal family hides the requested grain | Ground an outcome/action per concrete site/target/move, then preserve identity through execution. That is a potentially large, moving action set. |
| No arbitrary scalar goal priorities | Comparable marginal delivered work, urgency, harm, travel, remaining duration and abandonment cost | A utility number over opaque named goals (`Fight`, `Light`, `Collect`) is just the prohibited family-level scalar priority moved up a layer | Utility may select an *outcome/course*, but it needs evaluated concrete alternatives and explainable terms. “highest valid goal” cannot be the policy. |
| Torch/drop/ore/combat can cooperate on a journey | Partial order, route/time and explicit claims for body, firing hand, tool hand and incidental hand | A one-goal serial plan has no native reason to insert a compatible different-goal action, or to permit shot firing while body travels | Plan steps need resource intervals and insertion/reordering rules; independent firing must be a shared executor, not an action that monopolises the agent. |
| Player, region, reach and terrain keep changing | Tri-state observations, spatial dependency footprint, confidence/age and specific invalidation | Boolean `reachable=false` conflates unknown flood with proven absent; global invalidation or per-tick replan either churns or keeps a stale plan | Reuse the shared senses and record exact observed footprint. A goal must not be declared impossible from unresolved reach. |
| First usable action within 4 ms | Incremental/bounded refinement with an incumbent executable prefix and an explicit no-result result | Regression has to enumerate actions whose effects match unsatisfied conditions, then their target/site/weapon/move bindings and feasibility | Need budgeted expansion and an immediately valid fallback; do not claim a 4 ms result without a Terraria benchmark. |
| Combat sequence enables later geometry | Continuous enemy pose/velocity, projectile flight, hit time, life/debuff/knockback and uncertainty | `enemy-pushed` does not say where it will be when the later shuriken flies; `enemies-aligned` is a derived, transient geometric predicate | The existing simulator is a starting fragment, but presently carries life/debuff, not knockback-updated enemy trajectory. |

The last row is concrete rather than theoretical. `SimulateUse.cs:283-294` computes a `SimHit.PushX`, but changes only the life ledger; `SearchAttackPlans.cs:418-445` carries remaining life and debuff marks into later segments. It does not pass an updated enemy pose or velocity to later trajectory simulation. Thus the README's bow-pushes-Demon-Eye-onto-zombie-line-then-shuriken-pierces picture is not yet modelled by the existing sequence engine. A scalar “push danger” charge is not enabling geometry. Every contender that promises that outcome needs a truthful physical transition; GOAP has the extra modelling choice of whether to discretise it into predicates or retain numerical transition state.

## Counterexamples against a global GOAP layer

### 1. Goal selection recreates arbitrary family priority

Suppose a nearby dark tile is urgent but drop-then-torch-at-the-player is shorter, while a slime newly lands on the drop. Proposal 04 identifies all three decisions—urgency, new identity becoming step one, and the same two identities swapping—as unresolved (`proposal/04…:25-35, 79-87`). A design that computes `priority(Fight)`, `priority(Light)`, and `priority(Collect)` first must pick a scalar winner before it can see the shared route/order. Equal priorities need a tie policy; tiny score changes select a different complete goal and can churn the entire plan. Fixed priority merely relabels the rejected “combat always step 1” or task-kind rule.

Utility is not itself the failure. It can select among *grounded outcome courses* by actual expected work, urgency and cost. The failure is using utility to nominate one named desired outcome before candidate construction. To avoid it D must construct enough cross-goal candidate sequences for utility to compare. At that point the key object is a numerical course with resource/time effects, not a simple choice among symbolic goals.

### 2. One goal at a time loses useful, compatible work

The expected product has the body travelling while firing in combat and otherwise keeps weapons quiet; current code enforces combat-only hands in `CoordinateBrainTick.cs:241-271`. That is deliberate current scope, not proof that a future course must be exclusive. A travel-to-ore step can therefore be compatible with a firing sequence during combat, and a torch/drop/pot can be an insertion on an already-paid route when hands and edit permission permit it. The basic GOAP execution loop serialises an array of actions; making `GotoOre` own the agent hides a firing opportunity, while splitting it into parallel actions creates shared-body and hand conflicts that ordinary GOAP effects do not resolve.

This is a prerequisite shared by all contenders: an executor must receive explicit resource grants/claims and refuse incompatible effects. It is a particular weakness for a global GOAP plan because its natural single ordered action list suggests false exclusivity. D can repair it with temporal actions, resource intervals and partial-order planning, but that is materially beyond the simple approach described.

### 3. Boolean symbolic state cannot carry the relevant numbers

Duration, travel, player intent, clearance, health, weapon stamina, projectile time of flight, range, chance and spatially local invalidation are not decoration. They decide whether the companion should complete a vein, return to a player who entered a cave, wait for a future shot, or abandon a target. Proposal 04 says the system cannot adopt a frozen “stick” rule and specifically requires comparing finishing-then-coming with coming (`proposal/04…:27-32`).

Discretising each dimension makes symbolic planning possible only by accepting bucket error at the boundary where this product is most sensitive. Grounding exact numeric values, poses, trajectory samples and several-site subsets multiplies action instances. Keeping numbers procedurally inside actions avoids the explosion but makes effects conditional estimates whose truth the symbolic search cannot establish. The reasonable hybrid is numeric state plus symbolic causal dependencies, but D should name that as a hybrid and test its transition error; it is not classic Boolean GOAP solving this representation for free.

### 4. Regression search grounds the expensive choice too late or too broadly

Backward GOAP starts at a desired condition and considers every action with a matching effect, then recursively satisfies their preconditions. With `torch-at(site)`, `collect(drop)`, `mine(vein)`, `attack(weapon,target,stand,aim,time)` and `travel(from,to)`, effect matching must bind concrete identities before cost/route/physical feasibility can be evaluated. If it grounds eagerly, the branching factor is sites × targets × weapon/stand/aim alternatives × predecessor choices. If it grounds lazily, it may not have a usable first step when the budget expires, or it may compare estimates conditioned on an incumbent/first nominated site rather than all alternatives.

There is no measurement here that says such a planner misses 4 ms; the claim is an unmeasured asymptotic and integration risk. Current combat work demonstrates why a claim needs an actual envelope: cached attack planning was measured around 12.4 ms p99 in the P1–P8/C1 suite at commit `03986f0`, despite a much slower uncached path (commit body). A global planner must publish the first executable prefix under its own budget and record `unresolved:budget-cut` separately from absent; it cannot simply decline useful work when expansion has not finished.

### 5. Target churn and plan validity need more than a goal’s truth value

The current combat hold demonstrates a nontrivial validity contract. It keeps a plan while its stand remains admitted/reachable, learning revision matches, identities are alive, a new hostile crosses a meaningful urgency threshold, the intent-region gap has not doubled, and execution has not stalled (`CommitAttackPlan.cs:195-285`). This is correctly neither every-pixel replanning nor permanent commitment. It is also currently incomplete: terrain invalidation in combat is global rather than spatial, a known open limitation.

For GOAP, `goal still unsatisfied` tells too little: a plan can remain aimed at a dead/changed target, or a goal can still be true while its only viable route/stand has disappeared. Conversely, `player-region-changed` does not necessarily invalidate a safe retained first action. D needs per-step evidence and dependency footprints, plan repair that keeps an already valid prefix, an invalidation reason, and a policy for whether a new candidate may insert before the current step. Replanning the whole chosen goal each observation repeats the 0.30.5 churn shape; holding it until goal completion repeats the deep-cave/slime failure.

### 6. Predicted effects must be receipts, with repair for error

The F.E.A.R. value proposition is strongest where failed action effects become knowledge and lead to another causal route. For this companion, a torch attempt may be blocked by protection, a pickup may become invalid, a shot may miss, a mine hit may reveal terrain, and a planned knockback may fail to align targets. The plan must distinguish “action executed”, “expected effect observed”, “partial effect”, and “effect contradicted”, then repair from the last verified state. Current telemetry already distinguishes native attempt outcomes; Proposal 04's capture shows 53 torch attempts but no completed companion torch credit (`proposal/04…:17, 46-51`). Declaring `area-lit` on attempted placement would be a false GOAP fact and would conceal the failure.

This receipt/repair contract is again common foundation. GOAP uniquely relies on it to maintain the soundness of preconditions/effects; without it a symbolic plan looks coherent while the world diverges.

## Fair comparison: what is common, what D adds, and where it helps

Do **not** reject D because the shared foundation is absent today. Every global contender needs: (1) all concrete candidate sites/targets/actions rather than family nomination; (2) tri-state reach/light/terrain and spatial invalidation; (3) stable identities, receipts and retained execution; (4) resource grants for body and hands; (5) a bounded candidate/refinement protocol; (6) numerical forecasts whose uncertainty is visible; and (7) a way to preserve a valid prefix while re-evaluating a changed suffix. Existing source contains parts of this: current selection prepares one representative activity per action before ordering (`ChooseBehaviour.cs:120-139`), and `OrderNearbyTasks` evaluates/recomputes local permutations rather than retaining a global course (`OrderNearbyTasks.cs:1-240`; also its `573d9d4` commit body). These are observations, not evidence that a different architecture cannot supply them.

D adds a valuable thing where it fits: causal composition. A new prerequisite action can become available to every workflow needing its effect, as Orkin's lights-before-`Goto` example illustrates. It also makes missing causal facts inspectable: if no action can produce an unsatisfied precondition, that is an observable planning failure rather than an implicit score collapse. It is strongest for a small, stable, alternative-method subproblem: e.g., choose among legal ways to obtain a bounded interaction condition when the effects, failures, and resource claims are genuinely enumerable.

D adds three non-negotiable liabilities if it is global: goal arbitration is still a policy; symbolic literals need a truthful grounding discipline; and a serial plan needs temporal/resource/concurrency extensions. Calling these “implementation details” would be the category error—the stated product is made of their boundary cases.

## Decision gate and falsifiers

Use D globally only if a prototype can demonstrate all of the following on representative scenes, within a defined budget:

1. It enumerates and compares concrete sites/targets/moves rather than selecting a family goal first.
2. Its state remains tri-state for unresolved reach and records a spatial dependency footprint; an unrelated world edit does not churn a course, while a relevant edit repairs it.
3. It holds a valid prefix, repairs only the affected suffix, and logs observed effect versus predicted effect.
4. It permits compatible body/hand work with explicit resource claims and forbids incompatible combinations deterministically.
5. It selects the jungle's new slime as a possible new first step without the old collect/torch pair exchanging first and second repeatedly.
6. It returns a first *valid executable* action by the chosen budget under candidate load, with bounded-refinement rather than absent as a distinct result.
7. For combat, either it truthfully propagates knockback and enemy trajectory into later shots or it explicitly declines to claim geometry-enabling combinations.

Failure of (1), (2), or (4) would refute D as the global architecture, rather than merely asking for more tuning. Passing them would still not prove its performance or player feel; it would establish the minimum truthful model worth comparing.

**Comparative conclusion.** Approach D should remain a conditional candidate as a local causal subplanner under a shared numerical course/execution layer. The scheduled-course approach is more direct if the dominant problem is choosing and repairing a concrete, concurrent sequence across work types; a bounded tactical simulation is more direct where effects alter geometry. GOAP becomes the stronger global candidate only if play exposes repeated alternate causal workflows that cannot be expressed as course ordering and local simulation without bespoke sequences, and the above gate shows that its grounded state/action vocabulary stays small enough to be fast, observable and truthful.

## Evidence limits and source ledger

* Local evidence is source/history/telemetry interpretation, not a new build, replay, native run, or live playtest. No Terraria-wide GOAP benchmark was found or asserted.
* The current “4 ms” requirement is treated as a requested latency constraint; this report does not infer it from the 12.4 ms combat-planning measurement, which is a different bounded simulator and hardware/JIT context.
* The external desk sources establish what GOAP and two concrete implementations claim. They do not establish a community consensus that GOAP is inappropriate for Terraria. The relevant implementer corpus examined was CrashKonijn's maintained documentation and a current open-source GOAP implementation; it adds execution/state ownership detail but no comparative consensus. **Verdict: the implementer material adds the operational boundary the original papers leave abstract; no consensus claim is warranted.**
