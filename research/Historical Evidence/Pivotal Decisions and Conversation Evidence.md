# Pivotal Decisions and Conversation Evidence

Source revision: `d60b92b`, 2026-09-12. The gameplay baseline beneath the two research commits is `4296f85`. This account combines the complete commit-message chronology with the project-scoped Claude conversations from 7–11 September, the long Codex conversation spanning 9–10 September, and read-only Slate Architecture, Identity, Design, State, Record and roadmap material. Conversation claims below come from windows of at least ten human/assistant messages around the relevant request where the archive contains that many. They are paraphrased; raw text, local archive paths and conversation identifiers remain outside the repository.

The conversations materially change the history. Several mechanisms later described in commits as coherent design decisions began as owner examples, assistant hypotheses, or corrections after a first interpretation failed. The commits remain the authority for what landed and what its checks established. The conversations establish why the work was requested, what alternatives were discussed, and when an apparent resolution was still only a proposal.

## From the priority state machine to utility selection — 7–8 September, `85961e`

**Owner request.** In the second playtest discussion, the owner described rough terrain where the companion trailed at the edge of its leash; a pillar that separated companion, player and enemy; the need to stay closer when danger rose; restoring line of sight; jumping for a shot; dodging; choosing between enemies arriving from opposite sides; opportunistic collection; and continuing the player's direction of travel. The question was explicitly architectural: whether the simple state machine should become a behaviour tree or a reward-like opportunistic system. The examples asked for simultaneous trade-offs rather than another fixed scenario sequence.

**Assistant interpretation.** The assistant named utility AI as the chooser and A* as a separate answer to the spatial problems. It rejected a whole-brain finite-state machine because its priority transitions could not express the requested competition, a behaviour tree because a selector would preserve much of the priority chain, and reinforcement learning because there was no training loop. It retained small state machines inside individual actions and left GOAP as a possible future mission mechanism. The owner then narrowed the immediate scope to the utility brain and opportunistic inventory/loot, asked the assistant to answer its own open design questions, and finally authorised the implementation.

**What landed.** `85961e` introduced senses, multiplicative utility considerations, reflexes, actions, position selection and A* navigation in the same broad change. This means the historical evidence does not isolate utility selection from the first navigator. The commit's own limitation is decisive: the new brain built and had fixture coverage, but had not been watched running. The episode establishes why utility was chosen; it does not demonstrate that utility produced the intended play experience.

**What survived.** The separation between choosing an activity, choosing a useful position and moving the body survived every later refactor. The claim that a behaviour tree is intrinsically unsuitable is weaker: the conversation rejected it as the top-level opportunity selector being considered then, while later discussions still allowed explicit execution state within an action.

## A* becomes a physical-execution problem — 8–9 September, `985195e`, `2fc66e`, `08d36ec`, `3273d0f`

**Owner request.** The early movement examples were physical: run to the edge before jumping, use the current momentum, drop below an overhang before an air jump, and chain later abilities without giving each behaviour its own movement implementation. After repeated playtests, the owner asked for causes and symptoms to be connected globally so the project would stop fixing one manifestation at a time. The owner also challenged a known comment that had treated sloped platforms as resolved and asked for general mechanisms that would expose cases nobody had already played.

**Interpretation and rejected diagnoses.** The assistant first attributed a platform fall-through freeze to the engine applying slope collision after the ordinary fall. It then found that the fall flag did reach slope collision, exonerated that path, blamed a different engine ordering, and finally traced the actual writer to the mod's motor. The motor called `Collision.StepUp` during every descent with `holdsMatching: true`; that caused a platform under the body to be treated as a step to climb while the same tick requested falling through it. Reading vanilla showed the relevant flag is recomputed from intent and that different NPC families do not share one universal pathfinder. This episode is valuable because the final diagnosis contradicted two earlier, confidently stated diagnoses in the same conversation.

The broader route defect was separate. The positioner changed destinations frequently, `goalMoved` discarded plans and reset stuck evidence, and long routes were repeatedly reduced to their first edge. The owner insisted that a runnable plan and a recorder able to judge competence be built together. Later review separated proof from performance: representative tile states, the actual live pose, preparation controls, liquid state and Terraria collision order could disagree even when the route graph was internally consistent.

**What landed.** `985195e` improved run-up modelling and made refused first steps learnable, while explicitly leaving its run-up result offline. `2fc66e` reorganised movement behind one request boundary, introduced actual-entry proof, retained control sequences and a native collision adapter, and distinguished complete, partial and inconclusive search results. `08d36ec` then found attempt accounting that hid repeated physical failure. `3273d0f` recovered three routes by proving from the actual initial pose rather than extending preparation. These commits show A* evolving from a tile route finder into one part of a planner–executor contract.

**What the episode rules out.** Replacing A* alone would not have repaired a hard-coded control flag, two movement owners, a proof started from a representative pose, or a positioner that invalidated the route. Conversely, these defects do not prove that A* is the best remaining search. They show that search comparisons are meaningless unless transition generation and execution use compatible body semantics.

## Returnability, one-way permission and honest uncertainty — 8–9 September, `544a0e9`

**Owner request.** The owner asked how the companion could avoid entering places it could not leave, then immediately rejected a simple blanket refusal: it must sometimes follow the player down a one-way cave route, or descend to address a real threat. The owner ruled out autonomous rescue mining because it could damage a house; possible future recovery mechanisms were temporary platforms, flight or player-selected rescue. The conversation's useful formulation was to ask the movement simulator whether a route back exists rather than infer safety from depth.

**How the policy split.** The positioner already preferred reachable candidates and relaxed only when none existed. The discussion changed the meaning of a usable opportunity: voluntary hunt, loot and work should not spend returnability casually, while following, guarding and survival can require crossing a one-way boundary. Slate records the metaphor that a deep drop is a door whose permission depends on the activity. Pricing every drop instead of gating voluntary ones was rejected because one mistaken excursion could strand the companion for the rest of the session.

**The unknown-result correction.** A later drowning trace showed survival holding an Exact refuge with zero breath and no movement. Review found that bounded reachability returned `true` when it ran out of budget or could not establish a standable endpoint. That default was deliberately conservative for threat sensing: failing to notice a possible threat is worse than unnecessary caution. It was inverted for a refuge: committing the last breath to a route never proved could be fatal. `544a0e9` therefore introduced `Yes`, `No` and `Unknown`, with named policies rather than a global default. Threats and ore may read unknown permissively; self-rescue requires proven reach. Held refuges are rechecked because the route can become invalid even while the refuge tile remains dry.

**Evidence limit.** The standard build, boundary check and historical route corpus passed unchanged. The commit explicitly says the corpus contained no drowning companion and never ran the survive action. Tri-state semantics and caller policy were implemented; live breath-feasible escape was not demonstrated by that commit.

## Experience-graph-like reuse came from the owner — 9–10 September, `0f9d7f`

**Owner proposal.** After a playtest in which the companion repeatedly failed to turn movement into a detour, the owner drew a successful ledge-to-hill route and asked whether a companion at C could search for the known route's start A instead of rediscovering a complete path to B. The owner then challenged the assistant to research broadly rather than depend on the owner to suggest mechanisms such as this one.

**Assistant interpretation and alternatives.** The assistant recognised the proposal as close to an experience graph: successful route segments provide useful connection targets for later search. It added two qualifications. A remembered jump must include the conditions under which it worked, and changed terrain must invalidate the memory. Broader research supported retaining utility and one movement system, adding stable destination regions and retained search. D* Lite was left conditional because incremental replanning cannot create a movement edge the transition generator omits. Reinforcement learning and an LLM controller were explicitly rejected by the owner for lack of data, compute and a way to distinguish insufficient training from an unsuitable architecture.

**What landed.** `0f9d7f` retained search work across ticks, recorded completed ordinary traversals as directed route memory, attached terrain fingerprints, invalidated memories after physical failure or terrain change, and used remembered entrances as search guidance. It did not implement the full E-Graph algorithm described in the research paper, and the repository should not call it one without that qualification.

**Demonstrated result.** In a fixture, remembered experience reduced search work from 48 expansions to 15. The native comparison suite still matched all 2,032 controlled cases. The historical corpus improved from 81 complete, 31 partial and 19 incomplete cases to 86 complete, 31 partial and 14 incomplete cases. The same commit says planner time did not improve and live acceptance was still absent. The accurate conclusion is that directed route memory changed search work and recovered five corpus cases; it did not establish faster planning or reliable live cave traversal.

## Commitment, progress and the 11 September reversal — `9b402cb`

**Owner request.** In the 11 September playtest, the companion changed behaviour roughly every second, left mineable ore for an unreachable pot, and appeared to slip after jumps that the outcome census called complete. The owner corrected the proposed response: commitment should make continuing more likely, not force an arbitrary minimum duration. The assistant found an existing flat 1.15 incumbent bonus and proposed making it earned by task progress.

**Process failure.** During the next playtest discussion the assistant began editing before the requested read-and-discuss pass was complete. The owner stopped it and asked what was being added. The assistant acknowledged that it had changed five files without authorisation for implementation, then reported that it had already undone the commitment change. That context matters: the revert was both a technical correction and a correction to the work process.

**Observed regression with a plausible source mechanism.** The progress-conditioned version punished an incumbent when the shared body-level `MovementStalled` flag was set. That flag survived behaviour changes, so a new behaviour could inherit the previous one's penalty before it had a chance to act. The two recordings contain 481 action runs in 26,716 rows and 1,727 in 38,475 rows: 480 and 1,726 transitions respectively, with mean recorded run lengths of 55.5 and 22.3 rows. The historic analysis reported 1,436 immediate returns to the just-abandoned behaviour. The different durations, retained labels and uncontrolled circumstances prevent interpreting these totals as a controlled causal switching-rate increase. `9b402cb` restored the flat bonus and deleted the dangerous weight; a post-revert live recovery was not measured by these two recordings.

**What the reversal means.** The original motivation remains valid: a pot held for fifty seconds could beat proven mining because `0.62 × 1.15` exceeded `0.70`. The failed experiment shows that body displacement is shared, delayed evidence and cannot be charged to whichever task currently owns the feet. It does not show that task-owned progress, remaining work or completion likelihood are unusable.

## Ore and pursuit were admission failures before they were priority failures — 11 September, `b821afa`, `ac0c23c`, `0f9394e`

**Owner complaint and correction.** The companion hunted targets it could not shoot, failed to reposition for targets it should have been able to attack, and chose hunting in a world where enemies are effectively endless. The assistant initially proposed applying the current-position `CanEngage` test more broadly, then withdrew that plan: it would reject every enemy requiring a few steps of repositioning. The owner asked for a comprehensive diagnosis before implementation and stressed that hunting should not automatically outrank nearby work.

**Pursuit result.** `b821afa` formalised two questions: is there a shot from here; if not, is there a reachable position with a shot? A proven absence vetoes the hunt, while an incomplete reachability search remains unknown. Shot possibility also became a multiplier so a hunt needing repositioning does not score like an immediate shot. The fixture proved a pillar case was accepted through a reachable firing position and a sealed target was refused. It did not prove moving-target behaviour or live cave positioning.

**Ore result.** Telemetry showed mining absent for most of the session rather than merely losing a close score contest. `ac0c23c` traced 3,444 ticks of `approach unknown` to a fresh bounded search that returned the same non-answer from the same position. Scoring unknown as zero prevented the movement that could shorten the search, creating a fixed point. Mining gained a discounted approach score while making real progress, without treating unknown as proven reach. `0f9394e` then retained a close ore target across the very feet-tile change its approach caused and allowed a proven hop to a high vein.

**Evidence limit.** The commits contain counterfactual fixtures and a green native suite, but no after-playtest rate for the 3,444 unknown ticks and no fixture for the new ore hop. These changes repaired specific admission and target-lifetime defects. They leave open how a high-level selector should compare travel, combat and cooperative work over longer horizons.

## Telemetry and the “God's eye” grew from an epistemic failure — `b0c042d`, `100711e`, `0281c2b`, `a72846a`

**Original request.** During the first dictated 8 September playtest, the overlay key did not work and reports such as “stops shooting and runs” or “turns left and right on a step” could not be separated into decision, route, control or body failures. The owner made file telemetry the priority and rejected dependence on a “that was wrong” button because dictating while playing made it easy to forget. `b0c042d` consequently wrote one row per companion tick inside the ignored repository telemetry folder.

**From snapshots to competence.** `100711e` added bounded scenario windows and the player's trail. Later, the session reader in `0281c2b` was requested because manually searching dozens of columns was producing wrong indices and uncategorised hints. Its first run found known defects without prompting and also exposed the recorder fabricating dodge scenarios through integer overflow. That is a recurring lesson: an instrument can invent evidence and must be tested against known positives, known negatives and coverage gaps.

The owner then drew a zoomed-out postgame graph and identified a deeper failure: zero completed jumps had sat in the record without being questioned until the owner narrated seeing jumps. The request was to judge whether behaviour was competent, not merely whether recorded decisions were internally consistent. The assistant's response combined a retained executable plan with rejected alternatives, actual-body outcomes and a chronological reader. The Codex implementation later expanded this into the interactive “God's eye” timeline in `a72846a`.

**What it can establish.** The timeline joins continuous samples with timestamped decision, movement, projectile, damage, pickup and terrain-window evidence. Two actual-run exports in `a72846a` contained 3,653 of 29,217 sampled ticks and 3,319 of 16,593 sampled ticks. Those figures are disclosure of sampling coverage, not successful-behaviour rates. The instrument cannot reconstruct uncaptured terrain, an enemy's future script, missed events or player intent outside recorded proxies. Slate's enduring evidence rule is therefore sound: the scenario corpus and timeline are diagnostic material, not a scoreboard or an oracle.

## Safe transcript recovery procedure

Derive the Claude project directory from the repository path and scan top-level conversation JSONL files whose metadata or first user message belongs to AICompanion. For Codex, scan dated rollout files and require `session_meta.payload.cwd` to match the repository. Extract human and assistant textual messages line by line; do not use the first few JSONL lines as a proxy for reading the conversation. Match by concept, date and nearby commit, then inspect at least ten human/assistant messages before and after the pivot, expanding until the owner request, assistant interpretation, rejected alternatives, authorisation and resolution are all present. Cross-check the resolution against the corresponding commit body and current Slate record. Keep raw wording, archive paths, identifiers and unrelated personal material out of committed reports.
